LOCAL VERMONT HIKES – SUGGESTIONS from Outings Chair Kathy Astrauckas
We have many local treasures that you may not have yet (or recently) explored. In these non-carpooling times,
loops or out-and-back trips are the way to go. Many balk at retracing their steps on an out-and-back, but the trail
always looks different in the other direction, and there are usually quite a few surprises that went unnoticed
on the first trip through.

    •   Appalachian Trail: UVO Section maintains 45 miles from US-4 in Killington to Norwich, but be aware
        that most A.T. parking areas are not plowed. The two on US-4 near Killington ARE plowed. Expect
        to need snowshoes by this point in winter, although some areas are heavily trodden, so microspikes
        might be sufficient in some cases. Snow stays longer in the woods than it does in the open.

Here are some of the many options where parking areas are likely to be plowed:

    •   Hurricane Forest and Hurricane Wildlife Refuge, Hartford
    •   Wrights Mountain, Bradford
    •   "My Walden", Sharon (UVLT)
    •   Army Corps of Engineers Union Village Dam Recreation Area, Thetford
    •   Morey Mountain, Fairlee (Parking is plowed, but parking area is rough. You can also park along the edge
        of the road BEFORE, not after, the Morey Mtn. driveway.
    •   Brookmead Conservation Area, Norwich (Plans are in the works to groom part of this area for X-C skiing!)
    •   Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Historical Park, Woodstock (In winter, ONLY X-C skiing and
        snowshoeing are allowed from the Prosper Road side; walking is OK on SOME trails from the Billings
        Farm side.)
    •   Quechee Gorge from Dewey's Pond
    •   Section 5, Quechee
    •   Parcel 5, Norwich
    •   Ottauquechee River Trail, Woodstock (NEW! Completed Fall 2020!)
    •   Bald Top, Fairlee
    •   CrossTown Trail (and others), Strafford
    •   Ballard Trail, Norwich
    •   Paradise Park, Windsor (Definitely good parking, reached from the Price Chopper parking lot on the east
        side of U.S. Route 5 just north of the center of town. Cross Route 5 and walk in the driveway to reach a
        nice network of foot trails.)
    •   Mount Ascutney Road (paved, unplowed), ascending as little or as much as you wish; picnic pavilion with
        nice view two miles up. Plowed parking on north side of VT-44A, across from the entrance to Mount
        Ascutney State Park.
    •   Lots of road walk loops in Norwich. Park at the Park & Ride on Turnpike Road. (e.g. Church Street,
        around Carpenter Street, then up next to the Norwich Inn on Beaver Meadow Rd, left onto Hobson Road,
        and back down Elm Street; OR Hawk Pine Road and loop) Check your Vermont Atlas and Gazetteer for
        ideas!

And for a more rigorous workout:
   • Blue Ridge Mtn, Mendon
   • Long Trail heading north or south from Brandon Gap or Middlebury Gap (ned snowshoes by now!)
   • Mt. Ascutney, Windsor/Weathersfield (The Windsor Trail is the best bet in winter, though the others are
        also certainly do-able)

Other resources for loads of local trails:

https://uvlt.org Choose the "Get Outside" menu, then click on "Public Access Trails and Conservation Areas";
scroll down to see all the areas under their purview, click on individual areas for info and maps. Note: they are
only sort of in alphabetical order. They were numbered, so the new ones are at the bottom, no longer
alphabetical. They hope to be redoing and improving their web site soon.

https://www.trailfinder.info

https://norwichtrails.org (also has maps on the web site)

                           AN INVITATION TO JOIN THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
Who are we? Founded in 1910, the Green Mountain Club was established "to make the Vermont mountains play a larger part in the
lives of people". To meet that goal, the club constructed the 272-mile Long Trail (LT). The GMC is responsible for developing
policies and programs aimed at preservation, maintenance, and proper use of hiking trails for the benefit of the people of Vermont.

What is the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section? Our section, one of 14 chapters of the GMC, is located in and around the Upper
Valley. We schedule activities all year round for those who enjoy the outdoors, including hikes, bike trips, canoe outings, cross
country skiing and snow shoe hikes. The "O" Section is responsible for the maintenance of 46 miles of Appalachian Trail (AT)
between Maine Junction at Willard Pass near Killington and the Connecticut River at Norwich, VT, including four trail shelters.

We invite you to join! If you wish to participate in our activities, meet friendly, outdoors loving people and contribute to the
maintenance and quality of Vermont's hiking trail system, the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section welcomes you. Through the
quarterly GMC Long Trail News and the "O"Section Footnotes you will get information about section and main club activities and
issues of interest. For more information, call Iris Berezin at 802-295-2294.
For membership, fill out the application below and send to Green Mountain Club, 4711 Waterbury-Stowe Road,
Waterbury Center, VT 05677, att. Rick Dugan/Membership. Please enclose a check, payable to the Green Mountain Club.
