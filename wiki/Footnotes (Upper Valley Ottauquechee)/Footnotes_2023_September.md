Irene revisited Report by Debbie Markus
The storms of July 10 to 11, 2023 brought disastrous flooding to many towns in Vermont. Initially, the Green
Mountain Club asked section volunteers to refrain from hiking the trails until GMC staff could assess damage
and advise on safety and risk. When that directive was lifted, several UVO members hiked their adopted trail
sections. One positive: the AT in our area weathered the torrential rains fairly well. There were minimal downed
trees or washed out areas.
The bridge over the Gulf Stream at the Rt 12 AT crossing in Pomfret survived the flash flooding. The concrete
bridge piers and the anchored span are intact, solid, and not undermined. The approach to the bridge on the Rt 12
side and the lower reaches of the parking lot, however, were washed away. While a large tree trunk is impaled
through the side railing and ballisters, this does not impede hikers from crossing the bridge safely. The steep
drop off at the end of the bridge is now marked with caution tape, and there is a way to safely exit the bridge.
Debris on the bridge showing how absurdly high the flood water rose has now been cleared. AT thru hikers and
section hikers, both northbound (NOBO) and southbound (SOBO) reported encountering no remarkable
problems on the trail itself. Many thanks to our adopters for walking their parts of the trail to check conditions
after the storms. Kudos also to our members who volunteered for trail maintenance days for continually clearing
water bars and working to address “trouble spots” that are prone to erosion. This all helps!

  The floods of 7/10-7/11 also tragically claimed the life of AT thru hiker Steady Eddie, Robert Kerker. Kerker’s
remains were found on 7/28 by a VT State Police SAR team assisted by several other cited agencies, including
canine support, 1.5 miles downstream of the Stoney Brook AT trail crossing. After leaving the Inn at the Long
Trail on 7/09/, Kerker had not contacted his family, nor had he made it to his reserved hotel in Norwich. Days
went by while the family sought his whereabouts. On 7/24 GMC contacted section coordinators and adopters to
hike to shelters in our sections to review the shelter logs for any possible posts by Steady Eddie. Dick Ruben
checked Thistle Hill Shelter and Debbie Marcus checked Wintturi Shelter; neither found any evidence he had
been there. VT State Police officially launched a missing person alert on 7/25, and widely publicized the search
via TV and social media. An AT hiker provided information that he had seen Kerker at Stoney Brook Shelter on
7/09 but not anytime after. This focused the search. It is assumed Kerker fell into the raging flood waters at
Stoney Brook, normally a pretty and peaceful spot. The power of water can be horrendous.

Trail Work at Thundering Falls
  On 7/26 Duncan Love and Debbie Marcus worked on cutting down the vegetation on the sides of the
boardwalk. Most of this work was accomplished with hand tools since the battery powered weed whacker
conked out quickly. In addition to being part of the AT, the Thundering Falls boardwalk draws many visitors to
the area who come to enjoy an attractive walk to the scenic waterfall. Also, the boardwalk is accessible for folks
of all abilities. While working on this maintenance project, we chatted to people from all over: Pennsylvania,
New Jersey, Maryland, and Indiana, and more. Beautiful wildflowers and native bushes laden with berries line
the boardwalk and birdsong is everywhere. What a gem!

                           AN INVITATION TO JOIN THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
Who are we? Founded in 1910, the Green Mountain Club was established "to make the Vermont mountains play a larger part in the
lives of people". To meet that goal, the club constructed the 272-mile Long Trail (LT). The GMC is responsible for developing
policies and programs aimed at preservation, maintenance, and proper use of hiking trails for the benefit of the people of Vermont.

What is the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section? Our section, one of 14 chapters of the GMC, is located in and around the Upper
Valley. We schedule activities all year round for those who enjoy the outdoors, including hikes, bike trips, canoe outings, cross
country skiing and snow shoe hikes. The "UVO" Section is responsible for the maintenance of 46 miles of Appalachian Trail (AT)
between Maine Junction at Willard Pass near Killington and the Connecticut River at Norwich, VT, including four trail shelters.

We invite you to join! If you wish to participate in our activities, meet friendly, outdoors loving people and contribute to the
maintenance and quality of Vermont's hiking trail system, the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section welcomes you. Through the
quarterly GMC Long Trail News and the "UVO" Section Footnotes you will get information about section and main club activities
and issues of interest. For more information, call Iris Berezin at 802-295-2294.
For membership, fill out the application below and send to Green Mountain Club, 4711 Waterbury-Stowe Road,
Waterbury Center, VT 05677, att. Rick Dugan/Membership. Please enclose a check, payable to the Green Mountain Club.
