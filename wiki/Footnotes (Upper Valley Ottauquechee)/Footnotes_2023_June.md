Peter Hope 25 August 1935 – 30 March 2023
(a very personal remembering)

                                                      Peter Hope and his wife Caroline MacDougall joined the
                                                      O-Section in the fall of 2004. We knew that he had worked
                                                      as a medical doctor in private practice in earlier years, and
                                                      that he was a very active member of the AMC. We soon
                                                      found his love of mountains and hiking extended now to
                                                      UVO’s outdoor activities: His first scheduled hike led us up
                                                      Cannon Mountain, and from then on his name was on the
                                                      leaders’ list at least two times every month. He led trips in
                                                      the Green and White Mountains and the Adirondacks,
                                                      many of them 4000 footers. In times of inclement weather
                                                      he took us to conservation areas in the Grantham area, or
                                                      on extended road walks. Peter was always cheerful and
                                                      smiling and willing to share his knowledge of nature with
                                                      his hiking partners. He was holding a full-time job with the
                                                      US Postal service right up to last September, yet this did
                                                      not keep him from being ready and in time for his UVO
                                                      scheduled adventures. They got shorter and closer to home
                                                      during later years: One group photo with him was taken on
                                                      July 10, 2022, during a beautiful figure-eight hike in
                                                      Grantham’s Brookside Park area. Two months later, Peter’s
                                                      first stroke cancelled out a number of further trips he had
                                                      planned.
Peter’s “Limmer” hiking boots and peaked woolen cap, his impish smile and cheerfulness are one side of our
memory of him. Yet we also frequently observed another one: A certain stubbornness made it hard for him to
adapt to necessary changes in plans, were it for reasons of weather or a participant’s health problems. Adjusting
the length and difficulty of his outings according to his own age limits was not on his agenda. Peter did not like
to turn around and would rather use a shortcut than go back the same way – and these shortcuts often turned out
to be bushwhacks. They happened so frequently that we told Peter we would have a bushwhack-memorial-party
for him after his death. We did! On May 10, we walked the Grantham Brookside Park trails in his memory.
                                                                                               Inge and Heinz Trebitz


Reports from Trails and Shelters Coordinators
Nick Krembs: I haven't heard if the Forest Service is going to have any crews working this summer on our trail
stretches. Lorne Currier has signed up a school and teacher in Rutland to adopt the AT segment from
Sherburne Pass Trail down to Kent Pond through Gifford Woods St Park. He's excited about it and it might be a
good story about teaching stewardship with the sustainability of a committed teacher and a school program.

Bob Hagen: Trail and shelter adopters have taken advantage of the break in April showers to get out in the
woods, take care of business and report on what still needs to be done. Debbie Marcus led the first section
workday April 29 on the Cloudland Road to Route 12 piece. At least nine blowdowns were sliced up and
removed from the trail. Some drainage problems were addressed and a random assortment of limbs were
cast aside. A productive day. Adopters will continue to go out in coming weeks.

We may need another workday for Japanese Knotweed control at the AT crossing with Rte 12 in Woodstock.
Look out for future announcements.

A Beetlebung Tree? What is that?
Fitting for Arbor Day, Dick Andrews took us to a Black Gum Swamp, way down south, close to the Mass. border. Hidden deep inside the J.Maynard
Miller Town Forest of Vernon are stands of Tupelos, rare in Vermont because they are at the northern edge of their normal range. The trees, some
over 400 years old, have a twiggy, twisted look. Wind, snow, ice and fire have shaped them over time. The dark brown bark is deeply furrowed and
knobby. The trees tower above a deep layer of peat and a vibrant green forest floor covered in sphagnum mosses.
And the strange name? On Martha’s Vineyard, in Massachusetts, this tupelo species is called “beetlebung”, perhaps for its use in making the mallet
known as a beetle, used for hammering bungs (stoppers) into barrels.                                                             Inge Trebitz

                           AN INVITATION TO JOIN THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
Who are we? Founded in 1910, the Green Mountain Club was established "to make the Vermont mountains play a larger part in the
lives of people". To meet that goal, the club constructed the 272-mile Long Trail (LT). The GMC is responsible for developing
policies and programs aimed at preservation, maintenance, and proper use of hiking trails for the benefit of the people of Vermont.

What is the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section? Our section, one of 14 chapters of the GMC, is located in and around the Upper
Valley. We schedule activities all year round for those who enjoy the outdoors, including hikes, bike trips, canoe outings, cross
country skiing and snow shoe hikes. The "UVO" Section is responsible for the maintenance of 46 miles of Appalachian Trail (AT)
between Maine Junction at Willard Pass near Killington and the Connecticut River at Norwich, VT, including four trail shelters.

We invite you to join! If you wish to participate in our activities, meet friendly, outdoors loving people and contribute to the
maintenance and quality of Vermont's hiking trail system, the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section welcomes you. Through the
quarterly GMC Long Trail News and the "UVO" Section Footnotes you will get information about section and main club activities
and issues of interest. For more information, call Iris Berezin at 802-295-2294.
For membership, fill out the application below and send to Green Mountain Club, 4711 Waterbury-Stowe Road,
Waterbury Center, VT 05677, att. Rick Dugan/Membership. Please enclose a check, payable to the Green Mountain Club.
