Vic McGee remembered
       Vic McGee, 77, a member of the O-              Vic played cricket and rugby in his youth, in
Section for twelve years, died on Oct.6, 2013         his later life switching to tennis and running.
of prostate cancer.                                   In the O-Section we knew him mainly from his
                                                      fall roadwalks in the Pomfret area. We called
                                                      them walks, since they took us along roads.
                                                      However, they were not meant for the Sunday
                                                      afternoon strollers. Vic had his 6-8 mile loops
                                                      meticulously planned, up and down beautiful
                                                      country lanes, with many stops to enjoy the
                                                      views and contemplate nature.
                                                      About a week before he died, Vic allowed me to
                                                      spend some minutes at his bedside. He was
                                                      sorry not to be able to lead all the trips he had
                                                      planned. When I mentioned that we might
                                                      name one of the walks for him, he could not
                                                      decide which one was his favorite – “Maybe the
                                                      one on Wild Apple Road? Or when we started
                                                      on Cloudland Road? I wish I could still send
                                                      you an email!”. And if he had, I am sure he
                                                      would have signed it the way he always did,
                                                      with a big CHEERS!

YOUNG PEOPLE WORKING ON THE
    APPALACHIAN TRAIL

        If you have ever hiked our section of the AT
between Pomfret Road and Rt.12, you must
remember Totman Hill, that sloping, water-logged
field, where hikers sank into the mud up to their
knees, and desperately tried to find a way around.
Not anymore!
        In spring of this year, a scout by the name of
Graham Melville was looking for a trail to work on as
his Eagle Scout project. When Totman Hill was
suggested, it seemed to be an impossible piece of
work. But in a joint effort between Green Mountain
Club, Forest Service, Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller
National Historical Park, and the Scouts (and their
parents) it all came together.
        On a weekend in August, Graham’s scouts
dug trenches, lugged boards and tools, and built a
series of steps and boardwalks all the way up the hill.
You can now walk up (almost) without getting your
boots wet. It is not quite finished, but what an
improvement!

                  AN INVITATION TO JOIN THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
Who are we? Founded in 1910, the Green Mountain Club was established "to make the mountains of Vermont
play larger roles in the lives of people". To meet that goal, the club constructed the 272-mile Long Trail (LT).
The GMC is responsible for developing policies and programs aimed at preservation, maintenance, and proper use
of hiking trails for the benefit of the people of Vermont.

What is the Ottauquechee Section? Our section, one of 14 chapters of the GMC, is located in and around the
Upper Valley. We schedule activities all year round for those who enjoy the outdoors, including hikes, bike trips,
canoe outings, cross country skiing and snow shoe hikes. The "O" Section is reponsible for the maintenance of 46
miles of Appalachian Trail (AT) between Maine Junction at Willard Pass near Killington and the Connecticut River
at Norwich, VT, including four trail shelters.

We invite you to join! If you wish to participate in our activities, meet friendly, outdoors loving people and
contribute to the maintenance and quality of Vermont's hiking trail system, the Ottauquechee Section welcomes
you.. Through the quarterly GMC Long Trail News and the "O"Section Footnotes you will get information about
section and main club activities and issues of interest. For more information, call Patty Spencer at 802-333-3571.

For membership, fill out the application below and send to Green Mountain Club, 4711 Waterbury-Stowe Road,
Waterbury Center, VT 05677, att. Joe Sikowitz. Please enclose a check, payable to the Green Mountain Club.
