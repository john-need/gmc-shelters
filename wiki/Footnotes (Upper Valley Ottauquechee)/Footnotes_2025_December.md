                                                     Some ten years ago, the Boy Scouts layed down
                                                     a series of puncheons for safe footing and
                                                     keeping your boots out of the mud. They were
                                                     not well anchored and started floating down hill.
                                                     They deteriorated after a short while.
                                                     On October 8, a UVO crew organized by Bob
                                                     installed new puncheons. This was an all UVO
                                                     section project: Bob and Phil did the scouting
                                                     and measurements, John Morton (the XC trail
                                                     builder) provided the lumber – our section

         Annie Janeway’s late October Hike in Sharon
On November 2 thirteen GMC members and trip partcipants walked 3.2
miles of woods roads on the “My Walden” property conserved (1999) and
finally donated to the Upper Valley Land Trust 2024 by Shiela and Steve
Swett. The 236-acre property is near Downer State Forest and has many
paths to explore, but no signs by design. The picture (by Annie Janeway)
shows the group at an overloook that has benches and a memorial
sculpture. A fabulous view included the first snow-covered ski slopes at
Killington. Many of of those along for the hike had the pleasure of
experiencing this tranquil spot for the first time.     A.Janeway

                                                          Iris’ Hike on Marsh Billings All photos: A.Janeway
                                                          Monday, November 3 was a great day for a walk/hike
                                                          on the trails at MBRNP. Nine members of the UVO
                                                          Section gathered in the Prosper Road parking area
                                                          before heading out on the lesser traveled trails. The
                                                          beauty of these trails is that you can enjoy the park
                                                          without encountering any or many other people, and
                                                          there are four benches for stopping to sit, snack and
                                                          enjoy the scenery. The colors had faded, but the view of
                                                          iconic Mt. Ascutney did not disappoint. Although the
                                                          park was closed due to the government shutdown, the
                                                          trails are always open and free to walk on.Iris Berezin

                                                                Patrick’s Climb of Owl’s Head
                                                          On October 26, 2025, seven UVO friends and Whidbey
                                                          the dog signed up for a popular "52 with a view" hike up
                                                          Owl's Head that would also include a peakbag of Mount
                                                          Martha. The weather forecast that morning predicted a
                                                          twenty percent chance of precipitation with both rain
                                                          and snow showers possible. We decided to go for it, but
                                                          made sure to caution all to pack for winter weather. We
                                                          began the hike up Owl's Head Trail with a drizzle
                                                          that turned to a light rain as we gained elevation.

The twenty percent precipitation forecasted was clearly one hundred percent in our location and as we approached
                                                                  the summit of Owl's Head it changed to snow,
                                                                  with about an inch of accumulation on the
                                                                  ground that made for some slippery footing.
                                                                  The summit was socked in (see picture), but we
                                                                  stopped there anyway for conversation and a
                                                                  quick lunch. The precipitation wasn't slowing
                                                                  and since we knew there wouldn't be any view
                                                                  to enjoy, we all agreed to skip the planned hike
                                                                  over Martha's Mile to Mount Martha, and
                                                                  instead turn around to hike back to the parking
                                                                  lot and call it a day. On the way down the
                                                                  newly fallen snow on the trees made for some
                                                                  beautiful winter forest views, and we were
                                                                  treated to a flock of loudly yelping turkeys that
                                                                  Whidbey particularly enjoyed!


All things considered it was a great day, and the hot cup of coffee we stopped for on the way home really hit the
spot!                                                                                      Patrick O’Neill

                           AN INVITATION TO JOIN THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
Who are we? Founded in 1910, the Green Mountain Club was established "to make the Vermont mountains play larger part in the
lives of people". To meet that goal, the club constructed the 272-mile Long Trail (LT). The GMC is responsible for developing
policies and programs aimed at preservation, maintenance, and proper use of hiking trails for the benefit of the people of Vermont.

What is the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section? Our section, one of 14 chapters of the GMC, is located in and around the Upper
Valley. We schedule activities all year round for those who enjoy the outdoors, including hikes, bike trips, canoe outings, cross
country skiing and snow shoe hikes. The "UVO" Section is reponsible for the maintenance of 46 miles of the Appalachian Trail (AT)
between Maine Junction at Willard Pass near Killington and the Connecticut River at Norwich, VT, including four trail shelters.

We invite you to join! If you wish to participate in our activities, meet friendly, outdoors loving people and contribute to the
maintenance and quality of Vermont's hiking trail system, the Upper Valley Ottauquechee Section welcomes you.. Through the
quarterly GMC Long Trail News and the "UVO"Section Footnotes you will get information about section and main club activities
and issues of interest. For more information, call Iris Berezin at 802-295-2294.
