The blow-down and the rock-slide of the
1950's carried away most of the large timber.
     The patter of the helicopter    echoed         strangely
in the deep trough of Avalanche Lake.             So did
the banter of the trail crew. They had their
rights, of course.   They were up in the middle
of nowhere doing a job. They were not paid
for overtime.   They were eating meals that
cost about 60 ·cents a day, and "that's a lot
of porridge. "
    The atmosphere    of shop labor seemed odd
in a room with no roof.   But the table of snow
on frozen Avalanche Lake, and the huge presence
of Mt. Colden on one side and Mt. Caribou on
the other tempered the grousing, kept it low-
key and good natured.
     The hitch-ups will be rebuilt.   They will
have a more modern character.     In fact, they
will no longer be hitch-ups - sitting four feet
above the water line.
