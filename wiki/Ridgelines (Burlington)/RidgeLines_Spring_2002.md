                       WELCOME NEW MEMBERS
Membership in the Burlington Section has increased steadily over many years. We now have 637
memberships and since 234 of these are family memberships, we count 871 adult members. This year,
we contacted over 600 at-large members in Chittenden County inviting them to join the section; thirty-
seven did. Thank you to Amy Anderson Talbot for soliciting volunteers to staff membership tables at
EMS Club Day, the Taylor Series and other events.      -Dot Myer (from annual meeting report)

Last year, Ridge Lines completed 30 years of recording Burlington Section history.

Ridge Lines began when Newell Briggs, section president, and Shirley Strong, who later became the first
woman president of the GMC, approached me at annual meeting and said the section needed a
newsletter and I was needed to do it.

I had just joined the GMC because I was hiking the Long Trail on my way to becoming an end-to ender.
The idea of editing a newsletter was not on my mind when I went to the meeting, but when I went home, I
had agreed to do it. Being editor was not an elective position and no one said anything about term limits.
I've taken advantage of that.

Producing Ridge Lines has never been a one-person job. Of all the people who have contributed, three
stand out: Madeline Miles, who helped get it started; Corky Magoon, who for years did layout, typing and
copying; and Len Carpenter whose skills were invaluable when we began producing the newsletter on
computer.

Profiles of past members included personal reminiscences of James Taylor by Edward Crane (of the
Burlington Free Press) and John Cowles. John Cowles also wrote his recollections of Will Monroe. In one
issue, we honored Roy Buchanan.

Ridge Lines covered some heated issues, especially the debates over the removal of stoves from
shelters and the proposal to make the Long Trail a National Scenic Trait. Corky wrote a wonderful
editorial about presidential candidate Philip Crane who dragged 25 reporters and politicians to the top of
Camel's Hump but failed to keep them from stepping on the grass.

Ridge Lines reported on the section's money making projects. T-shirts we silk-screened were a success;
bumper stickers were not. Bottles collected in shelters and on the trail netted $8.00; a used equipment
sale, $600.

I have two enduring memories from my years as editor: 1) Burlington Section members are willing to go
