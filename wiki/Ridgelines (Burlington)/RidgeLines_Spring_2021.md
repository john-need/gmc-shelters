 FROM THE BURLINGTON SECTION’S ANNUAL MEETING
 Our Annual Meeting this year was an on-line ZOOM event, held Saturday evening, January 30.
 There was a brief business meeting in which the Section’s President and the chairs of various
 committees reported on activities during this most unusual year. Members of the audience had a
 chance to ask questions and make comments about this very unusual year in Green Mountain
 Club history.
 Then participants heard a presentation by Jack Mayer, doctor, poet, and member of the GMC
 Breadloaf Section. Poems from the Wilderness included some of Jack’s wilderness–inspired
 poetry, written along the trail, expressing love of the backcountry and trail-walking. The poems
 emphasized “wilderness effect,” that unique sensation of aliveness and deep connection that
 comes to so many people who enjoy Vermont’s hiking and walking trails.

President’s Report
Submitted by Jonathan Breen
The past year has presented many challenges to the usual activities of the Burlington Section. The
pandemic has and continues to cause significant disruptions to our usual set of in- person activities.
However, efforts to adapt have created new opportunities. The pandemic has also spotlighted the
importance of the Long Trail system and other Vermont outdoor resources, something our club members
know already, and our dedication to maintaining the accessibility of the Long Trail system did not wane.
Many of our activities are meant to develop personal connections between members and establish
connections with people outside our club. The usual way of advertising group hikes, meeting in large
numbers for trail maintenance outings and conducting executive meetings in- person were not available
to us for much of the year. Like so many others we have adapted to holding meetings by video
conferencing. While I look forward to being able to meet again in- person, this disruption has changed
our way of thinking and brought about new opportunities. One of these is the newly established monthly
meeting of Section Presidents. Established by Tom Candon, President of the GMC, we now enjoy the
opportunity to come together without the need to travel to discuss current issues and share ideas.
During 2020 we saw a surge in the use of the Long Trail system. Our club already knows the importance
of having outdoor spaces accessible; after all, “The mission of the Green Mountain Club is to make the
Vermont mountains play a larger part in the life of the people by protecting and maintaining the Long
Trail System and fostering, through education, the stewardship of Vermont’s hiking trails and mountains.”
The pandemic just put a spotlight on the topic and encouraged many more people to take a hike. Even
with restrictions on our ability to do trail and shelter maintenance, we still got out to clear blowdowns and
identify important issues. Maintaining our trails under the weight of increased use is now our challenge.
Speaking of taking a hike, being a relative newcomer to the Burlington Section I realized I need to
strengthen my own connection to the Long Trail and side trails we maintain. Over the past year I have
made a point to hike more of the trails our section maintains and to visit more of the shelters. Day trips
brought me to Taft Lodge (several times) and Puffer Shelter for the first time. I also finished hiking all of
the Long Trail within our section, covering sections from Bolton Mt. to Taylor Lodge and from Smuggler’s
Notch to the Chin. A couple of blue blaze trails included Profanity and Wampahoofus.
The challenges of the past year spotlighted the dedication of our volunteers and members. I would like to
thank Annette Seidenglanz, former Marketing & Communications and Education chair, and Kayla Weeks,
former Treasurer, for their service to our section. Dana Baron spearheaded our efforts to celebrate the
Taft Lodge 100th year anniversary, with many others pitching in to make it a memorable event. Steve
Titcomb, Toben Glavin and Clare Innes stepped forward to fill Officer and Committee Chair positions.
These are just a sampling of the many volunteer efforts over the past year. I also want to mention the
impact of our members on the financial health of our section and club overall. We are fortunate that
increased trail use has also come with some increase in members and member dues. In my role on the
Budget and Finance Committee of the main club, I have also seen the generosity of our members
stepping up to make sure our club has the financial resources it needs to thrive even during these
financially uncertain times.
Last year as I began my first term as President, I certainly did not predict the challenges that we came to
experience. However, I did come into this role knowing we had an engaged and dedicated Executive
Committee and a membership with strong connections to the Long Trail. Our section’s accomplishments
over the past year have only served to strengthen this and I know we are ready for the challenges ahead.

2020 Treasurer’s Report
Submitted by Steve Titcomb
The principal source of income for the Section is membership fees. This amounted to $10,217, which is
about $2000 more than for each of the previous two years. The 2020 budget reflected those results, so
this was a significant increase. The only other two sources of income were the annual meeting and the
Taylor Series. The annual meeting income of $686.90 was almost twice the expected amount. The
Taylor Series event brought in $215, which was somewhat less than expected. Our total income was
$11,118.90, which was $2468.90 more than expected.
Our Operating Expenses of $7,518.29 were only $86.71 less than our budgeted amount, but some of the
individual budgeted items were substantially different than planned. By far the largest expense was
printing and postage for Ridge Lines. Note that in previous years the printing and mailing costs were
combined in a single line item. However, I thought it would be more informative if I separated these.The
total cost of printing is indicated as $4,769.41, but this includes a late payment of $349.80 for the
December 2019 mailing that should have been accounted for last year. The total postage expense for
the year is indicated as $845.18, but this includes the mailing permit fee ($240). The budgeted amount
for the mailings line item was $225.00, which was apparently the previous fee amount. Thus, not
including the late payment for 2019 and the permit fee, the total Ridge Lines expenses amounted to
about $5000.00. This compares with the budgeted amount of $3500. Ridgelines accounted for almost
two-thirds of our total expenses for the year. I would also like to point out that the total includes over
$250 of state sales tax for printing. Apparently, we had not given the printer a tax exempt certificate last
year. I have submitted one for this year, so this will save us some expense. More generally, we should
provide a copy of our tax exempt certificate to the vendor for any significant purchase in the current year.
I can provide a PDF or printed version of this form, and it must be provided to the vendor at the time of
purchase.
A large relative disparity between the budget and actual expenditures was in the Trail Maintenance
category. $1000.00 was budgeted, but we only spent $31.08. Looking back at previous years reports, it
appears that chainsaw purchase, maintenance, and ancillary materials and equipment were a
significantexpense. We did not have these in 2020.
Another category in which there was a budgeted amount ($500) was Education, but there were no
expenses related to this category in 2020.
We had one special project last year that involved an expense, and this was the Taft Lodge Anniversary
event. Total expense was $328.39. We had budgeted $500 under the special projects category
ostensibly for the t-shirt project. However, there were no expenses last year for that project.
Our income minus expenses this year amounts to $3,600.61. In addition, we earned $22.74 on the two
CDs we have from bequests. Our total balance, not including monies that reside with the main club, is
$36,439.42. Some numbers for the proposed 2021 budget are included in the accounting report, but are
subject to change pending approval at the annual meeting.

2020 Outings Report
Submitted by David Hathaway
We had 16 trips scheduled before closing down group hikes due to COVID-19 on March 13, and another
28 that were scheduled but canceled for the rest of the first and second quarter. When things opened up
again in the summer and the GMC Section Outings Working Group COVID era outings guidelines were
put out at the end of June, we started scheduling a few trips. However, we opted not to announce them
far in advance or publish in Ridge Lines due to the uncertain future trajectory of the pandemic. This
proved wise, since trips were shut down again on November 13.
During the relatively open summer and fall, we had 15 section outings. A particular shout out to Dale
Schmit, who started as a trip leader this summer, and led 7 trips during the 4-month window in which
they were allowed!
Starting January 6 of this year, we are once again allowed to lead group hikes but with more severe
restrictions, including no carpooling outside a single household and mask wearing at all times. We have
not yet begun to schedule group trips, but will post them on the website and send out an email to Section
members when we do. We remain hopeful that we can get back to scheduling and conducting hikes in a
somewhat more normal manner later in 2021.

2020 Trails Report
Submitted by John Sharp
As with everything this past year, the 2020 spring walk-through and trail work were very different due to
COVID. Groups were limited to 2-4 people, and the use of chainsaws was prohibited until July. Both of
these restrictions limited what trail work we could get done early. A walk-through was completed by the
end of July. While the trail was cleared of blowdowns, little or no clipping was done and water bars were
hardly touched.
2020 trail clearing was done by Paul Beliveau and family members, David Hathaway (with his new
electric chainsaw), Ted Albers, Jonathan Breen, and myself. In addition, Paul, David and I have gone out
several time to remove new blowdowns.
Since the trails were well-maintained the previous year, hikers didn’t seem to be hampered by the fact
that we were able to do less than usual. However, clipping and water bar maintenance will be needed in
spring 2021. A work party of eight people is needed to clip and clear blow downs. We hope that enough
people will have been vaccinated by May so the Section can schedule work hikes, and we hope that
chainsaws are allowed.

2020 Shelters Report
Submitted by Dana Baron
As this strange year comes to a close, there isn’t much to report for shelters, but here is what I have.
    1. Shelter adopters: Early in my tenure as shelters coordinator, I contacted Lorne Currier of the main
        club to get a list of shelter adopters in our section. I had some concerns about some adopters’
        availability, and I communicated those to Lorne. We agreed on a protocol of communication
        between the section and adopters but it never really came into play as there was little or no
        adopter activity to coordinate given the pandemic.
    2. Taft Lodge: as this venerable structure was approaching its 100th anniversary, we pulled together
        a celebration to honor this fact. The celebration took place on September 19 in the lower parking
        lot at Stowe Mountain resort. Leading up to the event, some minor repairs were done at the
        lodge, including replacing several broken windows. As part of the celebration, a plaque was
        placed in the Lodge. After the event, one of the footings for the front porch was reconnected to
        the bedrock. Much of this work was accomplished by Michael Dillon and Keegan Tierney. For
        next year, the decking on the front porch should be replaced.
    3. Twin Brooks: the privy roof was badly rotted and in dire need of replacement. Michael Dillon
        stepped up again and took care of this problem near the end of the field season.
    4. Puffer Shelter: I’ve received reports that this shelter is in need of a fair amount of work. I’ll plan on
        an early outing in 2021 (depending, of course, on COVID restrictions) to assess the needs and
        make plans to get it done.
That’s all I know of right now. Here’s hoping 2021 is not as crazy as 2020 and we can get out in the field
more.

2020 Director's Report
Submitted by Michelle Connor
The GMC main club is doing well and remains focused on maintaining the trail and supporting hikers in
Vermont. We have seen trail use increase as more people get outdoors during the pandemic.
Membership and donations have remained steady, and the club received funding from the Paycheck
Protection Program. We did run a reduced field season this year and the Visitor Center has been closed

Hollow and Judevine -- which ensured that more sections of the trail will remain accessible to future
hikers.
Staff are preparing various scenarios as they work with the Board on a budget for the next fiscal year,
depending on how the pandemic plays out. We expect some continued disruptions to operations over the
winter and spring, but are hoping to roll out a full field season crew this coming summer. GMC is
planning for a full caretaker program, two Long Trail Patrols and a construction crew.
We are excited to partner with Lenny's Shoe & Apparel on a promotion where they donate $5 to the
GMC for every pair of Darn Tough socks sold. The club also recently finished loading all of our trails into
Trail Finder (an app that helps users find new trails); GMC is listed as the trail manager for each, with
information on how to contact and donate to the club.
Happy trails!

2020 History and Archives Report
Submitted by Ted Albers
As GMC history tells us, “in 1917 the Green Mountain Club was incorporated as a non-profit in Rutland.
Rutland remained the base of operation until 1977 when the headquarters moved to Montpelier, and
then moved again in 1992 to Waterbury Center where it is today.” (Reidun Nuquist)
The GMC was an all-volunteer organization until about the time the club moved to Montpelier and Larry
Van Meter became executive director. Even as the club hired professional staff, volunteers carried out
the day-to-day management of its sections. To this day, records of the operations of the sections are
generally kept by its members. Section archives like ours are often handed down from one generation to
the next, and may sit for a decade, or even two or three, in a barn, attic or basement before being dusted
off and handed to a successor.
By such happenstance I received some Burlington Section archives during my tenure as president.
These included several crates of records (and a couple of scythes) from former section president and
historian, Dot Myer. I also received records that were once in the possession of Marion Holmes, Roderick
Rice and others.               – continued next page -

History and Archives Report – cont.
Since I managed to keep these records dry when my cellar flooded (not once, but twice), Jonathan and
Nancy decided that that qualified me to be section co-historian with Dot. In 2019 and 2020 Dot passed
on to me Burlington Section records dating to the founding of the GMC. This included an original 1917
LT Long Trail Guide (the first edition) and the first GMC financial report. Records also included a dozen
or so LT guides from the World War II years, glass slides, and the original receipt signed by Theron S.
Dean, GMC Treasurer, for $1 in dues paid by Mrs. Laura E. Cowles when she became a member on
May 24th 1916, among other gems. In 2020 Mike Debonis helped set up volunteer Ed Fitzgerald and me
so we could index these and other records into the Filemaker database the club uses to keep track of its
archives. We transferred several boxes of material that had come to me to the GMC archives for safe
storage. It’s a good start, but it's likely there are more records to preserve. If you happen to have
meaningful Burlington Section records, please let me know at ted@ted-albers.net

Hiking in Vermont’s Fifth Season
After a long winter, we’re all eager to get out hiking again – but wet and muddy trails aren’t ready
for us! Stepping on saturated soil can cause irreversible damage, and if we step off the trail to
avoid the mud, we encourage erosion by creating trenches for run-off.
The State of Vermont closes hiking trails on state land (including the trails on Mt. Mansfield and
Camels Hump) from mid-April to Memorial Day. The state also joins with the Green Mountain
Club, Green Mountain National Forest, and Vermont Department of Forests, Parks and
Recreation in urging responsible use of all of Vermont’s hiking trails between sugaring season
and Memorial Day.
Give our hiking trails a chance to dry out. They’ll need less maintenance and will be safer
and more enjoyable all summer and fall. Here are some tips:
Stay off Vermont’s sensitive summits. The delicate and rare tundra plants at the summits are
easily crushed or ripped out of the earth by the combination of boots and water.
Walk through the mud. Don’t walk around it. If a trail is so muddy that you have to walk on
neighboring vegetation, turn back and find a different place to hike.
Plan lower-elevation hikes. A trail may be dry at the trailhead but very muddy higher up. Spring
is a great time to walk in lakeside parks or hike on dirt roads, rail trails and recreation paths.
Some Places to Hike During Mud Season
Spring is a great time to explore parks, dirt roads, rail trails and rec paths. Here are some close
to Burlington:
    ! Red Rocks in South Burlington
    ! Shelburne Bay Park on Bay Road
    ! LaPlatte River Marsh Natural Area and the Ti-Haul Trail
    ! The Winooski Valley Park District manages many scenic outdoor locations in the greater
       Burlington area, including the Ethan Allen Homestead, Colchester Pond, Woodside
       Natural Area and more.
    ! The Burlington Bike Path
    ! Charlotte Park and Wildlife Refuge
    ! Mud Pond Conservation Area and Mud Pond Country Park in Williston
    ! Champlain Causeway (AKA Colchester Causeway)
    ! Mills Riverside Park, Mobbs Farm, and Kikas Farm in Jericho
    ! Preston Pond in Bolton/Jericho (info at https://www.alltrails.com/us/vermont/jericho)
    ! Wolf Run Natural Area in Jericho


Dirt roads also offer great springtime walks and hikes – and the Jericho-
Underhill area has lots of dirt roads!
In Underhill, park at Casey’s Hill and hike up hill to Underhill State Park. Or park at
the bottom of Stevensville Road for a strenuous hike to trailhead parking.
