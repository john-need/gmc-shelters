  President’s Report – submitted by Ted Albers
  The Burlington Section’s winter Taylor Series was hosted at the Richmond Free Library (thank you RFL
  for the donation of your space - again!) and featured Garry Harrington, a Vermonter (now living in
  Swanzey NH), an extreme outdoors overachiever, and the author of Chasing Summits: In Pursuit of
  High Places and an Unconventional Life. (The GMC has a copy at the clubhouse if you want to borrow
  it.) Garry walked us through peak bagging and ultra-marathons, freelance sports writing and film-
  producing. His achievements, energy and ambition are amazing to behold. During his talk, I started
  thinking about a few of the GMC outings I have lead or will soon lead: a LaPlatte River Marsh Natural
  Area ramble (zero elevation gain), Snake Mountain picnic (900’ gain), and a Little Otter Creek bird-
  watching paddle. Extreme? Not quite. Accessible and fun? Totally!
  The Burlington Section is fortunate. We have experienced volunteer trip leaders who are knowledgeable
  and accomplished New England and Adirondack peak baggers and can skillfully lead outings in the most
  difficult conditions. But we also have a few leaders who lead trips that are shorter and more relaxed,
  allowing participants to enjoy a morning in the woods, or on a cross-country ski-trail, or on a river,
  reservoir or lake. Each quarter, when Ridge Lines is published, there’s usually an outing or two for
  everyone. Maybe you think there should be more of one type or another? If so, let Dave Hathaway or me
  know. We always welcome new trip leaders – including for the easier outings, which tend to be well
  attended, a testament to their desirability. And to the peak baggers – we love you too! Stay in touch!


  Save the GMC some expense - and save a tree or two!
  Many Burlington Section members have already chosen to receive Ridge Lines electronically instead of
  getting paper copies in the mail. They are reducing paper use and, at the same time, helping the Section to
  trim expenses. If you’d like to start getting your newsletter as an on-line PDF only, just send us your name, at
  gmc@gmcburlington.org. Each quarter, you’ll get an email message letting you know when the latest issue is
  posted online and where you can read it or download and print it.

COMMITTEE REPORTS

Trails Report – submitted by Co-Chairs Pam Gillis and John Sharp
The Burlington Section did 4 trail work hikes in May of 2017, with 50 worker-days! We had good turnouts
on all the outings. Average turnout was over 12 people. Many thanks to our new volunteers and our
regular volunteers! Special thanks to George Long and Phil Hazen, who went on all the outings (along
with John Sharp) and to Dave Hathaway and Scott Alberson, who went on 3 of the 4 outings.
For the second outing (May 13), 7 AmeriCorp folks helped us so that day we covered Frost, Rock
Garden, Maple Ridge, Clara Bow and Rock Garden Trails, as well as the Nebraska Notch Trail, the
Butler Lodge Trail, and the Long Trail from Butler to Taylor Lodges.
Overall, we had quite a few trees to cut, especially when we cleared Lake Mansfield to Puffer Lodge. We
are putting both the new and the old chain saws to good use. We have just purchased 2 chainsaw packs
that will make carrying the chainsaws much easier and allow them to be put in the pack while the muffler
is still hot. We have an accessory pack for one of them, as the space for all the equipment plus your
personal items is quite limited. We will need a trail worker to carry extra stuff and keep pretty close to the
person with the saw.
If you see trees down or other trail work that needs doing, please let us know. On the right side of the
BTV GMC web page below the calendar, you click to let us know. Or just email Pam
(gillis1986@gmail.com). We did have folks go out and cut trees that were reported down during the year,
as well as after the October windstorm—most often, it was Dave Hathaway!
One last item: This will be my (Pam’s) last year as Trails co-chair. This will make 29 years in that position
(I thought it was going to be 30, but I rechecked the old outings schedules and I didn’t start until 1990).
This will be John’s 25th year as co-chair, but he served in many positions prior to that, including well
before I had my first position in 1983. So for 2019, we could really use a volunteer to be trails co-chair.


Outings report (David Hathaway):
We had another successful year of outings. We had 97 total scheduled outings led by 22 trip leaders,
compared to 90 led by 23 leaders in 2016 and 99 led by 25 leaders in 2015. These included 87 "regular"
outings published in Ridge Lines, and 10 outings that were exclusively listed in our new meetup group
(14 of the regular Ridge Lines outings were also listed in meetup). It also includes 5 Young Adventurers
Club outings led by Michelle Connor. Since not all trip leaders send me trip reports (hint, hint) I don't
have (and didn't try to gather) information on how many trips were canceled and how many people went
on various trips (mine ranged from 1 - just me - to 15). People do look at our trip reports, so I encourage
all leaders to send them, however brief, along with pictures if you have them.
We encourage everyone to join the meetup group (there's a link on the right side of our web page), and
anyone who thinks they might want to lead trips should contact me to be put on the trip leaders mailing
list (it currently has 64 names on it but most haven't led trips since I've been outings co-chair) and to be
made "event organizers" on meetup. Our only requirements are that you be a GMC member, that you
are willing to organize a trip, including communicating with interested participants, and that you are
interested in helping others enjoy the outdoors. If you are uncertain, you can reach out to any of our trip
leaders and co-lead a trip with them to learn the ropes. We also have a trip leader handbook that you
might find useful. We also encourage (but do not require!) our "regular" trip leaders to post their trips on
meetup. You can continue to vet potential participants by omitting a meeting location (you do have to
specify a date and time) and telling people to contact the organizer (leader) for that information. Contact
me if you want to know more about meetup.
We had 3 new trip leaders who hadn't lead any trips in the past 5 years (or ever): Michelle Connor (5),
Beth Ruskai (4), and Nancy McClellan (2). Nine other leaders led 4 or more trips: David Hathaway (22),
Ted Albers (10), Robynn Albert (7), Kim Farone (7), Wes Volk (6), Pam Gillis (6), John Sharp (6), Adam
Heckle (5), and Sheri Larsen (4). Trips included hikes, overnight backpacks, snowshoe trips, birding and
wildflower walks, bicycle trips, and paddling trips.

Director’s Report – submitted by Michelle Connor
The Director represents our Section to the Main Club and provides information from the main GMC to
Section members.
The first season of Bryant Camp rentals went well, with 75% of the available nights last winter booked
and good feedback from guests overall. One comment was that the building was quite cold at night, so
the club updated with a larger woodstove and replaced the windows for added insulation. There was
an interesting article in the winter Long Trail News about different types of waste management in the
back-country, with info specific to the system used at Bryant.
In August there was an airlift with materials to replace the roof on Butler Lodge and replace puncheon on
the ridgeline trails of Mount Mansfield.
The Bolton Lodge renovation was completed and there was a re-opening celebration in December.
Overnights are booking now! (See page 7 for photos of the January 28 potluck and oyster stew supper at
the Lodge, hosted by the Burlington Section.)
We completed the membership survey, which revealed similar trends as in the past (our members
are highly educated and have a high median income), but one surprising finding was that the median age
of members increased by 10 years since the 2000 survey.
The GMC board adopted the five-year Strategic Plan, which you can find on the web site. We're now
working on many of the areas, and are looking at the financing needed to reach our goals. The major
focus areas are: Protecting and Managing the Trail Resource; Operational Excellence; Engagement and
Inclusion; and Strengthening Our Members, Sections and Volunteers.
The membership passed a series of bylaw changes at the 2017 Annual Meeting allowing the Executive
Committee to act on the board's behalf in emergency cases (reporting back to the full board within 48
hrs), changing the composition of the Exec Committee, and adding term limits.
The next annual meeting for the whole club will be hosted by the O-section in Fairlee on June 1-3.
 And finally, we were saddened by the passing of Dave Hardy, Director of Field Programs. He was an
icon in the GMC community and will be deeply missed. The club hosted a celebration of life in January.

Treasurer’s Report – submitted by Joel Tilley
Our section finances are in good shape. Our total income of $8,902.77 was actually $97.23 less than we
expected because the membership check from the main Club that normally would have come in
December actually just came yesterday (putting it in the 2018 year). It was for $1,767, so that would have
put us well over our budgeted income. We started getting these checks deposited electronically last year,
so it’s completely out of my control.
Our Operating Expenses in the past have tended to be quite a bit less than we budget for; last year was
a little different. Our biggest expense is Ridge Lines, and it was actually $179 over budget; the Annual
Meeting was $102 under. Trails included money for chainsaw maintenance, tools, and a chainsaw
backpack. We increased the budget amount to $1,000 to accommodate another backpack. We have
additional Outings expenses for the meetup group.
So, our Operating Expenses of $5,442.80 were $912.20 less than our budgeted amount. We made no
big donations to the Main Club last year, so the $2,645 amount for “Donations to GMC” wasn’t spent. Our
total Income was $3,459.97 more than our Total Expense, which is equal to the increase in our checking
account over the year. As you can see in the list of Section Assets at the bottom, we also made $25 in
interest on the CDs we have from the Payne Bequest that we received a couple of years ago.
The proposed budget for 2018 includes increased the amounts for Trails, Outings, and Ridge Lines, for a
total of $7,855. If income and expenses come in as budgeted, we’ll have $1,145 available for donations,
or to carry over into next year.
This will be my last Treasurer’s report. It has been a pleasure to give something back to the GMC in this
way, and I have been honored by your trust. Next year I expect to be sitting at the Annual Meeting and
listening to Kayla, and I have every confidence she will do an excellent job.

about any changes. Check with trip leader before bringing your pet along. Human guests are always
welcome. Trip leaders often arrange carpooling. If you ride along in someone else’s car, we recommend
