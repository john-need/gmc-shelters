                        A 3 Part History of Taylor Lodge (Part 1)

                        Editors Note:
                        It’s always fascinating to come across archival
                        material related to The Burlington Section of
                        the GMC, especially the development of its
                        shelters. This account of Taylor Lodge is
                        reproduced (with minor edits from) Remarks at
                        Dedication of 3rd Taylor Lodge, Sept. 1978, by
                        Jan Abbott, GMC Burlington Section President.
                        This is part I of three, with more to follow in
                        future Ridgelines. - Ted Albers

                        Taylor Lodge is in Nebraska Notch, a deep cleft
                        between the Forehead part of Mt. Mansfield
                        and neighboring Mt. Dewey to its south. The
                        trail to the Lodge starts at the sign-in at the end
                        of Stevensville Road in Underhill.




                        By the summer of 1925 need was felt for a
                        lodge in the vicinity of Nebraska Notch, and
                        Taylor Lodge was first known as Nebraska
                        Notch Lodge. The Club had been using the
                        Trout Club facilities. The Trout Club was willing
                        to donate the land but stipulated that an open
                        shelter be built. The Section wanted a closed
                        lodge, so the project was put on hold due to
                        lack of funds and the Trout Club’s stipulation.

on this site was constructed, the soaring stock
market, flaming youth, and the jazz age were in

the GMC, more liberated than the rest of the
country, had already had two Women
presidents. One of them, Mrs. Laura Cowles,
was the first woman to snowshoe to the top of
