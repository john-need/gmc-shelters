INTRODUCTION

The Green Mountain Club was organized in 1910 for the purpose of bringing the mountains closer to the life of the people, not only of Vermont, but of the entire country. It is building the Long Trail over the Green Mountains from Massachusetts to Canada, affording a high, scenic, mountain pathway where everyone who wishes may enjoy health and recreation at reasonable expense. It is hoped that those who tramp the Club trails will come to have a keen personal interest in the preservation and upbuilding of our forests.

This, our first, Guide Book and Year Book is published in the hope that our present 700 members may come to know and use our trails more frequently and through their influence bring others into the joy of the out-of-doors. One thousand Green Mountain Club members are desired by December, 1917. They will be enrolled if you but do your "bit."

It is hoped that by another year our trails may be accurately measured and mapped, distances at present being known only approximately chiefly through pedometer measurement. Cloth bound copies of this book may be purchased at 50c each from Theron S. Dean, Chairman Publicity Committee, 6 Masonic Temple, Burlington, Vt.

IMPORTANT

In following the trail it is important that care be used in the building of camp fires and in the use of shelter houses. Carelessness in the putting out of fires, or in the casting aside of burning matches may start a forest conflagration destroying thousands of dollars worth of timber and doing irreparable damage to the soil and underforest growth. Be a good sport; see that all fires are OUT before leaving camp; be careful where refuse is thrown that the camp may be in fit condition for the next occupant; foul no streams, for pure water is above price; destroy no camp property wantonly. So doing, Vermont bids you welcome and a helping hand will be extended everywhere along the Trail.

EQUIPMENT

The camp equipage carried is a matter of personal experience and desire, but a good rule is to wear woolen underwear with stout, serviceable overwear. A heavy close buttoning coat is better than a sweater. An extra suit of underwear and several pairs of woolen stockings should be carried if the hike is to last more than a single day. Heavy, high shoes will be found easiest on an all day's hike. Any of the several makes of knapsacks will carry all that one really needs. For dishes a tin or enamel plate, cup, knife, fork and spoon. A hand ax in the party is essential. For sleeping out, a light tent with floor cloth and screening is desirable, and a wool puff of good size will keep you warm. For food, bread (preferably made up in sandwiches) and bacon will keep you going with little weight to carry; tea, sugar and small cans of condensed milk. Pictures tell an interesting story of trail experiences and it is hoped that a good camera may accompany you on the trip. The Club is always glad to receive photographic prints. Many can be used to good advantage in making lantern slides. Prints should be sent Mr. Dean, 6 Masonic Temple, Burlington, Vt., and the thanks of the Green Mountain Club will be extended you.

MT. MANSFIELD REGION

JOHNSON TO MANSFIELD, VIA STERLING MOUNTAIN (LONG TRAIL).

From Johnson station (1½ mile southwest of village) on St. J. & L. C. R. R., 42 m from Burlington, cross track and follow highway 2¾ m southwest. From station climb short hill, turn to left at sign "French Hill." At 1¼ m fine view northwest. At 1½ m (mail boxes) bear right up hill. 2 m bridge crossing brook, falls at right. Johnson water supply comes from just above falls. Continue up steep hill, at 2¾ m bear left on less used road (instead of turning up to Boully farmhouse [Fuller's] the last on road) about ten rods to turnstile and Long Trail sign. Follow grassy logging road through old orchard. Before entering woods look back at view north and east. Eden (Belvidere) Mt., Mt. Hadley (long whaleback) Mt. Norris (ragged, thimble shape), Mt. Elmore (prominent to East), Hog Back range south of that. Pass through first grove, turn right, follow up old logging road enter woods again and after ¾ m (Note Sterling Mt. at left [West]) turn sharply to right up evergreen bordered alley. From head of alley there is one mile of level trail through hardwood forest, then climb begins. First 1½ m northwesterly to shoulder of mountain where trail divides. (Jay Peak looking like pair of domes visible to north).

To climb Sterling Mountain turn left (Water is abundant up to last m of mountain, but is found at summit only in wet times) southwesterly 1,000 feet, then left southerly or southeasterly to summit (Whiteface) 3-8 m north from main summit. Fine view north and east. From here to summit Sterling easy walk, little climbing. On Sterling summit is frame of shelter built 1915, can be thatched with fir boughs to afford rude shelter to 3 or 4 people. Excellent view Mansfield and Smugglers Notch. Trail directly west leads down slope to Morse's Mt., Smugglers Notch and Mansfield. (Boully place to summit about 3 m. Divided, 1 m to turn below evergreen alley, 1 m to steep climb, 1 m climbing).

The other trail keeps straight ahead from aforementioned shoulder down perhaps ten rods, left on plainly marked old trail to permanent spring. From spring trail at left climbs Sterling Mt. joining previously described trail. Trail right leads southwesterly along west side Sterling 1½ m where trail from summit Sterling joins and turns west toward Morse's Mt., southerly along narrow ridge crossing small ravine. Follow wood roads, watch for arrows and blazes to Morse's peak. Next mile or two rough but picturesque following ridge, occasional vistas east and west. Choice of routes 1-2 m north of "Chilcoot Pass."

Old route follows wood road southwest (right) diagonally down 1½ m turns and climbs again steeply. Region has been lumbered and is uninteresting.

The new follows south (left) to "Chilcoot Pass" (3,000 ft. elev.) where side trail leads down, left (east), about 1¼ m to shelter camp 1¼ m south Beaver Meadow and 50 ft. higher (Mould's. An easy day's tramp). Camp unlocked, well roofed, accommodate 10 people in emergency. Five single bunks. Five more could sleep on floor. (No blankets). Stove. Mountain brook beside camp. For two m or more north and south of "Chilcoot"

HYDE PARK OR MORRISVILLE TO MANSFIELD, VIA STERLING MT.

Hyde Park and Morrisville on St. J. & L. C. R. R., a few miles east of Johnson. Team through Morristown corners to Tevyaw farm. Through fence into meadow front of house, southwesterly across meadow to corner of lot where wood road enters forest. Follow road to Beaver Meadow, turn left along brook to Shelter Camp (Mould's) thence as above.

JEFFERSONVILLE OR CAMBRIDGE TO MANSFIELD VIA SMUGGLERS NOTCH.

Both stations on branch C. V. R. R., about 33½ m from Burlington. Team through Pleasant Valley to Morse Mills, 6 m. Through Notch to Barnes' Camp, 4 m. Running Water trail as above.

UNDERHILL TO MANSFIELD

Stevensville Trail. Underhill on branch C. V. R. R. 20 m from Burlington, east by highway to Underhill Ctr. Stage or team. (Conveyance may be arranged two miles further if desired). Keep through Underhill Ctr. past two stores to white school house on right. Turn right leaving MAIN road. Keep straight along this second road across flat, up steep hill, down hill to road branching left, about 2 m from Underhill Ctr. Auto may be taken to here. Do not take left road, keep straight ahead, up hill, along flat. About 1½ m from where road turns off left is sign on right indicating beginning of trail to Nebraska Notch. Continue along road from this point ⅝ m to watering trough on right, road turns sharp left ending at Kettell house. Pass front of house, across brook (Stevens') across pasture by old cellar hole to edge of woods. Sign here indicates beginning Stevensville Trail to Mansfield. From this point on up left of ridge is State Land, known as Mansfield Forest. Careful of fire. Two m to top of ridge good condition plainly marked by RED blazes. Top of main ridge connects with Long Trail from Nebraska Notch (South), (see Bolton Trail). Turn left (north) following RED blazes along east side range, 2¼ m to Mansfield.

Lee River Trail. From Underhill to Underhill Ctr., past two stores take first road right at white school house. About ¾ mile out road forks keep right. About 2 m from Underhill Ctr. road comes in on left, turn sharply left, around house, keep road until it ends at Suppley house, 3 m from Ctr. An auto can go to here. Go straight ahead between house and barn follow telephone line running to State Forest Rangers' Cabin near Lee River. Continuing past cabin trail crosses small pasture planted to Norway Spruce by State. Enters woods at easterly side of pasture, trail is plainly marked by RED blazes to top of Mansfield. First 3 m good condition, remaining part before joining Long Trail not so well cut out. 3 m from Rangers' Cabin to Clay Brook, short distance beyond trail crosses wood road to Nebraska Notch. About 1¼ m beyond this trail joins Long Trail. Turn north to Mansfield. Total distance Underhill Ctr. to Mansfield via Lee River Trail eleven m.

Halfway House Trail. This trail so-called starts from Underhill as given above, through Underhill Ctr. to the white school house where the road forks to the right. Keep straight ahead on LEFT road about 1½ m to fork, turn right, 1 m further on past road entering from Stevensville from right. (Autos to here, teams ¾ m further on), keep straight ahead toward mountain ¾ m to edge of woods, straight ahead 1 m to ruins of old Halfway House. Side trail here leads left (north) 5 rods to brook. Main trail leads straight ahead (east) up mountain past ruins. Is not so evident as side trail, is a footpath poorly marked if at all. Cannot be lost, however, as it goes straight up following bed of water course. Rough, steep, mussy in wet weather. Ridge of mountain reached 1½ m north of Nose on trail to Chin. Turn right (south) to Nose and summer hotel. 1½ m Halfway House to Hotel. Distance from Underhill approx. 4½ m.

STOWE TO MANSFIELD.

Via Sterling Pond and Smugglers Notch. Electrics to Stowe from Waterbury on C. V. R. R. 30 m from Burlington. Team to Bingham's Falls, 7 m. Trail to Sterling Pond and Long Trail to Smugglers Notch, Barnes' Camp and Mansfield. (See Johnson to Mansfield). Via electrics to Stowe from Waterbury, stage to summit Mansfield. Summer hotel.

BOLTON TO MANSFIELD VIA BOLTON MOUNTAIN (LONG TRAIL).

Bolton on C. V. R. R. 24 m from Burlington. Meals and lodgings at C. S. Brush's. Take main road north of Bolton station walking east to road turning off at left (north). Follow this left road. About 2 m up it enters clearing and is lost in plowed field. Is picked up again at upper end (northeast) of plowed field. Follow road, watching "G. M. C." and "Trout Club" signs, to abandoned Camp No. 4, 6½ m (4 hrs.) from Bolton. Continue through gate in high board fence between northern buildings, bearing east (right) for 100 feet then northerly along crest of ridge mostly within sight of brook to old trail entering brook bed. Follow brook bed north (left) to near head, ¾ m from Camp No. 4; 7 m from Bolton.

(Optional route: follow wood road left around Camp No. 4 bearing northerly to point where trail through Camp joins, thence north, and east to brook bed, north to head. About same distance but marshy going near camp).

From brook head easterly up timbered over valley, through heavy forest to top Bolton Mountain 7 7-10 m from Bolton. Little view from this point. Follow blazes and arrows down east slope to sag. Trail crosses brook about 1 mile below top of Bolton Mt. to lumber camp, logging road to west end Lake Mansfield, around left margin lake to Trout Club (delightful summer house where best of accommodations may be had). 11½ m. 8 hours (Summit Bolton Mt. to Trout Club 3½ m).

Note: Parties from Mansfield south were generally routed around the Bolton Trail in 1916. While not a "scenic" trail it is of easy grade and follows chiefly wide dry grass-grown lumber road through big second growth, for seven miles in sight and hearing of mountain brook. It is a delightful tramp for the student of nature. Plant forms and bird life in profusion.

Lake Mansfield is 5 m from Moscow on Stowe Electric Railway.

Trail to Mansfield leads westward up old road to 600 ft. cone, forest-covered, known as Sugar Loaf midway Nebraska Notch. Watch out for Long Trail Arrow pointing north at this point marking where Long Trail up Mansfield leaves old road. (Following road west leads to Stevensville and Underhill). Turn right (north) at marker through State Forest (upper part of trail marked with RED blazes) to Needles Eye, where great boulders from Forehead have fallen together, under which trail leads, along face of cliff, up Stowe (east) side of Forehead to Nose and summer hotel. A day's climb 9 m. A side trail leads up to the Nose. A trail north leads to Chin (alt. 4,406 ft.) 1 2-5 m. It is possible to drop down north side Chin to Lake of the Clouds. Also to scramble down over cliffs to Smugglers Notch by practically unmarked and DANGEROUS trail, but it is not advised.

BOLTON TO CAMELS HUMP (LONG TRAIL).

At Bolton station on C. V. R. R. 24 m from Burlington, face south, cross track, through mill yard to river where ferry is necessary. Best telephone Mrs. C. S. Brush at Bolton in advance, and arrange transportation as boat is usually on south bank of river. Pass between barn and house across main road, taking branch road bearing left marked by Long Trail arrow, across brook to old farmhouse in little valley some 200 ft. above river. 1 m. Keep right of house and barn along brook (do not cross brook) to upper right corner of clearing where wood road leads south marked L. T. arrows. Follow road and well cleared trail steadily climbing. An old bridge in poor condition across deep gully 2½ m from Bolton. Pass above left (east) end. Continue to edge of plateau (2 4-10 m from Bolton) where arrow points to spring at right. Trail from here to abandoned lumber camp (Randall) less steep. 3 7-10 m. Buildings in poor condition. Stove in ox stable, which affords fair protection from rain and cold. No water. Trail follows south between camp and stable, steeper as you ascend, through thick spruces to Camels Hump Club just under summit 4½ m. Meals, lodging and supplies furnished here. Turn right at northern cabin, over backbone of ridge, around and up at left to summit 300 ft. Trail a few rods directly south past southern cabin around and under cliff at right to bears den. (A few minutes' scramble).

NORTH DUXBURY TO CAMELS HUMP VIA CALLAHAN'S.

At North Duxbury station (C. V. R. R. 26 m from Burlington) take highway leading south up right bank of brook. Follow straight road crossing brook twice to small farmhouse (Farnsworth's 3 3-10 m) where road turns right between house and barn. Straight west up road to Callahan's. 3-10 m. (Follow telephone line). The old trail (2½ m) leads past Callahan's through pasture (easy grade for ¾ m) then up steep side of mountain to ledges over the north end of which it leads to Camels Hump Club Camp. Plenty of water.

Turning to the left along road a few rods down the hill from Callahan's to farmhouse directly south, one makes the ascent by the new trail, 3 m long but an easier grade. At south farmhouse turn west to right of house and barn, through lane, to gate in fence marked by L. T. arrow, ascend steep pitch west to tree marked by arrow thence by wood road arrow marked to ledges, around under ledges to left, swinging right up steep pitch to top, thence west up mountain side to Camels Hump Club. This trail crosses small brook. Spring at left of trail near top. Watch for arrow pointer.

HUNTINGTON TO CAMELS HUMP.

The trail begins at the Tart farm 3 m east of Huntington Ctr. Leave train at Richmond (C. V. R. R. 18 m from Burlington), take auto stage to Huntington Ctr., 9 m. It is possible to arrange conveyance to Tart farm but this should be done in advance. From Tart farm the trail leads through a pasture, then through virgin forest (Battell Preserve) 3 m to Camels Hump Club. Little water. Trail not well cleared or marked.

LINCOLN MOUNTAIN REGION

MONTCLAIR GLEN TO CAMELS HUMP.

A continuation of the long trail was cut and marked south from Camels Hump to Lincoln Mt. in 1916 by Prof. Wills S. Monroe, of Montclair, N. J.

This started on the west side of the notch known as Baker's Notch, south of Camels Hump at the point where Prof. Monroe camped during the first part of work, designated Montclair Glen. The trail to Camels Hump leads up east by northeast through Baker's Notch and down to a road used by the State Forest Service (2 1-5 m) and is plainly marked by WHITE blazes. Along this road north then to fork. Along left fork 7-10 m to new Callahan Trail up Camels Hump. Turn left, follow Long Trail arrows. 1 1-10 m up is stone fireplace for cooking. 1 1-10 m here to Camels Hump Club camp.

Right fork leads 1 1-10 m to new Callahan Trail down Camels Hump. Turn right 1 7-10 m to telephone post below Callahan's house at corner of road. 4 7-10 m to Farnsworth's where road turns north. 3 7-10 m to North Duxbury station.

JONESVILLE TO CAMELS HUMP VIA HONEY HOLLOW.

From Jonesville station (C. V. R. R., 20 m from Burlington), take team to point 4 m up valley where trail begins. Team or auto should be arranged for in advance (can be secured of F. G. Currier, Richmond, or entire trip made by auto from Burlington). Take road between station and Church's store east turning right across track and bridge, by houses, to road left. Follow this road to mouth of Honey Hollow, 2 m, where road turns off right (south) to Jewett's Mill (in sight). An arrow was placed here but it is safe to inquire. The road from here up the valley, 2 m., is quartered by other roads to left and right, confusing to strangers but one should cross to left (east) side of brook AND STAY THERE. The gate at left leading into pasture where trail starts was marked with G. M. C. arrow. A logging road weaves south among the low ridges. The mass of Camels Hump begins to show ahead, and for a moment the summit is seen, but is lost as the road draws near the steep mountain side. Rounding the south end of a ridge the road turns north and climbs along its side. The slide shows across a deep hollow, at right. This hollow ends where the ridge the road follows joins the mountain side just north of the slide. Turning right the road follows up the mountain on a steep grade to a tiny lumber camp. Locked. Stable is clean and dry should shelter be needed. Trail south leads to brook. The first blazed tree on trail is on south side of brook across from where camp trail leads to water. Follow blazes on same level 100 ft. to slide. The blazes again picked up across the slide high on what appears to be a promontory of the west face of the Hump. Half a mile out from the slide a blaze on a big spruce is the last in sight. Drop down 15 ft., follow along base of steep place and blazes will be again picked up in about 100 ft. A quarter mile further on cross brook by two spruce trees felled side by side. One-fourth mile further trail joins Huntington Trail, very steep from this point on to Hump, 3 3-10 m. This trail is not well enough marked to be safely followed.

MONTCLAIR GLEN TO BIRCH GLEN.

Montclair Glen 2,700 ft. elev., Birch Glen 1,900 ft. elev., by aneroid barometer.

About 7 7-10 m. 8 hrs. via "Monroe Skyline Trail," (a section of The Long Trail between Camel's Hump and Lincoln Mountain built in 1916 by Prof. Will S. Monroe, of Montclair, N. J.) Follow white painted blazes.

Permanent water only at Montclair Glen (brook); Nurian Spring, 1 m; Paris Run, 2 3-10 m; Cowles Cove (brook), 4 9-10 m; Brown Canon, 6 7-10 m; Birch Glen, 7 7-10 m (good brook).

At Montclair Glen fill canteens, cross brook just east camp site, following white blazes south, crossing lumber road which runs down northwest to Forest City. At first 4-10 m fine view of Camel's Hump through trees at north; trail ascends (rather steep) up and around west side of unnamed Mountain Peak (name General Ethan Allen suggested by Professor Monroe) to summit of ridge running south; cross three knolls (center one 3,350 ft.) 1 1-10 m ("High Point" between Montclair Glen and Birch Glen); Lago Seco (Dry Pond) on right 1 7-10 m. Pass "Rock Refuge" (on right) descend old "skidway" and long "game run" to brook (not permanent) in Tannin Ravine, 2,800 ft.

Turn right and south over Tannin Brook; follow logging road over fallen bridge (across stream possibly permanent); a few yards beyond turn right, up bare lumbered hillside; turn left; (if clear, note view White Mountains to northeast); enter moss carpeted forest making several sharp turns (watch white blazes) and crossing nine ravines, one of first 20 ft. deep and three feet wide (bridged). Further on ascend stairs (two felled trees form steps and hand rail); after fourth ravine trail winds up rocky knoll (3,000 ft.), fine view south; cross four more ravines to first and northern of three nearly bare rock domes which together are called "Burnt Rock Mountain". Trail winds and twists over these; follow cairns and white paint on rocks. Central dome highest, 3,100 ft. (unusual in Vermont to find a summit bare below 4,000 ft. level) about 3 3-10 m. Note fine views: east, Mad River Valley and on clear day Presidential Range; northeast, the Hogback or Worcester Mountains (Mt. Hunger the highest) running northeast from Waterbury; north, dome of General Ethan Allen with sharp point of Camels Hump near at left; west, Champlain Valley and Adirondacks; south, Lincoln Mountain with many peaks and ridges between.

Descend south from Burnt Rock Mountain over ledges, through fissures and down the "Skidmore" (very steep); at foot of latter turn right under high ledge and at foot of steep descent turn left and south through Taylor Gulf and stretch of timber to Slash Rock (big white rock, 2,875 feet on lumbered knoll); a few yards south descend steep bank and turn left under or around large fallen tree; follow east down steep bare hillside; make sharp turn to right and follow road through slash short distance south; do not follow road southeast down easy grade into woods, but follow WHITE PAINT and turn SHARPLY TO RIGHT up over knolls and among rocks and stumps southwest and south into woods; then

BIRCH GLEN TO LINCOLN MOUNTAIN.

Birch Glen, 1,900 ft.; Glen Ellen, north end Lincoln Mt., about 3,000 ft. About 6 m, 7 hours, WHITE blazes.

Trail starts up lumber road (76 ft. north of Cabin); follow blazes east and southeast slabbing side of Frank Beane Mt. and up two very steep grades past "1 mile" arrow to near crest of mountain; south to Molly Stark Mountain, turn right under high rock ledge then sharply to left, ascend Molly Stark Balcony, 1⅜ m, 2,700 ft., fine views; north, General Ethan Allen with Camel's Hump sharp cone at left; northwest, Champlain Valley. Turn south from Balcony, gradual descent (past "White Mountain Lookout") to big tree with many arrows beside roofless Beaver Meadow Cabin, 1⅞ m; spring south of cabin across grassy roadway; an abandoned road leads west to Starksboro. From Cabin follow grassy road east about 160 yds.; turn right across brook and ascend southwest and south up the steep cliffs ("2 mile" arrow top of third one); south passing near top Baby Stark Mountain, 2,525 ft. (note view toward White Mountains).

In birch thicket note arrow pointing to rock few yards at left (unusually fine view of Mad River Valley); then abrupt descent to abandoned road (2⅜ m); turn right toward west, ascend a few yards to high point of road (2,276 ft.) and note sign "Appalachian Pass." Following road west would lead to Starksboro and Hanksville, but follow down only few yards, then turn sharply to left into woods; turn right over knoll to Allen Chamberlain Glen, which is at the east base of first of the three densely wooded ridges constituting General Stark Mountain; ascend west up Stark Wall (very steep) around and under Cliffs passing "3 mile" arrow on large tree, and great rocks or boulders (Colonial Dames) to near crest of first ridge; then southwest near crest of ridge to southwest shoulder of same (note fine view of Adirondacks and Champlain Valley); east then south and by slight descent to Stark Pass separating the first and second ridges of General Stark; ascend west to foot Big Cliff and follow shelf under and at left of same to cave opening on shelf at right. Pass into Cave and climb through it up and over boulders to opening of cave at top of cliff; fine view all northeastern Vermont. Continue up and southwest passing "4 m" arrow and along crest of second ridge, turn left to shoulder northeast of high point of General Stark (3,350 ft.); note panorama of northern Lake Champlain, all northern and northeastern Vermont. South over High Point (thickly wooded) and 1⅛ m along third ridge gentle descent over moss, through ferns and 20 ft. spruce to Glen Ellen (estimated 3,000 ft.) at north base of Mt. Ellen the north Peak of Lincoln Mountain. Glen Ellen is the south end of the Monroe Skyline Trail. It is expected in July 1917 this trail will be extended up Mount Ellen to an old camp on the slope of same and then south along the crest of Lincoln Mountain (4,024 ft.) to the Batten Lodge (good spring near) where Long Trail is again picked up.

From Glen Ellen a lumber road leads westward to "Jerusalem" about 9 m northeast from Bristol where food and lodging can usually be obtained at Ed. Follansbee farm. (Address Starksboro, Vt.)

LINCOLN MOUNTAIN TO LINCOLN-WARREN ROAD.

From Batten Lodge at south end of Lincoln Mt. an old road descends So. and W. 1⅜ m below Lodge trail turns s. from road (which descends mountain) keeping high on west side of southern ridge for a mile of easy walking approximately along same level; bears east around southern shoulder of ridge where it is of the "side hill" kind and calls for some scrambling; descends, leading southwest winding among a series of small transverse ridges (east and west); bears approximately southeast 1⅓ m then bearing southerly 1⅜ m toward pass where it taps Lincoln-Warren road at height of land. 1⅛ m west along this road is Henry Davis farm (address Lincoln, Vt.) in whose care mail may be sent, and food and lodging usually obtained. From Davis place road west leads through Lincoln to Bristol where train may be taken to New Haven Jct. on Rutland R.R.

The road down the Mt. from Batten Lodge leads west to Rock farm from which is wagon road to Bristol.

STRATTON MOUNTAIN TRAIL

To reach this mountain one may leave the Rutland Railroad at Arlington or the West River Railroad at Wardsboro Station.

FIRST VIA ARLINGTON.

From Arlington go to East Arlington, about 1½ m. Pass through village following what is known as the road to the Kelly Stand and West Wardsboro. This is a mountain road, very narrow and steep in places but light autos may go fairly easy as far as the Kelly Stand which is some seven miles from East Arlington. Out a few miles from East Arlington a road branches off to right, but keep to the left. The Kelly Stand was once the headquarters of a lumbering concern but for the past few years the place has been kept open for boarders by Mrs. Robert Lawler. One is always welcome and may get something to eat when hungry.

From here it is about 5 m to the Grout Job, another abandoned lumber camp, located on branch of Deerfield River. Follow along road (passable for autos during dry periods) for about 1½ m passing on left what is known as the Daniel Webster place. A bronze tablet here marks the place where Webster made one of his famous speeches. 1½ m further along, the trail up Stratton Mountain branches off to left. It is plainly marked by a sign. The trail was located and built by the State Forest Service and is called the Ross Trail to Stratton Mountain. The grade is moderate and the trees are marked with "red blazes." The distance from road to top of mountain is about 4 m. A sixty-foot steel tower on the summit is used as a lookout station for the forest fire watchman. About a 1/4 m down from the tower on the east side is the cabin of the lookout man.

SECOND VIA WARDSBORO STATION.

Take West River Railroad from Brattleboro north to Londonderry. Leave railroad at Wardsboro Station, take auto stage to Wardsboro and then on to West Wardsboro. In this village here is a good country hotel, the Green Mountain House. Full directions for climbing Stratton Mountain may be obtained here. The distance from West Wardsboro to top of mountain is around 9 m.

BENNINGTON REGION

Bennington is recognized as the southern gateway to the Long Trail and although the trail proper starts from the Massachusetts-Vermont state line, practically all travelers over the trail start the trip from Bennington going by way of Bald Mountain and connecting with the Long Trail near Hell Hollow in Woodford. This start gives an excellent hike for the first day over the best viewpoint in the immediate vicinity of Bennington and brings one in good season for a comfortable night's stay at the Green Mountain Club camp at the foot of Hell Hollow. From here there is an optional route to Camp 11. The more scenic trail follows the Hell Hollow brook to Hagar Clearing, 3 miles, by far the best viewpoint on the southern section of the trail. The trail up the brook is rough but well worth the climb. Hagar Clearing to Little Pond is level going, 1 mile. Thence trail leads over the mountain to Camp 11, 3 miles. Trail fairly good over the mountain but wet near the camp.

The optional route from G. M. C. camp at Hell Hollow follows the Glastenbury stream over good trail about 1½ miles then along old railroad track to Old Glastenbury, 1½ miles farther. (Look out for bad holes in track). Continuing from Old Glastenbury trail in good condition leads over ridge and down into Camp 11, 3 miles, making route about 1 mile shorter than Hell Hollow Trail.

From Camp 11 to Somerset Bridge trail is slightly down grade, 6 miles. At bridge trail joins highway following highway up grade 2 miles then 1 mile down to Somerset Dam. The dam is an interesting piece of engineering. It is built entirely of earth except for concrete outlet and overflow and is one of the largest of its kind in the country. Required about two years to build and cost considerably over one million dollars. When filled backs up water about five miles.

From Somerset Dam trail leads in northerly direction and is practically level country and good going to the Grout Job, 6 miles. Here trail strikes Arlington-Wardsboro road and follows highway for 1½ miles to Stratton Mt Trail which is fairly easy grade to the summit, 3 miles. On summit is a sixty foot steel observatory erected through co-operation Stratton Mt. Club and Vermont Forestry Service, commanding splendid view over country for miles. On a clear day one can see far north over the Vermont mountains northeast over the White Mts., west over Adirondacks and south far into Massachusetts. This is the principal objective point on the Bennington Section of the Long Trail.

From the summit trail takes abrupt descent into "Dufresne Job," an abandoned lumber camp, 1½ miles. The trail from here to Prospect Rock, 6 miles, is level going. The first mile or two is over old corduroy road not in first class condition, but most of the way is excellent trail or secondary highway. The section of Long Trail beyond Prospect Rock is not properly located on map but the trail into Manchester is easily followed. At Manchester on Rutland R. R. excellent hotel accommodations and railroad connections may be had. At present get detailed information before attempting to follow Long Trail north from Prospect Rock.

The following camps and shelters are now available on the Bennington Section:

SUCKER POND--Fish & Game Club canvas tepee. Contains stove only.

HELL HOLLOW--O. M. C. Camp. Fully equipped, bunks for 10 persons, stove, cooking utensils and dishes. Get key at Griswold's store, Bennington.

GLASTENBURY STREAM--Tepee as at Sucker Pond.

CAMP ELEVEN--Small cabin. Has bunks for three or four persons, stove and few cooking utensils. Camp not locked.

SOMERSET DAM--Meals and lodging at caretaker's house. Address several days in advance B. B. Leonard, Somerset, Vt.

GROUT JOB--S. B. Hawks' permanent camp. Fully equipped. Get key at Griswold's store, Bennington.

STRATTON MOUNTAIN--Vermont Forestry Service cabin. Substantial log hut. Bunks and stove. Camp not locked.

DUFRESNE JOB--Cabin similar to Camp Eleven.

The route usually taken over this section from Bennington to Manchester can be easily covered in four days spending first night at Hell Hollow camp, second night at Somerset Dam, third night on Stratton Mountain, arriving in Manchester on fourth. An ideal trip is here given covering a full week which gives one plenty of time to enjoy every part and feature of the trail including the section south of Bennington.

FIRST DAY--Bennington to Sucker Pond traveling without pack (taking lunch only) and returning to Bennington for the night, 13 miles round trip.

Optional Route--Trolley to Pownal. Take highway past Barber Pond, over "County Road" hitting Long Trail into Sucker Pond and to Bennington, 16 to 17 miles walking distance.

SECOND DAY--Packed with provisions, blankets, etc., take trail over Bald Mountain to Hell Hollow camp, 8 miles.

THIRD DAY--Get early start from camp to Somerset Dam via Little Pond, 15 miles. This will include some of the roughest part of the trail, but comfortable sleeping quarters and meals may be had at the caretaker's that night.

FOURTH DAY--Somerset Dam to Hawks' camp at Grout Job, 6 miles.

FIFTH DAY--Visit Grout Pond in morning. To West Wardsboro in afternoon for supplies and hotel accommodations over night, 8½ miles.

SIXTH DAY--Double back on highway and climb Stratton Mountain, camping for night in Forestry Service cabin, 5½ miles.

SEVENTH DAY--Stratton Mountain to Manchester via Prospect Rock, 11 miles.

THE FOLLOWING SUGGESTIONS WILL SAVE FROM ONE TO THREE DAYS ON THE FOREGOING TRIP.

Cut out Sucker Pond trip first day.

Cut out Bald Mt. and take auto early in morning from Bennington to G. M. C. camp at Hell Hollow, hiking from camp to Somerset Dam same day.

If supplied with ample provisions make Somerset Dam to Stratton Mt. in one day, cutting out West Wardsboro.

ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION

Of the Green Mountain Club, Inc.

I. We, the subscribers, hereby associate ourselves together as a corporation under the laws of the state of Vermont to be known by the name of "THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB, INC."

II. The purpose of the corporation shall be to make trails and roads in the Vermont mountains, to erect camps and shelter houses therein, to publish maps and guide books thereof, and in other ways to make the Vermont mountains play a larger part in the life of the people.

III. The principal office shall be located at Rutland, Vt.

IV. The corporation shall have no capital stock.

V. Members of said The Green Mountain Club, Inc., while so remaining pursuant to these articles of association and its by-laws shall be entitled to the benefits, privileges, and advantages thereof and their rights therein shall terminate when they cease to be such members. Memberships shall be non-assessable.

VI. These articles may be amended at any regular or special meeting, duly called, by a two-thirds vote of members present provided written notice of such proposed amendment is mailed to each member at his last known address at least three weeks in advance of such meeting.

VII. This corporation is organized for charitable and educational purposes and not for profit.

Dated at Brandon, County of Rutland, March 23, 1917.

BY-LAWS

Of the Green Mountain Club, Inc.

ARTICLE I.--MEMBERS

SECTION 1. Any person may become a member of the Green Mountain Club, Inc., upon application to and approval of any officer thereof or any officer of a section, and upon payment of the dues for one year, or for a life membership, and the issuance to such applicant of a certificate of membership by the treasurer of the corporation or his delegate or the treasurer of a section or his delegate.

SEC. 2. The right to vote at corporate meetings and the right to hold elective office is limited to those members of the corporation who have reached the age of twenty-one years.

SEC. 3. All members of The Green Mountain Club (unincorporated) on pro 10th, 1917, shall on and from that date be members of the Green Mountain Club, Inc.

SEC. 4. Members whose dues have not been paid for a year or more may be dropped by the local sections or by the corporation.

ARTICLE II.--OFFICERS

SECTION 1. The officers of the corporation shall be a clerk who shall also be a trustee and fifteen other trustees, who shall be elected at the annual meeting of the corporation; a president, a vice-president, a treasurer, and a corresponding secretary, who shall be elected yearly by the board of trustees from its membership at its first regular meeting.

SEC. 2. The vice-president shall act as president in case of vacancy in the office of president, or in case of the president's incapacity or absence from the state for more than three weeks.

SEC. 3. The clerk of the corporation shall also act as clerk of the board of trustees.

SEC. 4. Officers elected by the board of trustees may be removed by it at pleasure.

SEC. 5. Officers may appoint and remove deputies at pleasure.

SEC. 6. The board of trustees shall have power to fill vacancies in the above.

ARTICLE III.--CORPORATE MEETINGS

SECTION 1. The corporate year shall begin January 1st and the annual meeting shall be held on the second Wednesday of each year. It shall be the duty of the clerk to mail notice of the annual meeting of the corporation to each member at his last known address at least one week in advance of such meeting.

SEC. 2. Special corporate meetings may be called by the president or by seven trustees provided notice stating the time and object thereof is mailed by the clerk to the last known address of each member at least one week in advance of such meeting.

SEC. 3. Corporate meetings shall be held at Rutland, Vermont.

SEC. 4. Fifteen members of voting age (or in case the membership does not equal that number two-thirds of the membership of voting age) shall constitute a quorum at corporate meetings.

<!-- page: 12 -->
ARTICLE IV.-TRUSTEES

SECTION 1. A regular meeting of the board of trustees shall be held on the second Wednesday of March each year unless the president shall deem an earlier date advisable in which case he shall issue a call in the same manner as for a special meeting.

SEC. 2. Special meetings may be called by the president or by any seven trustees provided notice thereof is mailed by the clerk to the last known address of each trustee at least one week in advance of such meeting.

SEC. 3. Five members shall constitute a quorum at any meeting of the board of trustees.

SEC. 4. The board of trustees shall appoint all standing committees naming the chairman from their own number.

SEC. 5. The regular meeting of the board of trustees shall be held at Rutland, Vermont. Special meetings shall be held at the place designated in the call.

ARTICLE V.-LOCAL ORGANIZATIONS

SECTION 1. Members of the corporation in Vermont or elsewhere may be organized into a section of The Green Mountain Club, Inc., with the approval of the board of trustees.

SEC. 2. A local organization shall be known as The Green Mountain Club, Inc., (Name) Section, at (place).

SEC. 3. Each section shall elect the following officers: president, vice-president, secretary and treasurer.

SEC. 4. A yearly report as of January 1st of the field work and also of the membership of each section shall be sent to the president of The Green Mountain Club, Inc. before the annual meeting.

SEC. 5. The board of trustees shall have the power, by a two-thirds vote of those present at any regular or special meeting, to declare any section nonexistent, and the former members of such section shall be considered members at large of The Green Mountain Club, Inc., and be treated as such. Provided that written notice of the proposed action be mailed by the clerk to the last known president and secretary of such section at least a week in advance of such meeting.

ARTICLE VI.-DUES

SECTION 1. The annual dues of members shall be one dollar.

SEC. 2. Persons becoming life members pursuant to Article I, Sec. 1, shall pay twenty-five dollars.

SEC. 3. Members of a section shall pay their dues to the treasurer of that section or his delegate; members of the corporation not attached to any section shall pay their dues to the treasurer of the corporation or his delegate.

SEC. 4. Ten per cent. of the dues received by a section for the calendar year shall be sent to the treasurer of the corporation on or before January 1st of the following year. The remainder shall be disbursed by the section.

ARTICLE VII.-FUNDS

SECTION 1. The funds of the corporation shall be disbursed under the direction and by the order of the board of trustees.

SEC. 2. Sums not in excess of ten dollars may be expended for ordinary business purpose by concurrent act of the president and treasurer without vote of the board of trustees.

ARTICLE VIII.-STANDING COMMITTEES

SECTION 1. Standing committees of the corporation shall be Trails and Shelters, Membership, Publicity, and Nomenclature to consist of not less than three members each.

ARTICLE IX.-TRAIL

SECTION 1. All projects for the building of Green Mountain Club trails or shelters shall be submitted for the approval of the board of trustees or the corporation or of the executive committee of the local section.

SEC. 2. Trails or shelters constructed other than by The Green Mountain Club, Inc. or a section thereof shall not be regarded as Green Mountain Club trails or shelters until approved and accepted by the board of trustees of the corporation or the executive committee of the local section.

ARTICLE X.-AMENDMENTS

SECTION 1. These by-laws may be amended at any regular or special meeting, duly called, by a two-thirds vote of members present, provided written notice of such proposed amendment is mailed by the clerk to each member at his last known address at least one week in advance of such meeting.
