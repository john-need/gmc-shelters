Northern Frontier Section
                  of the Green Mountain Club

                  Summer/Fall 2026 Newsletter


                                                           June 16, 2026
Summer Greetings!


Last winter began early, with snow in early November accumulating and
adding up to quite a depth during the winter. Lots of snow had to melt
before trail and shelter adopters could get out this spring for walk
throughs. We’ve scheduled a variety of hikes and events this season
and I hope you’re able to join us on the trail.

The snow depth last winter was great for snowshoeing and XC skiing
until a thaw during March. The NFS scheduled six hikes and four of
them were well attended.

In January, the Whiteheads hosted a snowshoe/potluck at their home
with ten of us attending. We did a short snowshoe hike in the
Enosburgh Town Forest.

Six of us attended a snowshoe/potluck at Kip Potter’s new home on a
sunny winter day in January. Kip led us on a hike up the hill in the
woods behind his home.

On a clear, beautiful day in February Dan Seeley led seven of us on a
hike on the Jay Recreation Area Trails.

<!-- page: 2 -->
In early March, seven of us snowshoed up to the ice formations
on Laraway Mountain. Unfortunately a thaw had just begun,
which made for a very difficult hike. A clear blue sky was the
backdrop for a slushy trail and full, running streams.

Our other two snowshoe hikes (in March and April) were
cancelled because of thawing snowpack on the trails.
We recently held our NFS Annual Meeting/Potluck on May 30,
2026 with 28 members attending.

Many thanks to everyone who came to the meeting and shared
their good food and good company. Many thanks also to Jeff
Miller, who brought his grill and cooked great bratwursts for
us! The meeting summary is below and includes some of the
latest information about our section.

We announced trail work to be done this season:
Reblazing north of Carleton Mountain to the Canadian Border.
And from Rt 105 south to Shooting Star Shelter and maybe
beyond. Helping GMC staff install wooden planks (puncheon)
over muddy areas between Rt 242 and Hazen’s Notch.
Jeff Miller talked about having the NFS purchase a high quality
hand saw to share among trail adopters for cutting blowdowns
on the trail. Jeff owns one (it’s a version of a Silkie) and
recommends it as an alternative to always carrying a chainsaw on
a walk through.
Our GMC Board representative, Kip Potter gave a short summary
of GMC issues including the governance update which will be
ongoing for a while. Kip is on the Land Conservation Committee.
Walter Pomroy gave the section treasurer’s report and he voiced
concern that membership dues collected so far this year are
about $350 less than last year at this time. Either membership is
down or members just haven’t renewed their membership
yet. Walter let the GMC know about this issue.
Ken announced that the GMC has insurance coverage for
volunteers. From the information provided by the GMC:
“Volunteers are included as an additional insured on GMC’s
policies.” That includes limited liability and supplemental
accident insurance for any “activity or event that is organized
under the GMC umbrella - outings, meetings, workdays,
etc.” The supplemental accident insurance covers volunteers
injured on non-federal land.
Elections were held and all positions will continue to be filled by
the 2025 officers.
Ken mentioned the importance for younger folks to become
officers and keep our section functioning years from now.
At the end of our business meeting, we recognized 2 of our
oldest members who were present: Dave and Louise Ransom,
who are now in their 90’s and hiked the LT end to end years ago.
They have a long history of participation in the GMC. Many

More Announcements
On Saturday, June 13th, The Green Mountain Club Annual
Meeting took place at the GMC Headquarters in Waterbury Ctr.,
VT. In attendance from the NFS were: Dan Seeley, Adele
Wolfson, Kip Potter, Ken and Sharon Whitehead. Officers and
staff of the club gave summaries of finances, work and changes
to GMC governance. Those of us present at the meeting voted
on a few of the governance changes, while other changes will be
decided and finalized later. The club is in good shape financially
right now, but cuts in federal funding will affect programs in the
next few years. Federal cuts in Forest Service staff are already
affecting projects on the LT in the GMNF.
Long Trail Day is September 19. A Trail Magic Table will be at Jay
Pass (Rt 242) from 9 AM to 3 PM. We’ll need at least 6 volunteers
to staff the table, pass out snacks and water and take contact
information from LT hikers who are interested in the GMC. In
addition to that, the GMC will have a fundraising page on its
website and our section will participate by having a “Northern
Frontier” fundraising team on that page. You can join with a
donation, large or small. Each donation is important and very
much appreciated!
Please submit a trail adopter or shelter adopter report after
you complete a workday on your trail or shelter. Go to the GMC
website, then Volunteer, then Volunteer Reports. Fill in the info
and submit your report. When an email copy comes back to you,
please forward it to Jeff Miller and Ken Whitehead so we’ll know
what work is being done. It’s an important part of our job as
volunteer caretakers of the Northern Frontier Section.
Many thanks to all volunteers who work on the trail, shelters, lead
hikes and contribute through their membership in the Northern
Frontier Section. Your hard work is what maintains the trail and
makes it such a special place. Each one of you is greatly
appreciated. Happy trails and all the best,
Ken Whitehead - President, Northern Frontier Section
