                                     President’s Message, March 2018


I’m looking forward to seeing many of you at our Section’s annual dinner and meeting on Saturday March
24 at the Cheshire Grange Hall. If you haven’t already registered, there’s still time. Contact Activities vice
president Dennis Himes at dennis@cookhimes.us and tell him you'd like to attend. The social hour begins
at 5:00 pm with dinner starting at 6:00. The Grange will serve their traditional roast beef, or vegetarian
lasagna, with all the fixings. We expect to be joined by Marge Fish, the current president of the Manchester
Section and former GMC president, who will share information on the fundraising effort for a replacement
observation tower to provide an unobstructed vista from the summit of Bromley Mountain on the Long
Trail. After dinner and the annual business meeting, Mark Starr will make a presentation on Native
American ceremonial stonework.


In the last Trail Talk | mentioned that Dave Hardy, the long-time Green Mountain Club director of Trail
Programs, had passed away after a year-long struggle with cancer. Jim Fritz and | attended a celebration
of Dave’s life in Waterbury Center, Vermont in early January.           Despite below-zero temperatures, the
turnout of Dave’s family, friends and current and former co-workers was enormous. There were several
speakers, all of whom did an excellent job of painting a picture of a special person who was so thoroughly
dedicated to trails throughout the Northeast.       Carol Langley has coordinated with Dave’s family in
Connecticut to schedule a memorial hike at Sleeping Giant State Park in Hamden on Sunday April 8. We’ll
hike up the graded Tower Path and up the stone tower for a brief reflection on Dave's life. A longer loop
hike will follow for those interested.   In addition, the Connecticut Chapter of the Appalachian Mountain
Club plans a trail work day in Dave’s memory on Saturday May 12 as part of their “Give A Day to the AT”
event. Please see our Section calendar for details on both.


We’re approaching Spring, which is a great time to get out and hike and wander in the woods. With warmer
weather of course, our insect friends come alive. After a hike on the Nipmuck Trail in late February
following the spell of abnormally warm weather, I found a tick starting to dine on my dog Molson and
found another walking up my shirt.       So, increased vigilance and judicious use of repellents are in order.
Can’t wait for the black flies!


See you on the trail,
Jim Robertson
