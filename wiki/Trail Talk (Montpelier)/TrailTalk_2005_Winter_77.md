  Many memories linger in the minds of the volunteers. One
added benefit of the shelter project was an excuse to spend
the night out of doors. "I stayed two full days and from the
porch at the Smuggs hut watched a beautiful shooting star
show," recalled Allen Jacobs. "It was magnificent -- one of
those gorgeous clear nights. It was a magic time."
  But why would fifty people work in mud, sun, rain, and bugs
more than a mile into the woods to build a shelter? "It's fun
and it brings people together." says Jacobs. "I like the Green
Mountain Club because it has such a diverse group of people.
It truly is a melting pot, and it is good to be with them.''
  Wilkie wonders how to make it even more diverse. At the
shelter dedication attended by fifty people, at least two young
