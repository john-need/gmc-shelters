CALENDAR OF EVENTS
                                       July 26, 2021 - August 29, 2021

                 The rating levels of difficulty are easy, moderate, and difficult, with a few outings falling in
between.
         • Easy - accessible to all in good physical condition; suitable for families with children.
         • Moderate - requires a degree of stamina; some previous experience is advisable.
         • Difficult - challenging events for people in good physical condition; previous experience necessary.
  Always bring appropriate gear for the event you are attending (i.e., lunch, liquids, snacks, suitable clothing,
footwear, and other items as mentioned in the Calendar of Events). Non-members are welcome at our events.
There is no charge, unless the destination charges one (example: State Park entrance fees).
  Our trip leaders are volunteers who are not necessarily trained in first aid, nor does the Montpelier Section or
the GMC certify or license trip leaders. Participants should attend these events with the understanding that
they are responsible for their own preparedness and safety, and that all contribute to the well-being of the
group.
                     PLEASE CALL TRIP LEADER BEFORE BRINGING YOUR DOG.


July 26, Monday - Paddle. Hyde Park. Easy to Moderate. 4-8 miles. Green River Reservoir, one of
the most scenic areas in Vermont! Distance depends on weather and abilities of the group. Must
have PFD and your own canoe/kayak. Contact Leaders: Steve or Heather Bailey, 622-4516 or ste-
vecbailey@gmail.com for meeting time and place.

July 28, Wednesday - Hike. Warren. Easy. 4 miles. 450' elevation gain. A brief stop to admire sce-
nic Warren Falls, followed by a hike on the Blueberry Lake trail system. Contact Leaders: Steve or
Heather Bailey, 622-4516 or stevecbailey@gmail.com for meeting time and place.

August 4, Wednesday - Hike. Eden. Moderate. 6 miles. Hike the LT and Babcock Trail through
Devil's Gulch near Eden, just south of Belvidere Mtn. There is a nice view from the Spruce Ledge
Shelter. Devil's Gulch requires climbing over a few boulders. Contact Leaders: Steve or Heather Bai-
ley, 622-4516 or stevecbailey@gmail.com for meeting time and place.

August 7, Saturday - Hike. Westmore. Moderate. 4.5 miles with 1500 ft. elevation gain. Mt. Pisgah,
on the shore of Lake Willoughby, is one of the most scenic areas of Vermont with views of peaks in
the White Mountains, Green Mountains and Quebec. We will hike the mountain and then go for a
swim at the beach afterward. Contact Leaders: Dana Lawrence, 371-9610 or hikeski51@gmail.com
or Nancy Thomas, 272-6650 for meeting time and place.

August 21, Saturday - Meeting. Groton State Park. Section Membership Meeting. Boulder Beach
Shelter reserved from 10 A.M. - 4:00 P.M. In the morning we plan to lead two short hikes in the park,
one up Owl's Head and the other on Big Deer. Both are easy hikes with nice views and a good leg
stretch for adults and kids of all ages. To coordinate the going and coming for the hikes, meet first at
the pavilion at 10:30 so we can leave together from there. Following the hikes the group will gather
at the pavilion for a potluck lunch and time to hang out afterward, go for a swim, a paddle, or play
games on the lawn.

August 29, Sunday - Hike. Groton. Moderate. Groton State Forest. Coldwater and Hosmer Brook
Trails from the Nature Center. 3.5 miles. Contact Leaders: George Longenecker or Cynthia Martin,
marlong@myfairpoint.net or 802-229-9787 for meeting time and place.

PRESTON POND LOOP
                                                     by Steve Bailey

  Sometimes you don’t want to spend all day on a hike. A good source of hikes that only take an hour or
two is the GMC publication The Walker’s Guide to Vermont. Unfortunately, the book is out of print, but
the Kellogg Hubbard Library in Montpelier has a copy. An updated version is in the works.
  One of my favorites in the book is the Preston Pond Loop in Bolton. The trailhead is found at 3643
Notch Road in Bolton, and the trails are located in the Bolton Town Forest. Some of the trails are old
woods roads and some are narrower, but all are rated easy. One of the highlights is Libby’s Look, a fine
viewpoint over Lake Champlain with the Adirondacks in the distance. There are also two scenic beaver
ponds with some nice picnic spots along the east shore of the larger pond. The whole loop is around 4
miles with just a few hundred feet of elevation gain. There are signs at each trail junction to help you find
your way, and a woodland artist has hung her paintings of various flora and fauna along the trails.
  The Preston Pond Loop intersects the old Long Trail (now Duck Brook Trail) briefly at the south end of
the loop. If you take the old Long Trail north for a quarter mile from there, you will find what I consider to
be the most interesting feature of the area, the Upper West Bolton rock climbing area. This area has
been preserved by CRAG-VT, a rock-climbing group, which has preserved several areas in Vermont.
You don’t have to be a rock climber, though, to enjoy this gem. First of all, there is a path (rated easy)
along the bottom of the cliff for about a quarter of a mile which leads you through an incredible field of




boulders, some of which are over 30 feet high. There is also a path (rated moderate) which winds its
way up the cliff to two great viewpoints. One viewpoint looks south to Camel’s Hump with Duck Brook
below (see photo). From the top of the cliff, if you walk to the north you will get a view of Mt Mansfield.
The path up the cliff starts to the left of the kiosk, which you will find as you arrive at the rock-climbing
area. If you want to go directly to the rock-climbing area without doing the Preston loop, you can park
along the side of the road at 2844 Notch Road and follow the Duck Brook trail south for a quarter mile,
where you will see a sign indicating Upper West Bolton and a trail branching off to the right.
  The Preston Loop can easily be done in the winter, as some of the loop is also a VAST trail and the
rest is usually well packed by foot traffic. I would probably leave the trail up the cliff until spring, as the
trail is pretty close to the edge in several spots.

          Why: The Green Mountain Club encourages Vermonters and visitors alike to take a hike (solo
              or with a team) and support the trail by raising $1 per mile of the 272-mile Long Trail.

                   This year proceeds from Long Trail Day will benefit GMC's Long Trail Legacy Campaign
                 developed as a response to the popularity of outdoor recreation and the need to secure
                 the future of our trails.

                  Call the Green Mountain Club (802-244-7037) for details. Also, more information should
                 be available on any of their social media pages for Long Trail Day that link to the Long
                 Trail Day Information Webpage.
