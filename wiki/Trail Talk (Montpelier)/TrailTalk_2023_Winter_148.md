                                             Why I Like Winter Hiking
                                                      by Steve Bailey
     Now that winter is here, our minds are turning to
snow-related activities. Many people enjoy cross-country
skiing during the winter, but I would like to remind every-
one not to forget about winter hiking. For me, winter is a
great time to hike up some of our local mountains, like
Worcester, Hunger and White Rock.
     Why do I like winter hiking? There are several rea-
sons. First, you get the clearest weather in winter. This
past summer has been one of our worst for visibility,
obscuring the wonderful views of the Green Mountains
and the Whites in New Hampshire. The winter snow
should take care of those Canadian forest fires, and on a
cold crisp winter morning there is so little moisture in the
air that you can see places as far away as Mt. Washington
quite clearly. Also, the snow on the ski trails makes cer-
tain mountains very easy to recognize.

Trails & Shelters Report
                      by Heidi Kunkel
     Well, what a summer and fall we have had here in
Vermont. The turbulent temperatures and the constant
rain made it difficult to schedule our fall work hikes, but
the Montpelier Section showed up!
     Our hike out of Barnes Camp started out in beauti-
ful, crisp weather, but true-to-form the rain found us. We
had a small group that weekend and we all had to push
hard to move the leaves and sticks that had collected over
the busy summer. A short way up the trail two puncheons
needed to be replaced. Carrying the heavy planks warmed
us quickly, and the frequent breaks we took gave us time
to connect with new and old friends.
     Our second work hike this fall was beautiful! Lots of
volunteers, lots of sunshine, and lots of puppies helping
us clean up the trail. The drainage ditches needed a size-
able effort to make them functional again, but the hard
work by our crew created perfect paths for the water to
flow.
     We were also fortunate to be joined by a volunteer
who brought an electric chainsaw, and who made quick
work of some blowdowns and deadfalls far up the trail.
I am so incredibly thankful for the efforts of the Montpe-
lier Section members for sticking it out and making these
trails ones that Vermonters can be proud of.
