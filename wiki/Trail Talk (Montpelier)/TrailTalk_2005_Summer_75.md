                                      What are You Doing This Summer?
                                                                        by Bill Clark, President
 The call of a hermit thrush breaks the morning silence as mist rises from the pond. A beaver carves a V through the water on
its way home. A trout briefly surfaces in search of a morning meal. And the fa lse sunrise brightens the sky to the east.
  The scene is Sterling Pond, a much-visited and much-loved spot on the Long
Trail. The Montpelier Section has taken a strong interest in the area around the
pond for many years. In 1972 a dedicated group packed in building materials
for the previous shelter. This year another group- and possibly some of the
same volunteers-will again climb to the pond for several weekends of work.
  Anyone can help by carrying lumber, clearing waterbars, removing brush,
doing maintenance work around Watson Camp, and helping with the construc-
tion . The section website will list the details but there is plenty of work to do
and everyone can participate-even if you've never used a hammer and saw.
The Schedule
  On Sunday, May 22 a small crew will conduct a final site survey, clean up the
site, orient the shelter, and establish a staging area for the materials.
  Early in July volunteer groups will carry in the building supplies. Construction
begins on Saturday, July 23. We need to drill into the granite. Position pins to                         Drawing by Dave Blumenthal
secure the posts. Build footings. Cut the posts to size.
  During the weekend of August 5-7, we will: Install the posts and temporarily fasten them in place. Frame the floor. Install the
joists. Lay the flooring.
  We will finish the floor and begin the roof during the weekend of August 13-14. We need to cut and position the rafters. then
install strapping and attach metal roof panels. With a little luck and skill, the shelter should be roofed and ready this weekend .
But if not, we plan ...
  A fourth weekend, August 20-21 , to complete the structure, finish the roof, and clean up the site. The new shelter should be
ready for occupancy and the ladders, tools, and other equipment hauled down the mountain.
  Then volunteers, staff, and friends can celebrate the arrival of the new shelter at a dedication ceremony planned for Sunday,
October 161h.
  Whether you come for a few hours, a day, or a weekend, we will appreciate the help and support. Bring tools, work gloves,
water, lunch, cameras, and enjoy the building spirit that seems to accompany these projects.
