                                                         On Hiking
                                                       by Alan Paschell
     Whenever I start a hike on an old logging road I sigh with relief when I
finally hit the narrow trail. Suddenly I fit in. There’s a primal sense of belong-
ing, and the forest feels more intimate.
     I love to go hiking, whether on a long, hard backpacking trip, or just sev-
eral miles on a local trail. Between hikes I reminisce about features of the
various trails I know so well. Planning the next hike, I think to myself, would
I like to see the “Two Carpenters” again on Abraham and admire the grain on
the dead birch tree there? Or would I like to see the view from the “Eye of the
Needle”, to stop, relax, and take it all in? I know the exercise is good; my body
tells me that, even when I’m sore from a long day. I know my brain is cook-
ing up those good chemicals, and I’m not doing it just because it feels so good
when I stop.
     But the compulsion to go hiking interests me. Is it the smells that draw
me back – that wet scent of earth and leaves – or the visual play of light
and shadow on the rocks and ledges, the vibrant green of the mosses? Is it
the repetitive motion of walking that becomes addictive, sets that metronome
going in my head?
     Even though I can’t sing a note on key, I find myself singing out loud
(when there’s no one around to hear). I guess, like a lot of things, I’ll never
really know the “why” of it. I just know I have to do it. It haunts me, but in a
good way. Yeah, I think I’ll go visit the “Two Carpenters” again.                           Alan at the Eye-of-the-Needle
