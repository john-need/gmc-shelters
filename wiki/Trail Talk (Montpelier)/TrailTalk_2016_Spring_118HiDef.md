                              The Splendors of the North Cascades
   Steve and Heather Bailey have been frequent visitors to North Cascades National Park since their son
moved to Seattle in 2003. Most of the park which includes more than 300 lakes and ponds and an equal
number of glaciers and snowfields, is designated as the Stephen Mather Wilderness. The Baileys will be
our featured speakers at the Montpelier Section an-
nual meeting on Saturday, April 9, showing their fa-
vorite slides and describing their adventures in the
North Cascades.
   Steve and Heather have explored many of the
park’s 386 miles of hiking trails. They have visited
glaciers from Mount Baker and Mount Shuksan to
Mount Rainier, and they have flown over—and hiked
along—Lake Chelan, a glacial lake 1,400 feet deep
and 55 miles long. They have trekked to scores of
the park’s mountain lakes and trails. They have
crossed the Pacific Crest National Scenic Trail
(which runs the length of the state of Washington on
its way to Canada) many times, and they have met
thru-hikers nearing the end of their journey. “You can tell the men by their long beards,” says Steve.
   The Baileys, retired teachers, outdoors people, and ardent travelers, have been active GMC members
since moving to Montpelier two years ago. Steve is serving his first term as trails and shelters coordina-
tor.

  The rating levels of difficulty are easy, moderate, and difficult, with a few outings falling in between.
         • Easy - accessible to all in good physical condition; suitable for families with children.
         • Moderate - requires a degree of stamina; some previous experience is advisable.
         • Difficult - challenging events for people in good physical condition; previous experience necessary.
  Unless otherwise noted, trips leave from the Montpelier High School (MHS) parking lot. Always bring appropri-
ate gear for the event you are attending (i.e. lunch, liquids, snacks, suitable clothing, footwear, and other items as
mentioned in the Calendar of Events). Non-members are welcome on our events. There is no charge..
  Our trip leaders are volunteers who are not necessarily trained in first aid, nor does the Montpelier Section or
the GMC certify or license trip leaders. Participants should attend these events with the understanding that they
are responsible for their own preparedness and safety, and that all contribute to the well-being of the group.
         The Young Adventures Club is a group to get parents and children (0-12 years old) outdoors to hike,
       play, learn about nature, and make friends. Events happen on the second weekend of every month.
       Please check out their website: gmcmontpelier.org/yac for details.
