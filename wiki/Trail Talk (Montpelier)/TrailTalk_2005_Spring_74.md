                                        Fiftieth Anniversary Update,                        by Priscilla Page
            In order to celebrate the fiftieth anniversary of the Montpelier Section's founding in 1955, a Com-
        mittee has been planning a number of special events and outings.
           The annual meeting on April 1, 2005, will be an anniversary celebration with the requisite birthday
                        cake, door prizes, and invited guests. In a program of slides and an exhibit of photo-
                                                                                                                                  I
                        graphs, Doris Washburn will show the history of the section's first fifty years; although GMC M t 1·              cf
                        there will be glimpses of individuals in odd costumes with outdated equipment, there                  on pe ier 5e ion
                        will also be photos of familiar people participating in favorite activities at favorite destinations.
                          Thomas Weiss has researched and planned a series of monthly Heritage Outings, these are outings that were
                        popular in the past but are less so now. These are incorporated in the outing schedule and are designated with
        the anniversary logo designed by Dave Blumenthal. The first of these outings was a January snowshoe trip on the LT that
        started with a free chair lift ride at the Mad River Glen Ski Area. In February, there was a Moonlight Mystery Snowshoe hike,
        and on March 12, there is a cross-country ski event through Hazen's Notch in Montgomery and Lowell. April's Heritage Outing
        is a re-creation of the section's first outing, a sugar-on-snow party at the Morse Farm, that was held exactly fifty years earlier.
          Other activities include section participation in Montpelier's Fourth of July parade. This is still in the planning stage and volun-
        teers are needed with ideas and to participate on the day of the event. Reidun Nuquist is writing a history of the section based
        on a wealth of material gathered from the GMC and from the section's archives. It is anticipated that the history will be com-
        pleted by late fall.

   A leadership retreat was held in Randolph on November 13,
2004, to consider the planning study for the GMC, prepared           jillaspinall@alumni.uwaterloo.ca for details.
by Demont & Associates of Portland, Maine, to evaluate the
potential of a capital campaign. Other objectives of the study
were to determine public perceptions of the GMC, evaluate
club priorities for needs, and recommend a plan of action for
fund raising.
  Some issues raised were the need for greater public recog-
nition of the value of the Long Trail and its relationship to the
club, i.e. a greater marketing effort in and out of Vermont.
Other recommendations were seeking the co-operation of
other groups, such as conservation commissions and cross-
country ski clubs. It was recommended that when seeking
funding from politicians and others that emphasis be put on
public health, votes, tourism, economic impact, as well as the
immediate needs, such as facilities and miles of Long Trail to
be maintained.
  The investment in a capital campaign was estimated to be
about 8% of the total goal which the Demont study suggested
should be $5 million rather than the $9.2 million originally
identified as a need as that seemed a possible amount that
could be raised. It was also recommended that before the
campaign is announced, some large donations should be
secured so the beginning point is not zero.                          Camel's Hump from Wind Gap Beaver Pond on Dean Trail.

• November 22, 2004 • Eight Bridges Over the North
Branch River, by Jill Aspinall
  Bridges. They are the key to transportation in any city built
around rivers. The cities either have easy traffic flow
(pedestrian, bicycle, and automotive), or they don't. I can think
of several cities that fall into the latter category. Travel plans
in those places frequently focus on which bridge to cross or
when to cross it. Returning to a local perspective, it is nice
that travel around Montpelier does not need to focus on
bridges, even though Montpelier has three rivers, two of which
flow through the center of town. We are so well served with
bridges that I often forget I'm traveling over them. Hence the
idea of my November 22nd evening walk of eight bridges over
the North Branch River. It was a chilly November evening and
our compact group of three walkers made good time crossing
the North Branch River eight times over eight different
bridges. We added two more bridges over the Winooski River.                 December 4, 2004, Road Walk, Mad River Valley.
