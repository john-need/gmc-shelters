                           CIRCUMNAVIGATING LAKE CHAMPLAIN:
                           PADDUNG AROUND OUR SIXTH LARGEST LAKE
                                 Cathy Frank to Speak at Annual Meeting
  Cathy Frank of South Burlington will be our guest speaker at the Montpelier Section's annual meeting
on Saturday, April 5, in the parish hall of Christ Episcopal Church in Montpelier.
   Many section members have dipped their paddles into Lake Champlain, but few know the lake as well
as Cathy Frank. Over four years, Cathy and her friend Margy
Holden paddled the entire shoreline of the lake, from Whitehall,
New York, to the Richelieu River and the northern shore of Mis-
sisquoi Bay, including all the major islands-a total of 650
miles. Their day trips ranged in distance from 5 to 22 miles,
from May through October. From their kayaks, Cathy and
Margy observed water conditions, wildlife, flora, geology, and
shoreline development, gaining along the way a deeper under-
standing of the lake's geography and rich history, which is also
Vermont's history.
   Much of what they learned along the way is found in their de-
tailed guidebook, A Kayaker's Guide to Lake Champlain: Exploring the New York, Vermont and Quebec
Shores (Black Dome Press) by Catherine Frank and Margaret Holden.
   Cathy Frank is a long-time member of the Burlington Section. As a web designer she created the
Green Mountain Club's first website. She is a former long-time instructor at Community College of Ver-
mont and has served on numerous nonprofit boards. A summer resident of the Lake Champlain is-
lands, Cathy is an avid paddler who kayaks every day in the summer. She also swims, bikes, skis, and
hikes, and is a Long Trail end-to-end'er.
  Come to hear Cathy share her love of Lake Champlain, to admire her photographs, to learn about the
natural and human history of the lake--and to get inspired to take your own boat out for an early season
spin.

MONYP.ELIEA
  The rating levels of difficulty are easy, moderate, and difficult, with a few outings falling in between.
        • Easy - accessible to all in good physical condition; suitable for families with children.
        • Moderate - requires a degree of stamina; some previous experience is advisable.
        • Difficult - challenging events for people in good physical condition; previous experience necessary.
  Unless otherwise noted, trips leave from the Montpelier High School (MHS) parking lot. Always bring appropri-
ate gear for the event you are attending (i.e. lunch, liquids, snacks, suitable clothing, footwear, and other items as
mentioned in the Calendar of Events). Non-members are welcome on our events.
  Our trip leaders are volunteers who are not necessarily trained in first aid, nor does the Montpelier Section or
the GMC certify or license trip leaders. Participants should attend these events with the understanding that they
are responsible for their own preparedness and safety, and that all contribute to the well-being of the group.
