                                        MANY HANDS
                                           by Andrew Nuquist,
                                             Trails & Shelters Coordinator
   The many volunteers who showed up for the section’s spring trail work on Bamforth Ridge, May 13,
and north of Barnes Camp, May 20, completed the work necessary to prepare our assigned Long Trail
                                         miles for the 2017 hiking season. Not only did we have enough
                                         hands for the routine
                                         clean-up tasks, but
                                         we also had hands to
                                         spare for more difficult
                                         trail-hardening jobs
                                         that needed attention.
                                           One such task on
                                         May 13 was to place
                                         a sufficient number of
                                         suitably large step-
                                         ping stones in mud
                                         holes on the LT south
                                         of Bamforth Ridge
                                         Shelter, so that hikers
                                         could stay on the trail without sinking ankle-deep in mud. This
                                         crew was led by John Buddington.
                                           Morgan Irons and Alan Paschell replaced rotted wooden steps
                                         on the LT between Barnes
                                         Camp and Sterling Pond, us-
                                         ing wood steps that they had
previously carried up the trail. While seven of us did routine work
on May 20 and Morgan and Alan replaced steps, John Buddington
and Ken Hertz began building stone steps down to a stream cross-
ing a little north of Barnes Camp. With routine work completed, in
the early afternoon several of the seven workers added their
hands to the step-building job, so the augmented crew could finish
the steps by late afternoon.
   While the many hands (fourteen workers on May 13, and eleven
on May 20) were a gratifying turn-out, all were experienced trail
workers and none were new to trail maintenance. Where were
you, by the way? We need fresh hands whom we can teach to par-
ticipate in this social activity―working together to keep the LT
passable.
   Do the photos scare you? No one is ever pushed to take on spe-
cial jobs that require strength, skill, experience, and a capacity to
enjoy mud, dirt, and exhaustion. We will teach you how to clean a
water bar or clip branches, and will let you stop when you or your hands tire. How about giving trail
maintenance a try by joining us on October 14 or 21? It is gratifying work, and we need you.
  (See Calendar of Events on page 4 for more details.)

CALENDAR OF EVENTS
                                    September 6 - November 16, 2017

  The rating levels of difficulty are easy, moderate, and difficult, with a few outings falling in between.
         • Easy - accessible to all in good physical condition; suitable for families with children.
         • Moderate - requires a degree of stamina; some previous experience is advisable.
         • Difficult - challenging events for people in good physical condition; previous experience necessary.
  Unless otherwise noted, trips leave from the Montpelier High School (MHS) parking lot. Always bring appropri-
ate gear for the event you are attending (i.e. lunch, liquids, snacks, suitable clothing, footwear, and other items
as mentioned in the Calendar of Events). Non-members are welcome on our events. There is no charge..
  Our trip leaders are volunteers who are not necessarily trained in first aid, nor does the Montpelier Section or
the GMC certify or license trip leaders. Participants should attend these events with the understanding that they
are responsible for their own preparedness and safety, and that all contribute to the well-being of the group.
         The Young Adventures Club is a group to get parents and children (0-12 years old) outdoors to hike,
        play, learn about nature, and make friends. Events happen on the second weekend of every month.
        Please check out their website: gmcmontpelier.org/yac for details.
