Greetings from Bethel, Alaska:
\'linter is trying to slip into sprin g but is not yet succeeding. In February
the mea.n temperature was minus l.'.3 degrees F, am that's not figuring the chill
factor. Today, for example, is 24 degr ees with 24 MPH winds; it's cold. When
the snow flurries aren't coming horizon tally, it's sand. Still the tundra is
beautiful, and while I'm eating last year's freeze-dried berries I'm looking
forward to this summer's crop. The river is still a runway and highway, break-
up comes sometime in May. Looking at my topo map~, there is the ma.in river plus
miles of sloughs to explore nearby. I also have a couple of outings planned
which will involve a plane trip. That is why I have a kayak on order.
Winter has been fine, a?Xi because Bethel had almost no snow•I flew up river for
a couple of days where there was a lot am skied.    There is a lot more sunshine
up here to even things out. Right now sunset is at 9:15 p.m. am we ' re gaining
six minutes almost every day. I've ecrl;en reindeer, moose meat and fried pike
livers. They're all delicious, I've also been introduced to steam baths• Building
a steam house will be the first thing I'll want to do when I get back to Vermont.
I'm waiting for the weather to warm up a bit so tha.t I can go down to the coast for
trout and salmon fishing. I would also like to watch the sealing. There is plenty
to do in Bethel.
