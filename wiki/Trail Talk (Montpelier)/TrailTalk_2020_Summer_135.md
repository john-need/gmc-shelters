Getting Outdoors in
            Summer, 2020
                      by Phyllis Rubenstein

  It’s early May and no one knows when life will return
to normal or what normal will look like. Governor
Scott’s stay-at-home order permits outdoor activities
within 10 miles of one’s home. The Department of
Forests, Parks, and Recreation closed state lands,
including the Long Trail and its side trails. The Green
Mountain Club has requested that people stay off the
trails and recommends outdoor activities that one can
begin from one’s home. When the trails open and
we’re permitted to drive to destinations, will we wear
face masks? Only carpool with household members?
Practice social distancing? Where can we recreate
now? Below are some suggestions with dates, in pa-
renthesis, of trip reports you can access on the sec-
tion website.                                                 Charlene Bohl, Snowshoeing, Hunger Mtn., February, 2017
  You can bicycle from your door to Moretown                                                                S. Bailey
(6/7/17), Middlesex (6/14/16), Adamant (6/13/17),
Worcester (5/11/16), etc. Within walking distance of
your back door, you may be able to walk to the new
multiuse trail, or hike in Hubbard Park (3/15/17), North
Branch Nature Trails (12/7/19), or Sabin’s Pasture
(6/21/17). You can drive to walk around Berlin Pond
(4/22/17), around Sodom Pond (4/16/16), around Mir-
ror Lake (11/3/18), or to East Montpelier to walk a
road loop (4/30/16). Or you can drive to hike Martin’s
Bridge Park (5/11/18), Millstone Quarries (9/24/17),
the East Montpelier Trails (8/16/15), Middlesex Town
Forest (6/3019), Calais Town Trails (9/22/19), Irish
Hill (7/19/17), or Stranahan Forest (6/30/19).
  When the higher-elevation trails dry out, you can
drive to hike Chase Mountain (12/28/19), White Rocks
(1/20/18), Hunger Mountain (7/4/13), or Worcester
Mountain (10/19/16). Assuming boat launches are
open, you can paddle on Wrightsville Reservoir
(6/17/19).
  So get outdoors and be safe!                                   More than a step-over: LT in Smugglers’ Notch
