NATIONAL SCENIC TRAIL DESIGNATION FOR THE LONG TRAIL?
                                                                                                by Reidun Nuquist

  I am one of many who feel torn over National Scenic Trail (NST) status for the Long Trail. Is this the best
way to protect the trail? Is this the time to apply for designation?
  What is clear is that it will be a challenge for the Green Mountain Club to safeguard the remaining 27 miles
of the LT in northern Vermont. Since the inception of the Long Trail Protection Fund campaign in 1987, we
have raised $1.8 million from private donors and state and federal governments, and saved 30-plus miles of
trail. This is a tremendous achievement. It has brought us new members and much good will.
  Money is, however, becoming increasingly hard to raise. There is fierce competition for private and public
funds. At the same time, the environmental movement is under attack from developers and property owners.
Who can ignore the recent assaults on Acts 250 and 200?
  A recent memo from GMC President Kim Simpson estimates that it will take ten years and another $5-10
million to give permanent protection to the LT. Can we do it alone any longer? Are our pockets deep enough
and our resolve strong enough? Or do we need help from the feds to save thi s major national recreational
resource?

       Little River State Park, January 2, 1993; 15 participants. Leader: Laurette Brady

         After a dark, cold, windy start, the sun emerged as we turned into Cotton Brook Road,
       entering Little River State Park on the Moscow side. Hikers set forth briskly up the gently
       sloping broad logging road that leads into the heart of the Rickers Mountain settlement area.
       Warmed by the sun and inspired by glorious views of the reservoir as the road tracked higher
       along its north shore, we walked somewhat longer and farther than the planned three miles. On
       the way back, we took a pleasant quick trip down to an old homestead ruin by the shore, ending
       up just after noon. This area is rich in historical atmosphere and has many inviting trails past
       abandoned early century fanns, a school, and a cemetery. A brochure and hiking guide is
       available from the State of Vermont Agency of Natural Resources.
