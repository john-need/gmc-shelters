                                 APRIL 13 EVENT YOU SHOULDN'T MISS!!
    Annual Meeting, Potluck, Program, Conversation, Camaraderie
     The Montpelier Section of the Green Mountain Club will hold its annual meeting on Friday, April 13, at
    the Vermont College of Fine Arts, Noble Reading Room, 39 College Street, Montpelier.
    NOTE: This year the meeting is located across the street from the previous location of the T. W. Wood
    Gallery. You will need to enter the OUTSIDE SIDE DOOR at Noble Hall, round the right corner facing
    the building, to enter Noble Reading Room.                                                        ·

                                             TIME: Social: 5:30 P.M.
                                                   Potluck Supper: 6:00 P.M.
       The annual business meeting will follow the potluck supper, after which Dave Hardy, Director of Field
    Programs, describes the exciting Winooski Bridge project expected to begin construction on Labor Day
    weekend. He and Will Wiquist, Executive Director, have agreed to answer your questions. President
    Marge Fish is expected to attend. This will be followed by Andrew and Reidun Nuquist's slides and com-
    mentary on their Scottish hiking trip, HIKING THE SPEYSIDE WAY: FROM MORAY FIRTH TO
    AVI EMORE. The Nuquists are long-
    time members of the Montpelier Sec-
    tion, who enjoy the outdoors and travel-
    ing, especially combining the two.
      They will tell us about walking the 65-
    mile long Speyside Way, one of the
    United Kingdom's national trails. It fol-
    lows Scotland's loveliest river from the
    Moray Firth to the foothills of the Cairn-
    gorm Mountains. Using trails and aban-
    doned railway beds, it passes through a
    beguiling mix of scenery: the wave-
    battered shores of the Moray Firth, pic-
    turesque towns, rich farmland, mixed
    woodland, heather-clad hills, and an-
    cient Caledonian pine forests.

GRANOLA RECIPE
        From Sairs to Nuquist to Bohl to You

  A recipe can be a social document; a record of a tradition passed down between friends. Several
years ago, Reidun Nuquist was kind enough to share a granola recipe with me. She and Andrew got
the recipe from Sally Sairs; they added their own touches, as I have added to mine. Here is the anno-
tated version:

 Mix together:
       5-7 c. oatmeal                         Sally says 5, the Nuquists and I use 7
       1 package dried milk powder            Sally says one cup, and that is what I use
       1 cup raw sunflower seeds
       1 cup raw sesame seeds                 Sometimes I go light on the sesame seeds
       1 cup soy flour                        Can be omitted if unable to find
       1 cup raw almonds                      Sally says peanuts are an option: I believe all nuts are
                                              good including pecans and walnuts
        1 cup raw cashews                     Reidun says pieces are cheaper; I use pieces too
        1 cup coconut                         Sally and I use coconut; Andrew and Reidun omit

 Heat together:
       1 cup safflower or canola oil          Sally uses safflower; Reidun and I use canola
       1 cup maple syrup                      Reidun thinks the darkest, but I like light syrup
       1 teas. vanilla

 Pour hot liquid over dry ingredients in a large shallow roasting pan or a baking sheet.
 Bake at 275-300 degrees. Stir every 20 minutes until brown. S~lly .says ebout 40,minutes total;
 Andrew and Reidun need about twice that. I usually need an hour.
                        :
  I like to add a couple handfuls of dried cranberries about half way through. Make extra to free away,
it's ready for future adventures. ENJOY!
