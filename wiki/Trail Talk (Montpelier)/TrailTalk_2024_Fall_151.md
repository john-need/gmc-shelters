                                    American Larch: A Deciduous Conifer
                                         from our Naturalist Phyllis Rubenstein
    Does that sound like an oxymoron? How can a
conifer be deciduous? Well, if the tree has needles
and cones rather than leaves and flowers, it is a coni-
fer. If it sheds its needles every fall, it is a deciduous
conifer.
    A little background: Deciduous trees, such as
maples, birches, oaks, ashes, and beech, have green
leaves. In the fall, when the days are shorter, chloro-
phyll breaks down so that the green color disappears
and other pigments are exposed. Leaves on decidu-
ous trees turn yellow, orange, red, or purple before
they fall off.
    Coniferous trees bear cones and have needles or
scale-like leaves which do not fall off every year.
