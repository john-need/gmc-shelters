                               180 Miles on the John Muir Trail
  John Buddington and Kathy Gohl will present slides of their August, 2014, hike on the John Muir Trail
in the Sierra Nevada of California at the Friday, April 10, Montpelier Section Annual Meeting.
  They began their hike at
4,040' in Yosemite Valley,
and after two warm-up
days, were at 9,400'. During
the next 18 days they
crossed only one road. The
elevation varied from 8,000' -
to 12,000' and the terrain
ranged from lodge pole for-
ests to mountain meadows,
mountain lakes, talus
slopes, and rocky passes.
They passed through the
John Muir and Ansel Adams
wildernesses, three national
parks, and saw mostly thru-
hikers and marmots. They
encountered no forest fires,
no snow, only overnight showers, and twenty days of bright sunshine.
  John and Kathy are experienced hikers whose tale of the John Muir Trail, will make you want to start
getting your gear together for the summer hiking season. Having hiked many miles on many trails, they
will be able to answer your questions on long distance hiking.

  The rating levels of difficulty are easy, moderate, and difficult, with a few outings falling in between.
         ·Easy - accessible to all in good physical condition; suitable for families with children.
         ·Moderate - requires a degree of stamina; some previous experience is advisable.
         ·Difficult - challenging events for people in good physical condition; previous experience necessary.
  Unless otherwise noted, trips leave from the Montpelier High School (MHS) parking lot. Always bring appropri-
ate gear for the event you are attending (i.e. lunch, liquids, snacks, suitable clothing, footwear, and other items as
mentioned in the Calendar of Events). Non-members are welcome on our events.
  Our trip leaders are volunteers who are not necessarily trained in first aid, nor does the Montpelier Section or
the GMC certify or license trip leaders. Participants should attend these events with the understanding that they
are responsible for their own preparedness and safety, and that all contribute to the well-being of the group.
