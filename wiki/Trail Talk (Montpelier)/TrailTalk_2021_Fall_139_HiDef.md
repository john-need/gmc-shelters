                                          October 2 - November 6, 2021

  The rating levels of difficulty are easy, moderate, and difficult, with a few outings falling in between.
         • Easy - accessible to all in good physical condition; suitable for families with children.
         • Moderate - requires a degree of stamina; some previous experience is advisable.
         • Difficult - challenging events for people in good physical condition; previous experience necessary.
  Always bring appropriate gear for the event you are attending (i.e. lunch, liquids, snacks, suitable clothing,
footwear, and other items as mentioned in the Calendar of Events). Non-members are welcome on our events.
There is no charge, unless the destination charges one (example: State Park entrance fees).
  Our trip leaders are volunteers who are not necessarily trained in first aid, nor does the Montpelier Section or
the GMC certify or license trip leaders. Participants should attend these events with the understanding that they
are responsible for their own preparedness and safety, and that all contribute to the well-being of the group.

  I cannot remember how soon after my arrival in
Montpelier I met Eric and Donna, and they became
close friends to Reidun and myself. Much of our
friendship revolved around the Montpelier Section
of the GMC.
  Of both the GMC and the Montpelier Section, Eric
was a member of numerous committees; as well as
a trip leader, a trail worker, and a shelter builder. In
1993 when our section was responsible for the
Club's annual meeting, the first held in GMC head-
quarters in Waterbury Center, Eric, a planning
committee member and a NECI employee, ar-
ranged for a fabulous dinner managed and provid-
ed by NECI. NECI also created a birthday cake for
the section's fiftieth birthday celebration in 2005.
For nine years in the 1990s Eric served as the sec-
tion's Trails & Shelters Coordinator. After the 1998
ice storm, Eric coordinated the section's participa-
tion in the massive spring clean-up required to
clear the Long Trail of debris totally blocking pas-
sage in many places. Up to 200 downed trees per
mile crossed some high elevation trail portions. Er-
ic also assumed a leadership role in the construc-
tion of shelters at both Sterling Pond and Bamforth

      morning when sev-
      en of us arrived at
      Green River Reser-
      voir State Park, but
      otherwise the
      weather was fine,
      sunny and in the

      July 28, 2021 – Hike. Warren Falls and Blueberry Lake Trails by Steve and Heather Bailey
                                                      Five of us started out the day with a brief stop
                                                    at Warren Falls, a very scenic spot on the Mad
                                                    River south of Warren village. It was a cool
                                                    morning, so none of us felt like swimming,
                                                    though there were a few hardy souls plunging
                                                    into the frigid waters. We then headed to the
                                                    shores of Blueberry Lake, where a system of
                                                    mountain biking trails was put in a few years
                                                    ago.
                                                      There were lots of switchbacks, and the climbs
                                                    were not steep. We had lunch at a nice lookout
                                                    over the Mad River Valley with views of Mts.
                                                    Grant and Cleveland on the Long Trail. There
                                                    were some bikers from a Sugarbush summer
                                                    camp for kids, but there was plenty of room for
                                                    all of us on the trails.



      August 4, 2021 – Hike. Devil's Gulch by Steve and Heather
      Bailey
        Six hikers made the trip to Eden, VT on a sunny, warm day. We
      started south on the LT, making a stop at Ritterbush Pond. There
      we were sad to see a cabin that had been used by the public had
      burned down sometime in the last few months. We continued on
      the LT through Devil's Gulch, scrambling over a few boulders, until
      we reached Spruce Ledge shelter. There we had lunch at
      Devil's Perch, a bench with a wonderful view of Mt. Belvidere and
      Ritterbush Pond below. We returned via Devil's Gulch and the Bab-
      cock Trail, which led us along the edge of Muddy Pond, which was
      much nicer than its name suggests.

August 7, 2021 – Hike. North trail up Mt.
Pisgah by Dana Lawrence, Nancy Thomas
  On a sunny, sultry, summer day, seven hik-
ers met at the northern trailhead to the peak
of Mt. Pisgah and its stunning overlook of
Lake Willoughby in Westmore. The group,
led by Dana Lawrence and Nancy Thomas,
included two members from the NEK Sec-
tion, four from the Montpelier Section, and an
exciting new member recruited on the trail.
The group enjoyed the outstanding rock work
on the trail and the camaraderie of connect-
ing with new faces across sections as evi-
denced by numerous very engaged conver-
sations. The outing was concluded with a
cooling dip at North Beach on Lake Willough-
by, overlooking the vista of the cliffs of Mt.
Pisgah and Mt. Hor towering over the lake in
the distance.




                                                August 29, 2021 – Walk. Boulders and Blackberries by Cynthia
                                                Martin & George Longenecker
                                                  Walking the trails in Groton State Forest is always enjoyable. On
                                                a recent late summer afternoon we hiked the Coldwater-Hosmer
                                                Brook loop from the Nature Center on Boulder Beach Road. The
                                                trail ascends through woods passing a stream and some glacial
                                                boulders before reaching the height of land and descending to the
                                                Hosmer Brook Trail intersection. The trail then follows Hosmer
                                                Brook for a stretch before returning to a woods ramble. We
                                                stopped to pick a handful of sweet plump blackberries near Big
                                                Deer Campground, then walked a short stretch of road back to the
                                                Nature Center.
