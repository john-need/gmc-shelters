                   July 3rd Parade
                       By Steve Bailey

       This year the Montpelier section of the GMC cel-
ebrated their 70th anniversary by marching in the capitol
city’s July 3rd parade. They carried a 27-foot-long banner
which depicted the Long Trail winding its way through
the Green Mountains. Mounted on saplings with foliage
intact, it was carried by GMC members, to the delight of
the crowd, who cheered and clapped. Andrew Nuquist,
the section’s senior member, carried the first sapling.

Peacham Bog Hike                                            A Sad Farewell to Ann Burcroff
                   By Phyllis Rubenstein                                                  By Heather Bailey

        On the morning of July 13, a warm and breezy
Sunday, two humans and one dog hiked to Peacham Bog
from Green Bay Loop Road. Most of the hike was along
a rolling old logging/woods road. Shortly after passing
a pond previously dammed by beavers, and lined with
sweet gale, white meadowsweet, and tall meadow rue,
the woods road joins the Peacham Bog Loop Trail, which
starts at the Nature Center in Groton State Park. After a
few tenths of a mile, the Peacham Bog Loop Trail heads
into the woods, where we saw forest flowers past bloom,
including bunchberry, starflower, Clintonia (bluebead)
lily, trillium, cucumber root, lady’s slippers, goldthread,
and more. In another few tenths, the trail reaches the bog
boardwalk. At the end of it there is a kiosk and a bench,
where we sat and observed the flora of this unique, domed
peatland bog, including black spruce, tamarack, Labrador
tea, sheep laurel, bog laurel, three-leaved false Solomon’s

Eshqua Bog and Quechee Gorge
               Trip report by Phyllis Rubenstein

       On Monday, June 23, three of us got an early start
to beat the heat and explore Eshqua Bog and Quechee
Gorge. The bog, which is actually a rich fen, offered a
spectacular display of hundreds of showy lady’s slippers.
After getting our fill of these beauties and other fen plants,
we hiked the one-mile loop trail through the woods.
       Next we drove to Quechee Gorge, where we hiked
to Dewey Mill Pond, which provided a stunning view
down the gorge. We stopped for lunch at a picnic table in
the shade and then hiked to the bottom of the gorge.
       At the end of our hike on this very hot day, we cooled
off in the Quechee Gorge Visitor Center and enjoyed its
historical display, which begins with the retreat of the
glaciers, continues to the first indigenous peoples, then
on through the early white settlers, logging, woolen mill,
railroad bridge, and the building of the current auto road.
       Despite the heat, we spent several enjoyable hours
outdoors.
