Green Mountain Club
                                           Montpelier Section Annual Meeting
                                                     April 13, 2007
 The Montpelier Section of the Green Mountain Club held its annual meeting April 13, 2007 at Christ Church in Montpelier.
Thirty-six members were present.
Business Meeting
  After a social hour and potluck dinner, President Deb Kirchwey called the meeting to order at 7:00 P.M. She told the mem-
bers that she had been fortunate to serve as president for one year, but would be unable to continue to serve because she
was moving to another part of Vermont. She praised the hard work and dedication of section members and wished them well
in the future.
A. Old Business
     Acceptance of the minutes of the 2006 annual meeting. MOTION: Accept the minutes. MOTION CARRIED.
B. New Business
     • Doris Washburn read a heartfelt tribute to long time GMC member David Otis who died February 18, 2007. This trib-
        ute will be re-printed in Trail Talk so that all section members can appreciate his contribution to the section and the
        main club.
     • Approval of the Treasurer's Report and Proposed Budget. Outgoing Treasurer Ken Hertz spoke in favor of the
        treasurer's report and the proposed budget. The proposed budget includes a $1,000 gift earmarked for the Second
        Century Campaign. Ken spoke of the tradition in the section of supporting the main club. MOTION: Accept the treas-
        urer's report and proposed budget. MOTION CARRIED.
     • The members then considered the Section Reports included in the annual meeting packet. Ann Burcroff praised the
        398 volunteer hours contributed by section members. Mary Garcia stated that the membership of the section wa~
        holding steady, with four fewer members than last year.
     • Second Century Campaign Update. Priscilla Page, co-chair of the Montpelier Section efforts for the Second Century
        Campaign, introduced Shawn Keeley, development officer at the GMC headquarters in Waterbury Center. She praised
        his patience and fortitude in helping the section's efforts and reminded the members of GMC Executive Director Ben
        Rose's power point presentation from last year's annual meeting. Before its 100th anniversary in 2010, the club needs
        to raise at least $5 million needed to:
                1. Protect the Long Trail,
                2. Maintain the Long Trail,
                3. Improve headquarters, including rebuilding the barn or a similar structure.
          Ann Burcroff, also co-chair of the Montpelier Section's efforts, spoke more specifically about what the section had
        accomplished. She and other members of the commi\tee have raised pledges from 53 members of the Montpelier
        Section for a total of $113,600. There are still 271 members of the section who have not yet been solicited, but will be
        contacted in the future. The model developed by the Montpelier Section will be used in other sections.
          Andrew Nuquist, co-chair of the Second Century Campaign, then spoke. The model developed by the Montpelier
        Section is in the process of being utilized by the Burlington, Ottauquechee and Killington sections. So far, all com-
        bined efforts have raised pledges of $3,929,787, which includes matching grants. There will be a public announcement
        of the campaign at the main club's annual meeting in June, followed by a mailing to all club members.
     • Duncan Wilkie updated the members on the Side-to-Side Patch. Badges have been earned by Chris Hanna, Mary
        Lou Recor, and Duncan Wilkie. This new rocker patch is given for completing all the side trails to the LT, and was de-
        veloped by Smith Edwards, Steve Lightholder, and Duncan Wilkie. There are 186 miles of side trails to the Long Trail.
     • Members then recognized the Outgoing Officers. Duncan Wilkie has been the chair of the Trails & Shelters Commit-
        tee for three years, during the lime the section constructed the Sterling Pond shelter. Ken Hertz has served ably as
        treasurer for the past three years, and Deb Kirchwey has been the president for the past year.
     • Elections. The Nominating Committee, composed of Jill Aspinall, Reidun Nuquist, and Duncan Wilkie, proposed the
        following slate of officers to the members present and elected were:
                President: Paul Deluca                       Membership Coordinator: Mary Garcia
                Vice-President: no nomination               Trails & Shelters Coordinator: Ken Hertz
                Secretary: Charlene Bohl                    Director: Ann Burcroff
                Treasurer: Steve Lightholder                Alternate Director: Priscilla Daggett
          On behalf of the Nominating Committee, Jill encouraged members to consider being vice president. She described
        the job of vice president who backs up the president, organizes the outing schedules, distributes the trip packets, gath-
        ers trip reports and organizes the annual meeting. There were no nominations from the floor. The bylaws give the Ex-
        ecutive Committee the power to appoint a replacement until the next annual meeting.
        It was moved to nominate the slate of officers proposed by the Nominating Committee, and to have the secretary cast
        one vote for the proposed slate. The motion passed.
     • Awards for Outings and Trail Work. There was a three-way tie for most outings led between Andrew Nuquist,
        Reidun Nuquist, and Ken Hertz. The most popular outing was a Young Adventurers trip in the North Branch Park led
        by Dave Blumenthal and Lexi Shear. George Longenecker and Cynthia Martin were most active in trail work. All re-
        ceived a prize.
 The meeting adjourned at 9:00 P.M., at which time our guest speaker, Burr Morse, spoke about the Morse Farm tradition of
keeping their land open and free from development.

                                                   Charlene Bohl, Secretary

David M. Otis (1923-2007)
                                                                  by Doris Washburn

  It was with sadness that I learned of the passing of David Otis on February 18, 2007. As I walked through the door of the
funeral parlor to pay my respect to his family, there stood his extra long wood-and-rawhide snowshoes. Many pleasant hikes
with Dave flashed through my mind.
  Dave joined the Green Mountain Club in the early 1930's as hut boy on Mt. Mansfield, his favorite mountain. He was first a
member of the Burlington Section, then transferred to Montpelier when he and his wife, Ann, moved to the area.
  I met Dave on January 25, 1964, on my very first trip with the GMC, a snowshoe hike to Taylor Lodge for the annual oyster
stew supper, which Dave and Ann hosted for several years. He was a frequent hike leader for the Montpelier Section, and
always participated in trail work on the LT. Dave was honored at our fiftieth anniversary for having led a total of sixty-four out-
ings. He became an end-to-end'er in 1974.
  Dave held many positions within the club: president, vice president, and outings chairman for the Burlington Section and
president and trustee for the Montpelier Section. He served on club-wide committees. In 1967, he was elected vice president
of the GMC.
  One of his proudest accomplishments was designing a club patch in 1960 for the intersectional at Lake Eden; it was quickly
adopted as the club's official logo. Dave was also chairman of the planning commi~~ when the Montpelier Section hosted
the 1969 intersectional at Camp Wihikowi in Northfield- a successful event despite the fact that it rained seven days out of
ten!
  A member of the Vermont Automobile Enthusiasts, Dave was a lover of antique cars. He owned a red 1923 Cadillac V ictoria
coupe (as old as he was) and a 1926 Model T pickup. He also enjoyed motorcycles, and at age 82, still rode his 1973 Honda
CB350 and a 1987 Suzuki GS650G.
  Dave was a devoted caregiver for his wife Ann, who suffers from Alzheimer's. He will be missed by all who knew him as a
true and faithful friend.
  According to his daughter Mary Ellen, these are some of the things Dave enjoyed:
          - Scenic vistas, he would stop at every one.
          - Walking in Hubbard Park at any time of year, except black fly season.
          - Stopping to drink at Big Spring, even though the sign says "Drink at your own risk. "
          - Singing "Here We Sit like Birds in the Wilderness", when it rained on camping trips.
          - Being fit in his eighties and proud of it.
          - The Lake Woebegone radio show.
          - No-pulp orange juice: "I want to drink it, not eat it!"
