                               Montpelier Section Annual Meeting
                                              Sunday, May 22, 2022
                 On Sunday, May 22, the GMC Montpelier Section will hold its annual meeting.
               For the past two years, we have had to cancel our in-person meeting and hold
               online voting to elect officers and approve the budget. This year we are going to
               try going back to an in-person meeting, though in a much different format than in
               past years. First of all, the meeting will be outside in the Old Shelter at Hubbard
               Park in Montpelier. With the COVID situation the way it is, we are not planning on
               a pot-luck dinner with shared food, but are asking everyone to bring some mid-
               afternoon snacks. We are also going to skip the outside speaker with a slide
               show.
                Here is the tentative schedule:
                      2:00 P.M. – Business meeting to elect officers and approve budget.
                                   There will also be officer reports for events of the past year.
                          2:45 P.M. – Snacks (bring your own) and socializing
                          3:15 P.M. – Hikes around Hubbard Park led by GMC members.
                 Of course, this is still a few months in the future. The COVID situation will
               change in the meantime in ways that are hard to predict. Be sure to check the
               Montpelier Section website, www.gmcmontpelier.org. There will be an update a
               few weeks before the event.

   If road walks aren’t your thing, and it’s too muddy for higher elevation
 trails, the logical solution is to seek out some lower elevation trails.
 They dry out earlier in the season and can afford some nice hiking op-
 portunities. We have found that mountain biking trails are very good in
 this respect. For example, one of our favorites is the Perry Hill trail sys-
 tem in Waterbury, which starts out at 500 ft elevation and goes to
 around 1200 ft. Perry Hill also has the advantage of only being 15
 minutes away from Montpelier. Another place we like is the Cady’s Falls
 trail network near Morrisville, which is at a similar elevation. There is a
 nice trail system around Hardwick High School, which also tops out at
 1200 ft. Blueberry Lake in Warren is mostly below 1600 ft. and has
 some very scenic trails. You can find these and many more trails on the
 web at trailforks.com, which is a great resource for biking and hiking.
   We have found that mountain biking trails in general seem to be drier        Lookout on Blueberry Lake trails
 and better engineered than your average hiking trail. Many of them have been created more recently,
 have better drainage, a more gentle pitch, and more switchbacks, which means less erosion.
   You might be wondering about possible conflicts with mountain bikers if you walk on these trails. First
 of all, I suggest not going on weekends. I have also found that most bikers ride in the afternoons rather
 than mornings. One nice thing is that in April and May, almost all of these trails are closed to bikes but
 not necessarily to hikers. Sometimes it’s hard to figure out if a trail closed to bikes is OK for hiking. It’s
 often best to just go and see the trail conditions for yourself. Please respect the trails and don’t hike if
 the conditions are muddy, but have a great hike if they’re dry!
   So the next time you look out the window and see only mud, don’t despair – you can to find some-
 place fun to go outside.
