►   Governor Clement Shelter, History At-A-Glance, contributed by Amy Peacock
    Built in 1929 by the family of William H. Field of Mendon.
    Named for Percival W. Clement, governor of Vermont, 1919-1921.
    The shelter was built near the Herb Haley Farm.
              Source: Long Trail Guide Book; Green Mountain Club, 2017

            Percival W. Clement, a Rutland native, business tycoon and reported arch-rival of the Proctor family, took
    over as owner-publisher of the Rutland Herald in 1882. He was elected mayor of Rutland fifteen years later in
    1897.
            In 1902, Clement ran for governor for the first of what would be three times. He was ultimately elected in
    1918 at age 73 after two bitter defeats, both involving Fletcher Proctor and his supporters.
            Governor Clement died at age 80 in 1927. The Rutland Herald was then passed to his son-in-law,
    William H. Field. William was also a Rutland native who had previously been a successful executive at the
    Chicago Tribune and a co-founder of the New York Daily News. William made many changes to the Herald,
    including adding its first comic strip, Little Orphan Annie, in 1934. A year later on March 15,1935, when facing
    progressive vision loss and ultimate blindness, William took his own life. He was 57 years old.
            Source: A Timeline of the Herald’s Own History: Two Hundred Years of Reporting the
                     News; www.rutlandherald.com; December 7, 2019.

Sweet Sugar River, by Herb Ogden
          Back when Sullivan County in New Hampshire was still “green” on Vermont’s COVID-19 travel map, I decided to
sample the Sugar River Rail Trail. One can enjoy it on any bicycle with tires wider than what the typical road bike has. I
used my Raleigh DL-1, a beautiful, heavy old machine with 28” wheels, 1½” wide tires, a three-speed hub and rod brakes,
bought in 1966. It did very well on all but a couple of somewhat sandy spots that were pretty hard pedaling. Figuring it
was better to start the trip with an upgrade and end with a down, I first cycled east from Claremont to Newport. I’m glad I
did. I didn’t notice the upgrade all that much, but it was great to fly back down to the trailhead at the end of the ride. The
trail is beautiful, mostly hard packed cinders. It follows the Sugar River almost all the way, crossing it several times,
including twice on covered railroad bridges. The west one is especially interesting because an arced laminated truss
supports the structure. The east one is the longest surviving covered railroad bridge in the country. I think that title used
to belong to the former St. Johnsbury and Lake Champlain Railroad bridge in Swanton, but very sadly, it fell to arson in
1987. The Sugar River is beautiful, and, for most of its course that the rail trail follows, there are no riverbank roads, so
it’s quiet and backwoodsy. The trail ends about a tenth of a mile short of the former railroad station in Newport, well
preserved and put to other uses since all but the Claremont to Claremont Jct. piece of the railroad was abandoned in
1977.

Prospect Rock, Johnson, VT, by Larry Walter and Vivian Bebee
         In mid-September, Viv and I spent a few days at a B&B in Newport, not far from on the west shore of Lake
Memphremagog and only about a 15-minute walk to the Canadian border, so we walked to Canada and back a couple of
times during our stay. Since the weather was really nice, we checked out our hike books and decided to drive to Johnson
one morning to hike up to Prospect Rock (a spur trail off the LT). We parked on Hogback Road near the bridge over the
Lamoille River and headed north on the LT. The trail has a series of terraces and steep sections with multiple side trails
once you get closer to the outcrop. I slid to the right for a bit while Viv continued up the main trail. It’s only about 550 feet
of climbing for a 1,040 foot view, so if you take your time (we did), it’s an easy hike. (P.S. Viv says . . . “gets steep pretty
quickly and continues most of the way and was not easy.”) The view from the top is a nice one of the river below and
there was a bench to sit on for lunch or just soaking up the sun. According to the hike books, you can see White Face
Mtn., Daniel’s Notch and the Lamoille River Valley in the foreground. Seems about right. I sidled down about 100’ from
the view point and could see a couple anchor rings (rock climbing), so there’s definitely climbing on the south face.
Indeed, according to the Mountain Project website, there’s an assortment of climbs ranging from 5.7 (only slightly daft) to
5.11D (for those who routinely walk on ceilings). The hike back down to our car was pleasant enough, but the steepness
made it a little tricky for Viv. After returning to the car, we crossed the road and walked a few hundred feet and took the
short trail to the suspension bridge across the Lamoille River. Before returning to the car and the drive back to Newport,
we walked out a ways on the bridge for closer view of the River.

New Wildlife Management Area Opened Next to Long Trail, by Sandy Bragg
        Vermont Fish and Wildlife recently opened a new Wildlife Management Area in Shrewsbury, VT that conserves
over 500 acres adjacent to the Long Trail, providing new hiking opportunities in the area.
        The new Town Farm WMA includes foundations of the Shrewsbury Town Farm, which was first acquired by the
Town in 1870. The 527-acre property contains a diverse landscape including early-succession forest as well as more
mature stands of sugar maple, red oak, hickory, hophornbeam and butternut.

The acquisition enhances the Long Trail by adding additional side trails to the LT/AT corridor. For a mellow hike
or cross-country ski, there is an easy and relatively flat hike heading west along the extension of Lottery Road from WMA
parking area (an open area immediately west of Gaynor Road). For a brisker hike, head north from the WMA parking
area, following a trail which quickly winds east to climb Kinsman Peak (approximately 2,500’).
         My personal favorite is a delightful trail that goes south from the foundations of the Shrewsbury Town Farm along
a ridge until meeting the LT/AT. To access the trail, head west along the extension of Lottery Road until you see the
foundations of the Town Farm barn on your left, along with the trail head. The trail descends along a ridge until reaching
the LT/AT south of the Clarendon Shelter. Head east on the LT and then climb up Gaynor Road for a loop back to the
WMA (or if you want a longer hike continue north on the LT until you hit Lottery Road and road-walk back to the WMA).
         Note that none of the trails in the WMA are marked so a GPS device is recommended. Only southbound trail
connecting the WMA to the LT is shown on the attached map.
         The Town Farm WMA represents Vermont’s 100th wildlife management area and its conservation in 2020 marks
the 100th anniversary of wildlife management area ownership and management in Vermont. The land was acquired with
State funds, supplemented by donations from local residents and a bequest managed by the Vermont Land Trust.




What I Did on My Vacation, by Sue Thomas
         I think we can all agree that this has been a hiking season like no other! Avoiding the more-crowded trails,
however, opened up many other possibilities. Though, of course, I miss our club hikes, I've had a lot of fun exploring out-
of-the-way spots and old wood roads I've always wondered about.
         Ruth and I have taken to meeting halfway between Rutland and her home in Montpelier to explore dirt roads in
the Pittsfield/Stockbridge/Bethel area. We met up at Raven Ridge with my friend, Robin, to check out the odd geologic
formation known as "the oven." Diane joined me for a beautiful day at Leffert's Pond, a climb up Mt. Tom, many dirt
roads, and we caught the showy Lady Slippers blooming at Esqua Bog.
         Sunghee, Isaac, Claire and I scaled Mt. Philo on a gorgeous day, where we stopped for a chat with my
kindergarten classmate, now a volunteer ranger, and the kids marveled at the thought that someone as ancient as I, was
once in kindergarten. I think they've forgiven me for abandoning them on the LT near Clarendon Shelter. Our favorite
excursion though was to Mt. Tom, where we happened upon a ranger with bagpipes -- not something you see out in the
woods every day! He obligingly played us a tune and told the kids a story, and made it a most memorable day for all of
us.
         My friend, Pat, and her dogs have wandered old roads in Chittenden and Mendon with me, and I spent a sunny
afternoon reading Robert Frost poetry on Wallingford's "Book Trail" with Rob and Sue Carey, both former presidents of

Trail Maintenance Work Party on October 11, 2020, by Larry Walter
         We pretty much skipped Saturday’s scheduled trip due to conflicting schedules and afternoon rain clouds.
Sunday was perfect for the three of us, including myself, Herb and Wayne. We managed to bluff our way onto the
gondola and enjoyed a quiet ride up onto the summit. When we dumped off the bottom end of the steel catwalk a couple
folks hailed us. Turns out it was Kati Christoffel (southern section backcountry caretaker and water-color artist) and
Andrew Rosenthal Baxter (ridge runner extraordinaire). They were headed in to service the famed Cooper Pooper.
When we told them we planned to maintain from Cooper south to the Shrewsbury Peak trailhead, Andrew informed us
that he’d already taken care of the blowdowns and water bars along that stretch, plus from there north to US4. With that
information, we stashed one of the combies and headed south to address the one blowdown that was too big for Andrew’s
bowsaw. There were masses of people up there. I managed to talk one group of college kids into helping us out with the
crosscut saw. They seemed to enjoy it, but the masks hid any smiles that may have sprung up. Kati showed up at the
blowdown, just when we were finishing, so she helped us put in some new corduroy on the way back. There’s a lot of it
up there and seems to be working in the sloppy areas. We also managed to put in some stepping stones, but they’re hard
to locate under all of the moss and debris. Total hours, including showing up at the park: about 6.

                               Killington Section Annual Meeting
 Minutes of Directors’ Meeting
 September 13, 2020
 By Allison Henry, Secretary

The Killington Section’s Annual Meeting was held on September 13 via Zoom. It was rescheduled from April 19, 2020
and postponed several times due to the covid-19 pandemic and resulting restrictions and safety concerns.


                                                               7

<!-- page: 8 -->
The meeting was called to order at 1:05 pm by Section President, Herb Ogden.

Main Club Executive Director, Mike Debonis, spoke at the start of the meeting about the Club in general as well as the
impact of covid-19.
   • The Club has fared well overall despite the pandemic. The Long Trail is in good shape, despite limited staff, due
        to extra efforts by Sections and local volunteers. Individual donations have increased, and the Club is at an all-
        time record high with online sales of books, maps, and gear. A small group has put together outing guidelines for
        Club activities based on covid-19 safety restrictions.
   • The Club has achieved another victory with the protection of 160 acres near Codding Hollow, including ⅓ mile of
        the Long Trail.
   • It is the 100th anniversary for Taft Lodge, the oldest and largest shelter on the Long Trail.
   • Very positive response to the GMC’s online events.
   • Discussion ensued regarding various trail and shelter issues.
            o Mike said there have been conversations between the Club, Powdr Corp. (Killington’s parent company),
                 and the Department of Forest, Parks, and Recreation. Reportedly, FP and R wants to keep Cooper
                 Lodge but any actions are on hold due to covid-19. We discussed the possibility of putting up a sign near
                 Cooper explaining the Club’s involvement and the concerns regarding maintenance and upkeep.
            o There have been no recent conversations regarding the former Long Trail Lodge site (near Sherburne
                 Pass Trailhead lot, across from Inn at Long Trail).
            o Larry Walter said there will be a new Forest Service headquarters built off of Route 4 in Mendon. The
                 project is the result of funding that Senator Leahy secured in the annual budget for the Forest Service’s.
            o Vermont Fish and Wildlife has purchased the “Hidalgo Property,” which abuts the Long Trail corridor in
                 Shrewsbury.

The 2019 Annual Meeting minutes were accepted (Thomas/Bragg).

Sandy Bragg, Treasurer, shared the Treasurer’s report.
   • The Killington Section’s finances are in good health. We have had no major expenses this past year.
   • Our main costs include materials as well as the Smoke & Blazes mailings.

Larry Walter, Director to the Main Club, reported on Main Club happenings.
    • There are no major projects in the Killington Section.
    • The Sherburne Pass Trail (from Inn at Long Trail lot to where it joins the Appalachian Trail) has been brushed in.
       Hopefully this will keep traffic to one footpath and limit erosion.

Vivian Bebee reported on our newsletter, Smoke & Blazes.
    • Because there have been no club outings, we can send articles about our own hikes instead. We could also each
        write about our favorite hike.
    • Herb offered praise for the most recent issue, saying that despite the limited activities due to covid-19, it was one
        of the best-looking issues ever with lots of photos and other interesting things.

Sue Thomas gave the Outings Report, of which there have been none, saying, “We’re all missing it.”
   • She has spoken with various hike leaders and no one is comfortable leading a hike at this time due to safety
       concerns. It would be difficult to enforce safety regulations and group sizes would have to be very limited.
   • When asked, Mike Debonis said that other Sections and groups have had some outings, but have required
       attendees to wear masks, keep distance and have had limited group size. Also, most groups have only gone out
       with people they already know and have not invited the public.
   • Herb suggested that if we were to schedule outings, to give only the date, general location, leader’s name, and
       phone number so any attendees would have to contact the leader before showing up. Sue said that we have
       discussed this option in the past, pre-covid, to “pre-screen” attendees, but that hike leaders have had mixed
       opinions due to not wanting to field multiple phone calls. We also noted that some people including some of our
       most dedicated outing attendees don’t decide what they are doing until the day of, so if they don’t call ahead, they
       won’t be able to come last minute. Upon discussion several meeting attendees concurred that “no one wants to
       be the enforcer” (of rules).
   • Several members shared their own hiking experiences since covid-19. Experiences on trails in and around
       Vermont have varied. Some of us have mostly encountered individuals and small groups wearing masks and
       trying to maintain social distance, while others have run into large groups without masks. In general, most of us
       have had better experiences on the Long Trail and other less-used backcountry trails. Popular trails have

Our History and Archives report was given by Amy Peacock.

   • Dave Coppock will co-chair Trails and Shelters.

Elections were held for Killington Section Directors.
    • Herb Ogden, President and Cindy Taylor-Miller, Vice President have reached the end of their second three-year
        terms and are ineligible for re-election. Sue Thomas has reached the end of her current first three-year term.
    • Herb nominated Wayne Krevetski and Amy Peacock, seconded by Sandy Bragg.
    • Gerry Martin nominated Melissa Reichert, also seconded by Sandy Bragg.
    • Gerry Martin nominated Sue Thomas, seconded by Diane Bargiel.
    • Sue, Wayne, and Melissa accepted the nominations. Amy said she’d be interested next year as she is still very
        new to the club.
    • The slate of Wayne Krevetski, Susan Thomas, and Melissa Reichert was elected unanimously.
    • The Directors are now: Sandy Bragg, Gerry Martin, Ruth Hare, Allison Henry, Larry Walter, Spencer Wilson,
        Wayne Krevetski, Sue Thomas, and Melissa Reichert.

New Business was led by Herb Ogden.
   • School Groups: We have wanted to work with school groups on trail maintenance projects. This discussion was
      put on hold due to covid-19.
   • Larry Walter suggested that our Trail signage include a line about who hikers should contact if they observe
      maintenance problems on the Trail.
   • Our Welcome sign needs to be updated with current information. We discussed removing Officers’ individual
      names and including a link to the website instead. Herb and Barry will update the information and set this up.
   • We discussed setting a date for next year’s Annual Meeting. This discussion was postponed because of the
      unknowns due to covid-19.

Officers were appointed, with the discussion led by former President Herb Ogden.
     • Wayne was nominated for president by Sandy Bragg, seconded by Allison Henry. All Directors were in favor.
     • Melissa Reichert was nominated for Vice President, which she respectfully declined. Gerry Martin was nominated
         by Sandy Bragg, seconded by Allison Henry. All Directors were in favor.
     • Allison Henry was nominated to continue as Secretary by Ruth Hare, seconded by Sandy Bragg. All Directors
         were in favor.
     • Sandy Bragg was nominated to continue as Treasurer by Sue Thomas, seconded by Ruth Hare. All Directors
         were in favor.

The meeting was adjourned at 2:32 pm.
