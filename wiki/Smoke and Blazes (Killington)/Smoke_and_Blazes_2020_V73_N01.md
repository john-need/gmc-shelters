►   To our Four-Legged Members: The Killington Section has always welcomed friendly dogs on hikes. However,
    with trail use increasing in recent years, more trail systems are posting rules. Some, like all Nature Conservancy
    preserves and some private lands, prohibit dogs. Others, like Pine Hill, Pittsford Trails, some state parks and
    campgrounds, require that they be leashed and cleaned up after, while many areas are still open to all. Knowing
    ahead of time will make things easier for everyone, so hike descriptions may now list that information. It is still a
    good idea to check with the hike leader if you plan to bring a pet, as some hikes require spotting cars at different
    locations, meaning that your dog will have to ride in another car. For whatever reason – allergies, new
    upholstery, etc., not everyone may welcome a wet or muddy dog into their car. Hike leaders can also fill you in on
    trail conditions – a cliffy hike with rock scrambling may be too difficult for your dog, some trails have no water
    source, requiring you to carry extra for your dog, and some may cross traveled roads – always bring a leash. One
    last caution, our area is home to many porcupines, so beware!

►   Thanks, Allison! (by Sue Thomas)

►   A Vermont Ruck Weekend in Killington, Vermont:
    The Vermont Ruck, a new event promoting outdoor education and camaraderie, will take place the first weekend
    of spring, March 20-22, 2020, at the Church of Our Saviour, Mission Farm Road in Killington. The Killington
    Section of the Green Mountain Club (GMC) will, for the first time, co-sponsor this indoor/outdoor event with the
    Appalachian Long-Distance Hikers Association (ALDHA) in order to share their knowledge with both long-distance
    hiking dreamers and anyone new to hiking or actively planning the possibility of section hiking or long-distance
    hiking the Long Trail or Appalachian Trail, or any one of many long-distance hiking trails scattered around the
    world. For complete weekend registration/schedule details, contact kkrevetski@hotmail.com or call Kathleen
    directly at 802-779-1485.85

►   GMC Volunteer Opportunities:
    Volunteer Long Trail Patrol: The GMC’s Volunteer Long Trail Patrol (VLTP) is a 1-week volunteer trail crew that
    completes trail projects on the Long Trail/Appalachian Trail in southern Vermont. VLTP hitches run from
    Thursday evening to Tuesday afternoon, and crews consist of 4-6 volunteers led by two paid GMC staff leaders.
    Volunteer crew members work for one week at a time with the option to sign up for multiple weeks. The crew
    spends Thursday night in the front country and then packs into their worksite Friday morning, working on the
    project and spiking out in the backcountry until departing from the woods on Tuesday afternoon. Housing is
    available on the off-days for those staying multiple weeks. The project slated for 2020 will be treadway restora-
    tion and hardening on the LT/AT just south of Rt. 9. This will consist of quarrying rock, placing step stones, in-
    stalling stone drainage structures, making crush and various other tasks associated with rockwork. The work
    is physical and challenging, yet also immensely rewarding with an outcome that has a decades-long positive
    impact on the hiker experience and the Long Trail. For more details visit Green Mountain Club VLTP 2020
    at greenmountainclub.org.

    Adopter Training Workshops: This year we’ll be rolling out new training and skills opportunities for volunteer
    adopters. Whether you’re a 30-year veteran trail adopter, a former caretaker or brand new to trail maintenance
    please consider joining us. These opportunities are meant to support and develop the skills and capabilities of
    adopters, improve the relationship between GMC Staff and adopters and contribute a valuable day of work to trail
    sections which need it. We’re going to be hosting trainings for Trail Adopters, Shelter Adopters and Wilderness
    Area Adopters, in addition to a basic tool maintenance training. We’ll do our best to geographically disperse these
    trainings across the state while also recognizing population centers and project details play a big role in
    determining a location. For more details, visit https://greenmountainclub.org/education/workshops.

►       The first edition of Smoke & Blazes, by Amy Peacock, Killington Section historian
                                                       “SOMEBODY HAD AN IDEA
                You know how it is on hikes when everyone sits around after dinner and after the third cup of
                coffee the talk turn to trails, shelters, and future outings. There is always wishful thinking of things we
                hope to do some day. One of these ideas has been that we have a monthly news sheet for the Killington
                Section. This might give some information on activities of members which would fill in between issues
                of The Long Trail News and supply Killington section members with news of interest to them which
                would often not be of interest to the main club. We decided to do something about this idea. This
                sheet you are now reading is what we did.
                Send your: Criticisms to Craigue S. Perkins,
                              Suggestions to Mary Perkins,
                              Favorable comments to Vera A. Perkins.
                This first issue was concocted by a self-appointed committee with the approval of President
                Fred Field.
                September 1948 Vol. 1 No. 1”

        What a wonderful start and a wonderful gift to all of us! Smoke & Blazes is now fully online, archived and up to
        date on the Rutland Historical Society website: https://rutlandhistory.com. This process began in 1999 when
        Robert (Bob) Perkins donated copies of Smoke & Blazes to the Historical Society in memory of his parents. The
        original dedication, written by Bob, said this:

                 “This set of copies of Smoke & Blazes is donated to the Rutland Historical Society in memory of my
                 parents, Mary and Craigue Perkins and my aunt, Vera Perkins. They conceived the idea for a Killington
                 Section newsletter in 1948 and served as editors and publishers for the first several years of its existence,
                 cutting stencils, and running mimeograph copies in our living room. They continued as frequent con-
                 tributors to the publication and as active members of the Club for over half a century. Fortunately, Dad
                 had a packrat proclivity which I seem to have inherited. By combining his collection of Smoke & Blazes
                 with mine, I have been able to put together this complete run of the publication.”

        I will confess that I have spent far more time than I probably should have reading old editions. The pages are
        filled with stories of trail hikes, work parties, membership lists and even a story about a shelter deer mouse
        named “Mr. Whitefoot.” All of it captivating. Thank you to the Perkins family for creating and preserving this
        Killington Section legacy.

Cook Mountain Preserve, Ticonderoga, NY, November 24, 2019, Allison Henry, leader
          The Cook Mountain Preserve, established in 1990, is part of the Lake George Land Conservancy. In the
southwestern part of Ticonderoga, it is near the northern tip of Lake George. There are some good views of Lake George
when the trees are bare. The Killington Section has been to Cook Mountain several times, in all kinds of weather. I have
also planned several hikes there only to have them canceled due to several kinds of weather. A few days before the hike,
the ever-optimistic National Weather Service folks and local armchair meteorologists were predicting not so great weather
for the date, with rain and snow (Channel 5 even used their “nuisance icon.” The horrors!). While the weather can be
unpredictable, our usual group of hikers were not. Predictably, I fielded phone calls the morning of the hike along the
lines of . . . “are we going,” and “what do you think the weather is going to do”? Seeing as we had interested participants
other than myself, we went. Three of us “hard core” K-Section hikers left from Rutland in a mix of big snowflakes, wet
snowflakes, drizzle, and clear weather. We spent most of the drive discussing (arguing) whether electric vehicles are a
viable option in Vermont. We had a rendezvous with a fourth hiker at a convenience store a few miles from the start of
the hike. When we reached the trailhead, there was much discussion (arguing) regarding appropriate footwear and lots of
hopping, staggering, and contorting about in order to put on traction devices that mostly had not been used since the
previous winter.
          As we set out down the trail, we were treated to large, wet snowflakes that instantly soaked through everything we
were wearing. After meandering around a marshy area and following a woods road, the trail then turns uphill and goes
steeply up the mountain. Steep as in about 700 feet in under a mile with few switchbacks. The wet snow on top of wet
leaves made for an interesting footpath. After the steep part of the climb, the trail reaches an open area near the summit
and then loops around for another half mile or so to several viewpoints over Lake George. We had a chilly, chatty lunch
break while admiring the lake, which was visible most of the time except when it was not. The hike down was a lot faster
than the way up and, in particular, my journey downward was enhanced with the assistance of gravity. Near the end of

Merck Forest, Rupert, December 8, 2019, Larry Walter and Vivian Bebee, leaders
         John, Mark and Sue joined Viv and I for this hike. After a stop at the Visitors Center, our little group started out on
some recently fallen snow, chugging up that first steep pitch after leaving the barn area and took a break at the inter-
section of Old Town Road and Antone Road. Sue and Viv decided to take a slower pace on Antone Road, so John, Mark
and I walked on up past Clark’s Clearing cabin (very nicely rebuilt with a new wood shed). After we hit the turnoff to the
north towards Antone, Mark decided to head on back due to time constraints. Viv hiked part way to the top of Antone and
then headed back to Clark’s Clearing Cabin for a lunch break. Sue continued up to Antone and met Mark on the way
back down. They returned to Clark’s Clearing Cabin and met up with Viv on her way back to the Visitors Center. From
the Visitors Center, Mark returned home and Viv and Sue drove to West Rupert to visit Viv’s mom for a few minutes and
then drove up East Street into the Hollow to pick up John and I.
         Meanwhile, John and I had lunch on Antone. It was a bit windy and cold, so we didn’t stay too long. My little
thermos with hot tea hit the spot. We then continued on out to the south on the Masters Trail which ascends and then
runs across a well-defined ridge for a ½ mile or so before dropping off towards the brook in a series of 24 switchbacks and
one slow curve. The views were beautiful. There were only a few blowdowns along the way, probably due to a pretty
active sugaring operation being leased out by the folks at Merck. We cut out what we could with our little Silky. I can’t
imagine how hard it is to repair all of the plastic line up there. There’s an impressive sugar house at the base of the hill,
down by the brook. From the South Gate area of Merck, we walked out on Hidden Valley Road. From there, it was an
easy road walk through the Hollow until the ladies plucked us off the gravel road. I always enjoy our trips to Merck. There
are over 3,000 acres with several camping options to choose from as well as 30+ miles of trails and roads for hiking,
horseback riding, snowshoeing and cross-country skiing.

Lottery Road Wildlife Corridor Loop, Shrewsbury, December 14, 2019, Sandy Bragg, leader
         On a blustery, early winter day, 11 hikers (see cover page picture) sampled a 500+ acre property adjacent to the
Long Trail/Appalachian Trail that the Vermont Department of Fish & Wildlife is in the process of conserving. There are
two good hikes in the future Wildlife Management Area at the end of Lottery Road in Shrewsbury: a wood road heading up
Kinsman Hill (which we did not take) and a wood road heading south toward the LT/AT (which we did take). Our route
followed a north/south ridge on the property, starting from the foundations of a former barn of the Shrewsbury Town Poor
Farm. As we descended the ridge, we passed through south-facing oak stands. After having a snack in Clarendon
Shelter, half of our party ascended Gaynor Road to return to our parked vehicles while the remainder continued north on
the LT/AT to Lottery Road, concluding with a road walk, for a total excursion of about 4 miles.

Route 4, The Elbow-Catamount Loop, Mendon, January 25, 2020, Larry Walter, leader
         Small group today with Nick from the O-Section, Bamby from Salisbury and her friend, Tess from Burlington. The
last time I’d walked this was counterclockwise, so decided to reverse direction and take the ski trail first. Starting point
was the AT/LT parking lot on Route 4. Only a couple minutes up the LT from there is where the Catamount trail branches
off to the left. Even though there wasn’t much snow, someone had been skiing up there, so we put on our snow shoes to
save the track from a bunch of annoying post-holes. I had forgot some of the houses along Elbow Road, but the next
intersection looked right so we banked right and hiked to an old landing where we sat on some logs for lunch. From there
it was only about 10 or 15 more minutes to the LT crossing. At that point, we were glad to have the snow shoes, since it
was powdery and a little slippery on the side hill. There’s a nice stretch of pretty remote woods in there. We recorded the
times and took pictures of the blowdowns for the local trail maintainer. The white blazes that Dave Coppock and I put up
a couple years back still looked pretty fresh. It pays to smooth off the bark a bit before painting. We stopped for a snack
at the Tucker Johnson shelter where I entered our little group foray into the register. Hadn’t been notations for quite a
while, although there’s a lot of traffic in there from the ‘not-a-trail’ off the north end of the Robinwood subdivision. The

Mount Zion, Hubbardton, February 2, 2020, Sue Thomas, leader
        Ground Hog Day dawned overcast and grayish – not the best news for a hike, but spurring hopes that similar
weather in Pennsylvania would prevent the official ground hog, Punxsutawney Phil, from seeing his shadow. The rodent
has spoken – an early spring it is! And, as it turned out, not such a bad day for us either – 11 hikers and two dogs found
the view just fine and the sun and some small patches of blue were even seen, if briefly. We had our lunch at the picnic
table on the ridge, with ground hog cookies for dessert – I assured everyone there was only a little ground hog in them –
and then made our way down to the Japanese Garden. I can’t be there without thinking of the first time I saw it – what a
magical surprise it was, and continues to be. We took the long way back to the cars, enjoying the sweeping views of the
Taconics to the south, thanking Kit for his incredible gift to us all. And thank you also, to Larry, who gallantly hiked back
up the next day to recover my thermos, which I accidently left behind. He reported that the water was still hot!

Killington Section Directors’ Meeting
Minutes of Directors’ Meeting
January 10, 2020
By Allison Henry, Secretary

Present: Herb Ogden (President), Cindy Taylor-Miller (VP), Allison Henry (Secretary), Sandy Bragg (Treasurer); Directors Larry Walter,
Sue Thomas, Spencer Wilson, Gerry Martin, and Ruth Hare; Co-Webmaster Andy Jones, and friends Kathy Krevetski and Wayne
Krevetski.

    •   Meeting was called to order at 7:04 pm
    •   Kathy Krevetski presented regarding Vermont Ruck, a gathering that will be put on by the Appalachian Long-Distance Hikers
        Association (ALDHA) the weekend of March 21, 2020. Ms. Krevetski said that the goal of the event is to increase local
        awareness of our long-distance trails and to get more families involved in hiking. Ms. Krevetski asked for the support of the
        Killington Section, primarily with leading an outing as well as with sharing publicity. It was moved by Cindy Taylor-Miller that
        we co-sponsor the event for 2020, along with leading a hike (seconded by Gerry Martin; passed).
    •   Herb Ogden and Wayne Krevetski as an “FYI” presented new Volunteer Service Agreement forms given to us by Keegan
        Tierney (Director of Field Programs) that is expected to be a requirement in the near future.
    •   Old Business:
        •     We have no minutes to approve from a previous meeting. Treasurer Sandy Bragg shared that we have had no big
              changes in our accounts since our last meeting.
    •   New Business:
        •     Larry Walter inquired whether he is able to access funds up to a particular limit for trail work projects, meaning that any
              amount above the prescribed limit would have to be approved by the K Section Directors via email and/or vote. After
              discussion, we determined that this is actually already in place. Allison and/or Cindy will check prior meeting minutes to
              clarify (Editor’s note: Allison Henry searched Smoke and Blazes archives from 2013-2019 and could find no information).
        •     The Directors discussed whether we should address the issue of dogs attending our group outings. Should we adopt a
              Dog Policy, or at least include guidelines for dogs in one of our newsletters? Some of the potential problems that have
              arisen with our canine participants include: dogs are not allowed in some places (and hikers could show up with their
              dog(s) and then be disappointed); some areas have leash rules; aggression between dogs; and when a hike requires car-
              spotting, the dog(s) would have to ride in a car other than their owner’s at the beginning or end of the hike. There was a
              motion to add language at the top of our Outings schedule stating that not all hikes are suitable for dogs, and to call the
              leader first (motion by Ruth Hare; seconded by A. Henry; passed). There was also a motion to amend our Killington
              Section Outings Leaders Guide to include language that the leader must be prepared to answer questions about pet
              participation, factors may include whether dogs are allowed at the hike location as well as car spotting (Allison
              Henry/Larry Walter; passed).
        •     We will be putting hikes on the Rutland Herald calendar again—if leaders do not want their hike in the Herald, let Sue
              know when submitting hikes for the schedule.
    •   Web Site: Andy Jones, partner of Web Master, Sunghee Jones, reported that the website is going to be updated. Some
        changes will include: making the site mobile friendly; having hyperlinks to contact info for hike leaders; and possibly changing
        the platform to Wordpress. Our web team recently solicited suggestions for improving the site via email, and lots of helpful
        suggestions have come in. Some include: reworking the Section Maintenance dropdown menu; including hike suggestions in
        our area; links to other local resources including Pine Hill Park and the Delaware and Hudson Rail Trail; and links to buy the
        Day Hikers Guide and Walkers Guide through the Main Club. Larry will write a few blurbs for the site regarding local trail
        options and the local bus schedule. Cindy and Herb will be “bouncers” and will weed through any additional suggestions as
        they come in.
    •   Taylor Series: We discussed whether we want to sponsor a Taylor Series event for 2020-2021. Possible venues could include
        the Rutland Library or a town hall in neighboring area such as West Rutland. We are interested in hosting a talk and will
        discuss again at annual meeting.
    •   Directors’ Term Limits: We need to discuss Directors to replace Cindy Taylor-Miller and Herb Ogden, as they are not eligible
        for re-election.
    •   Section History: local, Amy Peacock, has registers from our shelters and from several trailheads. She is going to go through
        and compile funny or memorable comments from the logbooks.
    •   Banff Film Fest: In the past year, we have had some discussion via email and in person regarding the possibility of bringing
        the Banff Film Fest to the Rutland area. This is a worldwide tour of outdoor-themed short films, typically sponsored by local
        clubs, organizations, and businesses. No one was interested in seriously pursuing this possibility in the immediate future.

Meeting was adjourned at 9:15 (Gerry Martin/Sandy Bragg).
