SEE YOU JN VERMONT. That's the word being passed up and down
               the A.T. this year as anticipation builds for the 25th meeting of the Appalachian Trail
               Conference next August.
                  The 1985 Appalachian Trail Conference is set for Green Mountain College, Poultney,
               Vermont, August 2-9.
                  The Green Mountain Club, host for the conference, is also celebrating its 75th anniver-
               sary in 1985, and some facets of the G.M.C. celebration will coincide with the A.T.C.
               Conference.
                  More than 40 hikes have been set for the conference period, ranging from easy after-
               noon rambles to full day climbs up Stratton Mountain in Vermont or Mt. Marcy in New
               York.
                  Workshops covering topics such as alternatives for Land Protection; Cold Weather
               Hiking; Photography on the A. T.; and Public Relations and Political Action have been
               set.
                  Excursions to points of interest; rides up the Killington Gondola; and cruises on nearby
               Lake Champlain are all part of the varied program.
                  For those keeping physically fit a daily jogging course is planned, and guided and
               equipped bicycle tours are being made available at extra cost.
                  Panel discussions on contemporary topics are being planned, and a wide variety of eve-
               ning entertainment including slide shows and square dancing.
                  Plan now to make the 1985 ATC Conference in Vermont. Stand atop Stratton Moun-
               tain where Benton McKay dreamed the Appalachian Trail or dangle your bare feet in
               cold mountain water at Clarendon gorge; or hike Inn-to-Inn on the Long Trail. See you
               in Vermont.
