# Green Mountain Club News

Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.
Rutland, Vermont, August, 1928

## Special Meeting of the Trustees

At the True Temper Inn, Wallingford, on June 23, the trustees met in special session, and found plenty of topics for discussion to consume the time during the day, broken only for lunch.

It was voted to continue to publish the News six times a year and send same to members. It was also voted to print a large edition of a new illustrated folder for publicity purposes, and this was referred to the publicity committee, Prof. W. C. O'Kane, chairman.

The plan to make the year 1931, when the Club shall have reached its majority of 21 years, the goal for the final and full completion of the Trail, was definitely decided upon, and all plans will be made with that end in view. By that time, it is hoped, all re-locations will be made, the bad spots removed, all necessary shelters built, more side trails completed, and the Trail from end to end finally sand-papered and polished.

It is probable that a camp will be built this season at Hazen's Notch. The Dunsmore camp, north of Bolton, will be replaced by another at nearly the same site. Another shelter will be built somewhere on the Dennington section of the Trail. A number of bridges, destroyed by the flood, will be rebuilt. An important change in the Trail will be made in the southern section. From Hell Hollow northward the pathway will lead to the top of Glastenbury mountain, instead of running along the side, which will avoid a wet territory and will have the added advantage of leading to a fine view, as there is a new tower on the mountain where a fire warden is stationed, from which an extensive view of the surrounding country may be had. The new piece of trail will reach the Arlington road near the Webster camp, and will be laid out and worked by the Worcester Section.

As a guide to hikers and a publicity feature, it was voted to place framed cards at the junction of main roads with the Trail, with directions to shelters and other points of interest, and other information. A suggestion that women and others, inexperienced on the Trail, would come out if some definite time and place of meeting were fixed, with a leader provided, was received favorably and it was voted to try the experiment on next Labor Day, at three points, viz., at Brandon (Du Val trail), Breadloaf Inn and Summit House, Mt. Mansfield. Also at Long Trail Lodge on Columbus Day. A committee consisting of Trustees Storey, White and Puffer was appointed to arrange the details.

Those at the meeting were Mortimer R. Proctor and W. M. Fay, Proctor; Prof. L. B. Puffer, Burlington; Profs. P. C. Voter and R. H. White, Middlebury; C. J. Cleveland and W. M. Ross, Rutland; E. S. Marsh, Brandon; R. M. Ross, Montpelier; Prof. W. C. O'Kane, Durham, N. H.; J. A. Allis, New York; G. F. E. Storey, Worcester, Mass.; F. H. Tucker, Boston. C. W. Webb, treasurer of the N. Y. Section and Louis Bigelow, of Worcester, Mass., were also present.

## An Explanation

We understand that some of the prospective visitors to the Trail have taken alarm at a statement which appeared in the Rutland Herald of June 23, in an article announcing the trustees' meeting of that day, which read as follows:

"The summer meeting of the trustees of the Green Mountain Club to discuss improvements on the Long Trail that are necessary for this year and to make other arrangements for enterment of the large number of hikers who are expected this season will be held" &c.

The trouble comes from that word "enterment", which, it seems, many have understood to be a misprint for "interment", and have inferred that the trustees are looking for a heavy mortality on the Trail this season, and are making preparations accordingly. We have investigated this matter, and are able to reassure the timid ones, and inform them that the word intended was "entertainment", and there is nothing to fear. There is nothing dangerous to life or health on the Long Trail. On the contrary, frequent tramps on it can be confidently recommended as an infallible recipe for a long, healthy and happy life.

               Publications

   A new folder has just appeared, well
printed on tinted paper, and illustrated with
the following views: The three young wo-
men who did the entire Trail last year,
nearing Jay Peak; The Sky-line of Mt.
Mansfield; The Long Trail skirting the
shore of Lake Griffith; South from Duns-
more Lodge on the Long Trail; Montclair
Glen Lodge, and the Long Trail Lodge; also
a map of the entire Trail. We regret to
note under one of the illustrations the term
"Crouching Lion" (for Couching Lion) and
the same error repeated in the text. This
mistake is not uncommon in the newspapers,
but should not appear in a Club publica-
tion. The statement is made that the Green
Mountain Club is made up of about 1400
men and women. This is an under state-
ment. The Secretary's report as of Janu-
ary 1 last placed the total membership at
1635. The folder includes much useful in-
formation about the Trail, shelters and the
Club.

   A neat leaflet on the Long Trail Lodge
has been issued, with three good illustra-
tions, one showing the Lodge, the others
scenes in the vicinity. It includes a full
schedule of rates, both for members and
others.

   The new guide book has just appeared.
We have not yet seen a copy.

   The first two mentioned items may be had
free of charge, and the guide book for 52c
each, from the Secretary, or at the Long
Trail Lodge.

Notes

   The Long Trail Lodge is doing an excel-
lent business so far this season. Several
large parties have been entertained there.

   The Club officials are bending every effort
to provide all the comforts of home for the
camps on the Trail. That at Deer's Leap,
opposite the Lodge, has been protected by
chicken wire to discourage porcupines, and a
shower bath may be had by sitting under
the faucet in front and turning on the wa-
ter.

   Mr. and Mrs. G. O. Hutchinson, two na-
ture lovers of Passaic, N. J., heard about
the Du Val trail, and two years ago came up
to investigate, staying with Henry Watters,
at the starting point of the trail. They be-
came so enamored of the attractions of that
region, that they have fallen into the habit
of repeating the experience twice each year.

   The Board of Governors of the Adiron-
dack Mountain Club extend the courtesies of
their Johns Brook Lodge to members of the
G. M. C. for the season of 1928. This
means that all members of the G. M. C. in
good standing will be received at the Lodge
at member rates. Further information may
be had by addressing the A. M. C. at 93
State St., Albany, N. Y.

   Hon. Redfield Proctor, who recently pur-
chased Mt. Carmel peak, has erected a steel
tower there which affords one of the finest
views in Rutland County, superior in some
respects to those from Killington and Pico.
The Long Trail passes the foot of Mt. Car-
mel, and all trampers along here should
make the short climb to the top and get this
view.

   We have endeavored to get a report from
the committee appointed on the proposed La-
bor Day outings, without success, which in-
dicates that there will be nothing doing
along that line this fall, which seems rather
a pity, as it sounded like a good idea. Pos-
sibly something of the kind can be arranged
at the Lodge on Columbus Day, in October.

   Found: On the Trail south of Killington
a fountain pen. Owner may apply to W. M.
Ross, Rutland.
