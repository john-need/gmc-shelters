                      e are pleased to report that fiscal
                      year 2005 was a year of accomplish-
                      ment for the Green Mountain Club.
                      A new Rolston Rest Shelter was built
in Chittenden and Douglas Shelter in Winhall underwent
major renovation. The Lamoille Valley Long Trail reloca-
tion, including a remarkable 135-foot-long suspension
bridge built entirely by GMC volunteers and staff, neared
completion. With our centennial just five years away, the
board looked seriously towards the club's second centu-
ry, and laid the groundwork for a major campaign to sus-
tain the Long Trail. Our education and group outreach
efforts continued to reach thousands of hikers, despite
the loss of our South Barn, which burned in 2003.
  We celebrate the dedication and competence of our
year-round staff and the forty plus seasonal staff who
are passionately committed to GMC's mission. We thank
each and every one of the over 800 volunteers-trail and
shelter adopters, section officers, outing leaders, com-
mittee and board members, corridor monitors, Thursday
office volunteers, and others-who contributed thousands
of hours caring for the Long Trail and other Vermont
hiking trails.
  In an ever-changing world, we take comfort that certain
cherished places and experiences such as the Long Trail
remain essentially unchanged. The Green Mountain Club
is committed to preserving this special place and the
opportunities it offers to future generations.
