PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE

The broad objectives of the current administration are
the motivation of those who will continue the work of
maintaining the Long Trail System and shelters in the
challenging years ahead and the establishment of good
communications with the 51 GMC Committee Members,
the Section leadership and the membership in general.
These priorities are obvious when it is considered that both
President and Vice President are not on the scene and that
key individual contributors have done more than their share.
    It has been inspiring to find wholehearted cooperation
among Trustees in accepting suggested responsibilities. Each
was asked to complete a first, second and third choice form
as well as to provide details of their commercial, professional and service organization backgrounds so that the
assignments would make the most sense. You will note that
maximum exposure has been given through multiple committee placements. Knowledge is the forerunner of interest
and commitment. Occasional meetings, correspondence
and some telephone communications by Committee Chairmen will expose more interested GMCers to more facets of
our complex operation. While we employ a large summer
staff on the Long Trail Patrol and 22 Caretakers, we must
remain a volunteer directed organization, while recognizing
that the continuity of talented, full time aid such as our
Assistant to the President, Gardiner Lane, is an essential.
    Previous Presidents have had the local expertise to answer trail inquiries. Our strong office, with Gardiner in
frequent attendance, is fielding those letters and, of
course, telephone calls helpfully. All policy or key communications continue to be the President's "bag." Some
are criticisms, often involving a lack of knowledge about
the work being done. Others are very knowledgeable contributions such as the lucid and detailed report on the condition of the entire LT/AT trail by Charles S. Sampson,
M-A-L, Alexandria, Va., from observations during his
May 1973 trek with Mrs. Sampson and Mr. & Mrs. Lenn
Whitten of Ipswich, Mass. Mr. Sampson criticised inadequate wet area steps particularly and identified these helpfully. He had thoughtful suggestions about overexpansion
of our role and responsibilities. Every member of the Trails
and Shelters team as well as Section Leaders received
prompt copies of the complete report.
    The completion of an impressive Trails and Shelters
Committee structure by General Chairman George Pearlstein was just in time for testing by the need for massive
input on the new problems caused by the incredible excess
in late June of Vermont's blessing . . . Our future Long
Trail News issues will have the benefit of Committee contributions for the first time. We will make the LTN informative about the main thrust of our activities, starting with
details of Trustee meetings and a condensation of the first
meeting of the current Executive Advisory Council, which
meets between Trustee meetings to aid the President to
execute Trustee approved policies.

JOHN W. NUFFORT, President

INTERSECTIONAL 1973

Killington Section of the Green Mountain Club will

ANNUAL MEETING 1973

The 63rd Annual Meeting was held at the lovely old
Baptist Church in East Poultney May 26th. We were
fortunate again this year to have a beautiful day. An excellent luncheon and dinner were served by the ladies of the
church. Mrs. Marion Arbo and Mrs. Gerald Martin are
to be commended for planning such good meals and for
efficient service.
    138 members and guests attended the meeting which
was held upstairs in the church. The slide presentation
"Flora of the Alpine Zone," narrated and shown by Al
Oxton of the Mt. Washington Observatory, was enjoyed
by all. Also shown by Dick Church was the GMC's new
slide show for group camping "Backpacking on the Long
Trail."
    A large number of GMC campers enjoyed the facilities
at Lake St. Catherine State Park. Sunday turned out to be
another nice day and the Connecticut Section Breakfast
was a huge success as always.
    A vote of thanks is due Al Mead, George Bragdon and
all their helpers. Hikes on the Long Trail and to Bird
Mountain were ably led by Churchill Scott, Ray Secor and
Wilda Gigee.

MINUTES OF THE ANNUAL MEETING

BAPTIST MEETING HOUSE, EAST POULTNEY, VERMONT
MAY 26, 1973

President George Pearlstein convened the meeting at 10:15 A.M. He asked for a moment of silence for Waldo Holden and other members who passed away since the previous meeting.

Peter Palen, chairman of the Annual Meeting Committee, led the pledge of allegiance to the flag. A member of the Committee, Wilbur Weston, traced the interesting history of the church.

After dispensing with the reading of the minutes of the previous meeting, Treasurer Granget Kammerer presented his report. The report was accepted as given. The auditor, Armand Lampron, reported that the Treasurer's statements were complete and correct. George Saunders, Chairman of the Trust Fund Committee, sent a report on the Trust Fund showing the present program of balanced investment for income and growth. These reports were approved.

Next on the agenda was the introduction of the five proposed changes in the ByLaws as published in the February Long Trail News.

Article I, Add Section 8. The Contributing Membership proposal was defeated.

Article II, Section 1 & 4. The membership approved deletion of the number of Trustees wherever it appears.

Article II, Section 4. The Club approved a change in the wording from Green Mountain National Forest Service to read Green Mountain National Forest.

Article II, Section 4. A fourth At-Large Trustee was approved.

Article IV, Section 7. The Executive Advisory Council members must be members of the Club.

Vice President John Nuffort presided over a guess-a-number game during which he presented a child's pack and a baby carrier to the winners.

The next piece of business was the election of officers. The slate of officers was received from Dane Shortsleeve, Chairman of the Nominating Committee. Clerk—Elsie Washburn; Corresponding Secretary—Minerva Hinchey; Trustee At-Large—Ralph Bryant. The nominees were elected. George Pearlstein won re-election as Trustee At-Large in the expanded program.

Section reports were presented proudly by representatives of Pioneer Valley, Bennington, Connecticut, Brattleboro, Worcester, Killington, New York, Burlington, Montpelier and Sterling. Mr. Charles Mooty reported trail and shelter activities by Newport people who might form a new section. This active, informed group is able to contemplate responsibility for the northernmost miles of trail and maintenance of 5 shelters. All reporters were congratulated by President George Pearlstein.

President Pearlstein reconvened the 1973 Annual Meeting at 2 P.M. Wilbur Weston displayed a handsome State of Vermont rug created by his wife.

Peter Dunning, Special Projects Coordinator for the Appalachian Trail Conference, stressed the need for everyone to work together to protect the environment of the AT and the Long Trail.

Ira T. Ellis, reporting on End-to-End, determined that many end-to-enders were attending the meeting.

The Membership Committee reported next on its successful campaign to enlist members from register signers.

Wilbur Weston reported on the progress of the 1973 Intersectional which will be held at Tamarack.

John Nuffort reported for the Office Committee: 1) Debbie Barlow, Assistant to the Corresponding Secretary, 2) new location, 3) new furniture, 4) copier obtained through Doris Washburn.

President Pearlstein, representing the Historical Committee, said that significant Monroe archives have been obtained. The information covers the period from 1910. The ad hoc Book Publication Committee will report to the Trustees in October on the information available for an up-dated GMC history.

The report of the Public Information Committee as presented by Doris Washburn included significant accomplishments including a new slide program, and wallet cards.

Guide Lines for the use of the Trail were stressed in the report of the Youth Services as presented by Richard Church.

The summary report of the Trails and Shelters Committee as presented by Don Wallace for Robert Attenborough is attached. Don Wallace will be in Milwaukee with Allis Chalmers as a Post Doctorate Associate for the next fourteen months. President Pearlstein extended him best wishes and thanks for all his contributions to the club.

Mr. Pearlstein stressed that every shelter location should be considered individually. John Nuffort expressed the need for shelters because of severe weather and the emergencies which might arise.

Assistant to the President, Gardiner Lane, presented the attached detailed summary report on the progress in 1973.

President Pearlstein read the Guide Book Report for Ben Rolston, Editor. The report is attached as presented.

The activities of the 1973 Annual Meeting were presented by Chairman Peter Palen.

The Burlington Section has taken action on search and rescue communications as the result of a recent experience with lost young people.

The 1973 Annual Meeting was adjourned by President George Pearlstein at 3:35 P.M.

Respectfully submitted,
ELSIE WASHBURN, Clerk

THE GMC OFFICE CAPER

The signing of our 5 year lease at 27 Center Street, Rutland, provided the permanence necessary for a major effort to modernize the office equipment to support expanded service to the public. What to do? We advertised in the LTN for metal furniture and received responses from Dr. Richard Goldsmith, Somerville, N.J., and Andy Donohue, Garden City, N.Y. Dr. Goldsmith, with deep roots in the Northeast Kingdom, contributed files. Andy's dad, Joe, turned out to be a procurement genius with a sharp eye for surplus matched sets.

A U-Haul trip delivered the fine new furniture in good shape, together with our new 120" Formica (green) counter top, complete with massive shelf space below. Gill Barlow joined Debbie and Minerva Hinchey in placing the desks and files as well as constructing the counter cabinetry. The "loaner" furniture was returned to Doc Howard to whom we extend our thanks for getting our office started.

Come see our efficient office, now complete with electric typewriter and copier. It is becoming a true center of communications. Doug James, the new Office Committee Chairman, is bequeathed with problems of records organization and archives indexing and protection.

JOHN W. NUFFORT

A DRAFT OF A PROPOSED POLICY STATEMENT
OF THE STATE OF VERMONT CONCERNING THE
LONG TRAIL SYSTEM

I. IT IS THE POLICY OF THE STATE OF VER-
     MONT TO SEEK PROTECTION OF THE LONG
     TRAIL SYSTEM ON PRIVATE PROPERTY.

II. THE JOINT RESOLUTION OF THE SENATE
     AND HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES OF
     FEBRUARY 25, 1971 RECOGNIZED THE GREEN
     MOUNTAIN CLUB AS THE FOUNDER, SPON-
     SOR, DEFENDER, AND PROTECTOR OF THE
     LONG TRAIL SYSTEM.

III. IT IS OF MUTUAL INTEREST TO THE STATE
     OF VERMONT AND THE GREEN MOUNTAIN
     CLUB TO PROVIDE SOME TANGIBLE PROTEC-
     TION TO THE LONG TRAIL SYSTEM CORRI-
     DOR AS IT NOW EXISTS THROUGHOUT THE
     STATE.

IV. IT IS THEREFORE PROPOSED
     A. THAT THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
          WITH THE COOPERATION OF THE
          STATE OF VERMONT ENCOURAGE PRI-
          VATE LAND OWNERS (NAMES ARE ON
          RECORD WITH THE GMC) TO ENTER IN-
          TO A VOLUNTARY AGREEMENT WITH
          THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB TO PRO-
          TECT THE LONG TRAIL CORRIDOR.

     B. THAT THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB
          ENGAGE THE HELP OF THE REGIONAL
          PLANNING COMMISSIONS AND LOCAL
          TOWN PLANNING COMMISSIONS IN IN-
          CORPORATING THE PROTECTION OF
          THE LONG TRAIL SYSTEM IN THEIR
          PLANNING PROCESS.

V. IT IS FURTHER PROPOSED THAT BY OCTOBER
     1, 1973, THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB RE-
     PORT TO THE STATE THROUGH THE SECRE-
     TARY, AGENCY OF ENVIRONMENTAL CON-
     SERVATION, THE PROGRESS BEING MADE
     AND THE NEED, IF ANY, FOR LEGISLATIVE
     ACTION IN ORDER TO FULFILL THE OB-
     JECTIVE OF TOTAL PROTECTION OF THE
     LONG TRAIL SYSTEM.

LONG TRAIL MAINTENANCE CONFERENCE

Preparations for an expected record number of people using the Long Trail hiking system were made May 12th and 13th by over sixty representatives from the Green Mountain Club, the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks, and the Green Mountain National Forest. The preparations took place at a special "Long Trail Maintenance Conference" held at the Forests and Parks Ski Dorm in Stowe, Vermont, which is a short distance from one of the most heavily traveled sections of the Long Trail.

The conference was organized by the Green Mountain Club, the hiking organization responsible for the overall supervision and maintenance of the famous "footpath in the wilderness," in cooperation with the State and Federal Agencies. The chairman of the conference was Donald Wallace of Northfield, Vermont, a trustee of the Green Mountain Club.

Most of the emphasis at the conference was placed on improvement of the physical facilities needed to accommodate hikers. Top priority was given to upgrading the quality of the outhouses based on a study and plan prepared by the Green Mountain National Forest. The next priority was given to improving the trail itself, particularly in wet and steep areas so that the trail will have a tread that is both suitable for hiking and in fitting with good environmental practices. A demonstration was conducted by the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks on good construction techniques including proper layout and the use of water bars to divert water from the trail.

In addition to physical improvements to the trail, an expanded program was planned to educate trail users about things they should and should not do in order to protect the trail system. A new educational slide program, "Backpacking on the Long Trail," was shown by George Plumb, Public Information Chairman from Washington, Vermont.

The program shows groups how to prepare for and conduct a successful backpacking trip. The slides and taped narration are available from the Green Mountain Club, P.O. Box 94, Rutland, Vermont 05701.

The Green Mountain Club, Vermont Department of Forests and Parks, Green Mountain National Forest and some ski areas will cooperate to provide the resources needed to accomplish the objectives established at the conference. The club will provide twenty-two caretakers who will reside at heavily used shelters during the summer and work with the hikers using those shelters. It will also provide a six man Long Trail Patrol who will spend the summer doing trail work, building outhouses, and repairing shelters. The Vermont Department of Forests and Parks will employ nine maintenance personnel who will work in the Killington, Camel's Hump and Mt. Mansfield areas. The Green Mountain National Forest will be working on special trail projects with the Youth Conservation Corps. Some of the ski areas will make available their terminal stations where caretakers provided by the Green Mountain Club will supervise the use of the building for overnight accommodations for hikers.

During the weekend, there were several seminars and training sessions to decide on areas of responsibilities of the cooperating parties and to train the people who will be doing the work during the summer. Participating in the program were Arthur Heitmann, Commissioner of the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks; Rodney Barber, Director of Parks; Benjamin Hoffman, Chief of State Land Management; Joseph Sposta, Recreation Specialist from the Green Mountain National Forest; and Kenneth Boyd, Chairman of the Green Mountain Club's caretaker program. Representatives were also present from many of the club "sections" located in Vermont and northeastern United States. Volunteers from the sections will contribute much of the work that will be done during the summer.

GEORGE PLUMB

BURLINGTON SECTION

The activities of the Burlington Section this summer encompass a wide variety of trips from a bike hike on Isle La Motte to a canoe trip to Victory Bog in addition to hikes to such places as Spruce Mountain. The Outgoing Chairmen have planned the activities to spread the group hikes over areas away from the Long Trail. In an editorial in the Rutland Herald, March 31, 1973, entitled, "Giving the Long Trail a Rest," problems of overuse of the Long Trail and the resulting threat to plant life were discussed. The editorial gave credit to the Burlington Section outing chairmen for ingenuity and imagination in planning this year's trips. Obviously, the overuse could be avoided by closing sections of the Long Trail, but, as the editorial points out, a more feasible alternative is to plan activities in a variety of geographical locations in the state as well as on the Long Trail.

A highlight of the early summer hikes was the Mt. Mansfield Arctic Alpine Flower Hike led by Vic Henningson, a senior at Yale and a ranger for the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks. The hike up Sunset Ridge Trail culminated in a 2 hour tour of the top of Mt. Mansfield during which Vic explained the ecology of the mountain, identified wildflowers and discussed the damage done by man to the mountain. This unique alpine area has dozens of species of flowers found only on Mt. Mansfield and Camel's Hump in Vermont. This outstanding hike was enjoyed by 16 GMC's who look forward to another hike of this type next year.

JANICE LANGE, Publicity Chairman

A family hike to Cantilever Rock on Mount Mansfield was the biggest turn-out. Eleven children and ten adults scaled the short but steep path to the Rock where they stopped for lunch. Other spring hikes went to Mount Elmore, Middlebury Gap, and Cardigan Mountain, N.H.

The annual clean-up of Sterling Pond took less effort than usual as it was found to be in relatively good shape. Trail clearing took place in the middle of June.

New officers for the year 1973-74 are: President Charles Bristow; Secretary Reidun Nuquist; Treasurer Richard Babcock; Trustee Sally Sairs.

MONTPELIER SECTION

This spring the Montpelier Section tried a new kind of outing: a combination canoe and bicycle trip. While canoeists paddled down the White and Lamoille Rivers, other section members biked alongside to watch them slide through the rapids—and sometimes capsize! We liked these combination trips, and are ready to try them again.
