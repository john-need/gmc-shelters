"A suitable site for the new Tucker memorial lodge was found near the Blue Ridge side trail north of the Long Trail Lodge. Permission to build here was obtained from the owners, the Vermont Marble Co., but due to uncertain property rights, permission could not be obtained from the U.S. Forest Service to get the required logs. This matter, we hope, can be adjusted by spring so that the new lodge can be built next season."

"The large road signs were replaced on all of the important crossings from Canada to Sherburne Pass."

"Many of the interesting peaks on what is really a new sky-line section north of Hazen's Notch are unnamed. It would add character and interest to this part of the Trail if they were named."

The report closed with a list of suggestions of things needed to be done during the coming season, for the consideration of the trustees.

E. A. Boyce, chairman of the house committee, reported for the Long Trail Lodge. He stated that the season was the best ever, both in the number of guests served and the gross income. The net was somewhat smaller, due to higher cost of labor and supplies. The total capacity was used on more week-ends than ever before.

The addition of the new cabin "Ledges" increased the capacity by four more people, and another new cabin as yet unnamed will be ready for this next season and add another four. Running hot water was installed in four large and two small cabins with showers in two large and two small ones that were joined together. It is intended this year to install more showers where practicable. An addition has been added to the end of the kitchen for a store room and bake shop and the old bake shop made over into living quarters and office for the manager, which makes his former quarters available for guests.

The work of the naturalist was further extended by the building of a bog garden at the left of the main entrance walk opposite the pond. In this he laid out numerous walks of stones and marble samples found in the vicinity and planted in the garden over fifty specimens of swamp and similar plants that may be found growing naturally within a few miles of the Lodge and many on the Trail itself. This is an attractive addition to his other work of labeling the trees and shrubs surrounding the Lodge and lures many people into trying to find unusual plants, which he has placed there. All of this work is of course educational and has drawn many teachers and other people to the spot to study these natural features. Much additional work is planned along these lines in an attempt to make the place a center for nature study.

The naturalist has also done much to familiarize the guests with the bird life of the surrounding country. Over sixty-five different birds which can be seen within two miles of the Lodge, have been cataloged. He guides parties to hunt out the different species and will guarantee a sight of over thirty different kinds on almost any day.

Reports from the Sections were next in order, and all were heard from except Wyanokie and New York. The silence of the latter was the occasion of much wonder. Such a thing had never happened before within the memory of the oldest inhabitant. No representative appeared, no letter or telegram was received. An explanation of this strange delinquency is awaited with interest. Prof. Buchanan reported for Burlington, Prof. Voter for Middlebury, Mr. Fay for Proctor, Capt. Dorney for St. Albans, Mrs. Ruth A. Fostman for Rutland, E. L. Bigelow for Manchester, Dr. Ross for Bennington, and W. E. Cherry for Worcester. Most of them reported a flourishing condition, activity in outings and trail work, more or less plethoric treasuries, except St. Albans, which was down to 30 cents, having spent their money, not in riotous living, but in improvements on the Trail. Middlebury seems to be in a somewhat moribund condition, but with a goodly sum of cash in the treasury it is expected that the laggards will come in, if for no other reason than to help dispose of the funds. The Burlington and Worcester Sections, as usual, have regular printed programs of walks and outings.

The proposed lodge on Killington dedicated to the memory of former president C. P. Cooper came up for discussion. It was the unanimous sentiment that this should be built, and Cola J. Cleveland of Rutland was elected

E. H. Loveland of Burlington called attention to the fact that the east side of the State is not represented in the Club as it should be, either by Sections, members or interest. They look at the same mountains, only from a different viewpoint. There should be more side trails on the east side. He was appointed chairman of a committee to take the matter up.

A recommendation was made to the guide book committee that when new maps are drawn they be contour maps, showing altitudes.

The matter of names for the new peaks on the Trail, as mentioned in the report of the patrol, was discussed, and it was voted that the nomenclature committee, of which P. H. Merrill of Montpelier is chairman, should consider the matter and confer with the State nomenclature committee with a view of selecting names.

Otto G. Koenig, supervisor of the Green Mountain Forest, attended and addressed the meeting. He stated that the forest preservation program would not interfere with the Long Trail. Any camps or other property of the Club on land acquired by the government would be allowed to remain as they are. Paul Stacy, also of the forestry department, spoke of the winter sports development in Vermont, which would tie in with the Club's aims.

Telegrams were received from Prof. Walter C. O'Kane, from three members of the Worcester Section, and from Mrs. Margery Ludlow, who is to present the Club with a tablet to be placed at the southern end of the Trail.

COMMITTEE FOR C. P. COOPER MEMORIAL

Local members of the Killington Section of the Green Mountain Club met at the home of C. J. Cleveland, to formulate plans for a new building to be erected near the top of Mt. Killington. The present shelter house there is inadequate and the cause of much complaint from those who frequent the Long Trail.

At the annual meeting of the Green Mountain Club held in Rutland, January 9, it was voted to erect a building on Killington in memory of the late Charles P. Cooper. Cola J. Cleveland was appointed chairman with power to appoint his committee. Mr. Cooper was for several years president of the Green Mountain Club and it was partly through his efforts that the Long Trail with its shelter houses at frequent intervals became one of the most popular trails in New England.

It is the purpose of the committee to erect a building of durable material that will withstand the ravages of weather, fire, hedge-hogs and vandals. It is hoped to erect the building this coming summer if funds become available from the various Sections and former friends of Mr. Cooper and all those who may be interested in the project.

The committee in charge consists of C. J. Cleveland, Rutland, Vermont, General Chairman. Killington Section: Geo. C. Cobb, W. M. Ross, Miss Lulu M. Tye, Craig Perkins and Mrs. Ruth Fostman. Other members of the Committee are Prof. R. O. Buchanan, Burlington, Vt.; Prof. P. C. Voter, Middlebury, Vt.; Capt. R. H. Dorney, St. Albans, Vt.; Dr. L. H. Ross, Bennington, Vt.; Wallace M.
