THE GREEN MOUNTAIN NEWS
Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.
Brandon, Vermont, December, 1923

This, the third number of our little paper, appears at the end of a period of great activity and achievement for the Green Mountain Club. It has witnessed the building of the picturesque and unique clubhouse in Sherburne Pass, and the construction of 25 miles of new trail north of Johnson, bringing the Long Trail within 25 miles of the Canada line and the realization of the Club's dream of a trail extending from the Massachusetts line to the Canada line in sight. It has also seen the formation of a new section, and the growth of the membership at large to the greatest number yet reached. We are glad to chronicle these things, and hope in future issues to unfold a tale of still greater growth, achievement and prosperity for the Club.

Special Meeting of the Trustees.

A special meeting of the trustees of the Club was held at the new clubhouse in Sherburne Pass on Sept. 15. The special object of this meeting was to determine a policy for the management of the clubhouse. It was realized that the Club was striking out into new territory in the management of a clubhouse, that the first season would be experimental, and any policy adopted more or less tentative, and subject to change according to the teachings of experience. The probable popularity of the house, and how far the public could be accommodated without interfering with the rights of members, were the questions principally discussed. It was generally conceded that members of the Club should have prior rights, non-member hikers on the trail should come next, and the general public in automobiles third. It was decided that lodgings in the clubhouse should be reserved for members and their guests, and that non-members should be served with meals, as far as possible. It is probable that members will get the advantage of somewhat lower rates than non-members. It was decided to name the structure the Green Mountain Clubhouse. The open shelter across the road, called Pico Lodge, will be furnished with blankets, cooking utensils and fuel, and will accommodate up to 24. A small fee will be charged for lodging there. Trustees present were Messrs. Cooper and Ross, of Rutland, Mathers of Bennington, Proctor of Proctor, Voter and White of Middlebury, Dean and Taylor of Burlington, Marsh of Brandon, Brock of Montpelier, and Tucker of Boston. Others present included W. M. Fay, Proctor, Chas. F. Cummings, editor of the Vermonter, and R. H. Torrey, of the N. Y. Evening Post, and J. A. Allis, of New York. A house committee to assume for the present the oversight of the house, and to engage a competent married couple to take charge next season, was appointed, consisting of C. P. Cooper, Mortimer R. Proctor and Prof. R. H. White.

Trail Work.

   In addition to special work mentioned elsewhere, the trail committee report that the Bennington Section have cleared out their part of the trail from the Massachusetts line to Stratton Mountain, and have painted the blazes white, in conformity with the rest of the trail, instead of red as heretofore. The committee have in view changing the trail north of Stratton Mountain, so as to run the trail via Stratton Pond and Bourn Pond, thus making it more scenic and not so wet, increasing the distance to Prospect Rock not over one half mile. A beginning in this direction was made this season by starting the new trail from Stratton Mountain.
   The large camp at Swazey's at the Buck Job near Prospect Rock has been thoroughly renovated and repaired and will be used as one of the club shelters.
   The roof of the cabin on the Bondville road was blown away and a new one put on. Caretakers have been kept at Griffith Pond and Killington Peak, in addition to those at Camel's Hump and Taft Lodge.

Monroe Lodge.

   The committee in charge of the building of this proposed Lodge on Camel's Hump decided to take more time for consideration and preparation, and postponed its actual construction till next year. Plans have been prepared by Paul W. Thayer, of Wallingford, architect of the new Clubhouse, and show an artistic as well as a comfortable building. The committee are striving to keep the cost down to $2500, which amount they have in hand. Logs will be used largely in its construction, and these will be cut near by and drawn to the site of the building on the snow. A year from this time we hope to chronicle the completion of the Club's second mountain house. The sheet metal huts, which have heretofore served as camps, will be repaired and put in good order to be used as adjuncts to the building.

Skyline the Long Trail.

   The Lake Pleiad section of the Club (Middlebury) has completed the work of relocating the trail on the skyline from Sucker Brook south to the Brandon-Rochester Pass, finishing it in November, with some financial help from the general treasury. There is now a skyline trail from Camel's Hump to the Brandon-Rochester Pass. All new trail is constructed on this principle, and it is the intention of the trail committee to relocate the old trail in accordance therewith, so far as it needs it, as rapidly as possible. The new piece of trail referred to above opens up six good lookouts, where before there were none, and thus makes it much more interesting. The same Section has rebuilt the Lake Pleiad Lodge, destroyed by fire late last year, so that it is better than before.

NOTICE

   The annual meeting of the Green Mountain Club will be held in Rutland, Vt., Saturday, January 12, 1924, probably at the new clubhouse. All members of the club are invited to attend. Reports of officers will be read, trustees and a clerk elected, and action taken on a proposed amendment to the by-laws placing some restrictions on the admission of new members. A snowshoe and outing program will be provided for Sunday.

The Marjorie Hulburd Trail.

The trail shown in the above map is now practically complete, it being cleared, blazed and blazes painted for one half of the distance, and blazed for the rest of the way. It will be fully complete in time for use next season. It will be known as the Marjorie Hulburd trail, in memory of a young woman, lover of the mountains, daughter of Ex-Lieut. Gov. Hulburd, who met her death in an automobile accident near Bethel, and whose family have furnished the funds for the expenses of this part of the trail. The route of the Long Trail south of Johnson was changed slightly, so that it now runs two miles west of Johnson, crossing the Lamoille River on a highway bridge. It will form an interesting new walk. It passes over two peaks of over 3000 feet, Laraway Mountain estimated at 3200 feet, and Belvidere at 3800. On the latter mountain are extensive asbestos deposits. It brings one in contact with such attractive names as Frying Pan Brook, Devil's Gap and Redderbush Pond. Willis M. Ross of Rutland, chairman of the trail committee, has had charge of the work, and has been assisted by Dr. L. H. Ross and Henry B. Walbridge of Bennington, Wallace M. Fay of Proctor, Rev. Frank W. Hazen of Richmond and Fred W. Mould of Morrisville. Mr. Ross says of this part of the trail: "It is extremely scenic, very diverse, and not at all difficult."

The Green Mountain Clubhouse.

The new clubhouse has at length been complete and must be seen to be appreciated. No description will do it justice. However, with the aid of the above cut, we will try to give our members who have not seen it a faint idea of the reality. It is a three story building, somewhat in the Swiss chalet style of rustic architecture built of native woods from the vicinity, and the huge boulders deposited on its site in pre-historic time by some glacier or fallen from the cliff opposite. These boulders are a unique feature being enclosed in the house in several places, forming parts of the walls, with ferns mosses and lichens growing on them as vigorously as outside. The support and the beams inside are the natural logs of the white and yellow birch, with the bark on, the bark of the latter hanging and producing a particularly pleasing effect. The stair treads are the same logs split, the flat side uppermost.

The bedsteads, window frames, mirror frames, door handles, etc., are formed of smaller limbs, while the great chandelier of ten light is constructed of white birch limbs, and the lamp shade of birch bark. The huge double fire place and chimney which will take five foot logs, is built of cobble stones. The main fireplace is in the large assembly room, while back of it is another on the main porch. The chimney rests on solid rock. The south wall of the kitchen is formed of one of the big boulders, as will be seen in the cut, at the left. The iron door latches and hinges are hammered by hand by a blacksmith.

There are nine bedrooms, a bathroom, a men's room and a woman's room, ample toilet facilities, a writing room, a large assembly room occupying the whole of the second floor, 24 by 45 feet, a kitchen 16 by 32 feet, a pantry, a front porch 16 by 45 feet, and above this a sleeping porch which will accommodate twenty. Most of the sleeping rooms have two beds, in some cases

placed one above the other. Sewerage is provided by a cesspool, well away from the building. The house and surroundings are lighted by a Delco electric lighting system of its own. Pure spring water is piped from a point on the mountain, across the pass. In addition to the fireplaces, the building is supplied with a pipeless furnace, for cold weather use. The house is well and fittingly furnished with rustic settees, hickory and splint bottom chairs and other furniture to correspond. The beds are furnished with mattresses and blankets of excellent quality. The kitchen is equipped with a huge double range, and an ample supply of the best cooking utensils, silverware and Wedgwood china. Meals will be served in the main room, and also on the porch. Besides the clubhouse there is a garage accommodating ten cars, an insulated ice house and a woodshed.

The location could not be equalled on the Long Trail, being midway between the Massachusetts line and Johnson. The house faces the rocky cliff, towering 500 feet above, known as Deer's Leap. Here, in some tremendous convulsion of nature ages ago, the face of the mountain was split off and fell into the valley below, where it was broken into the great boulders which now abound there. Interesting caverns have been discovered where these boulders have fallen together and on top of each other, and have been explored by the Club, wires run thru them and bridges and ladders placed where needed, so it is now quite feasible, with the aid of flashlights and considerable wriggling and squirming in the narrow passages, to go thru them. Pico Mountain towers above the house, and Killington Peak is six miles distant, Pico Pond a mile and a half, Noyes Pond five miles, and Mt. Carmel ten miles to the north. The elevation of the house is 2180 feet, and it is directly on the Long Trail. A few rods distant, north of the highway, is the cabin called Pico Lodge, built this season by the Killington section of the Club, with its natural boulder fireplace in front, and bunks inside for 24 lodgers. Altogether, there are at present sleeping accommodations for over 60.

This Clubhouse, which Allen Chamberlain in an illustrated article in the Boston Transcript calls a "camp de luxe", and Prof. W. S. Monroe says is "without doubt the most luxurious mountain lodge in the world", was made possible by the munificent generosity of Mortimer R. Proctor of Proctor, Vt., one of the trustees of the Club, a former president, and always its generous supporter, who furnished the funds to build the house, and of his mother, Mrs. Fletcher D. Proctor, who gave the furnishings. Others without whose assistance the project could not have been carried to success are the Vermont Marble Company, owner of the land, which has given the Club a ten-year lease of about seven acres, including the caverns and Deer's Leap; the architect, Paul W. Thayer, to whose ingenious skill and careful attention to every detail is due the unique character of the completed whole, and the building committee, consisting of C. P. Cooper, W. M. Ross and W. M. Fay, and more especially the chairman of the committee, President Cooper, who has given of his time and efforts without stint.

The house was formally opened and dedicated Saturday, Dec. 1, by a public reception. Over 200 were present during the afternoon and evening, and enjoyed dancing to the music of a five-piece orchestra, and a general good time. A buffet lunch was served. Eight of the trustees were present, most of them with their wives, also a representative of the new Jay Peak section of the Club.

The Jay Peak Section.

This latest addition to the Club is reported to have at present 23 members, with the following officers: President, F. B. Hammond, of North Troy. Secretary and treasurer, Mrs. John Cunningham of Newport. Chairman of Trail Committee, E. E. Currier. The Club looks to this section for important help in completing the Long Trail to the Canadian boundary, and hopes to find a much larger membership when the trail reaches Jay Peak next year.
