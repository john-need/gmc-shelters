From The Sections.

Middlebury.

The Section here reports considerable ac-
tivity and accomplishment to date. It was
hoped to start work on the new camp to be
built on the saddle between Breadloaf
Mountain and Mt. Wilson, but snow there
in May prevented this, so the work was
postponed till later in the season. From
Sucker Brook lodge to Boyce lodge the Trail
has been cleared, clipped and fallen timber
removed, of which there was a great deal.
The remainder of this section of the Trail
will be cleared before travel over it begins.

Worcester.

Six of the members of this Section, led
by Messrs. Storey and Bigelow, made a trip
to Stratton Mountain during the week end
of May 25, and painted blazes and removed
blow-downs. The entertainment committee
is planning a big field day at the farm of
Mr. and Mrs. Story for July 4. The im-
portant task of this Section is to build Wor-
cester's camp at Stratton Mountain, so that
the Section can act as host there to the
other Sections on October 12.

Burlington.

This Section has paid off its indebtedness
on the Bolton Mountain lodge. Owing to
its accessibility, this camp has been well pa-
tronized thru the winter by skiing, snow-
shoeing and plain walking parties.

Rutland.

The Killington Section reports under date
of May 24 that one-half of its part of the
Trail had been cleared and camps put in
good condition, and that most of the re-
mainder would be finished by the time the
report appears in print.

Montgomery.

The lumber for the Fred H. Tucker camp
at Hazen's Notch is on the camp site, and it
is expected that the work of construction
will soon begin.

Montpelier.

The Mt. Hunger Section has been in a
state of coma for some time, and it was
feared that it was a hopeless case, but late-
ly some signs of life have appeared and
there may be hope for the patient yet. The
secretary says that a drive for members is
in progress, with some results, and that a
renewal of work started two years ago on
the trail is planned.

Proctor.

At the annual meeting March 22 Miss
Catherine Robbins gave an account of her
hike over the entire length of the Trail, in
company with two other young women.
