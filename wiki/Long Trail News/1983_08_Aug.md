Beaver M eadow Lodge Gets a
             Face/Floor Lift

    Beaver Meadow Lodge was the site of ex-
tensive reconstruction activity this past
spring. Planning for the work was begun
more than three years ago when it became
obvious the floor was in sad shape, especial-
ly around the door and stove, from people
splitting wood directly on the floor. Further
study showed that the soil bank around the
camp had crept downhill, burying three of
the sill logs which completely rotted through
as a result. Also rotted were the ends of
several floor joists.
    The Sterling Section, at its January 1982
Annual Meeting, decided to proceed with the
necessary repairs, and decided on a plan
whereby the replacement logs would be cut
and peeled in the spring of 1982 and allowed
to season a year before being used. The
rough-cut boards for the new floor were also
purchased and seasoned during the summer
in the Section President's garage. InJ anuary
( 1983), members of the Section packed the
almost three-mile trail to the camp so that
the boards could be dragged there behind
Carroll Lawrence's snow machine. The
boards were then hidden under the camp un-
til early June when the actual reconstruction
began. Originally scheduled for two
weekends, the job was completed in 1 )12
(rainy!) days.
    On a Friday night, Bob Lindemann, Car-
roll Lawrence, and Smith Edwards (carry-
ing 90-pound packs containing most of the
tools, hydraulic jacks, a chainsaw, and a few
beers) hiked in to Beaver Meadow to allow
for an early start on Saturday. That morn-
ing other Section members arrived with the
rest of the tools, food, and a few more beers.
Ben Davis, Northern Field Assistant, also ap-
peared and was pressed into service as
lech1"1ical cxperl. The old floor was quickly
removed at the cost of two chains, the camp
lifted up, and th new sills moved into posi-
tion. By late afternoon the Lhree sill logs we re
in place, the floor joi ts in posit ion the foun-
dation rocked, and the first layer of floor
boards laid. The stove was quickly reinstall-
ed in time for supper. On Sunday, everything
was leveled out and the new floor nailed down
(except for a small portion where we ran out
of boards). Not bad for 1 )12 days of work!
    The job was completed in June when a
Morrisville Job Service work crew under
GMC supervision carried in the remaining
boards, built a bridge across the stream,
relocated the outhouse, and worked on the
rock "patio" in front of the lodge.
    If there was one lesson learned from this
reconstruction it was the importance of keep-
ing sill logs high and dry.
Bob Lindemann, Sterling Section President
