LONG TRAIL PATROL

The Patrol started work for the 1965 season at Buchanan Lodge where they put in the fourth new floor since 1949. Next they lengthened the bunks at Birch Glen and then put a new roof on Glen Ellen #1 and gave it a creosote oil bath. Hikers were having trouble in lumber slashes north of Whiteface and near Codding Hollow. After these bad spots were cleared and marked, the Trail gang did the usual trail work between Route 15 near Johnson and Canada. They standardized the trail from Ithiel Falls to Parker Camp and speed cleared from Parker Camp to Canada, including all of the side trails in that distance. During this time new roofing was put on Camp French and blazes were painted from the Lamoille River to Codding Hollow, from Jay Road to Jay Peak, and from Route 105A to Journey's End.

On July 19 the Patrol moved to Glastenbury Mt. where they built a new open front log shelter which should sleep 6-8 hikers. The roof, the floor and the toilet came from the old fire wardens shack at the tower location on the mountain. They completed this job on Aug. 3.

August 4th they moved to Glen Ellen #2 where 8 rotten logs were replaced and a new floor was put in. Floor lumber was back packed in via the Barton Trail for the lift wasn't running. Since the roof and the foundation are in good condition, this camp is as good as new, so it should be good for 32 more years.

New logs were put in Shooting Star Shelter where several of the original logs were rotted out and then a toilet campaign was "enjoyed" for the rest of the season. New ones were built at Barrows, French, Parker and Ritterbush, while rather temporary latrines were built at Sterling Pond, Tillotson, Whiteface Beaver Meadow, and Laura Woodward.

The roof on Ritterbush Camp was leaking and the boards were badly rotted so new boards were moved in and new roofing was applied. New bunks were built at Beaver Meadow.

Then creosote oil was applied to the camps and latrines at Hazens Notch, Journey's End, Ritterbush and Tillotson.

With that lingering smell of creosote, the gang closed the season.

ROY BUCHANAN, Chairman

VERMONT SCENIC PARKWAY STUDY

The members present at the annual meeting last May voted to instruct the trustees to appoint a special committee to study the Vermont Scenic Parkway proposal. Shirley Strong (chairman), Robert Attenborough, William Bartlett, Helen Farrington, Marion Gorham, Arthur Koerber, Madeline Miles, George Pearlstein, Roderick Rice, Ben Rolston and George Saunders were appointed.

Committee members have met with various state agencies and officials and have studied the proposal that was submitted to Washington by Vermont. The committee members are keeping in close touch with the contacts they have made. The Club will be kept informed.

Committee members are also making a study of alternate routes that could be used if a parkway is actually built. They are considering everything involved such as topography, scenic features and the wilderness. A most important point is the fact that it is Vermont's wilderness areas that attract tourists and vacationers to the state. If the wilderness is so cut up and "developed" that these people think it isn't wilderness any more, they will stop coming. Who else, then, would be attracted to such a little state as Vermont? Would another new road attract anyone? Probably not because there are new Interstate highways all over the U.S. There is no novelty there.

It is doubtful that anything more elaborate than our Long Trail can be built in wilderness without damaging it excessively. A trail does practically no damage. The committee is very much aware that the best wilderness left is the Camel's Hump area. It surely should be protected and preserved as the only unspoiled one left. The rest is precious too, even though already spoiled to some extent.

Green Mountain Club members should use every opportunity they get to let someone know that our 250 mile long Long Trail exists, and where it is. Let them know too about the many shelters. Let them know that all this was built by a club that even now has only 1300 members. About half of the members live in Vermont—but all are interested in Vermont. Tell people how much this club has done for Vermont—and at no cost to the state. Let them understand that we want Vermont to prosper. Make them see that about the only unique thing little Vermont has is its mountains and the wilderness on those mountains.

The special committee is continuing its fine work and the club will be kept informed of what is going on. But too, too many Vermonters don't know a thing about Vermont's basic attraction. And they don't know that the only reason the wilderness can be enjoyed is the Long Trail that this Club built.

Our "Short Walks Folder" helps to describe the Trail. Have you seen it? A self-addressed envelope, long size, to Miss Hinchey at 108 Merchants Row in Rutland will get you one, or more, free.

JOHN ROHRBAUGH

Many thanks to all who have written letters of concern regarding the Vermont Scenic Parkway. Every letter does help and your letter is important! Much recreational planning and development is taking place in Vermont and it is important that mountain wilderness always remains a part of its recreational plan. GMC'ers can contribute significantly by pointing out the aesthetic and economic value of the wilderness, the many threats to the precious little which remains, and the need for careful planning to insure that it remains a vital and attractive asset to the state. It can be easily destroyed, never replaced!

SHIRLEY STRONG, Chairman
Scenic Parkway Committee

BENNINGTON SECTION

The Bennington Section visited the new Glastenbury Mt. open log shelter which was built in late July and early August by the Long Trail Patrol. That was Sept. 19th—we were there 12 strong—put up signs for water, dump, toilet and then cleaned up and walled up the dump (new style)—enough for this fall ... big stones seem to be scarce.

Our three new junior members were most helpful as was a fourth boy who hasn't joined yet.

We plan to visit the new Seth Warner Shelter as a group on Oct. 31st.

Looking ahead—Nov. 13—Pot luck supper at Old Bennington Schoolhouse; Dec. 11—Walk from Rt. 9 to Woodford State Park; Jan. 29—Annual meeting and pot luck supper.

Junior memberships in the Club are 50¢ per year, and the Treasurer is Mrs. Edward Bullock, Harmond Pond Road, Williamstown, Mass. Special Junior Walks are planned by the group as desired.

BURLINGTON SECTION

Up The Trail Apiece—

Not a week-end went by without the Burlington Section being on the go! It was either hiking, mountain climbing, canoeing, boating, camping or eating all the way through.

On July 4th weekend, Jack Harrington was leading them on a sweaty work-out in the Adirondacks, with more heights added to the list of those in the group working on their 46er memberships.

July 10th found the group tagging after Dorothy Myers up the sharp little slope of Robins Mt., on the relocated stretch of the Long Trail from the Hump to Jonesville.

On July 18th, Jim Forrester led the group on a ramble about Smuggler's Notch and on to French Camp and the Johnson Highway, some eleven miles of real "hoofing-it."

July 25th Lee Brown showed the group Ricker Mt. down Waterbury Dam way.

Aug. 1st Jack Harrington led a big group up worthwhile Mount Hunger to find that Gene Bamforth had reached the top before them.

Aug. 8th Dr. and Mrs. Walter Burnett were hosts to the group at their lake shore camp at Keeler's Bay. "Walt" showed his prowess on his tennis court and then took those sporty ones for speed boating and water skiing. Here Andy Buchanan and the Doctor came out a tie on the art of water skiing.

Aug. 15th Ed Salvas and the group took off from Montclair Glen to Birch Glen on a hot sweaty tramp.

For the week of Aug. 28–Sept. 6, a number of the Burlington Section followers had an enjoyable time at Boy Scout Camp Plymouth. Understand a questionnaire is going out to locate those old faithfuls of the past who didn't show—such as the Deans, Don Kent, the Mitchells and others. While the Burlington group showed a good number, they fell below the former stand-out leads of the past. I understand Roy and Helen have designing eyes on the Dr. Paul Jinks' snoozer-cruiser after their tour in it during the Intersectional.

On Sept. 11–12, George Saunders and his canoeing party had a swell trip through the Adirondack lake country.

Sept. 18th Fred Smith took a group up Brownell Mt. for an easy afternoon tramp and then brought the group and some more back to his house. Here they found Betty dishing out her famous baked beans and other goodies to the folks; 31 in all got fed.

Next day Coralie Magoon steered a small party who had feasted the night before, up Mt. Pisgah in the Lake Willoughby area.

Our last hike and week-end outing was centered in the Spruce Mt. region and a campout at Groton Pond, in Groton State Park. The event was headed up by Helen Garrington with the help of Emily Rice.

LARRY DEAN, Reporter

KILLINGTON SECTION

Once again the Killington Section has survived the onslaught of another Intersectional and now we hope to get down to the work nearer to home.

The fall and winter hike schedules are out and the Section is continuing to have trips over the trail to enable members to work on their end-to-end awards. A moonlight hike to Clarendon Lodge on Jan. 8 via snowshoes is planned. Also this year we hope to have a winter week-end at one of our shelters.

Our big work party projects this fall are to improve the quality of our toilet facilities to compare with the ones found in the National Forest areas. As Mr. Rohrbaugh has suggested this is the area in which the Green Mountain Club is poorest and a great deal of effort must be made to provide the campers with better sanitary facilities.

Perhaps many of the old timers remember the Pico Camp Shower. If plans materialize this next spring, we hope to be able to reconstruct this unique structure. Any information about the former set-up will be very helpful to the Section.

Looking ahead: Nov. 7, Pico Shelter; Nov. 27, Sunnyside Lodge; Dec. 5, Tucker Lodge; Dec. 19, Buffum Lodge; Jan. 8, Moonlight hike to Clarendon; Jan. 23, Balch Pasture Snowshoe Hike; Feb. 13, Spaghetti Supper, Tall Timbers.

CHURCHILL SCOTT, Reporter

THANKS TO FARM AND WILDERNESS CAMPS

During the past summer once again the Farm and Wilderness Camps of Mr. Kenneth Webb have been busy covering the Long Trail from Sherburne Pass to Shrewsbury Cutoff Trail and in addition the Juggernaut Trail.

The groups replaced roof boards and roofing at Cooper, tarred the roof, replaced bunk boards, turned over table boards, cleaned up interior of the lodge and replaced two plexiglass windows. Also improved latrine, put up trail, dump and water signs and cleaned up the camp dump area. Also they worked to improve the Shrewsbury Cut-off Trail and are at present working with George Pearlstein and the Vermont State Department of Forest and Parks to relocate this trail so that it will pass over state land and not over private land.

We owe this group a great big thank you for the long hours of hard work they have given to improve our section of the trail and Cooper Lodge.

MONTPELIER SECTION

At this writing our summer activities have ended and our fall activities begun. Seemed like an awfully short summer.

Way back July 11 the Bryants, Otises, Wallaces and Warburtons (sounds like an investment firm, doesn't it?) enjoyed a family cookout and hike to Cantilever Rock. Doris Washburn managed the affair.

On July 24 several members (three to be exact) participated in an 11 mile overnight from the Lake Mansfield Trout Club to Bolton Lodge and Route 2. We lunched at Taylor Lodge and spent the night at Puffer Lodge where we were entertained during the night by porcupines and "fog horns," at least that's what they sounded like. The next day we lunched at Bolton Lodge. A swim in Joiner Brook and a race to Route 2 completed the trip. Dave Morse was our leader.

On August 8 we hiked from Middlebury Gap to the Clark Brook Trail and down in the rain. Originally scheduled as an 8½ mile hike we lengthened it to 13. We found the Long Trail in this area very overgrown.

Young Brad Warburton (assisted by Doris Washburn) substituted for his father as leader of our blueberry hike up (and down) Mt. Hunger on Sunday, August 22. The reason for the substitution was an addition to the Warburton family.

Seven of our section attended the intersectional. Gene Bamforth, Doris Washburn and I were there for the entire week. Our President, Dave Morse, was there the first weekend and our past-president Dave Otis with Ann and Mary Ellen came for the second weekend. Doris and I covered about 60 miles of the Long Trail during the week.

On Sunday, September 12 we ended our summer schedule with a hike in the Adirondaks led by Dick Babcock. We were joined in New York State by Mauri Winturri and climbed 4621 foot Giant Mountain. Dave Morse and I also climbed Rocky Peak Ridge, also known as Giant's Wife, since it happened to be there.

We held a schedule planning meeting at Juelia Desilets on September 13 and started our fall schedule that very weekend with a corn roast at the Montpelier Fish and Game Club Picnic Area at Shady Rill. Harlan Farnsworth engineered the affair borrowing a rig from the Recreation Department and we roasted the corn over charcoal in a large concrete-mixing tray covered by what looked like a wire gate.

On Saturday, September 25 the Burlington Section was surprised to find us on the road ahead of them on the way up Spruce Mountain and walking—they were driving. However, we shamed them into abandoning their cars. Our scheduling the hike on the same day was a pleasant coincidence. On top of the mountain we had a pleasant visit with the Rogers family who live there during the fire danger period. Neil is the ranger and a member of our section.

This brings us up to date. Coming up this weekend is an 11 mile hike from Lincoln Gap to Appalachian which I hope to lead if I can shake a sore throat which is plaguing me. The remainder of our fall schedule follows: Sunday, Oct. 10—an afternoon walk from upper Terrace Street to Route 2; Frances Holmes, Leader. Sunday, Oct. 17—White Mountains (Liberty) up Falling Waters and down the Flume Slide; Dave Morse, Leader. Saturday, Oct. 30—Halloween Mystery Hike—"Marshmallows at Midnight"; Dave Otis, Leader. Saturday, November 6—Drandon Gap to Middlebury Gap with chowder at Voter-White Lodge; King Warburton, Leader; Jerry Dix, Cook. Sunday, Nov. 14—Smugglers Notch to the Chin; Dave Otis, Leader. Saturday, Dec. 4—Ithiel Falls Campground to Barrows Camp for baked beans and hot dogs; Marion Gorham, Leader; Doris Farnsworth and Ann Otis, Cooks. Wednesday, Dec. 8—Moonlight hike; Dave Morse, Leader. Join us if you can. We'd like to make our hikes intersectional.

MARION GORHAM, Reporter

NEW YORK SECTION

The Club seems to be offering a multiplicity of events, many weekends. These are well attended by the members. Labor Day weekend the calendar was crowded. At the Intersectional in Vermont were about 14 of our members. On the Long Trail Pack Trip were 5. The Thendara weekend had thirty or forty present. At an invitation weekend with the Appies at Cardigan Lodge in New Hampshire were several of our people.

The weekend of Sept. 18-19 was warm and humid. Many people in the New York Section were not yet back from vacation. But the four events scheduled for the weekend were well attended and enjoyed by all. About 14 were at the Thendara weekend, Peg and John Nuffort hosts, with about 10 more showing for the day. About twenty participated in a weekend canoe cruise on the Wading River, Win James, leader, in the South Jersey Pine Barrens. Twelve went for a walk in the beautiful Pines Lake area in New Jersey and for a swim in the lake and ate lunch under the beautiful trees at Eunice and Herbert Goepel's. We and 9 others went to Ray Darrenougue's Saturday walk, at the Jamaica Bay Wildlife Refuge on Jamaica Bay, in Queens. So I will tell a little more about that.

This is an astonishing area of 12,000 acres of shore, dune and marsh and pond, although still within the formal limits of New York City! It is on the main Atlantic flyway and is visited by thousands of migratory birds each year. You ride the Rockaway subway line or drive. Under Ray's expert direction we saw American and snowy egrets, glossy ibis, gallinules, bittern, black-crowned night herons, green and big blue herons, godwits, sanderlings, ruddy turnstones, black ducks, ruddy ducks, Canada geese, mallard ducks, coots, the rare black-backed gull, herring gulls, baldpate ducks, scaup ducks, pied-billed grebes, green teal ducks, blue teal ducks, shoveller ducks, a marsh hawk, quail, house finch, and black-bellied plovers. Many of these were in great numbers, undisturbed by the nearby traffic and subway trains, or the quiet observers on the cinder walks! We saw a rare Baird's sandpiper! We sat in the shade of a small building in the long waving grass and ate our lunch and decided a hot day had been put to good account. WORCESTER SECTION

Our fall hiking season has just begun but we have already had 3 hikes on which I can report. The first was on Sept. 19, a rather gray Sunday afternoon, when we hiked through the woods over to Crow Hill near Princeton. As we sat on the rocks on top of Crow Hill we had a chance to contemplate and enjoy the beautiful woodlands which stretched for miles around us. When we had drunk our fill of the beauty of nature, we carefully made our way to the trail at the bottom of the gigantic rocks which comprise Crow Hill and were a little awed by the immensity of these rocks.

Our second hike, the following Sunday afternoon, took us up into the Royalston area not far from the New Hampshire line and was a pleasant 4 mile hike over seldom traveled back roads and then into the woods to see the Royalston Falls. Mushrooms aplenty were found in the woods ranging in color from black velvet to bright reds and yellows and even purple ones. After the hike we had the added pleasure of being invited by our leaders, Mr. and Mrs. Norman Rayner, to their season campsite about one mile up the road to enjoy coffee and sandwiches around a campfire.

Hadar and Nellie Sternstrom were the leaders of our third hike, a nature walk, in the Holden area. We were again royally entertained after the hike in the Sternstrom home and were served coffee and home made buns and home made doughnuts as well as sandwiches. If there is anything that hikers enjoy better than walking I guess it is eating and we have certainly done justice to both of these pleasures. More is yet to come!

LINNEA ELDRIDGE, Reporter
