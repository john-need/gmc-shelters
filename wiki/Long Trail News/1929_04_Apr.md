Annual Meeting of the Trustees

    This was held at the Hotel Berwick, Rutland, Vt., on Saturday, March 9, beginning at 10:30 a. m., and continuing till nearly 6 p. m., with an hour's intermission for lunch, and those present found plenty of material for discussion to fill up the time.
    One of the most important questions was how to maintain the Trail in fit condition for trampers. It was realized by all that if the Trail is left in parts unkept and uncared for, making tramping difficult and disagreeable, it would earn a bad reputation, and its sponsors would be held responsible. It was decided to have four experienced woodsmen patrol the entire trail, two over the northern half and two the southern half before the tramping season opens, renew the blazes where needed and do ordinary clearing, and in case any extraordinary bad places should be found requiring extra help, those to be reported and additional help sent to finish at these places. In this way it is hoped and expected that the entire Trail will be in reasonably good condition during the season. The patrol will be in charge of the general organization, but the Sections will attend to their parts of the Trail, as heretofore, and supplement the patrol in the difficult parts. It is hoped and expected that this plan will result in a better trail than ever before, and with complaint reduced to a minimum, if not entirely absent. There was some discussion as to the relative merits of blazes and white metal disks for marking the Trail. It was finally decided to stick to blazes on the southern part, but to try out the disks to some extent on the northern part, and see whether they are satisfactory.
    The question of what sort of a crossing should be provided at Bolton, where the Trail crosses the Winooski river, came up, whether to rely on a ferry boat, as heretofore, or try to have a bridge built, and was left with Professor Puffer, who will report in June.
    It was voted to send a delegate to the Appalachian Trail Conference in April at Washington. This delegate will report that the trustees are willing that use may be made of such part of the Long Trail as may be needed for the Appalachian Trail, and the markers of the latter may be placed there, but such part of the Long Trail will remain under the control of the Green Mountain Club, as heretofore.
    The usual appropriations were made including $700 for maintenance of trails and shelters.
    Officers were elected as follows: President, Mortimer R. Proctor; Vice President, Professor R. H. White; Treasurer, C. J. Cleveland; auditor, J. A. Allis; Corresponding secretary, W. M. Ross; Editor of Long Trail News, E. S. Marsh. Committee chairmen: Trails and shelters, W. M. Ross; membership, H. W. Congdon; publicity, Professor W. C. O'Kane; nomenclature, R. M. Ross; guide book, Prof. P. C. Voter; House committee for Long Trail Lodge, E. A. Boyce of Proctor, in place of W. M. Fay, resigned.
    Those present included all of the above named except Mr. Boyce, and F. H. Tucker, of Boston, and Professor L. B. Puffer, Burlington.
    Two other matters which were brought up will be found treated in special articles herein.

Two Outings in 1929

    The question of one or more outings for the members this year was discussed at the Trustees' meeting, and it was voted to have two such gatherings, one in June and the other in August, where headquarters would be maintained at some central point and hikes or excursions could be made from that point, with competent guides in attendance, the whole to last for one or more days, according to individual choice.

From the Sections

BURLINGTON

The usual Washington's birthday outing was utilized this year for a house warming at the new Bolton Mountain Lodge, recently built by the Section. More than 100 members of the Club and friends made the ascent of three and a half miles, and were enthusiastic over the beauties of the lodge in its snow setting. There was plenty of snow for skiing and snowshoeing. On the return the party had supper together at Essex Junction. Groups from Morrisville, Waterbury, Montpelier, Underhill and Rutland participated.

NEW YORK

President Proctor was the guest of honor at the Section's annual dinner. In a graceful speech, he expressed his appreciation of the Section's support of the parent organization and paid a high compliment to the scenic character and well-kept condition of that part of the Long Trail maintained by the Section, the Monroe Sky Line Trail. He likewise described the Club's plans with regard to completing the Trail by 1931, referring particularly to the shelters that still remain to be built, the funds for which are now in process of being raised. (Funds for one shelter have already been donated by a member of the Section in honor of his wife and the Section itself has given the funds for another from its treasury.) Mr. Proctor's address, which he interspersed with a number of excellent stories, was received with the utmost enthusiasm.

At the annual meeting, the following officers were elected: Walter Mueller, president; William Burton, vice president; Clarence W. Webb, treasurer; Anna M. Michener, secretary; David Paine and Arthur C. Tate, trustees. Professor Will S. Monroe, founder and honorary president, and Laura E. Woodward, the retiring president, who has served as an officer of the Section practically since its inception, were elected life members in recognition of their invaluable services.

In the August, 1928, issue of the "Long Trail News" reference was made to the experiment that was being conducted at Montclair Glen Lodge with a view to determining whether chicken wire strung across the front of the shelter would prove an effective barrier to porcupines. The experiment proved successful, and it is now planned to protect other shelters on the Monroe Sky Line Trail in a similar manner during the coming season.

It is expected that the new cabin at Birch Glen will be ready about the Fourth of July. It will be a log structure of ample proportions. A slab lining will tend to make the cabin considerably warmer and more weatherproof than the average shelter. It will also embody several unique features that will provide more adequate ventilation and lighting of the bunks. The cabin, which will afford comfortable sleeping accommodations for from sixteen to twenty, will be equipped with stove and cooking utensils. It will be close to an adequate water supply and within easy walking distance of Hanksville, with its shopping and mail facilities.

On June 1, the Emma Kemp Memorial Library, consisting of some two hundred carefully selected nature books, will be formally presented to the Waterbury Public Library by Professor Monroe in behalf of the Section. Emma Kemp, a charter member, was drowned at Lake Tiorati, on which the Section's Camp Thendora is located, several

New Trail Cabins

President Proctor announced at the Trustees' meeting that gifts of several new camps had been received, leaving only four still to be provided before the great jubilee celebration of 1931. The new camps to be built this year and next are as follows: The Bennington Section will repair the Deerview cabin and build a log shelter on Glastenbury Mt. The Worcester Section will build a cabin at Stratton Pond, and provide the entire expense. Fritz Von Briesen of New York City offers a stone open camp, similar to those in the New York state reservations. This will probably be located in the vicinity of the old Hell Hollow camp, north of Bennington. Mr. and Mrs. Wm. H. Field of Mendon will give a camp also of stone, in memory of Ex-Governor P. W. Clement. This will probably be located near the former Herb Haley place, about five miles south of Killington. The New York Section offers a camp, to be located wherever the Trail committee shall decide. The Montgomery Section will complete the Fred H. Tucker camp at Hazen's Notch, and a camp started two miles north of Sterling Pond under Tillotson Peak will be completed early in the spring.

Vermont Public Forests

It has been announced that the Federal government will set aside a 100,000 acre tract of forest land, situated among some of the higher peaks of the Green Mountains, as a national forest reservation. The land will be purchased from the owners. The section to be acquired is located where the counties of Windsor, Windham, Bennington and Rutland meet. The streams having their sources in these forests feed the Connecticut and Hudson rivers.

The state has recently completed the purchase of 1,238 acres of forest land lying on the west slope of Mt. Mansfield, in the town of Underhill. This purchase brings the area of the Mt. Mansfield state forest up to 5,084 acres, which includes practically all of the east and west slopes of the mountain, but does not include a narrow strip along the ridge, which is owned by the University of Vermont.

Another Photographic Contest

Last year's Long Trail photographic contest was such a great success that President Mortimer R. Proctor has with his usual generosity offered another series of prizes for another photographic contest to take place this year, and has named the same committee in charge as last year, viz., Herbert W. Congdon, Prof. P. C. Voter and Prof. W. C. O'Kane. The conditions of the contest have not been fully worked out as yet, and will be announced later. It is probable that groups of pictures will be called for and judged, rather than single prints, as last year, such groups to consist perhaps of three to five pictures. Full details in next issue.
