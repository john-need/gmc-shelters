                              The responsibility of the Long Trail Protection Taskforce will
                              be to review the status of Vermont's Long Trail on privately owned
                              lands, with an emphasis on lands known to be for sale in Lamoille,
                              Franklin and Orleans Counties, and to recommend to the Green
                              Mountain Club a strategy for permanently protecting both the con-
                              tinuity and scenic values of the Long Trail.

                              The Taskforce will have completed its mission upon the submis-
                              sion of its findings, with the Green Mountain Club responsible for
                              adopting and implementing, as appropriate, the actions and
                              strategies recommended. A report is desired on or before April
                              30, 1986

    Rain, snow, sleet, hail, heat, cold, bugs,
nettles, porkies and mud are all possibilities
to be encountered on the Long Trail.
However, during 1985 they failed to
discourage 56 hardy souls who have now
swelled the number of End-to-Enders to a total
of 1,517.
    Nineteen states plus Canada are repre-
sented in this group with the largest number
(12) coming from our own Green Mountain
State. One hik r travelled all the way from
Hawaii to hike the Long Trail. Ten of the 56
(or about 15%) were not GMC members at
the time they hiked the Trail.
   There were no large groups reporting dur-
ing 1985, and it seems to us that reports con-
taining complaints were at an all-time low.
The GMC must be doing something right as
far as trail and shelter maintenance go.
   Reports came in all sizes and shapes, the
most unusual for 1985 being submitted by
John Tefft of Littleton, Colorado, who sent
