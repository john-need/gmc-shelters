A Photographic Contest for Long Trail Pictures

Through the generosity of our President three prizes are offered to Green Mountain Club members for pictures of the Long Trail under the following conditions:-

PRIZES

First prize, $25; second prize, $15; third prize, $10. No contestant will be awarded more than one prize. Contestants must be paid-up members of the Green Mountain Club or one of its sections by the closing-date of contest. Announcement of winners will be made in the columns of The Long Trail News.

CLOSING DATE

Entries are to be delivered, securely packed, to Herbert Wheaton Congdon, Arlington, Vermont, not later than November 15, 1928.

RETURNS

No entries will be returned to contestants.

PURPOSE OF COMPETITION

(1) To stimulate interest in the beauties of the Long Trail and the joy of life thereon, and (2) To secure illustrative material that will make these features evident to others.

OTHER CONDITIONS OF CONTEST

There will be no restrictions on the number of entries from any one contestant, nor on the size of prints, nor the kind of paper on which prints are made.

(1) Subjects of pictures must have to do with the Long Trail of the Green Mountain Club: Views of or from the Trail, and of life thereon, or in and around its camps.

(2) Every print must bear identification of location depicted and name of contestant. (Take care writing does not show through.)

(3) Judging will be on the following basis of comparison:-
(a) news interest, showing what the Trail or trail-life is like.
(b) excellence of photographic technique.
(c) suitability for reproduction.
(d) artistic quality.

HERBERT WHEATON CONGDON,
P. C. VOTER,
W. C. O'KANE,
Committee.

Photograph Contest
By Mortimer R. Proctor

We cannot emphasize too strongly the importance of this contest. The Secretary's office has innumerable calls for trail information and pictures. Written material can of course be provided on comparatively short notice, but unless it is well illustrated its effectiveness is tremendously diminished. We all know how people nowadays glance thru periodicals and advertising material generally, stopping only when a picture attracts their eye. There is so much published on every conceivable subject that such procedure is only natural for any of us.

In other words, to attract the public, we must show them pictures. Those who have travelled the Trail realize what numberless attractive, stimulating views it possesses. We must make it possible for the public to see these. City people as well as those from the country are becoming more and more anxious to spend all or part of their vacation in the woods. Pictures of the Long Trail will do more than anything else to arouse active interest in those who would surely come to the Trail if they but knew something of its beauty and charm. Furthermore, from a large collection of photographs, the Club will be able to make a selection for an illustrated booklet which will worthily represent the Green Mountains and the Long Trail. Some day in the near future we must have this.

But to make all this possible, we must kodak the Trail. Of course, all true lovers of the Trail dislike to have too many of its secrets and treasures ruthlessly divulged, so to speak. They should be earned, as all the greatest prizes are, thru effort. At best, however, the product of the camera is only a tantalizer—but a most effective one. The colors in the woods, the fragrance, the freshness, the vigorous feeling, in fact, all of nature's charms are actually divulged only to those who meet her face to face at sunrise, and at sunset; at full noon and in the shimmering shadows of moonlight.

Unless this tendency were checked, there is no knowing to what lengths it might go. One member suggests that the next speedster might travel over the Trail by the air route. We might, in the course of a year or two, meet on the Trail stunt artists, with their sweaters emblazoned with the names of firms who made their boots and other articles of equipment. Record smashers would dash by, accompanied by camera men who would take motion pictures of them in various attitudes and performing various functions. Enforcement officers would patrol the Trail and invade innocent packs, looking for contraband. Crowds of sight-seers would be attracted by the stories and pictures in the papers. Hot dog stands would spring up at short intervals to accommodate the crowds. Some may think this picture exaggerated. But none can deny that the recent tendency has been in that direction.

Some Suggestions

One of the members has asked if there could be something in the News giving suggestions or instructions about one or more desirable trips on the Trail, telling what to do and how to do it. He thinks that only a small percentage have been on the Trail, and those who have not and are contemplating a venture at it, would welcome something of the kind. We have not had time to secure such an article for this issue, but hope to publish hereafter descriptions of several such trips by some recognized Trail authority. In the mean time we have some suggestions to make. First of all, get the latest edition of the guide book and study it carefully. That will answer most of your questions. In the next place read or consult Walter C. O'Kane's book, Trails and Summits of the Green Mountains. That tells the motorist with climbing aspirations how he can motor to the bases of most of the principal Green Mountain peaks, leave his car and make an ascent, and regain his car in the afternoon in time to reach a hotel or lodging for the night. One of the best ways for a tenderfoot to get acquainted and wonted to the Trail is to put up at the Long Trail Lodge for a few days. This can be reached by motor from Rutland or Woodstock, and by bus from Rutland or White River Junction. Here one can make many interesting short hikes, including those to the summits of Deer's Leap, Pico and Killington, and pleasant woodland walks in any direction. He would here have the advantage of meeting trampers coming thru on the Trail from both directions, who could post him on what best to see and how to get there, and on conditions of the Trail, etc. Lastly, Theron S.
