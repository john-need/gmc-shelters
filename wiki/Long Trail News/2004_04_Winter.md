PROTECTING TRAILS
& MOUNTAIN LANDS
Since 1986, the Green Mountain Club has completed seventy-
nine trail protection projects protecting 61 miles of the Long
Trail, 16 miles of side trails, and 24,266 acres ofland. As of
April 30, 2004, 7 miles of the Long Trail and 4.5 miles of side
trails in northern Vermont (only about 3 percent of the entire
trail) remained without legal protection. The Club is fortunate
to have 35 dedicated volunteer corridor monitors who help
steward these conservation lands and easements by inspecting
properties, maintaining boundaries, and reporting violations.

PROTECTION PROJECTS
,; Belvidere View. Fourteen acres in Eden near the Long Trail
     surrounded by state forest were purchased from Don Hill at
     a bargain sale price. The land's frontage on Vt. ll8 made it
     vulnerable to future development across the road from
     Belvidere Pond and the Long Trail parking area.
r;   Bald Mountain Easement. Eva Jane Cole donated a right-of-
     way easement for 0.8 mile of the Bald Mountain Trail, a side
     trail that provides access to the Long Trail and the Green
     Mountain National Forest from the town of Bennington.
r;   Bolton Ridge. Thirty acres in Bolton with 0.2 mile of the
     Long Trail were purchased from Leland and Betty Wheelock.
     Located on the ridge between Stimson and Bolton Moun-
     tains, this property is adjacent to land GMC acquired from
     Bolton Valley Resort in 2003, now part of the Mount Mans-
     field State Forest.
r; Sterling Ridge Easement. The Mount Mansfield Company
