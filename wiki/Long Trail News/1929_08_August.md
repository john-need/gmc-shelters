The Long Trail Brings Real Estate
Development.

It is generally recognized that the Long
Trail and the Green Mountain Club are
factors of importance in the development
and prosperity of Vermont. Here is a case
in point:

In 1925 Mrs. Alexandria Brown, of Long
Island, N. Y., came up to Vermont with a
party of friends. They started on the Trail
at Breadloaf, going south. One evening
they reached the Herbert Haley farm south
of Mendon and Shrewsbury mountains,
where they spent the night, intending to
resume the tramp in the morning. But
their stay lengthened into days, and each
day found them more enamored with their
surroundings. Mendon, Little Killington,
Shrewsbury and other mountains about
them, the Dorset and Wallingford peaks
to the south, and towering above all, Kil-
lington Peak, not far away, and not least
among the attractions, two good trout
streams with their many tributaries running
thru this Eden. Regretfully they left, only
to return the following summer and pur-
chase the Haley farm. Adjoining land has
been purchased, until Mrs. Brown now owns
about 1000 acres in a body. One of the
farmhouses has been remodelled, and last
summer the family spent the entire season
in Shrewsbury, entertaining many friends.
Several of them have since bought adjoin-
ing properties which they have improved
and beautified, so that there is now a size-
able summer colony of people of means
and taste located in the "backwoods" of
Shrewsbury, in place of the almost
abandoned farms, and the end is not yet,
all due to that tramp over the Long Trail
in 1925.

New Camps.

The Bennington Section has erected a
closed metal hut on the summit of Glasten-
bury Mountain. It is 13 feet square, made
entirely of copper alloy steel. Inside are
four bunks, with room for six others, a
stove, table and cooking utensils. Within
100 yards is a spring. From one side is
a fine view of Searsburg, and also the bodies
of water at Whitingham and Somerset
dams. From the other side the cabin over-
looks Pownal and Greylock Mountain to the
south. The men who did the work were
John N. Leonard, A. J. Holden, Jr., and
Dr. L. H. Ross, of Bennington, Prof. Carl
S. Hoar of Williams College, and John B.
Clark of the Williamstown High School. W.
W. Holbrook, fire warden at Glastenbury,
also assisted.

The Fred H. Tucker camp in Hazen's
Notch is nearly completed.

A Long Trail Poem.

A bard has recently struck his blooming lyre and produced an alleged poem on the Long Trail in five stanzas. It starts in as follows:

Hiking-time to homelands
   And on the Vermont Hills,
Upon the tingling Long Trail
   With breezy sky-line thrills;
And cabin consolations,
That soothe all tribulations,
   While my free, expanding spirit feeds and fills.

We have known a variety of epithets to be applied to the Trail, but "tingling" is a new one. Here is the third stanza:

Feeding-Inn at Breadloaf,
   At Pico, Killington,
With Abraham and Mansfield,
   When these are highly won;
And it's always bright weather
When hikers get together
   Around the sparkling log of climbing fun.

"Feeding-Inn" may not be a strictly poetical expression, but it conveys an idea very alluring to hungry trampers. But if the author means to imply that such establishments may be found on the other mountains mentioned, he is mistaken. At the cabins on Killington and Mansfield a limited variety of canned goods may be found, but they do not aspire to the dignity of "feeding-inns". Did any hiker, in his perigrinations on the Trail, ever come across such a log as that described in the last line? If so, kindly specify when and where.

The other stanzas are of equal merit with those quoted.

The Long Trail Lodge.

The season has opened with a rush, the following large parties having already been entertained: The Young Ladies' Academy of Rutland for tea; The Proctor University Club for a banquet and business meeting; the Vermont marble salesmen; the Eastern New York Eye, Ear, Nose & Throat Specialists, with the Rutland physicians as guests at a banquet, filling the Lodge to capacity over night; the Engineers of the N. Y. Central lines; the Whist Club of Clare-mont, N. H. for dinner and over night; the Arlington Vt., Refrigerator Co's. salesmen and wives, and other smaller parties. Several parties have started north and several south from here. All report the Trail to be in very good condition.

As Mrs. Bates (the manager) truthfully remarks: "The delightful coolness which pervades the Lodge at all times makes it a most attractive place to visit in the warm summer months."

Obituary.

James B. Wilbur, of Manchester, Vt., was a member of the Green Mountain Club, and a capitalist and philanthropist of unbounded generosity. Besides large monetary gifts to the Library of Congress, the American Antiquarian Society and the Vermont Historical Society, he built and presented to the University of Vermont its beautiful Ira Allen Chapel. His collection of Vermont books, pamphlets and MSS. was one of the most valuable in existence, and these will go to the University of Vermont, to which institution he left also the bulk of his estate, subject to certain life interests. It is estimated that the University will ultimately receive from this source upwards of $1,500,000. His hobby was the early Vermont statesman, Ira Allen, brother of Ethan, and founder of the University of Vermont. He collected a great amount of material relating to this celebrated character, and was the author of an exhaustive biography of him, which was published shortly before his death.

Notes.

The Du Val trail (the old part) has been thoroly cleared and put in Al condition, and the new part has also been much improved.

Theron S. Dean of Burlington recently delivered an illustrated lecture on the Long Trail to a large and appreciative audience at the summer school at Breadloaf.

Travel on the Long Trail bids fair this season to be heavier than ever before. Registrations at the camps on Killington and Mansfield to date are considerably ahead of last year.
