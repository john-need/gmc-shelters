a'rns- those carefully balanced, visually
          imaginativ   rock piles often found high
          above the tree line--silent sentinels on our
          trails-reflect the magnetism between man
and nature.
   The building of cairns is an age-old, cross-cultural
practice that can be found on every continent with
purpose both meaningful and practical. Here in the
Northeast, hikers recognize these landmarks as trail
markers-there to help navigate ambiguous terrain
and keep travelers from becoming disoriented or lost.
   The sight of a cairn in dense fog can stimu-
late our survival instinct, letting us know we have
reached a space that demands our attention. Proceed
with caution. At other times cairns simply convey
the message to slow down and admire a wild and
beautiful place.
    Whether hiking across the expansive table on
Katahdin in early fall-when the wind is blowing rain
sideways reducing visibility to barely ten steps-or
to the summit of Camel's Hump on a bluebird sky day,
cairns reassure us that someone has safely traveled the
path before.
