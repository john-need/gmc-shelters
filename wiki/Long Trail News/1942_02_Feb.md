   The Committee addressed its recommendations to the Trustees,
but its specific proposals, as phrased by Walter S. Atwood, Chair-
man, will apply to every group:
   "If personality and leadership are valuable, along with scholar-
ship, what is being done in the colleges to stimulate these qualities?
Every National Park has its nature and camp leaders. Why not offer
training for this leadership and tie it in with the Green Mountain
Club? A start has been made with the Boy Scouts, and it should be
followed up.

home parties, strawberry festivals, sugar parties, or suppers, and

varied and printed program of activities, with some competition,

                        By CHARLES fuRDIN
     ODAY's the last day of Hiking for my Daddy and I-, and were sorry
T    its all over. We started in at Butler Lodge 10 days ago and will
leave in a few moments for Jay Peak, Jay Village, New York and then
home to Virginia Beach. Where Mother will probably give us both
a good hot bath, burn the smelly clothes we're wearing , and chuck
us in the ocean to soak until we smell sweet again.
   We have had a marvelous time, though we know we haven't
made any records for grounds covered, I know we have at having a
good time.
    "Dad says," we're real Green Mountain Boys , but I don't like the
way he emphasises "Green."                                        -
   He probably is right, however, for it is unlikely that seasoned
Long Trailers would have all the acheing muscles, breaking backs,
blistered feet, sweaty clothes that we have accumulated in these ten
days . Nevertheless we love it and are planning to get ourselves real
 tough, come back again, and make every peak from Mass. to Canada
say "Uncle."
    During this hike I have seen my first Chipmonk, porkypine, wild
 deer (on Butternut Mountain) fly catcher and song sparrow, grouse,
 eagle, trilium, bunchberry, turtlehead , and many other wonderful
 and thrilling things which I can't name.
    It has all been a great thrill. A thousands thanks to the G .M.C.
 P.S. And I also wish to say I've liked every person I've met on the
hike

             PORCUPINE
Nothing to fear wherever I go,
It's my own good time , if I do seem slow,
And I am not trying to make a show,
   So I do whatever I please.
My teeth are strong and my wants are few ,
And who else cares what I try to do ,
There is all the bark that I want to chew,
   For the woods are full of trees .

               Clambering, clambering over the stumps,
               With my rolling gait and my clumsy jumps ,
               For I am the boy with the long, sharp spines
               That save the lives of porcupines .

The bears and wildcats let me be,
One clash is enough with a chap like me ,
So I roam the forests far and free,
   With my small complaining voice.
But I'm not complaining of anything,
It is only the way I try to sing.
In fact I'm a kind of woodland king ,
    For I do what I do from choice.

               Nobody ever bothers me ,
               They move aside and let me be,
               And life moves on to its prickly goal,
               In the woods where the hedgehogs love to stroll.

Lower Bridge, Fairfax      EDMUND HOMER RO YCE



The Covered Bridge by Herbert Wheaton Congdon and Edmund
Homer Royce, Stephen Daye Press . $3 .50 .
   Although bridge building before i850 was an empiric craft
rather than an exact science, the men who in the early nineteenth
century erected Vermont's covered bridges "by guess and b y gosh"
are now classed by modern engineers among the empire builders. It
was something in itself to span a mountain stream or rocky gulch,
but to construct a bridge of native timbers to endure the weight of
snows , the force of gales, the jarring of rural traffic for a century or
more, is a feat deserving of highest praise.
   Mr. Congdon, long a trustee of the Green Mountain Club and
author of Old Vermoiit Houses, has produced a volume in which these
builders finally receive their due, as a class and as individuals . As an
architect, the author has analyzed the factors of durability as well
as of picturesqueness in their handiwork. The text is beautifully
illustrated with ioo photographs from the camera of Mr. Royce ,
whose lifetime hobby they represent , and by a number of structural
diagrams. Sprightly anecdotes and the crisp format take the volume
out of the text book class and into the realm of true delight .
