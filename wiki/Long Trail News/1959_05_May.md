HIGHLIGHTS FROM THE
TRUSTEES MEETING

The April 11th meeting of the Trustees in Rutland, Vermont brought forth a record attendance of all seventeen trustees of the Green Mountain Club. It was a full day of business, and the meeting did not adjourn until six p.m.

President Koerber announced that a 1960 Anniversary edition of the Guide Book will be ready for distribution at the Annual Meeting May 30, 1959. Due to the almost complete sell-out of the 1956 edition it was considered necessary to publish the Anniversary edition a few months earlier than had been planned. The price of this new edition is to be $1.50 for single copies and $1.00 per copy in packages of ten.

Don Kent announced that the Annual Meeting of the GMC will be held at Long Trail Lodge in Sherburne, Vermont on Saturday May 30th. At present the program has not been completed but he assured members that there would be an interesting program. He also reported that The August Intersectional plans have not been completed but that at the Annual Meeting a complete Information Sheet will be distributed.

Jack Martin had a box of GMC Buttons for sale at 10¢ per button. He requested members to come to the Annual Meeting with orders from their Sections for a supply. These are a 7/8" pin button, a miniature pattern of our GMC Sleeve Patch, that may be worn on lapel, shirt or dress. These may also be ordered individually from Miss Hinchey, 108 Merchants Row, Rutland, Vt.

Don Havens, a Burlington Section member, who was a guest at the meeting, showed a Green Mountain Club Car Badge that his Section has ordered for members to purchase who may wish to advertise the Green Mountain Club by attaching the badge to their cars. These badges will be on sale for $1.00 each at the Annual Meeting. Orders from all sections or individuals may be filled at this time. The trustees agreed that the use of a car badge is good publicity, and recommended the purchase of them.

A model of a cabin built to scale was shown by Richard Krasker, past president of the Middlebury College Mountain Club. The Cabin is to have double walls, a big stove, and its 16' x 24' will accommodate thirty-six people. It is being built especially for winter use by the Mountain Club and is located about one hundred feet behind Voter White Lodge. It is expected to be completed by October.

Mrs. Lodge suggested that the auditor of the Green Mountain Club's books should not be a member of the board of trustees, and moved that there be a change in the by-laws to correct this existing condition. This motion was carried. Members of the Club will vote on this change in the by-laws at the Annual Meeting. Notice of this, and the wording of the motion is printed elsewhere.

Roy Buchanan reported that work on the new Long Trail highway signs will be completed soon, and that the Patrol expects to work on the building of the Pico shelter this summer. He also stated that a schedule of proposed work for the Patrol for this summer will be presented at the next meeting of the trustees. In view of the fact that the shelters in the Green Mountain National Forest are required to have a garbage pit, toilet, and clean surroundings, it was suggested that this work be taken care of this summer. Any flagrant disregard for these requirements could lead to the termination of our permit to have shelters located in the Green Mountain National Forest.

The Emily Proctor Shelter, long in dis-use and abandoned, is to be burned. For some time it has been a hazard in the forest. Paul Newcomb consented to seeing to its disposal.

Contributions up to $100. have been added to the Taylor Fund. In making this announcement, President Koerber displayed a chart showing the ages of the various shelters on the Trail. This brought forth the fact that funds are needed now to replace many of the shelters that need replacing. Two sources of revenue seem to be the answer to this problem; contributions to the Taylor Fund, and an increase in membership.

Don Kent reported that he has done everything he can think of to promote a GMC 50th Anniversary Postal stamp. It was suggested that Vermont Stamp Clubs be contacted for help and interest in the project. Don, the chairman, also stated that he would be happy to receive suggestions from any GMC member who may have ideas as to how to promote this project further.

1959 INTERSECTIONAL

This year the annual Intersectional will be held on August 22nd and 23rd in Danby, Vermont. Don Kent and Alan Gormley are co-chairmen, and will have information sheets to distribute at the Annual Meeting giving complete details.

This location, the Danby-Tabor valley, is considered one of Vermont's scenic beauty spots. Its rolling terrain surrounded by steep mountains gives the hiker the opportunity to climb to many interesting spots. A few of the points of interest nearby are: Mt. Equinox, Griffith Lake, Little Rock Pond, Spruce Peak, and Prospect Rock.

The committee has requested that members be asked to bring their own equipment and food.

50TH ANNIVERSARY GUIDE BOOK

We are very pleased to announce that the demand for the 1956 Long Trail Guide Book exceeded our original expectations and our complete stock has been exhausted. We are pleased, because this is to us an indication that more and more people are becoming interested in the Long Trail. However, this unforecasted demand did catch us off base, and upset our well-laid plans to issue a 50th Anniversary Edition of the Guide Book in March 1960 to commemorate the founding of the Green Mountain Club on March 11, 1910. Faced with the alternative of prohibitively high reprint costs for a small quantity of 1956 Guide Books to tide us over until March 1960, and an advance printing of our 1960 50th Anniversary Edition, we decided that the latter course was the one to follow.

Fortunately the important ground work for the 1960 Guide Book had already been done as Ben Ralston had been faithfully keeping our log book of trail conditions and needed text revisions up to date. Faced with the emergency of an advanced printing date, Ben immediately dropped all other outside activities and started to revise the text of the 1956 Guide Book to include all known changes in shelters, trails, bridges, highways, ski developments, mailing addresses, lodging, transportation, etc., etc. At the same time, Roy Buchanan immediately started revisions on the master tracings for the 15 maps in the Guide Book, working closely with Ben to insure that the revised text and revised maps were in exact agreement. Throughout this period Gene Bamforth, father of the 1956 Guide Book, acted as proof-reader and checker to be sure that the new text was as accurate as it was possible to make it. Several times during the period when there was doubt about the accuracy of the trail data, Ben, Roy and Gene went out on the Trail in person to verify the details in question. After countless hundreds of hours, the careful, painstaking, and detailed work came to a climax, and the manuscript and maps were delivered to the press on March 30th.

We are very hopeful that the new 1960 50th Anniversary Guide Book will be off the press in time for distribution at the Annual Meeting on May 30th. It will have a new cover designed by Janet Martin, and include in the text several of her sketches of life-along-the-trail. It will also have a section commemorating the founding of the Long Trail and the Green Mountain Club, using liberal quotations from an article written several years ago by Jim Taylor, the founder of our Club. Be sure to pick up your copy at the Annual Meeting, or if you can't attend, drop $1.50 in an envelope and send your order to our Rutland office. No, that was not a misprint, like all other things, the cost of printing, paper and postage has gone up, and we could no longer give you the kind of Guide Book you want for the former $1.00. We think that you will find that it is still a bargain at $1.50---order yours now!

ARTHUR R. KOERBER

NEW YORK SECTION REPORT

Spring finds us with renewed enthusiasm for our varied program—weekends at Camp Thendara; Camping, Conservation & Canoe weekend trips; also Saturday and Sunday one day trips for those who must stay nearer home.

At Thendara, until it's warm enough to swim, there will be many little odd jobs for those wishing to help brighten camp up for the new season. Others will be wearing out their boots on the local trails. There is always a jolly group of workers for official House Cleaning. Every weekend, from now on, you will find enthusiastic campers.

Our Trail Clearing crew is a busy one. They also enjoy themselves and would be glad to have some of you join them. See the bulletin for their very select activities.

Better start practicing for the Bicycle Trip, a hardy group had a wonderful time a couple years ago.

The Saturday Walkers will usher in the spring time blossom season with walks to many parks and countrysides.

The trail clearers depend almost entirely on the reports made by the Sunday Walkers who go scurrying around all over the trails each week.

Some may be interested in statistics. Since the last LONG TRAIL NEWS, we have had three weekend camping trips which included skiing and snowshoeing; 10 Thendara weekends; 9 Saturday trips; 15 Sunday trail walks; three Program Meetings, a Square Dance and a well attended Annual Meeting. What is the saying? "Variety is the Spice of - - the G.M.C.-N.Y. Chapter." Come join us when you can.

KILLINGTON SECTION REPORT
(From SMOKE AND BLAZES March-April 1959)

At the annual meeting of the Killington Section the following officers were elected: Bennett Bell, president; Mary Perkins, vice president; Eleanor Walker, secretary-treasurer.

After the supper and business meeting the group enjoyed a showing of the new GMC slide collection. Well worth seeing!

Moonlight Hike

January 24th, the time set for the moonlight hike to Tucker, was a snappy winter night but not too cold for comfort. On the trip in we enjoyed a light snowfall which added fresh beauty to the winter woods. The snowshoeing was fair, a bit tricky in spots owing to a slippery crust, but this added excitement to the trip.

The starting of the fire at Tucker brought the usual smokey period while the chimney warmed up, but this soon passed, and the stove became the center of attraction as a pleasant warmth began to surround it.

After the usual interval devoted to doughnuts, coffee, and conversation, we closed camp. We were delighted to find that the skies had cleared, and on the hike out we enjoyed the light of the full moon promised for this occasion.

Buffum

Eleven hikers braved a cold north wind and slippery walking to make the trip to Buffum on February 8th. Though it wasn't as cold on the Trail as it seemed in the city, we welcomed the "Valiquette special" (stove) which has replaced the old broken-down stove formerly at Buffum.

We found Ben Rolston, that GMCer from Schenectady who gets around so much on the Trail, already there when we arrived. Ben was spending a long weekend on the Trail, and had stayed at Clarendon Lodge the previous night.

After lunch a few ambitious hikers went on to the top of Button Hill, and it was while on this trip that Section President, Bennett Bell, had the misfortune to fall and crack a rib (as we learned later).

Though indications could be felt of the below zero night which the weather man predicted, we enjoyed sun and blue skies on the homeward trek.

Winter Weekend

Late afternoon on February 20th found the first contingent arriving at Craigue Perkins' Camp, Tall Timber, for the annual winter weekend. The arrival of a party of five from Medford, Massachusetts soon after supper brought the overnight guests to nine. Everyone slept at the cottage this first night, and settled down fairly early, some of those drawing room assignments upstairs taking along a hot brick which proved a most effective bed-warmer.

Saturday dawned clear and cold, and most of the morning was taken up with greeting new arrivals, shifting of sleeping quarters, unpacking and settling. Twenty-five gathered around the table for the noon meal. After dinner everyone participated in hikes of varying lengths and in varying directions.

The weather during the day showed some variety, alternating bright sun and swirling snow. About the time everyone was assembling at the cottage for a supper of baked beans and brown bread, it looked as though a big blizzard were in the making. However, by the time supper was over, the moon was out. In this setting, with the tall white frosted evergreens making long shadows on the snow, and overhead a big moon in a star-studded sky looking near enough to touch, it all combined to make as perfect a winter night as any of us had ever seen. It was too good to miss, and everyone took advantage of it.

The final check up Saturday night showed a total of twenty-six—thirteen in the cottage and thirteen in the two cabins.

Sunday was another bright day, and everyone was up and doing early. People were in and out, starting and returning from hikes all morning. There were still more new arrivals, and thirty-two were on hand for chicken pie Sunday noon.

By mid-afternoon the people from Shrewsbury, Massachusetts and Springfield, Vermont were taking their leave, but there was still time for some good hikes for those who lived nearby. Thirteen die-hards were around to help clean up the leftovers at supper.

But all good times come to an end, and finally the last pack was shouldered, the door closed and locked, and another winter weekend was over.

BURLINGTON SECTION ANNUAL MEETING

The Burlington Section of the Green Mountain Club held its 49th annual meeting at the Waterman Building. The business meeting and entertainment followed a dinner in the faculty room Wednesday evening, Jan. 21. Seventy-two gathered around the supper tables which were attractively decorated with a miniature fir tree and trail sign at each place. An attractive arrangement of snowshoes, fir branches and artistically painted shelf mushrooms adorned a mantle shelf.

A husband and wife team was elected to steer the Club for the ensuing year. Ann Otis was elected president and her husband, David, vice president. Other officers and committee chairmen elected were Miss Shirley Strong, secretary; Leland Brown, treasurer; Fred Smith and Harris Abbott, co-property chairmen; Don Havens, Trail chairman; Donald Remick, outing chairman; Jerry Grow, membership chairman; Sam Hatfield, entertainment, and Larry Dean, publicity chairman.

During the business meeting outgoing president, Roderick Rice called for reports from the officers and committee chairmen. Don Havens reminded the group of the trail mileage covered to care for the Section's trails. Don Remick told of the extensive repair job done at Bolton lodge and reported on the caretakers stationed at Taft and Butler lodges last summer. Dave Otis reviewed the twice monthly outings of the Section stressing the week trip to the Great Gulf region of the White Mountains and the trips to the Adirondacks, the beef stew cook out and the oyster supper.

Fred Smith spoke of the new members taken into the Section during the past year. Sam Hatfield reported on the largely attended maple sugar party and square dance and the Halloween party and square dance.

President Rice paid tribute to the late Mrs. C. P. Cowles, first women president of the Club and life member, and to Frank Post, long time member of the Club.

A letter was read from Judge C. P. Cowles, charter member of the Club. He and Mr. K. R. B. Flint are believed to be the only two living charter members. The Judge urged the Club to make a special observance of the Club's 50th anniversary in 1960. He also suggested that the Section take part in the 350 Champlain Anniversary Festival this year and Mrs. Ann Otis was named to head the project.

Prof. Roy O. Buchanan was called on for a report regarding Long Trail Patrol activities. He reported that the New York and Burlington Sections cooperated in arranging for a new boat at the trail crossing of the Winooski River as a ferry for hikers. He reported that he was constructing and painting 7 regulation sized large Long Trail signs to be placed by the highways where the Trail crosses. He also reported on trail clearing and shelter building during the past summer. Buchanan assured the group that the new Sugar Bush Ski development on Mt. Abraham did not affect our trail area.

The two items of new business presented at the meeting were increase in insurance coverage on section lodges and increase in Section membership dues. It was voted that the Junior Membership be raised to $1.00 and adult dues to $3.00 per year.

After the business meeting Sam Hatfield announced the entertainment of the evening to be in three parts.

The first part of the entertainment was colored slides of European countries and shown by Larry Gardner, Jr. the guest speaker of the evening. His slides and talk included Holland in tulip time, mountain views in Italy, Germany, Switzerland, Norway and other countries.

During the second phase of the program Ken Fisher showed colored slides taken by club members of section activities during the past season.

The third phase was the showing of the Club's official slides arranged by John Vondell.

The arrangement of the meeting was under the chairmanship of Sam Hatfield, entertainment chairman, assisted by Misses Helen Puffer, Agnes Richardson and Shirley Strong. The young women of the committee cared for the decorations.

LARRY DEAN

BURLINGTON SECTION'S OYSTER STEW SUPPER

The oyster stew supper at Taylor Lodge drew out the largest attendance in history of the annual event according to Don Remick, outing chairman of the Burlington Section of the Green Mountain Club. Thirty-nine took part in the event. Sixteen of the group were guests from the Montpelier Section headed by section president Howard Bloom. There was standing room only in the cabin.

Miss Betty Paulsen, former club president, of the Rutgers University faculty, New Brunswick, N. J., made a special trip to be present.

The stew supper was prepared by Mr. and Mrs. Donald Remick and had all the makings of a full course meal. The Remicks have had charge of the event throughout the history of the event. They with several helpers packed in to the lodge early Sunday to arrange the supper.

Following the supper Dr. Maynard Bryant of the Montpelier section rallied the group into singing old favorite songs long into the evening.

The moon had shone over the range by the time the group broke up and snowshoed out in two directions to their waiting cars some two and a half and four miles away. The last half of the trail was lighted only by the light of the moon, while lanterns and flashlights were brought into play in the deep woodland area.
