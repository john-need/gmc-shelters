                LONG TRAIL PATROL
   The Patrol worked from June 17 to Aug. 24 with three men on the gang. Aside from the usual trail work and camp repair and clean-up after vandals, the big job this year was the trail relocation between Gorham Lodge and Bolton Lodge. This was made necessary by the folding up of the Bolton ferry. It never did function successfully but it ended when the boat went down river during a freshet in Sept. 1962. The Long Trail now crosses the Winooski River by the highway bridge at Jonesville, some three miles west of Bolton. This relocation starts at Gorham Lodge and ends at Bolton Lodge, or vice versa, an entirely new trail. It adds some mileage to the trail so that now Massachusetts and Canada are farther apart than they were but it also adds some very good views especially off Robbins Mt. and its ridge. Robbins is only 2080 ft. in elevation but Camels Hump is very imposing from it. We have signed the trails at both Gorham and Bolton as follows:

                  Long Trail South                         Long Trail South (or North)
                  New Route 1963                           Old Route 1963
                  Via Jonesville Bridge                    Via Bolton - No

Of course the "new route" is the relocation and the "old route" is the old route.
   Vandalism was mentioned above. It is getting worse. Since last August, more wreckage has occurred at Bolton. Last spring, about half of the glass and much of the window sash was smashed there. The Burlington Section Properties Chairman decided to patch up the old sash as much as they could and put plastic sheet over the rest of the openings to keep the wind out. This was as they left it at the end of August. Last week all of the glass was gone and the plastic was all torn off and thrown into the dump. Probably we shall have to leave the camp open for it is impossible to keep ahead of the vandals, just as has been done at Cooper Lodge.
   I wish we could find these vandals. If we could have a conference with them, we could find out how they want the camps maintained. At last they have shown us that they don't want any glass or sash in the window openings. There seems to be a feeling against stoves and bunks, although we aren't sure about that yet. Of course bunks are for burning so they may be tolerated for a while. We know that toilets are to be tipped over and later used for kindling. Surely a conference with our friends, the vandals, would let us keep the camps in the condition they desire.
                                             ROY BUCHANAN

BENNINGTON SECTION

Bennington Section has a fall schedule of walks nearly every second Sunday afternoon, ending in November with a climb up Styles and Tabor or one as light permits and supper at Mad Tom Shelter.

We have several new members and hope to have some more.

Our only hike since the Intersectional was an effort September 15th to replace the side trail up the back side of Bald Mt. Got started up a wood road which led to more lumber roads; had a grand walk nearly to the top of the mountain (a glorious day) but didn't find the proper trail, till we came down a side road near the bottom. Then John blazed maybe a half mile up it (it needed it) and it was time to come back for a cook-out at Fay Fuller. Cleaned up the camp as much as possible. But there were no boughs or ferns available and the bare springs didn't look inviting. Best we could do in a rough neighborhood. Left some dry wood inside.

We meant to go back and finish our blue paint job today, but the weather man is saying "no" very definitely and I for one am grateful for the rain.

MARION HARDY, Reporter

BURLINGTON SECTION

Up The Trail, Apiece-

Back again to tell you what we've done up this way:

On the week-end of July 6-7 the Burlington Sectioners crossed over the Connecticut River for climbs of Mt. Cardigan (AMC climbing country) and Mt. Kearsarge.

On a hot July 21st, Day Outing Chairman Pete Keelty got together a large group at Barrows Camp for a picnic after several had swam in the Lamoille River and tramped up over the trail to Prospect Rock.

On Aug. 4th fifteen aspiring End-to-Enders took a day walk from Brandon Gap north to Middlebury Gap. They agreed it was a nine mile up and down route over Mts. Horrid, Cape Lookout, Romance, past the new Sucker Brook shelter and on over Worth Mt. Shirley Strong led the pack on this trip.

After that walk, a beach party at the Thomas lakeshore cottage on Aug. 17th was a welcome change.

The Burlington Section "egged on" by Roy Buchanan threw its support behind the Intersectional week camp at the Girl Scout camp near Rutland. We were fifty-six strong. The singing busmobile Paul Jenks family from the Waterloo, N.Y. branch of our Section were newcomers this year and we hope they come again.

During Aug. 19-25 Smitty threw in an extra with eight making the trip up Mt. Katahdin in Maine.

Sept. 8th, an ideal early autumn day, brought thirty-seven to the west slope of Mt. Mansfield for a corn roast feed "thrown" by Joan and Andy Buchanan after an afternoon of hiking on Mansfield's slope.

Mt. Marcy, Adirondack's highest, challenged our Section and perfect weather made a grand week-end in the area. Most of the group made the fourteen mile round trip from Heart Lake to the summit of Marcy. Dean, Laura and Nicky Zwick came all the way over from Rochester to tramp the trail with Larry and their Burlington Section pals.

It's Being Breezed Around-

'Tis said that the Burlington Section Trustee, Fred Smith, has gotten the ball rolling for the 1964 Intersectional week camp. Smitty has cleared the ground for a return to the Mt. Norris Scout Reservation Camp at Eden, place of the 50th anniversary celebration - remember? He hopes you will plan your next year's vacation for it. How about it?

Did you know that Harris Abbott, former Long Trail patrol member, thought of an idea to save the Club money? He figured it out that if he and Jan Timmerman got "hooked up," married that is, the Club could save money on postage by sending one Long Trail News out for the both of them to read. So he up and did it this past August. - LARRY DEAN, Reporter

MONTPELIER SECTION

Our summer activities included a ten mile hike on July 13, led by Dick Babcock, beginning at the Lake Mansfield Trout Club, going up to Taylor Lodge, through Nebraska Notch, over the Forehead, by the Nose, over the Chin and down into Smuggler's Notch. We were accompanied by five members of the Adirondack Mt. Club; in fact they outnumbered us.

On July 28 we enjoyed an outing at Groton State Forest beginning with a swim party and ending with a chicken barbecue at Owl's Head; 30 persons attended. The blueberries were delicious on top of Owl's Head and so was the view. Ann Otis was in charge of this event. I think I'd better not mention what happened on Aug. 10th. On Aug. 17th a small group led by Dave Otis climbed Camel's Hump. On Sept. 14th we held a corn roast at Underhill State Park and climbed to Cantilever Rock. We can legitimately call it a corn roast because two of the nineteen persons attending roasted their corn; the rest ate it boiled. On Sept. 18th we met at Dick Babcock's house to plan our fall schedule, which is as follows:

Sat. Sept. 28 - Mt. Lafayette - Leader: Dick Babcock. Same night - slides at the Farnsworths at 8 P.M.

Sun. Oct. 13 - Robbins Mt. near Jonesville to hike a new section of the Long Trail - Leader: Don Wallace.

Sat. Oct. 26 - Mt. Hunger - a hike and wiener roast - in charge of hike, Dave Otis - in charge of wiener roast, Dave Morse.

Sun. Nov. 3 - Berlin Pond - Leader: Dot Babcock.

Sat. Nov. 16 - Sterling Pond Lodge - Leader: Harlan Farnsworth.

Sat. Dec. 7 - Ritterbush Camp - a chowder party - Leaders: Ann Otis and Marion Gorham.

Sun. Jan. 5 - Hardwood Flats - a snowshoe walk - Leader: Frances Holmes.

We welcome members of other sections on any of our hikes. If you happen, or expect to be in our area give us a call and join us. We're all in the phone book.

MARION GORHAM, Reporter

NEW YORK SECTION
Word has come from the New York Section that its long time member Mason Stone passed away some time in March. Mr. Stone has the distinction of being with the late Judge Cowles, James P. Taylor and some twenty others a charter member of the Vermont Club, being present at the famous organization meeting held in Burlington, March 11, 1910. It is believed that as the result of the recent deaths of Judge Cowles and Mason Stone, Professor Kent Flint, residing at Great Neck, Long Island, N.Y., is now the sole surviving member of the original founding group.

WORCESTER SECTION
The summer months found the members of Worcester Section scattered in different directions, some hiking, some camping, and some just staying home and picknicking and swimming at our camp in Leicester.
An overnight canoe trip was enjoyed in July by 14 of our members on the Saco River in Maine. A good time was had by all.
Several other outings were held during the "hotter" months all of which were well attended. No matter what the activity, it seems like we always have a good time when we are together.
By the time this is being read, we will hope to have had several fine hikes during the month of October which is such a beautiful month here in New England.
We are sorry to report the passing of another one of our members, David Flower.

LINNEA ELDRIDGE, Reporter

NEW BIG BRANCH SHELTER
The United States Forest Service, under the direction of District Ranger Sidney B. Davis, has constructed another new shelter for Long Trail hikers.
This new shelter, of lumber construction, is at a new site, thus giving us an additional shelter in our Long Trail System. It is located along Big Branch, on the north side of the stream, about 600 feet downstream from the new suspension bridge. Although not on the present Long Trail route, the new shelter will be on the Griffith Lake - Baker Peak - Route 10 relocation scheduled for completion by early spring.
Our sincere thanks to Forest Supervisor Paul S. Newcomb, Ranger Davis, and the USFS staff for their consideration of the future needs of the ever-increasing number of Long Trail hikers. Their choice of this location for a new shelter is an excellent one!

ECLIPSE AND KATAHDIN

On July 20th, the Cahees, Days and Weeks, GMCers, departed from Springfield, Vermont for Dexter, Maine, to view the eclipse and then scale mighty Mount Katahdin.

We all arrived at Dexter in early afternoon and had plenty of time for a refreshing swim in a nearby lake and a picnic lunch. With eager anticipation we watched nature's phenomena take place. The sky became dark as midnight, stars came out, automatic devices turned on street lights and birds twittered night songs. Everything perfect until the very last instant, at totality, a cloud drifted across and blotted out the last ten or twenty seconds of the eclipse.

We pitched tents at Roaring Brook Campground, Baxter State Park, by flashlight. Betty Weeks was awakened in the night by queer noises and thought that raccoons were in the garbage can back of the tent. Next morning she was informed by the ranger that a bear had been the culprit.

Sunday we watched a young bull moose feeding in Sandy Stream Pond and then climbed South Turner Mountain. This is a beautiful mountain to climb, the last quarter mile above timber line, just big blocks of granite and a stunning view of Katahdin and miles of woods, ponds and lakes.

Monday we back-packed into Chimney Pond camping area and set up camp. Chimney Pond is a gem of pure, sparkling water at the very foot of the most magnificent mountain east of the Mississippi. Everyone spent hours sitting on the shore gazing in awe at Baxter Peak, the Knife Edge and Pamola, with its plume of white cloud.

Tuesday morning dawned fair and clear. Everyone was eager with anticipation, for this was the day of ascent of mighty Katahdin. We started up the Cathedral Trail, which was the steepest and most rugged any of us had ever encountered. Massive blocks of granite made the going slow and the ends of fingers sore from grasping and clawing the rough, abrasive surfaces. Noon found us at the top of Baxter Peak, a mile above sea level.

Leaving Baxter Peak, we were soon crossing the fabulous Knife Edge. This is all the name implies. In many places the trail was only two feet wide with sheer drops of fifteen hundred feet on one side and only less sheer on the other. Everyone made it and sighed with relief on reaching the Chimney Notch.

After a short, steep ascent we reached the summit of Pamola Peak. Taking the Dudley Trail off Pamola was a slow, tiring process. The blocks of granite grew larger and everyone more fatigued with each successive step. Finally, after seven and one half hours we had completed the circuit which was completely in view from the shore of Chimney Pond by the campsite!

The remainder of our stay was spent in short hikes, taking pictures of bear, deer, spruce hens and enjoying some of the other campsites in Baxter State Park. We would recommend Baxter State Park to all GMCers who like to view wildlife, study glacial terrain, hike to more or less primitive camping areas and above all climb majestic Mount Katahdin.

OUT ON THE TRAIL

In August we took a trip up to Cooper Lodge to begin what we thought would be a routine trail clearing adventure. But an experience at the Lodge far outweighed the work that we did, and impressed in our memories something far greater than the removal of a few blowdowns.

At the Lodge we met a man and his wife who, on first impression, appeared to be average Long Trail hikers. They had begun the Trail at the Massachusetts border, and had leisurely walked north to this point. They were planning to complete their trip at Sherburne Pass.

The man spoke with great detail on their experiences. Getting off the Trail at a confusing turn. The people that they met. Steep and rough portions of the Trail. The rains. The good camps. The poor camps. The sounds of wildlife. The singing of the birds. The views from the summits.

An average hiker, you say? Yes, from all appearances an average hiker. Until we made a discovery. This man was sightless.

Yes, completely sightless. He walked along the streams. Beside the ponds. Through the evergreen forests. Over the wind-swept summits. But with his wife's eyes to lead him.

We walk the same Long Trail that Dan had walked. But Dan saw more than we see. Without sight.

With his hands, he felt the pitch of the spruce boughs. The moisture-retaining softness of the moss. The delicate texture of the leaves. With his feet he felt the softness of the forest floor. The age-old hardness of the granite summits. With his cheeks he felt the gentle breezes. The tingle of the cold morning air. The warmth of the campfire.

His nostrils sensed the fragrance of the evergreens. The sweetness of the wild flowers. The first wisp of smoke as the fire was kindled. The pleasant aroma of food on the table, or the bacon in the pan, or the coffee in the pot.

His ears heard sounds of distant villages. The scurrying of the chipmunk. The drumming of the partridge. The hooting of the distant owl. The music of his beloved birds.

Yes, though sightless, Dan felt, and sensed, and heard. He SAW the blessings of the Vermont woods, and the wonders of nature. Thank you, Dan, for helping US to see them too.

BEN ROLSTON

LONG TRAIL HIKER AWARD

The Green Mountain Council, Boy Scouts of America, Rutland, Vermont, is the sponsor of the new Long Trail Hiker Award.

The Long Trail has been given three county divisions:

Bennington County-Blackinton to Mad Tom Notch
Rutland County-Mad Tom Notch to Brandon Gap
Addison County-Brandon Gap to Winooski River

Special Long Trail Hiker Award patches have been prepared, with 120-degree border segments for each of the three counties.

All Scouts, Explorers and adult Scouters who complete the specified distance in one or more of the three counties, and who perform some trail improvement work and submit a report per the requirements of the award, will become eligible to receive the award and patch.

Full information may be obtained from the Green Mountain Council, 128 Merchants Row, Rutland, Vermont.
