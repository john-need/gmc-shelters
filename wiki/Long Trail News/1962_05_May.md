APRIL 7TH TRUSTEES' MEETING

The Spring meeting of the Board of Trustees was held at the Club Office in Rutland on April 7, 1962, with John Vondell, President, presiding.

Highlights of Section Reports

Bennington—Marion Hardy reported that hikes suitable to include family groups were enjoyed and that dinner outings were popular.

Brattleboro—Don Kent stated that a committee will plan the activities for the coming year, placing emphasis on trail clearing, the Annual Meeting and the Intersectional.

Burlington—Don Havens indicated that their section membership was on the increase and now they plan a variety of hikes and outings on Saturday, Sunday and week-ends to interest family groups as well as the individual.

Killington—Ben Rolston reported a very active schedule and that the dinner outings drew large groups.

Middlebury—John Maslack, U.S. Forest Service, reported that Ranger Lutz, newly appointed at Middlebury, would meet with the members of the Middlebury Outing Club this Spring to plan future trail work and activities.

Montpelier—Harlan Farnsworth said they had joint outings with Burlington and Sterling to the delight of both groups. Their full moon snow shoe hikes were also popular.

Briefs on Committee Reports

Annual Meeting—see notice about it.

Conservation—The proposed new ski development on Mt. Ellen between the existing ski areas of Sugarbush and Mad River Glen was discussed. It is understood that the proposed new development would be on privately owned land but that a request had been made for the use of some 700 feet of National Forest land at the summit for the lift line. The trustees will consult with sections and make recommendations at the next meeting. (See "Conversion or Conservation")

End-to-End Award—Ten names were added in 1961, making a total of 113 End-to-Enders. Articles about them have appeared in the Long Trail News.

G.M.C. Trust Fund—no report

Guide Book—Sales are still high with an additional 292 books sold and 128 out on consignment, so plans will soon be made for the next edition.

Historical—The location of early club records was discussed, as well as the whereabouts of the original club charter.

Intersectional—see article about it.

Publicity and Membership—The allotted budget will be used to assist the Burlington Section in its display at the Girl Scout National Jamboree to be held at Button Bay this summer. (See Patrol Report)

Trail Conference—no report

Trails and Shelters—see Patrol Report

The proposed G.M.C. Directory of all section members and members-at-large will be prepared for distribution as soon as possible.

The parent club dues of fifty cents per adult member and twenty-five cents per child member should be forwarded to the parent club treasurer for each membership, regular, life, honorary, etc., carried on the Section Roster.

ONE, TWO, THREE! CONVERSION OR CONSERVATION

First, it was Mt. Mansfield, second, it was Killington Peak, and now the skiing literature has just revealed plans are already underway for the CONVERSION of Vermont's third highest mountain, Mt. Ellen, into a ski development. Our present generation may boast that we are making progress through growth in the Soaring Sixties, by pushing back new frontiers in space science, promoting materialism, higher GNP, and a better standard of living—but where is the voice from the wilderness that our forefathers intended to act as part of a system of checks and balances in our American form of government? It would seem the voice of CONSERVATION has been neutralized by removal of too much wilderness and by justification of too many Government, State and private organizations that conservation must be subjugated to conversion at any cost to future generations. This feeling pervades even the U.S. Congress under the subliminal titles of "Recreation" and "Wilderness" bills.

One may wonder what future Americans from crowded cities and suburbia will think when they read about the creation of the "Footpath in the Wilderness" in the early twentieth century, only to see that all the unspoiled wilderness of Vermont's mountains has been converted instead of being conserved. Can we honestly justify spoilage of the wilderness surrounding the third highest mountain in the State for another ski development half-way between Mad River and Sugarbush under the heading of real progress, or is this just another example of material progress for the present generation?

THEODORE H. TALBOT JR.

BENNINGTON SECTION
The Bennington Section Green Mountain Club has enjoyed two outings this winter. In February, about twenty members enjoyed a clam chowder supper, topped off with maple sugar on snow. In the afternoon over half the group set out for a snowshoe and ski walk to Little Pond, but the snow had too hard a crust for skiing and too tender a crust for walking. The youngsters had a grand time, but nobody went very far and all were back for supper early.
   In March a back-road walk in Shaftsbury brought out over a dozen hikers who made a circuit on roads. Half the way was unplowed all winter, with deep snow in places, but in areas where the snow had melted, it was very muddy. It was a beautiful day and fine views both North and South.
   Monthly trips are planned for the winter and every two weeks in summer. If the laurel, which grows only in one spot in western Vermont, will be obliging and bloom on schedule, a walk through it on June 10 will be delightful.
   A trip up Mount Abraham, with some of the group camping the night before, is planned for the Fourth of July.
                            MARIAN HARDY, Reporter

BRATTLEBORO SECTION
   The Brattleboro Section held its annual meeting on Sunday, February 25, 1962, at the Newbrook Firehouse in Newfane, Vermont. About fifteen members attended. Officers were elected for the ensuing year (see Who's Who), and, for the first time, the Section elected a Trustee. He is Rexford Coleman, a native of Winhall and Bondville, Vermont, and a resident of Newfane. A carpenter by trade, "Rex" is a gardener, and Choir Director of the local Congregational Church. He was formerly a saw mill operator. His wife, Elizabeth, is Treasurer and Town Clerk of Newfane, as well as Librarian, church organist and church Treasurer. Rex and Elizabeth have hiked 116 miles of the Long Trail over the past few summers and are looking forward to becoming End-to-Enders.
   Another member of the Brattleboro Section, Charlie Smith, writes to say he has married and is now living in Hawaii. His wife is a Director of the Hawaiian Trail and Mountain Club. Charlie is a real "outdoors man" who was very active clearing trails when he lived here in Manchester Depot. Any members who are Hawaii-bound might enjoy looking him up.
               MRS. LOUISE H. DUNKLEE, Reporter

BURLINGTON SECTION
Up the Trail Apiece—
   Since our last get-together, we had our Annual Business Meeting and Election of Officers for the ensuing year.
   In addition to the officers listed in Who's Who, the following were elected: Dane Shortsleeve, Trail Chairman; Harris Abbott-Joseph Frank, Property Chairmen; Andrew Buchanan, Outing Chairman; Miss Shirley Strong, Entertainment Chairman; Miss Mary Forrester, Membership Chairman. One-hundred ten members turned out to this annual meeting and dinner to put this slate of officers over. We had our weekly activities during the winter months, day and afternoon.
   The most fitting to remember were: The oyster-stew supper at the James P. Taylor memorial lodge, in Nebraska Notch. Ben and Don Remick did it again—they fed forty-eight who made the crusty and icy two odd mile trail trip up to the cabin. Again the Remicks furnished a meal that couldn't be beat. The Montpelier Section joined us for the feed.
   Thirty-five dug out their skates a week later for an afternoon on the ice of Lake Champlain. Andy Buchanan, Outing Chairman, had a roaring bonfire on shore and provided hot coffee for the group.
   Then there was the snowshoe hike to Wiley Lodge on February 3, headed by Professor Al Wurthmann, and a snowshoe and ski trip on Mt. Mansfield in the Tear Drop Ski trail region. During this trip a group worked a wood cook stove down around the Forehead to set up in Mabel Taylor Butler Lodge. Clyde Smith headed up the ski-snowshoe party February 18.
   On Sunday afternoon, March 4, twenty-seven did some sliding out at the Westford home of Professor and Mrs. Roy Buchanan. Helen furnished coffee and her well-known doughnuts. She claims the fellow that got her to back down Mt. Liberty, in the White Mountains, several years ago, also forced the doughnut-making onto her this time, but we don't believe it.
   Our annual maple sugar party and square dance party March 24, drew an attendance of 108. Several of the party visited a sugar camp and watched the boiling of sap for a while and then climbed a healthy little hill before returning to Colchester for the supper and dance. A colored picture on white water canoeing was also shown. Shirley Strong and her committee pulled off a good party, this one.
   Our outing listing for the spring and summer months welcomes anyone up this way to swing in beside us.

HAL MARCH BECOMES END-TO-ENDER

The first entry on the End-to-Ender list for 1961
was Hal A. March of Brattleboro, Vermont, and
Middlebury College. He claims no connection with
quiz shows.
   Mr. March started his E-to-E trek in 1956 with a
group hiking from Lincoln Gap to Smugglers Notch.
The next summer, right after college exams (and in
the midst of black flies) he hiked from Route 103 to
Lincoln, staying the first night at Clarendon Lodge,
"which has remained one of our favorite camps."
Skyline Lodge is also mentioned as a favorite, with
Sunrise a strong candidate for the worst. His group
always carried dried foods (Siedel's Trail Packets)
on their trips.
   In June 1958, they started at Smugglers Notch on
June 21st, and cruised into Journeys End Camp on
June 29th, mentioning a "great stove at Barrows
Camp and bear scratches on the wall of Parker
Camp."
   1959 found him hiking with a group from Arlington
Road to the Long Trail Lodge. This was done
between September 3rd and 9th. They found a big
crowd at Little Rock Pond, and saw a black bear
near Clarendon Lodge.
   In 1960 he finished the southern part and became
eligible for the 104th End-to-Ender Award.

OUT ON THE TRAIL

Have you ever tried trail clearing in January? We did—and it was successful too. We picked a sunny day, and what little snow there was in the woods had a hard crust which made snowshoes unnecessary. We cleared about a mile of trail, clipping little branches and sawing bigger ones as we went along. Cut out a lumbering area, installed a few arrows, and painted some blazes where needed. We're anxious to see how well winter-applied paint stands the test. For our reward after our work, we went to the top of the open ridge, built a fire, and how good that hot soup and tea tasted! For a mid-winter break from the monotony of indoor living, try a trail-clearing trip.

Got a letter recently from Lee Kochenour, age 12, of Williamstown, Massachusetts. Lee and his father ALMOST became End-to-Enders last August. They began at the Massachusetts line and set their sights on Canada. An illustrated clipping from the North Adams paper described their departure. They had a great father-and-son trip together—for 159 miles of the Long Trail. But, unfortunately, at the Hedgehog Brook Trail Lee developed a pain in his side which later was diagnosed as a kidney strain, so they wisely chose to return to civilization and a doctor. They expect to return this summer to finish their trip to Canada, so we anticipate the pleasure of awarding two more End-to-End certificates sometime this year to Lee and Charles Kochenour of Williamstown. Congratulations on their 159-mile trip, and best wishes for the other 96 miles to go.

Speaking of interesting trips on the Long Trail, we just got word of another trip being planned for this spring from Route 11 at Bromley to Wallingford Gulf. Sounds good, but what's so unusual about that? Well, the two GMC'ers are planning to hike it AT NIGHT. They will leave Route 11 at 9 PM and hike all night, reaching Old Job in time for breakfast Saturday morning. They will sleep in the sun (they hope) at Old Job, then leave at 9 PM for another all-night hike, reaching Buffum for Sunday breakfast. They claim that the nighttime views from Bromley, Styles and Peru Peaks should be very unusual. You know, stopping at Griffith Shelter for tea and soup at 2 o'clock in the morning does sound fascinating! We hope that these promoters of night hiking will keep the readers of the News informed of the results of this trip.

As we prepare this article, it's mid-winter, the snow is deep, and the temperature may drop to zero tonight. Yet it's interesting to know that the GMC office is constantly receiving inquiries on the Long Trail. One this week from a minister in Montreal who would like to take a group of boys on the Trail this summer. Another from a lad in New York state who wants to get out with some of his school chums for a few days on the Trail. And yet another from one who wants to hike from Camel's Hump to Smugglers' Notch.

But the most interesting of our recent inquiries concerns a college student from Massachusetts who plans to start at Journey's End in July and spend about 35 days on an End-to-End trip to Massachusetts. This is a sample of the increasing interest in our End-to-End program.

It looks like we'll have a new trail to conquer this season. Some old timers in the GMC may remember the old Dorset Trail which used to lead over the Aeolus-Dorset ridge. An outdoor group from Connecticut has been working on the project of reopening this old trail. At the latest report, the trail is now complete from the summit of Aeolus to the west summit of Dorset Peak. We later hope to have full information on this new trail for GMC'ers who are interested in hiking these so-called "outlying mountains."
