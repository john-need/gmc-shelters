John Nuffort's Long Range Planning Committee has studied various GMC matters and made several recommendations for consideration by the Trustees.

The GMC grew by over 350 members during the last year and now has a total membership of 2,500 including 1,354 Sectional Members and 1,146 At-Large Members, according to Hazel Bullock, Membership Chairman.

Each of the Sections reported considerable activity during the year. All are working hard on cleaning up dumps and initiating the "Carry In-Carry Out" policy. The Sterling Section has been reactivated and there is some interest in a Section for the Newport-Montreal area.

At the annual meeting, auctioneer Jack Nuffort accepts the highest bid for these obsolete trail signs.

David Otis, Legislative Chairman, reported that the "ATV Bill" (S55) passed the Vt. Legislature placing strict regulations on the use of all terrain vehicles. However, the "Snowmobile Bill" (S63) did not pass and a special legislative committee will be studying the snowmobile situation during the summer and new legislation is expected to be introduced during the next session. Dave also told the interesting story of the conception of the Joint Resolution idea by Alan Howsmon, the preparation of the resolution, and the passage of the resolution by the Vt. Legislature.

Many of the reports and much of the discussion during the meeting focused on changes in policies and new programs being initiated to handle the increased traffic on the Long Trail. Dane Shortsleeve explained the new "Trail Supervisor Program" that puts responsibility for five geographical sections of the Long Trail under individual supervisors who can work more closely with sections than could the one overall chairman. The "Adopt A Shelter Program" has been initiated and is proving to be a good program. The "Caretaker Program" has been expanded this year to provide caretakers at the Mt. Mansfield, Camel's Hump, Stratton Pond and first thirty mile areas of the Long Trail. The Green Mountain Club, U.S. Forest Service and the Vt. Forests and Parks Department will undertake studies this summer to ascertain the number of people using the Trail.

John Nuffort, End-to-End Chairman reported that fifty persons completed their end-to-end in 1970. This compares with an average of 22 per year during the period 1961-65 and 38 per year during the period 1965-70.

On May 1, 1971 a new agreement was signed with the Howard National Bank of Burlington, Vt., giving the bank complete authority for changes in the portfolio. The new agreement also specifies that the fund shall be managed for 1.) security, 2.) income and 3.) growth, in that order.

The membership present voted to change the By-Laws as proposed in the February, 1971, "Long Trail News."

The membership present voted to elect Minerva Hinchey as Corresponding Secretary, Alan Howsmon as Clerk, and Perry Tooker as Trustee for Members-at-Large.

GEORGE PLUMB, Clerk

1971 INTERSECTIONAL

This year's event will be held August 28 thru Sept. 6, which is Labor Day. The location is Camp Ondawa, the Bennington County 4-H camp, located at Sunderland-just south of Manchester on Route U.S. 7. There are a limited number of cabins and one bunkhouse with several sleeping rooms.

Intersectional this year will provide an opportunity to rest and relax-or if you wish there are miles and miles of trail to be hiked. There are several major relocations of the Long Trail in the southern part of the state which we plan to cover. There will be Square dances, Campfire programs and our new programs covering Hike Leadership, Map Reading, Outlying Mountains and Sign Making. "Bean Hole Bob" Vickstrom will put on a couple of meals in the finest GMC tradition.

Fees will be charged to cover expenses; Tent or Trailer space 50¢ per person per night, max. $2.00 per family per night. Cabins or Bunkhouse: 75¢ per person per night, max. of $2.50 per family per night.

To reserve space or for a copy of our hike schedule write Alan R. Mead, 52 Skinner Rd., Rockville, Conn. 06066.

To members of Burlington & Sterling Sections who may think this a bit far south in Vermont for an Intersectional we just want to say "Y'all Come."

SUMMARY OF TRUSTEES' MEETING
MAY 29, 1971

Shirley Strong reported her three months activities as consultant to the Club. She said in general her most important function as consultant was keeping informed of developments of other organizations—public and private, local, state, and federal—whose programs overlap areas of interest of the GMC, and channeling this information into the hands of appropriate Club members as input for future planning.

As an example she cited the proposed North Country Trail of the 1968 National Scenic Trails Act. A study of its feasibility, location, etc., is presently underway by the Bureau of Outdoor Recreation and will be completed by mid 1972. Whether or not the GMC desires this trail in Vermont, she suggested the Club should have a strong input into the study. Otherwise, the Club will have no influence on the outcome of the study and the resulting trail location and operation.

She reported progress on a procedures manual, and a system of collecting and recording data on land ownership along the Long Trail. Further consulting activities will include attendance at forthcoming environmental hearings, looking closer at the question of tax exempt status of the Corporation, and following developments with regard to the proposed East-West Highway.

Alan Mead of the Connecticut Section presented a proposal for a pilot "Wilderness Camping" project. He said the purpose of this type of camping area is to provide non-vehicular camping at lower elevations somewhat adjacent to but not on the Long Trail to lessen the pressure on the Long Trail system itself. It was mentioned that Vermont's Department of Forests and Parks strongly supports the concept but at present has no money to aid in its implementation. Joe Sposta, U.S. Forest Service, stated that they have in their management plan two "backwoods" camping areas. He felt the Mead proposal was similar in concept and would certainly complement their effort.

The Trustees endorsed the wilderness camping project in concept and directed the President to appoint a committee to give it further study. The committee is to report its findings to the Trustees, and upon approval, could commence the pilot project with cost not to exceed $450.00.

A budget for the coming year was approved by the Trustees which includes two very key items with regard to the future direction of the Club. The first of these two budget items is for continued consulting services. The second item is for rental and operation of a GMC office. By mail ballot the President was given full authorization to rent appropriate space in Rutland and to open a new GMC office there subject to the bounds of the approved budget.

Dave Otis, Chairman of the Nominating Committee, presented a slate of officers for the coming year. The following persons were unanimously elected:

TRAILS AND SHELTERS

A successful workshop was held at Rawsonville, the first weekend of May. Representatives from Trails and Shelters, Long Trail Patrol, Caretakers, National Forest, State Forest and Parks, and the various Sections were in attendance. The agenda was varied covering subjects such as trail standardization, water supplies, outhouses, dump removals, trail use and problems in general. Many individuals spoke or gave practical demonstrations on such things as sign making, blazing, use of various tools used in trail clearing, water bar construction and so forth. It was felt to be a very worthwhile weekend and it is hoped that similar workshops will be held in the future. Excellent meals were prepared for the occasion by Al Mead of the Connecticut section.

The summer program seems to be going well. The majority of the dumps have been removed and trail and shelter maintenance seems to be well in hand. This summer we have caretakers at the following locations: Stratton Pond (2), Montclair Glen, Gorham, Bolton, Taylor, Butler, Taft, Buchanan, also possibilities of a caretaker covering Killington-Pico area plus a roving patrol man covering the first twenty miles of the Trail. In addition, we have four men on the Long Trail Patrol. Indications to date are that the above are well worthwhile in the services and information they provide to hikers on the Trail.

DANE H. SHORTSLEEVE
Chairman of Trails and Shelters

CLARENDON REMODELED

BEFORE

Clarendon Lodge

In recent years the maintenance of Clarendon Lodge has become increasingly difficult due primarily to misuse. After much deliberation the Killington Section decided to remodel from the closed lodge to an open shelter. On May 15 some 40 people were present to do the carpentering, pick up an entire pick-up load of trash (all accumulated since last September, clear up the remains of one of the twin pines right in front of the shelter which some "kids" had cut down with a hand-axe (it was about 50 years old), fell the other twin since "kids" had started hacking into it in such a way that it would fall directly ON the shelter, and build a new privy using lumber taken from the front of the lodge. The fence around the shelter is to keep out the cows in summer; there will be a gate at the opening.
   The 40 people included some 18 students from the Rochester High School, who made countless trips down to the road with bags of trash, patched the roof, helped with carpentering, cleaned up the pine debris and many other jobs. The did a tremendous job!

AFTER

Clarendon Shelter

The Trail north of Route 103 was re-routed to avoid passing right by a private cabin that has been subjected to considerable vandalism in recent years. The Trail goes east on Route 103 for about 200 yards, then across the highway on to an old (not maintained) town road, which leads directly to Clarendon Shelter.
   The Killington Section took this drastic action in hopes of making the shelter a little less hospitable, hoping in turn that fewer "hippies" and drug addicts will make use of it as a semi-permanent abode. Also, that fewer people from the area will use it as a walk-in or drive-in picnic grounds, including those on snowmobiles and trail bikes.

HERBERT OGDEN, Shelter Chmn.
Killington Section

BACKPACKER'S TIPS

Occasionally GMCers write in about a new product that they have discovered to enhance their outdoor pleasure or they may react to an item in the News, relating personal experiences. The sharing of these tips should help the novice backpacker and also provide 'food-for-thought' for the experienced backpacker.

The Bubble-Pad

After a three month study of air mattresses and foam pads, Tom Martin is convinced that the Bubble-Pad is the lightest possible sleeping comfort on the market. A complex mat with hundreds of air tight bubbles in between two sturdy sheets of polyethylene, the Bubble-Pad is capable of withstanding any reasonable human weight and will raise the body ½ inch from the ground. Although no dimensions are mentioned the Bubble-Pad weighs only 5 ounces and rolls into a tiny cylinder. The Bubble-Pad is available through Sport Specialties, P.O. Box 2254, El Modena, California, for under five dollars.

The Unbeatable Canned Heat

From meeting with backpackers innumerable during my 1,000-odd miles of Long Trail and Appalachian Trail hiking in recent years, I am convinced that few people realize the amazing superiority of the well-known "canned heat" as a hiker's cookstove. With the tiny tripod stove weighing just 74 grams, and each aluminum can of fuel (small size costing about 23¢ today) weighing just 90 grams, I will match it against any other backpacker's favorite for efficiency-weight per heating capacity, at lowest cost, too. One small can will serve for two hot meals, for two people; or, a single backpacker will use fewer than three cans per week! Hot cereal and hot drink for breakfast; hot soup and hot drink and hot pudding for supper. Beat it if you can! And of course it requires no wood chopping, heavy axe to tote, heavy stove or fuel reservoir. A piece of cardboard can serve as a windscreen, and the alcohol flame is wind and rain resistant. It will usually boil water in the Green Mountains before a fellow backpacker can set up, fuel up, prime, and possibly clean out, his complex, noisy, and heavy Primus or Svea investment. The familiar brand of canned heat (Sterno) is available at most large drugstores or supermarkets.
   Am about to set out on another 200 miles of Appalachian and Long Trail with the unbeatable canned heat!

JOHN T. COWLES
Life Member, GMC

BRATTLEBORO SECTION

Since our annual meeting last February, the Brattleboro Section has had a sugar party at the old All Souls Parish House in Brattleboro in April with 25 members present. After enjoying maple sugar, doughnuts, pickles and coffee served by Roger and Ann Davis and committee, the group viewed the Green Mountain Club slide collection. Then, Louise Mullen, a former member gave a talk about the birds of the area. Louise writes a bird column in the Brattleboro Reformer and she really had a lot to tell us about our birds.

In May, several groups got out on the trail after the snow finally left. The trail has been cleared from Bourn Pond north to Bromley Mountain. However, the section being relocated has yet to be paint blazed. Repairs were made at Swezey camp and Swezey shelter and an old dead tree over-hanging Swezey camp was taken down by Brandon Douglas and his crew. One member of the work party, Bill Hawkins, fell into Bourn Brook when rotten timbers of the high bridge gave way. The bridge was lowered to a safer level until another work crew can get up there to build another high bridge.

Norman La Moria took a new stove and stove pipe into Bromley Camp and set it up in early May.

Charlie Smith, who recently returned from several years stay in Hawaii, cleared trail from Spruce Peak to Prospect Rock and hiked on further visiting his old haunts. He found a big beaver pond south of Bourn Pond which covered the trail in that area and made him take a 2 mile detour.

All workers noted motor bike activity on the trail.

The Sunday of Memorial day Weekend, Frank Dearborn and family enjoyed beautiful weather on a trip in the Little Rock area. Other members of the club who planned to hike in to Little Rock Pond remained home because of heavy rain in Brattleboro. Many members, however, had conflicting engagements.

One evening in June, Frank Dearborn talked to a 4H group in Jacksonville about hiking the trail.

The next scheduled event is the Intersectional in August.

Recently, some members have been receiving cards from Don Kent and his wife (Don's Brother's widow) (they were married March 17, 1971) who were visiting southern Ireland. They had a wonderful trip.

LOUISE DUNKLEE, Reporter


BURLINGTON SECTION

Burlington section members have been "doing their thing" in a variety of ways. An April Earth Day exhibit under the supervision of Kenn Boyd, Newell Briggs and Shirley Strong presented information about the GMC and the Long Trail. A newsletter, Ridgelines, prepared by the publicity committee, was mailed to all members. Work on the hiker survey of Mt. Mansfield continues under the direction of Chuck Hadden. Responding to public demand, Elizabeth Myers presented the new GMC slide show to an audience of about 30.

In recognition of her many contributions to conservation, Shirley Strong received the Governor's Award in June. A lean-to shelter will be dedicated in her name on the Conservation Society of Southern Vermont Trail.

Recent work parties have concentrated on trail maintenance and dump removal. Work has been done on the Long Trail in the Mansfield and Jonesville-Bolton areas. Dumps at Bolton, Buchanan and Taylor Lodges have been removed. Perhaps someday, someone will sit down, do some figuring and come up with one of those mind-boggling figures like: all the trash removed from the Long Trail shelters, if stretched end to end, would reach from Journey's End to the garbage scows of New York City!

SALLY S. SPEAR, Publicity Chairman


MONTPELIER SECTION

Our annual meeting in April had about forty members present. Officers elected were President---Sally Sairs, Treasurer---David Hine, Secretary---Charlotte Langer. Our spring trips were spent mostly canoeing, as snow did not leave the upper trails until very late in the season. These trips included one in the Dead Creek area, moderate white water canoeing on the Mad River, and one on the Lamoille River, rescheduled from the Winooski River as that became too low and too polluted.

The section aided in Green-Up Day around Montpelier. On May 15, deep snow kept us in the low country, so to speak, as we worked on the section of the Trail south of Bolton Lodge. Several families hiked Scragg Mountain in Northfield following the older, overgrown trail from the east. A week later, we worked on cleaning up around Sterling Pond Lodge, removing as much of the dumps as could fit in a scout and trailer. Work was also done on the Long Trail in that area and on the Elephant's Head Trail.

CHARLOTTE LANGER, Reporter

Trail Conf. of leaving no rubbish heaps, carry in carry out, has been very successful in our area. Each year since its inception, Jack's party has found the Fingerboard Shelter in better condition than the year before, in fact, the 11 bags this year were gathered from back in the woods mostly, where people had camped at spots other than at the Shelter. He finds definitely that the cleaner they find an area the cleaner they leave it. Two trail clearing trips are planned for fall, one at High Point Oct. 24, one the annual standard trip on the Harriman Trail. All trips have been for one day, not the weekend. About half the group have been regulars and half different people each time, new people. He has several new people on the Trail Comm. Two more people have taken over sections of trail on their own. Already Bob Schultz has been caring for the Lichen Trail in the Harriman, the Coriell family from Brink Rd. to Rattlesnake Hill in the Kittatinies. Now Bea Van Orden has taken responsibility for the Hurst Trail and part of the Long Path and Louise Kaiser the Rd. from Times Sq. to the Fingerboard Shelter. Beyond this, Louise has for years been maintaining on her own the Red Cross Trail from 7 Lakes Pkwy. to the Tiorati Brook Rd.
