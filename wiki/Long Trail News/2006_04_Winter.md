GRE E N      MOUNTAIN             CLUB           2006       ANNUAL          REPORT




                     inety-six years ago, a group of twenty-three Vermonters led by James
                     P. Taylor met in Burlington to found the Green Mountain Club
                     (GMC). Since achieving the goal of building a footpath along the crest
                     of the Green Mountains, the club has maintained, managed and pro-
                     tected the Long Trail. Through the Great Depression, through times
of war, through the backpacking boom of the 60's and ?O's, through technology changes
and national crises, GMC has staved off threats to the trail and the Green Mountains,
maintaining a place where hikers can escape the stresses of modern life.
  Today we carry on that tradition. The Green Mountain Club's fiscal year 2006 featured
some great achievements.
  On June 11, 2005, the club's annual meeting at Smugglers' Notch Resort featured the
grand opening of a new bridge across the Lamoille River, dedicated to Senator Robert
Gannett and his wife, Aldie. In September, the white blazes were moved off Route 15
and the Lamoille Valley Long Trail relocation officially opened, culminating more than a
decade of effort.
  A new Goddard Shelter was built and the Glastenbury Mountain firetower was reno-
vated. Sterling Pond Shelter was replaced. The club published new guidebooks to snow-
shoe hikes and fire tower hikes in Vermont. We launched e-Blaze, an electronic news
bulletin, and revamped GMC's website. New stoves were donated and installed at the
Wheeler Pond Camps.
  Showing how much the Long Trail has woven itself into the hearts of Vermonters, the
Governor and state legislature came through once again with a capital appropriation to
assist with future land acquisitions. The countdown to a permanently-secured route for
the trail continues. A n:ajor land acquisition project during the year secured the north-
ernmost mile of the Long Trail, up to the Canadian border.
  Andrew S. Nuquist completed his third and final year as GMC President. Andrew did a
great job. Under his leadership, the Second Century Campaign began, which will secure
the future of the trail and GMC in their next century. During fiscal year 2006, in the "quiet
phase" of the campaign, five private six-figure gifts were pledged. Public announcement
of the campaign is anticipated in the first half of 2007.
  Thank you to the thousands of members, hundreds of volunteers, federal and state
agency partners, private landowners, neighbors, and countless friends who pitch in to
keep Vermont's hiking trails open, clean, safe, and welcoming to all.

Sincerely,




  Ben Rose, Executive Director                               Richard Windish, President
