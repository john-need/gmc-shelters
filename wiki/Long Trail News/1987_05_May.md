        REVIEW
Harold A. Meeks, Vermont's Land and
Resources. Shelburne, Vt.: New England
Press, 1986. Pp. 332, illus., maps. $11.95
paper.

   This book by Universi ty of Verm ont
geography professor Harold Meeks is a com-
panion volume to his Time and Change in Ver-
mont: A Human Geography (1986). To quote
from the introduction, the author discusses the
state's physical features, "not only how they
came to be ... but also how they are used." It
is a book that should appeal to GMC'ers who
want to read the Vermont landscape as well
as the trail signs.
   Briefly put, Professor Meeks deals with
the state's geology, natural resources,
meteorology, energy production, land use,
and environmental legislation. His helpful
summary of Act 250, Vermont's revolutionary
1970 land use law, as well as a chapter on elec-
tric power needs and policies, make it a topical
book.
   There is much of interest here about moun-
tains. The Taconic range slid or was pushed
off the Green Mountains years ago, and some
think it may be upside down! Camel's Hump
is unusual in that its sharp drop-off is to the
south, a result of the quarrying action of the
glacier on the lee side. Killington averages 200
inches of snow a year, compared to Mount
Mansfield's 150, both impressive amounts
caused by orographic uplift or air forced up
the mountain side, which then cools off, con-
denses, and becomes precipitation.
