THE GREEN MOUNTAIN NEWS
Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.
Brandon, Vermont, April, 1925

Annual Meeting.

The annual meeting of the Club was held at the Hotel Berwick, Rutland, Vt., Saturday, January 10, 1925, with an attendance of about 40 members. The usual business was transacted. Reports from the sections showed them to be in a flourishing condition. The New York section reported an expenditure of $870 on its part of the Long Trail (the Monroe Skyline), and that 40 of its members had tramped over that part of the trail during the season. The trail committee reported progress on the trail during 1924, as stated in our December issue, and predicted that it would be put through to the Canada line this year.

The former board of trustees was re-elected, viz., Will S. Monroe, Montclair, N. J.; Walter Mueller, New York; Fred H. Tucker, Boston; Herbert M. Dockham, Holden, Mass.; J. L. Griswold and G. A. Mathers, Bennington; C. P. Cooper, C. J. Cleveland and W. M. Ross, Rutland; Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; E. S. Marsh, Brandon; Profs. P. C. Voter and R. H. White, Middlebury; Capt. H. W. Congdon, Arlington; Jas. P. Taylor and T. S. Dean, Burlington. W. M. Ross of Rutland was elected clerk.

The treasurer's report showed receipts from annual dues of $950; life members, $250; guide books, $256.62; advertising in guide books, $214; gifts, $89.15; clubhouse, $550; sections, $200.25; interest, $92.73; cash from last year, $990.27. Disbursements: Various items of expense for clubhouse, $714.56; Associated Mountaineering Clubs, $15; N. E. Trail Conference, $20; printing and stationery, $46.25; trail committee, $344.81; guide book, $429; clubhouse addition, $550; bond purchased, $95.85; tablet for clubhouse, $64; Green Mountain News, $62.72; officers' expenses and sundries, $201.40; leaving a balance of cash on hand in banks of $1,009.14.

Investments were Liberty bonds at par, $1450; German bond, $100, and U. S. savings certificate, $86.80.

The membership, as reported to the treasurer, was, at large, 468; Bennington section, 45; Burlington, 99; Middlebury, 87; Montpelier, 24; New York, 328; Proctor, 25; Rutland, 400; Worcester, 69. Total, 1546.

Amendments to the by-laws were carried raising the annual dues to $3, and life membership to $50 for members at large. Sections are left to regulate their dues as they see fit from $2 up.

A committee on forestry, to cooperate with the state forestry department, was elected, consisting of Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; Prof. R. H. White and J. J. Fritz, Middlebury; B. F. Smith and C. P. Cooper of Rutland.

State Forester Ross and his assistant, Mr. Merrill, were present, and addressed the meeting on forestry and kindred topics.

In the evening there was a public meeting at the high school, when H. A. Reynolds of the Massachusetts state forestry commission spoke on town forests, and Prof. E. P. Jackson of Middlebury College gave an illustrated talk on the Windy River mountains in Wyoming.

Annual Meeting of The Trustees.

The trustees held their annual meeting at the clubhouse on Saturday, March 14. Twelve of the 16 trustees were present, and two others sent representatives, the only absentees not represented being Mr. Dockham of Worcester, Mass., and Mr. Dean of Burlington.

Officers were elected as follows: President, C. P. Cooper, Rutland; vice president, Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; treasurer, C. J. The Clubhouse.

An additional room, to serve as an office, has been built, which was much needed, and will add greatly to the efficiency of management and the convenience of guests. The cost of this was estimated at $550, and that amount was appropriated for it and paid from the earnings of the house, but the actual cost was something over $1000, the additional amount having been paid from the club treasury, since the report of the treasurer at the January meeting. Mr. and Mrs. Charles H. Lester have been re-engaged as steward and housekeeper, and Miss Elizabeth Farmer has been engaged as hostess and office manager. Our generous benefactor, Mortimer R. Proctor, has offered to build two cottages for use of families or others who desire to remain several days or a week or two, and the offer has been thankfully accepted. It is expected that these cottages will be ready for use before the end of this season.

Guest cards will be printed for the use of members who wish to extend the privileges of the house to friends, and may be obtained from secretaries of the sections, and from the clerk and treasurer of the Club.

The house will open May 1, when it is expected that the new office furniture will be in place, and everything ready for the comfort and convenience of guests. It is hoped that any deficiencies in the management which may have existed last season will hereafter be remedied.

Some changes have been made in rates, etc. A folder giving rates, regulations, etc., will be issued, and can be had on application to the chairman of the house committee, Wallace M. Fay, Proctor, Vt. We understand that it is planned to serve two or more club breakfasts at different prices.

The road from the east to the clubhouse will be rebuilt the coming summer, and while it is said a passable way will be kept open during the work, we recommend that during operations the house be approached from the west, via Rutland.

An Approach Trail.

Guy Du Val, of Brooklyn, N. Y., an ardent Long Trailer, who makes his headquarters in Brandon when he comes to Vermont, objects to walking nine miles over a dusty highway to reach the trail, but still prefers walking to automobiling. He has hit upon a plan to solve the difficulty, viz., an approach trail, to start about two miles east of Brandon village, following a picturesque old wood road to a point about a mile and a half from the Long Trail, thence a new trail thru the woods connecting with the main trail where it crosses the Brandon-Rochester road. Thus the hiker will reach the trail by a pleasant walk of about seven miles thru the woods.

Manager Moore of the Brandon Inn says he will deliver passengers at the starting point of the new trail at a cost not to exceed 50c each. It is probable that this trail will be completed early in the present season. It will be, we believe, the first trail connecting a railroad town with the Long Trail.

The Trail, The Skyline Trail.
Air: "Maryland, My Maryland"

Written for the New York Section of the
Green Mountain Club by Professor
Amos W. Farnham.

We leave the quiet haunts of men,
  And take the trail, the Skyline Trail;
The Couching Lion in his den,
  Upon the trail, the Skyline Trail,
The trail Monroe and Cowles, I ween,
With Woodruff, Allis, Theron Dean,
And others built on Mountains Green,
  The long, long trail, the Skyline Trail.

'Neath frowning cliff, o'er chasm deep, we keep
  The trail, the Skyline Trail;
'Cross narrow bench we almost creep
  To keep the trail, the Skyline Trail
But as it twists from ledge to ledge,
With stunted growth of spruce and sedge,
"It recreates," we all allege,
  So like the trail, the Skyline Trail.

Burnt Mountain with its rocky dome
  Extends the trail, the Skyline Trail;
Through its ravines spring torrents foam
  Below the trail, the Skyline Trail.
On Skidmore's slope abrupt and steep,
Pedestrians and Flora must keep
Speed regulations, though they weep,
  Upon the trail, the Skyline Trail.

Dean Panorama comes in sight
  Upon the trail, the Skyline Trail,
With views inspiring day and night,
  Along the trail, the Skyline Trail.
These stretch away to farthest bound,
The most inspiring to be found,
And fill the soul with thoughts profound,
  Upon the trail, the Skyline Trail.

We catch the spirit and the joys,
  When on the trail, the Skyline Trail,
Of victories of Green Mountain boys
  Who hiked in arms the mountain trail;
Of General Stark of iron will
At Bennington and Bunker Hill;
Of Ethan Allen, living still
  In hearts who hike the Skyline Trail.

Glen Ellen is our present goal
  Reached by the trail, the Skyline Trail;
Our admiration is the toll
  She takes from all who hike the trail.
The Lincoln group will next extend
The trail, and other scenes will lend;
So here mid-summer days we'll spend
  Upon the trail, the Skyline Trail.

Notes.

President Cooper attended the New England Trail Conference at Boston, Jan. 23 and 24, and presided as Chairman. He also attended the Appalachian Trail Conference in Washington, March 2 and 3.

W. M. Ross, Rutland, chairman of the trail committee, has on hand a supply of wooden trail arrows, in two sizes. Application should be made to him for these, if any are needed by the sections.

The Duncan Camp of Boy Building has a camp called "Camp Duncan" in Peru, not far from the Long Trail, and extends a cordial invitation to hikers over the trail to visit the camp and enjoy its hospitality.

The general passenger department of the Rutland Railroad is preparing a summer booklet for distribution among its patrons, describing attractions in Vermont for summer visitors. Several pages will be devoted to the Long Trail, including a map of same. Mention will also be made of the clubhouse and shelter huts.

Book note. "Skiing, with a chapter on Snowshoeing." By Ornulf Poulsen. New York, The MacMillan Co., 1924. Illustrated. This book, by a Norwegian authority, aims to present the subject of ski-sport in all its branches. Includes a clear and concise explanation of the technique of skiing.

The clubhouse will open for the season May 1, and May 2 a shore dinner will be served at noon and at 6 p.m. Other meals as usual.

Spring cleaning has begun on the Long Trail. Chairman Ross of the trail committee and party have been over the section from Clarendon camp to Wallingford Gulf and put it in good condition, including camps. Other sections will be covered as soon as time and snow conditions will permit.

April 13 a belated snow storm deposited 12 inches of snow in the vicinity of the clubhouse. The autobus from Rutland to Montpelier, on its first trip, struggled as far as the clubhouse, then gave it up and returned to Rutland.

We should be glad to receive items of news from the Sections. Such items sent to the editor before Dec. 1 next will appear in the next issue.
