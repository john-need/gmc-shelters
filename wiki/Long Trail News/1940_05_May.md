                               THE COOPER MEMORIAL
       Of all the early leaders of the Green Mountain Club, none was more loyal,
    energetic or unselfish than Charles P. Cooper, and his successors are glad
    indeed that a suitable memorial to him has been erected on Killington
    Peak. The proposal to build a "Cooper Lodge" was made several years ago,
    but the plans were slow in taking shape until Mr. Merrill, the State Forester,
    offered the services of C.C.C. labor, under state supervision, to do the work.
    This required that it should be erected on state land, so Mr. Mortimer Proctor,
    with customary generosity, deeded a parcel of land to the state, near the
    site of the old hotel on Killington, and on it the completed lodge now stands.
    The dedication ceremonies, plans for which are not yet complete, will be
    held on Sunday, June 2nd, when the bronze plaque, shown in the accom-
    panying cut, will be unveiled. It is hoped that all who attend the Annual Meeting
    on Saturday will plan to stay over for the dedication, and thus show their respect
    for a true-hearted gentleman, who loved the woods and the Green Mountains.
