                                att Larson became serious about photography
                                while working as a GMC caretaker on Mount
                                Mansfield ten to twelve years ago. Being outdoors
                    gave him many opportunities to take photos, and he bought
                    his first good camera - a digital one. It was the beginning of
                    the digital revolution, and he was fortunate to learn the new
                    technology early.
                        Since that time, many of Matt's outstanding photos of
                    nature, the Green Mountains, and hikers have graced the
                    cover and interior of the Long Trail News and other GMC
                    publications. For several years, as Communications Assis-
                     tant, Matt helped produce the LTN, including doing most of
the photography and photo selection (see "Matt Larson Departs," fall 2012 LTN).
    Matt had long been interested in art, and was a fine arts major in college,
but that interest was on the back burner until he picked up a camera. Photog-
raphy has allowed him to express his artistic side. "I enjoy the whole process,
not just the end result," says Matt. "Photography makes my outdoor experience
deeper - almost Zen-like. It holds you in the moment and erases other thoughts
from your mind ." Matt's favorite subject matter is high-elevation Vermont, par-
ticularly its montane spruce-fir forests: "I started taking photos of spruce-fir
forests because of their beauty, and I like the expansive feeling of being high in
the mountains. But I've come to realize that they are one of our most threatened
ecosystems (due to climate change and related causes]. Every time I'm out there
now, I wonder if my photos will be looked at as documentary photographs hun-
dreds of years from now - so it's bittersweet. I hope to make people think about
this through my photos and make better choices."
    Matt has recently gotten into black and white photography. His favorite
photographers are masters of black and white from the early to mid 1900s -
Ansel Adams, Edward Weston, Paul Strand. Since he enjoys working with his
hands, Matt has been exploring printing his photos using some of the older pro-
cesses such as platinum, cyanotype, and kallitype printing from the nineteenth
and twentieth centuries. These prints have different color tones and a softer
look than those printed by the silver process that became predominant in the
early 1900s. He also prints on different papers - "the possibilities are endless,"
says Matt.
    When asked to relate a memorable photography experience, Matt recalls, ''A
couple of summers ago, I left my house around 3:30 A.M. and hiked up Camel's
Hump for the sunrise. I set up just as it was getting light. I expected to be alone
up there, but I turned around and saw Tom Anderson clicking away. [See "The
Photographs of Tom Anderson," summer 2012 LTN.] I realized I'm not the only
one crazy enough to get up that early! Tom and I still laugh about it."
                                                                   -SUSAN SHEA
