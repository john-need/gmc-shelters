THE LONG TRAIL NEWS

Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.

Brandon, Vermont, April, 1927

Annual Meeting.

The annual meeting of the Club was held at the Hotel Berwick in Rutland on Saturday, January 8, forenoon and afternoon, with an attendance of about fifty, including a larger number of women than have ever appeared at a meeting before. The members lunched together at the hotel.

Trustees to serve for one year were elected as follows: Mortimer R. Proctor, Wallace M. Fay, Proctor; Raymond H. Torrey, J. Ashton Allis, New York; Profs. R. H. White and P. O. Voter, Middlebury; Robert M. Ross, Montpelier; Prof. L. B. Puffer, Miss Mabel Brownell, Burlington; Miss Edith Estabrook, Boston; Arthur H. Tucker, Milton, Mass.; George E. F. Storey, Worcester, Mass.; Prof. W. C. O'Kane, Durham, N. H.; C. J. Cleveland, W. M. Ross, Rutland.

Of these Messrs. Torrey and Puffer are new to the board, the others were re-elected.

Reports from the sections were read, setting forth their activities on the trail and in other directions during the year.

Report of officers were read:

W. M. Ross, chairman of the trail committee, reported an expenditure of nearly $1100 on the trail during the year, of which nearly $600 was contributed by the New York section and expended on the Monroe Skyline trail, under the supervision of the trail committee.

C. J. Cleveland, treasurer, reported assets of cash and bonds in the treasury amounting to $3209.59, a gain of $535.68. Receipts aggregated $2148.10, including dues from members at large, $1301, dues from sections, $276.41, advertising in guide book, $201, sales of ditto, $186.06, gifts, $73.48, interest, $110.15. Disbursements: Printing, stationery and supplies, $256.09, officers, services and expenses, $185.60, trails and shelters, $500, guide book, pamphlets, etc., $510.63, Burlington section for Taylor lodge, $50, W. M. Ross, expenses to Trails Conference meeting, $28.90, books, $7.90, meals served to trustees, $31.25. Total, $1570.37. Cash in banks, $1567.99.

W. M. Fay, chairman of the house committee of the Lodge, reported the best season it has ever had, with a profit of $1015, which will be sufficient to carry it thru the winter, provide wood, ice, &c., and leave something over with which to start the next season.

Miss Lula M. Tye, secretary, reported a total membership of 1537, a gain of 194. They are divided as follows: At large, 459; New York, 351; Rutland, 300; Burlington, 171; Bennington, 51; Middlebury, 124; Proctor, 42; Worcester, 41. She also reported that some 10,000 pamphlets and leaflets, advertising the Long Trail and the state, had been distributed during the year.

An offer of a gift of $500 from Miss Florence Siverston of Chicago to provide for a cottage near the Lodge was accepted with thanks, as was an offer from Miss Aurelia S. Harwood of California to improve the camp at Hell Hollow, near Bennington, and to present a life membership to the Club.

An offer was received from John Barrett of Grafton to give prizes of $15 and $10 for the best two essays written by Vermont high school students on the subject, "Why should Young Vermont be interested in the Long Trail?"

Suggestions for patrolling the trail, for increasing the membership, for a campaign of publicity to advertise the trail and Vermont, and for placing caretakers with supplies in more of the camps, were discussed. No action was taken, except to refer them to the trustees for consideration.

The meeting was animated and interesting.

Sunday a party who stayed over, with some of the local members, visited the Long Trail Lodge and enjoyed some snowshoeing in the woods, on about two feet of snow.

Annual Meeting of the Trustees.

This was held Saturday, March 12, at the Berwick Hotel, Rutland. The following officers were elected: President, Mortimer R. Proctor; vice president, Prof. R. H. White; clerk, W. M. Ross; treasurer, C. J. Cleveland; office secretary, Miss Lula M. Tye; editor Long Trail News, E. S. Marsh; auditor, J. A. Allis.

The following chairmen of committees were elected: Trails and shelters, W. M. Ross; publicity, Walter C. O'Kane; membership, George F. E. Storey; nomenclature, R. M. Ross; Long Trail Lodge house committee, W. M. Fay.

There was considerable discussion of plans for a publicity campaign, in connection with a membership drive. It was decided to carry on such a campaign with vigor, and one plan adopted was to communicate with each member of Vermont clubs in other states, and invite them to join the Club. Another suggestion which was approved was that several of the members write articles descriptive of different parts of the Long Trail, and about the Club, Lodge, etc., and have these syndicated in the newspapers.

Trail trampers who have complaints to make will know from the above statement who is responsible for each section of the trail, and can act accordingly.

The Long Trail now ends at Blackinton, Mass., about three miles south of the state line, instead of at North Adams. From there it continues as the Appalachian trail over Greylock. A new camp is planned to be built a little north of the state line, in co-operation with the Williams College Outing Club.

The management of the Inn at Wallingford will build a side trail from that village to White Rocks on the Long Trail, which will be about three miles long, and is described as a short but sporty trail.

It is likely that the Du Val trail at Brandon will be extended to more than double its present length, but plans are not yet sufficiently matured to warrant a more definite statement.

The Long Trail being now completed, the energies of the Club will be directed toward keeping it in condition, and building side trails, tho doubtless some changes in the course of the main trail will be made. New cabins will no doubt be built here and there, especially in the newly finished trail at the north.

Vermont's Long Trail from the north to the south of the state is one of the state's best assets. The Long Trail is being used by an increasing number of people each summer, and its charm is appealing to all those who traverse its various sectors. In sponsoring the Long Trail, the Green Mountain Club is doing an important work for Vermont along the line of attracting new people to the state.

—Barre, Vt., Times.

The Coolidge Range.

The Vermont legislature has passed a bill enacting that that part of the Green Mountain range which includes Saltash Mountain in the town of Plymouth, Shrewsbury Mountain, Killington Peak and Pico Peak, shall hereafter be known as "The Coolidge Range", in honor of President Coolidge, who was born in the town of Plymouth, within sight of all of these mountains.

Killington is second in height only to Mt. Mansfield of the Vermont mountains, having an elevation of 4241 feet. Pico is 3967 feet high, Shrewsbury 3737 feet and Saltash considerably less. The text of the bill follows:

"That part of the range of the Green Mountains extending from Salt Ash Mountain in the town of Plymouth northerly to Pico Peak in the town of Sherburne, shall hereafter be named and designated as 'The Coolidge Range.'"

The New York Mixup.

In the December issue of the News the statement was made that "the New York section were unable to care for their part of the trail, as they have done heretofore, and the trail committee have taken charge of it." Also "It was reported" (at a special meeting of the trustees) "that the trail had been put in excellent condition with the exception of the Monroe Skyline, which had not received as much attention as usual." We had no personal knowledge of conditions on that part of the trail, but made the statements on information received from the trail committee, and from what we have learned from other sources subsequently, we are satisfied that the above statements were true. What the trail committee failed to tell us, however, and what we should certainly have stated if we had known of it, was the important fact that the New York section had contributed funds to the extent of $600 to be used on their part of the trail, and nearly all of this sum was expended there.
