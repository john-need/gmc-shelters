BIRTH OF THE GREEN
MOUNTAIN CLUB

(From The Burlington, Vermont Free Press
     of Saturday, March 12, 1910)

Green Mountain Club
New State Organization with Patronage of
      Governor and Many Others.

   Under the name of the "Green Mountain
 Club" an organization was formed yester-
 day afternoon at a meeting at the Van Ness
House, which will undertake to promote in-
 terest in mountain climbing in this state.
Many of the most prominent men in Ver-
mont are interested and the initial opera-
tions of the club are well provided for by a
gift of $100. from M. E. Wheeler, a business
 man of Rutland.

   Besides a regular board of officers a set
of councillors was appointed, having a mem-
ber from each county in the State, which
will make future plans for the operations
of the club and stimulate growth and in-
terest by counties. The club begins with
a membership of about 25. A constitution
was adopted.

   The officers of the club are:—
   President, J. P. Taylor, of Saxtons River,
vice president, Judge Seneca Haselton, of
Burlington, secretary, E. L. Ingalls, of Hart-
ford, treasurer, C. W. Brownell, of Burling-
ton.

   Mr. Taylor was instrumental in founding
the club and is one of its most enthusiastic
supporters.

   The board of councillors is as follows:
Chittenden County, John E. Allen of West-
    ford.
Addison County, Superintendent A. W.
    Eddy of Bristol.

    chester.

    Danville.

    Wright of St. Albans.

    Motte.

Original Members Of The Green
         Mountain Club.

   The Burlington Free Press, in its issue of
March 9 last, carried a descriptive and his-
torical sketch of the Club, illustrated by a
large cut of one of the roadside Trail signs.
We are indebted to this article for the
names of the 22 men who attended the
original organization meeting at Burling-
ton, March 11, 1910, and who became the
original members and founders of the Club.
They were the following:

   James P. Taylor, Saxtons River; M. E.
Wheeler, Rutland; Rev. W. H. Spence, Rut-
land; Frank H. Clark, Windsor; O. K. Hol-
lister, Barre; H. G. Thomas, Stowe; Judge
Seneca Haselton, Burlington; Elwin L. In-
galls, Hartford; Carroll H. Drown, Wal-
lingford; Arthur W. Eddy, Bristol; C. W.
Brownell, Burlington; J. L. Southwick, Bur-
lington; H. G. Whitehill, Waterbury; Prof.
K. R. B. Flint, Northfield; J. E. Tracy,
Burlington; M. D. Chittenden, Burlington;
Mason S. Stone, Montpelier; Clarence P.
Cowles, Burlington; Edward K. Allen, Bur-
lington; C. H. Morrill, Randolph; D. T.
Page, Bakersfield; C. W. Gates, Franklin.

   Of the above named original members, the
following are still members:
   James P. Taylor, Clarence P. Cowles,
Prof. K. R. B. Flint.

The Acorn And The Oak.

   When the Club was started in March,
1910, with 22 members, not even the most
enthusiastic of them could have visualized
its progress in the coming 21 years of its
minority. Then it had nothing but faith
and enthusiasm. Now it has over 1500 loyal
members, including seven live Sections, two
located outside of the State, the trunk line
Trail completed, 260 miles, from Massa-
chusetts to Canada, a unique and beautiful
mountain clubhouse, representing with its
cottages and out-buildings and furnishings,
an investment of over $40,000, 51 trail
cabins, numerous side trails, an all season
patrol system inaugurated, and a perman-

   Leland H. Fish, secretary-treasurer of the
Killington Section, recently broadcast a five
minute talk thru the Rutland radio station
on the G. M. C. He packed a surprising
amount of history and up-to-date informa-
tion into his brief talk.
   Since our last issue went to press the
treasurer of the Killington Section at Rut-
land has reported a membership of 251, and
paid the per capita tax to the Club treas-
urer. This makes the total membership of
the Club 1503, not including life members of
Sections.
  The Appalachian Trail, to be some 1300
miles long, from Maine to Georgia, lacks
only eight miles of being half completed,
and this stretch, on the crest of Virginia's
Blue Ridge mountains, will be completed
this Spring by the national forest service.
About half of the incomplete portion of the
Trail has been scouted.

and his successor, Senator Partridge, and
Mrs. Partridge, are members. We mention
this, not in a boastful spirit, but to show
that Vermonters of standing, who know the
work the Club is doing, are supporting it,
