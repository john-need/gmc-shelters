   Leland H. Fish, secretary-treasurer of the
Killington Section, recently broadcast a five
minute talk thru the Rutland radio station
on the G. M. C. He packed a surprising
amount of history and up-to-date informa-
tion into his brief talk.
   Since our last issue went to press the
treasurer of the Killington Section at Rut-
land has reported a membership of 251, and
paid the per capita tax to the Club treas-
urer. This makes the total membership of
the Club 1503, not including life members of
Sections.
  The Appalachian Trail, tcr be some 1300
miles long, from Maine to Georgia, lacks
only eight miles of being half completed,
and this stretch, on the crest of Virginia's
Blue Ridge mountains, will be completed
this Spring by the national forest service.
About half of the incomplete portion of the
Trail has been scouted.
    The late Senator Greene of Vermont had
been for many years a member of the Club,
and his successor, Senator Partridge, and
Mrs. Partridge, are members. We mention
this, not in a boastful spirit, but to show
that Vermonters of standing, who know the
work the Club is doing, are supporting it,
