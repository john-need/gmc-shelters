                         By R. A. v AN METER
     CHECK LIST is   a great convenience to anyone making up a trail
A    pack and most experienced hikers use them. This is a suggestion
as to their helpfulness , especially to those of limited experience.
   There is no thought that everything on the list should l:e taken
on each trip . It is rather a compound list of items considered es-
sential to trips of various lengths and for various purposes. Check-
ing each item .against the question, "Shall I need that?" insures
against leaving behind some such item as a first-aid ki.t or flashlight
that may be worth its weight in gold in an emergency far from
the nearest habitation.
   There is no such thing as a standard check list that is worth
anything . Each individual must build his own from his own ex-
perience. The one below is offered as a sample only. It is mine. I
have used it for years and have not changed it much lately. It is
entirely satisfactory but I'll not say a word in its defense , for no
one else could use it- please don't try. But if you have never used
a check list you will find it a great convenience if you develop one

    THE GLASTENBURY TO
    STRATTON SJ[TUATJ[ON
                      By Lours L. BIGELOW

T tonsection
   HE        of the Long Trail between the Brattleboro-Benning-
      highway and Stratton Mountain has seen many changes.
More are scheduled to take place.
   Originally, the Long Trail came up Hell Hollow from the south,
passed over Hager Hill to the Somerset Bridge, then northerly to
the Grout Job . Glastenbury Mt. was a side trip , reached by a fire
trail.

<!-- page: 7 -->
When the Somerset Dam was built about 1928 by the New
England Power Company, the water level was raised sufficiently
to make the trail wet, and unusable in many places. The situation
caused the trustees to consider running the Long Trail due north
from Hell Hollow, up over Glastenbury Mt., and finally coming
out on the Arlington-Wardsboro road at Black Branch .
   Willis Ross , chairman of the Trails and Shelters Committee, in
one of his persuasive moods convinced us that the Worcester Section
should undertake the job. We put it through, but it has never been
highly satisfactory because of the three-mile hike on the road from
Black Branch to Webster Shelter . And a highway is no place for a
mountain trail.
   So , after more nudging from Willis Ross , and the trustees , we
are making another relocation. The new trail will leave the old one
at Caughnawaga Shelter, swing northeast, slab the ridges, cross
the Deerfield about one and a half miles below Grout Job , skirt
the north side of Grout Pond, thence over the hill to meet the
trail again at Webster Shelter.
   This is wild country and we have had to put our most talented
and highest paid scouts in there to locate the route. When the
entire relocation will be completed no one knows-time, tires, and
gasoline are the determining factors .
   Instead of keeping to deep woods north of Caughnawaga, the
new trail will be more open with views of the Somerset Reservoir,
Mt. Monadnock , and Haystack Mt. Material for a cable bridge
across the Deerfield has been obtained. A new shelter will be
erected at Grout Pond, and will add another attraction to this
section of the trail.
   At present the only completed section is the trail that starts
from across the road opposite Webster Shelter and swings over
the hill to Grout Pond.

                    END IN SIGHT- BUT WHICH END?

   Widespread interest in the automatic trail cutting machine (See NEws,
May, '42) prompts the Guest Editor to give space to further correspondence
relative to its development. As we go to press no public demonstration has
been announced.

D EAR Loms:

   Just a line by way of further report on the Bigelow Automatic
Trail Cutter. (Formerly known as the B.T.C. and M.G.)
   I am having trouble with that electric eye, which is intended to
follow a line of blazes automatically. I am really proud . of that
attachment but must confess that it doesn' t work too well. I think
it is a little nearsighted. At ten yards the machine can't tell a blaze
from a billboard. It chased a spotted cow a mile and a half the
other day before I could get it turned around. However, an ordinary
pair of spectacles or perhaps a monocle should correct that.
    Aside from this minor indisposition the B.A.T.C. has withstood
the most grueling tests. I slipped it under a pile driver when the
engineer wasn't looking , to get unbiased and impartial treatment ,
and finall y ran it off the south side of Camel's Hump . When I got
out of the hospital I went over the machine with a powerful lens
and could find but two light scratches in the enamel. Aside from
this weakness in the paint job the outfit is up to your standards.
    I note your concern relative to the usefulness of the B.A.T.C. on
wet land. Let me assure you that this is no problem. I find that by
feeding in a large rock an inch at a time the machine will cut slices
of uniform thickness which are ideal for paving wet spots in the
trail. This , in itself, opens possibilities in trail maintenance hitherto
undreamed of.
    For crossing streams and small ponds I am working on a dynamite
tailpiece suggested by George. With a. flick of the wrist you set

    you think some kind of simple public ceremony would be appro-

ANNUAL MEETING REPORT
                       By WILLIS M. Ross

T HEwasregular annual meeting of the Green Mountain Club , Inc.,
         held at the Long Trail Lodge , May 30th , i942.
   In the absence of President Holden , in Washington on war work,
Vice-President Atwood presided in his usual happy manner.
   BTlaws due for amendment were conscientiously considered and
voted one by one. On the motion of Mortimer Proctor, it was voted
that a new edition of the By-laws be mailed with the August issue
of the NEws.

<!-- page: 12 -->
Hawley reported for the Nominating Committee the following
slate of new trustees : Raymond H. White , Alexander Burkhard,
Albert L. Wiley, Perry H. Merrill , and Louis B. Puffer. They were
duly elected. David Paine asked for his release as trustee and John
Banker was nominated and elected to serve out his term.
    On the suggestion of Allis for obtaining new members-at-large
and for establishing new sections , the following were appointed a
committee for the purpose: Dorney, Merrill , Mould, Buchanan,
Vondell , and Crane .
    The Treasurer made a report, which was accepted, announcing
that the regular payment on the new debt had been made and $soo
paid on the old. Atwood reported for the Committee on awards.
Twenty-three persons have already applied for the award to be
made to those who have completed the entire length of the Trail.
The choice of an emblem was left to the Committee.
    A special hour had been set aside at this time for the purpose of
doing honor to Jim Taylor , the Father of the Green Mountain Club ,
and to the other Founders who were present at the first meeting of
the organization at Burlington, March iith, i910. Invitations had
been extended to all who were at the first meeting of the Club ,
and could be located. Professor K . R . B. Flint of Norwich Univer-
sity was the only one of the original members who was present.
Called upon to play his part, he reviewed that first meeting and
gave many characteristics of those who founded the Club as far as
he knew and remembered them. Letters were read from Clarence
P . Cowles , Edward K . Allen, Carroll H. Drown, and Jim himself,
all expressing loyalty to the Club and regretting they could not be
present, mostly due to the exigencies and necessities of war activi-
ties. Some of Jim's beautiful sayings and writings were brought out
and read . A resolution was adopted and inscribed testimonials were
presented to the following Founders : Edward K . Allen , Clarence
P. Cowles, Elwin L. Ingalls, K .R.B . Flint, James P. Taylor, Carroll
H. Drown, and W . H. Spence. A few others could not be located.

ODE TO THE BEAN
Hail to thee , gentle bean!
Mere veg' table thou never wert ,
But, even tho encased in tin thou com' st,
Like to the grub once called ambrosia,
Whereon the Gods of old did sup,
Thou art .

Packed full of vitamins and calories and food ,
Besides tomato sauce and pork,
Thou givest to the legs more strength,
The lungs more wind to climb
Or curse aloud the cool and smoky fire.
Thou fillest up the heart with peace,
And not alone the heart is filled, when thou
Dost from thy heated can pour forth
Into the quickly emptied plate.

But Ah , Alas, and Oh, Alack, for him
Who, in the said tight can dost fail
To make an orifice, or punch a hole ,
Before he sets it on the stove to heat.
For that he failed in this small thing
Full many a hardy hiker has gone supperless to bed ,
And cabins have within with beans been sprayed.

Mainstay of armies, and of navies too, -
What say the Scriptures,
That man cannot live by bread alone;
'Tis true!
He must have beans I

WALKERS Killington Section members were guests of International Walkers'
Association at a fireside party held at the Long Trail Lodge in June. These
walkers , 45 in all, hiked from Albany, N.Y., and made the Lodge their head-
quarters for a week of Vermont climbing.

" DO IT YOURSELF" For several years New York's section of the trail
running from Lincoln-Warren Pass to Bolton has been cleared by hired local
help. Not so this year. Charles Pomeroy and Bob Ernst came up from New
York and whacked out the job themselves, taking the four days after Memorial
Day. They report the trail all cleared; Battell , Glen Ellen, and Birch Glen
camps in fine shape; too much competition with porcupines at Montclair Glen;
and Theron Dean and Cowles Cove shelters nearing abandonment. They're not
sure of a caretaker at Couching Lion Farm this year, but hope that the Vermont
Forest Service cart keep this popular hiking center open.

"HIKING TRAILS OF NEW ENGLAND" is a map listing the princi-
pal trails in this area, together with a key telling who takes care of them , and -
where you can get guide books. You can find all the answers on one page,
instead of hunting through two city libraries. To get a copy send a stamp to
W.R. Hamlin, Secretary, N. E. Trail Conference, Amherst, Mass.

"LIVELY LAD WEED CUTTERS" are still strong favorites with
veteran trail worker Louis Bigelow of Worcester. He says one cutter will do
the work of four men with pruning sheers. The genuine article comes from
Tennessee, and costs $1 each in lots of six, or $i.50 per single.




CAMP COFFEE has always taken the brunt of much discussion. There was
the cook at Webster Shelter who dumped in an extra half-pound of coffee,
made such a stock solution that the boys now require him to use a "coffee
stone." They will not use his brew if the stone floats. A recent bulletin of
Worcester GMC had the following note: Someone's oilsilk slicker was found
in the Club coffee pot after the Amherst trip. Owner please claim .
