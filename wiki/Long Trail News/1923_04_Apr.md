THE GREEN MOUNTAIN NEWS
Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.
Brandon, Vermont, April, 1923

The first number of The Green Mountain News met with a very kind reception, and seemed, tho perhaps inadequately, to fill a long felt want. Encouraged by this reception, we make our bow for the second time, and trust that our shortcomings may be indulgently overlooked.

Annual Meeting of the G. M. C.

The annual meeting was held at the Hotel Berwick in Rutland, Vt., Saturday, Jan. 13. There was a good attendance of members. The following trustees were elected for 1923: George A. Mathers, Bennington; Fred H. Tucker, Boston; E. S. Marsh, Brandon; Chas. A. Harris, Brattleboro; T. S. Dean, Jas. P. Taylor, Dr. L. J. Paris, Burlington; Prof. R. H. White, Prof. P. C. Voter, Middlebury; Prof. Will S. Monroe, Montclair, N. J.; Dr. W. N. Brock, Montpelier; Capt. H. W. Congdon, Kerson Nurian, New York; Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; C. P. Cooper, W. M. Ross, Rutland.

W. M. Ross was elected clerk.

The treasurer's report showed receipts for the year of $1667.10, and disbursements of $1513.64, leaving a balance of receipts of $153.46, which added to cash brought over from last year left $1274.88 in the treasury. Investments on account of receipts from life members are liberty bonds at par, $350, and treasury savings certificate, $82. Receipts included annual dues, $644, life members, $125, guide books sold, $367.50, advertising, $182, gifts, $75.50, interest, $66.73, and dues from sections, $124.85. Disbursements included new camps and trail work, $590.89, cost of guide books, $438.53, printing membership lists, $106.40, Green Mountain News, $36.98, treasury certificate, $82, slides, $15.75, etc.

Members at large, including 19 life members, numbered 351, the largest on record, residing in 17 states and Canada. Massachusetts stood first in number, New York second, and Vermont third.

Membership in the sections, as reported by the treasurer, was New York, 247, Rutland, 125, Burlington, 98, Middlebury, 58, Bennington, 44, Proctor, 21, Montpelier, 18, making a total membership of 962.

A gift of $2000 from Mortimer R. Proctor for a club house was announced, also a gift of $300 from the family of Miss Marjorie Hulburd, to be used in building a piece of trail north of Johnson, on the extension to Canada, as a memorial to Miss Hulburd, who lost her life last year in an automobile accident.

At the dinner in the evening, 48 persons were present. Vice President Fred H. Tucker presided, and interesting after dinner remarks were made by Dr. Will S. Monroe, State Forester W. G. Hastings, and others, after which slides of scenes on the Long Trail were shown.

Sunday there was a sleigh and snowshoe outing to the F. H. Smith farm in East Clarendon, where Secretary Ross turned out griddlecakes ad libitum, served with genuine Vermont maple syrup, while a small party, headed by J. A. Allis, of New York, climbed Pico on snowshoes.

This meeting was held at the Hotel Bardwell in Rutland on Saturday, March 10. There was a small attendance, only six being present, the others being prevented from attending by various reasons. The sum of $1500 was appropriated for trails and shelters, including $50 for the Haselton trail on Mt. Mansfield. It is expected that several new shelters will be built, and the trail extended to Canada.

Announcement was made that the gift of Mortimer R. Proctor for a club house had been raised to $2500, and that his mother, Mrs. Fletcher D. Proctor, would donate the furnishings of the house, and supply the water. Messrs. Cooper, Ross and Wallace M. Fay of Proctor, were elected a building committee to see to the construction of the house, and Paul W. Thayer of Wallingford, was selected as the architect. Plans for the building were exhibited.

The New Club House

will be located in Sherburne Pass, where the highway crosses the Long Trail, a most suitable location. It is easily reached by automobiles, and is within easy walking distance of Mts. Pico and Killington, and Mt. Carmel to the north, and of several ponds, and close to the interesting caverns at Deer's Leap. It will consist of two stories and basement, and have a double fireplace, and a hot air furnace, so that it can be used in cold weather. Mr. Thayer, the architect, specializes in rustic architecture; and will see that the structure harmonizes with its surroundings. It will

be a desirable stopping place for those who prefer short tramps which can be done in a day to longer ones, and a comfortable place to lodge, and also for those passing thru on the trail who wish to rest for a night or two. An open shelter will be built just across the road from the club house, and probably there will be tents for those who prefer to camp, and a garage. The club will control several acres of land surrounding the building, which will be leased from the Vermont Marble Co. at a nominal rental. Building will start as early in the spring as possible.

Mortimer R. Proctor, to whose generosity and that of his mother the club is indebted for the new club house, completely furnished, is one of the trustees of the club, and a former president. He has always taken great interest in the objects and purposes of the club, and in its success, and has heretofore been very liberal in contributions of money for trail work and camps.

The Haselton Legacy.

The first legacy received by the club was paid into the treasury recently, it being the sum of $1000 from the estate of the late Judge Seneca Haselton of Burlington. Judge Haselton, who was for many years a judge of the Vermont Supreme Court, was an ardent mountain lover, especially of Mt. Mansfield, where he usually spent his summers. The Haselton trail up that mountain, from Barnes Camp to the mountain road, a short distance below the hotel, was the work of his own hands. He was a member of the Green Mountain Club from its foundation, and always greatly interested in it. For the present the money will be left on interest and the income devoted to the upkeep of the Haselton trail, so far as needed.

Monroe Lodge.
   Second in importance only to the club house is Monroe Lodge, so named in honor of Prof. Will S. Monroe, the trail builder, which the New York section has undertaken to build on Camel's Hump (Couching Lion), with some help from the club at large. This will be a large lodge, built of logs, but the exact size, form and type have not yet been determined. It is sure to be built this year, as J. A. Allis, of New York, chairman of the Lodge committee, reports that nearly $2000 has been subscribed, which insures the erection of a building amply sufficient for all purposes. Here will be another point, like the club house at Sherburne Pass and the Lake Mansfield Trout Club, where the weary wayfarer on the trail can halt for a time and recuperate, meanwhile indulging in some of the comforts and even luxuries of civilization, before plunging again into the wilderness.

The Worcester Group.
   Herbert M. Dockham, of Holden, Mass., who, with Mrs. Dockham, are loyal members of the G. M. C., and have both been on several long hikes over the Long Trail, has been stirring up an interest in the club in Worcester, Mass., and nearby towns, with the result that, with the aid of some efficient lieutenants, the membership there is now between 40 and 50, and they write that there will be no let-up till 100 members are gathered in, if then. They do not contemplate forming a section of the club, but will come in as members at large. They will have their own local organization, and their local outings and meetings. They will co-operate with the local branch of the Appalachian Mountain Club, whom they have already entertained at a snowshoe and maple sugar party. The Worcester papers have given the new movement much publicity. Here is a good example for other towns to follow. Let the congenial spirits, who are fond of the mountains, of hiking and of the out-of-doors in general, get together, and double the pleasure in their favorite pursuits by companionship.
   LATER.—Since the above was put into type, information has reached us that a section of the G. M. C. was formed at Worcester March 26 with 45 members, and the following list of officers: President, H. M. Dockham, Holden; Vice Pres., Dr. G. M. Cobb, Worcester; Sec'y., E. P. Jackson, Clark Univ.; Treas., L. L. Bigelow, Shrewsbury; Chairman Activities Committee, F. W. Wilder, Worcester; Chairman Photo. Committee, H. A. Stevens, Holden.

Club Library.
   The Club has a nucleus of a library consisting of 57 volumes on mountaineering, walking, and other phases of out-of-door life and contact with nature, besides numerous magazines and pamphlets. These are at present loaned to the Rutland Public Library, and are in a bookcase belonging to the Club. They will probably be removed to the new club house, when that is ready for occupancy.
   The Club also owns a collection of 300 slides showing views of the Long Trail, for use in illustrated lectures.

The Pine Tree Rust.

By C. P. Cooper.

   Every lover of the forest as well as every user of lumber and the last includes every one of us, should be alarmed and ready to help to stop the blister rust or the White Pine will soon go the way of the Chestnut. The disease cannot be cured, it is fatal to the trees it attacks, but by the removal of all currant and gooseberry bushes both wild and tame from the vicinity of the pine the spread of the disease will be instantly stopped. It does not spread from tree to tree. If you own pine lands or can interest any owners get busy.

                              Air, "Solomon Levi".
    Written for the New York Section of the Green Mountain Club by Emma L. Kemp.
        To those who think that life is dull and good times all too few,
        To those who're tired 'most the time and likewise often blue;
        To those who think they're growing old, long before their time,
        Whose nerves are all a-frazzle, and with headache 'most the time,
             Cheer up, old young men, we'll cure you right away;
             Cheer up, old young girls, we'll drive your blues away.
        Just come to us for treatment and you'll find us on the square;
        Our hikes will cost you little cash beyond your railway fare.
        We sell no patent medicines, nor anti-thins or fats;
        Nor salts nor pills nor tonics cheap, because we are not quacks;
        We'll not agree to make you fat, nor yet to make you thin,
        But if you've any place for pep, we'll surely put it in.
              Fresh air and sunshine, rain or hail or snow;
              Jolly pals and good times on all the hikes we go.
        We leave our cares behind us when we quit the noisy towns,
        And find our rest in solitudes of forests, hills, and downs.
        For we are scouts from the G. M. C. who hike from hill to dale,
        And what we have to offer you is nowhere else on sale.
         No doctors, nurses, osteopaths or other medicine men
        Know half the recipes for health that our Green Mountains ken.
              Cheer up, old young men, we'll cure you right away;
              Cheer up, old young girls, we'll drive your blues away.
        Just come to us for treatment and you'll find us on the square;
         Our hikes will cost you little cash beyond your railway fare.
