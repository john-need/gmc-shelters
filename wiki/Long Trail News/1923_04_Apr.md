                             G. M. C. Medicine Song.
                              Air, "Solomon Levi".
    Written for the New Yo1-k Section of the Green Mountain Club by Emma L. Kemp.
        To those who think that life is dull and good times all too few,
        To those who're tired 'most the time and likewise often blue;
        To those who think they're growing old, long before their time,
        Whose nerves are all a-frazzle, and with headache 'most the time,
             Cheer up, old young men, we'll cure you right away;
             Cheer up, old young girls, we'll drive your blues away .
        •Just come to us for treatment and you'll find us on the square;
        Our hikes will cost you little cash beyond your railway fare.
        We sell no patent medicines, nor anti-thins or fats;
        Nor salts nor pills nor tonics cheap, because we are not quacks;
        We'll not agree to make you fat, nor yet to make you thin,
        But if you've any place for pep, we'll surely put it in.
              Fresh air and sunshine, rain or hail or snow;
              Jolly pals and good times on all the hikes we go.
        We leave our cares behind us when we quit the noisy towns,
        And find our rest in solitudes of forests, hills, and downs.
        For we are scouts from the G. M. C. who hike from hill to dale,
        And what we have to offer you is nowhere else on sale.
         No doctors, nurses, osteopaths or other medicine men
        Know half the recipes for health that our Green Mountains ken.
              Cheer up, old young men, we'll cure you right away;
              Cheer up, old young girls, we'll drive your blues away.
        Just come to us for treatment and you'll find us on the square;
         Our hikes will cost you little cash beyond your railway fare.
