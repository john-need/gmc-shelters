MISS TYE RESIGNS

We are very sorry to have to report at this time the resignation of Miss Lula M. Tye as Corresponding Secretary of The Green Mountain Club and as Business Manager of THE LONG TRAIL NEWS.

For the good of her health, Miss Tye feels that she must curtail some of her business activities and has submitted her resignation as of June first this year.

Miss Tye has served The Green Mountain Club a long time, faithfully, and well. It was at the Club's Annual Meeting held at the Hotel Berwick in Rutland, Vermont, on Saturday, January 9th, 1926 that it was voted to make arrangements with her to act as paid secretary for the Club, and to relieve the treasurer of the routine duties of collecting dues, mailing notices, and answering letters about The Long Trail.

Over a period of twenty-nine years Miss Tye has made many Green Mountain Club friends who at this time, through the pages of the NEWS, sincerely wish her good health, happiness, and long life.

NOTES FROM THE NEW YORK SECTION

For the past events of importance, we'd like to mention the annual dinner-dance of the New York Section held February 4th at Hotel Shelburne in New York City. About one hundred members and guests enjoyed the evening and called it very successful.

On March 31st our Annual Meeting was held. A report from the Election Committee showed that officers had been elected for the coming year. (Ed: they are listed elsewhere under Directory of Section Officers.)

There were reports from all committee chairmen and the financial accounts of the club were given close attention. We have had a busy year. Our membership is 360.

For coming events, a look at our April-June bulletin, just out, discloses that Camp Thendara will be open all week ends and that the popular Annual Bird Census will be held there May 7th to 8th under the expert guidance of Mrs. Laura Abbott. There are to be many Saturday and Sunday hikes. We are featuring two week end canoe cruises and a week end bicycle trip through New Jersey. If these attractions aren't enough to whet your appetite for outdoor adventure, we can offer you a couple of trail clearing expeditions, a boat ride around Manhattan or swimming on a warm June afternoon, followed by a strawberry shortcake supper.

At this point our bulletin runs out of suggestions and your correspondent out of words—both temporarily!

BURLINGTON SECTION'S WINTER OUTING ACTIVITIES

In sunshine, rain, hail or snow the Burlington Section outing schedule is still on the go.
   On Feb. 2, 1955 eleven took part in the Section's annual oyster stew supper at Taylor Lodge, in Nebraska Notch. The group vowed the four mile snowshoe tramp in to the lodge for a fill of Don and Bev's tasty stew was worth the trip even though little oysters had to stew in beaver pond water-according to Roy Buchanan, "that was what went to make it good." Them Remicks sure know how to stir up a good oyster feed. The reason why the following was so small this year was the competition we had with Community Concert. The star lighted trail walk back down to the cars was a fitting climax to the event.
   Sunday afternoon February 20th, Flossie and John Gardner were host to the Section for the afternoon and evening out at Rock Point. The group spent the afternoon walking around the Point on the Lake ice in the warm sunshine. They visited some of the ice fishing locations and got an unusual looksee at the world famous and much studied rock strata of the sheer walls of the Point.
   In the evening the group gathered at the Gardner home for basket lunches. The hosts furnished coffee. The evening was spent gab-festing and a family sing around the piano. Twenty-four turned out.
   Ten rugged men and women braved the blustery weather of March 6th for a snowshoe tramp up Snake Mt. in Addison and then came back for a beef stew lunch at Pres. and Mrs. Havens home in the evening.
   March 13th four turned out for a tramp up to Butler Lodge. It was a stormy day and the snowshoe tramp up the steep trail, in spots so crusty that the footing was slippery, gave the group a work out.

COMING EVENTS IN KILLINGTON SECTION

Killington Section has a busy schedule of activities planned for the coming season. This year, work parties, especially those for shelter repairs and trail work near shelters, are being combined with outings at regular intervals.
   On Sunday May 29th, the day following Annual Meeting, alternate outings are being planned. We hope that many members here for the meeting will join with us for one of them. The trip to Killington has become a somewhat standard post-annual meeting hike, while the other, to Sunnyside, will offer an opportunity to visit Killington Section's new-old camp; Spring Lake, relocated, rebuilt, and renamed.
   Another outing on the schedule to which we are especially looking forward is our 4th of July camping trip. During this we plan to hike over about 20 to 25 miles of The Trail in the Mt. Mansfield area. This will be the fourth of such annual camping trips, through which, to date, we have covered the Long Trail from the Canada Line to Smuggler's Notch.
   This coming season we also plan to enjoy some joint outings with other sections and other hiking clubs. Arrangements have already been made to meet the Glens Falls, N.Y., Adirondack Club group on Saturday, June 11, for a climb up Buck Mountain on the east side of Lake George. Other joint outings which we hope to plan later are, one with members from the Barre Section, possibly in Mt. Abraham, and one with the Bennington Section. We would be glad to hear from other sections who would be interested in arranging joint hikes. Our present schedule carries through July but dates could be arranged on the schedule for the second half of the year if we hear from anyone interested before our schedule cards go to the printers, about July 1st.

bushes, vines, mosses, flowers, unique fungi, rocks and wonderful ferns. Overhead a beautiful blue sky and now and then a peek through the underbrush to a land far below makes a most enchanting picture.

The Long Trail supplies everything a camper could ask for, because the hiking can be regulated to fit all sizes and ages, and best of all, it is safe. The shelters are so placed that hike leaders can make definite plans for rest and cooking. No one gets "bushed" for by good planning, hikers get where they want to get at about the time they plan for. The hurry of the world is left behind in the quiet of The Trail.

Camp directors who plan on having their campers use the Trail are very grateful to those folks who give of their time to make it possible to use all the good things found on it. One of the greatest lessons to be taught to anyone on the Trail is of thinking of the other fellow; the fellow who may land at a shelter after you, in a rain, and finds you have left him a clean shelter with a nice pile of dry wood. He doesn't know who you are but thinks you are the greatest "guy" in the world to think of his welfare.

We, camp directors, feel that a trip on The Long Trail is one of the great summer experiences for any camper both physically and spiritually.

LEONE E. SMITH
