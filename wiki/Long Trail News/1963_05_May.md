BRATTLEBORO SECTION

The annual meeting of the Brattleboro Section was held at the home of the President, Lynn Kent, on January 3, 1963. Plans were made to repair Bromley Lodge during the coming year. A discussion of problems facing the parent club was prompted by Don Kent and Rex Coleman, both trustees of the parent club.

The following officers were re-elected: Lynn Kent, President; David Dunklee, Vice-President; Louise Dunklee, Secretary-Treasurer; Brandon Douglas Trails.

Program was discussed and many plans were left in the hands of the members present to plan and execute.

After the business meeting, Gerald Coane, uncle of Lynn and brother-in-law of Don, showed his collection of Indian artifacts, arrowheads, hammer heads, arrow making tools, bits of pottery and stone hatchets which were all dug up within Windham County in the Brattleboro area.

LOUISE H. DUNKLEE, Reporter

On Mar. 3rd sixty of us swarmed to the home of Prof. Roy and Helen Buchanan for an afternoon of snowshoeing, sliding and skiing. After the exercise in the open, we all gathered in Helen's kitchen for bowls of fish chowder, coffee and cakes.

Our annual Maple Sugar Party brought out about one hundred twenty-six eaters and square dancers. Under a beautiful warm spring sun nineteen spent the afternoon snowshoeing on Arrowhead Mt. before the supper. The calling for the dancing was carried out by one of our own members, Douglas Fay, who has done it on several occasions. And they came from far and wide to have fun with us—Audrey Dorning and friends from Montreal, Chet and Dave Buchanan from Fayetteville, N.Y., and the Donald Shoreys from Liverpool, N.Y.

Pete Keelty and his committee offer a full schedule of activities during the Spring and Summer months and we cordially invite any who would like to join us when they are up this way. Just call Pete Keelty at UN 2-4648 for the details.

LARRY DEAN, Reporter

BURLINGTON SECTION

Up the Trail Apiece—

Come sit on this snowbank a spell and we'll review the happenings up hereabouts. We've had business, fun and deep sadness since our last chat.

First we went out and exercised our right to choose our section officers for the ensuing year as President and Mrs. Ben Rolston observed the Burlington Section annual meeting. Our new slate of officers are: President, Albert Wurthmann; Vice-President, Shirley Strong; Secretary, Wilda Gigee; Treasurer, Lee Brown; and our committee heads are: Outing, Pete Keelty; Trails, Dane Shortsleeve; Properties, Jack Harrington and Joe Frank; Entertainment, Julia Hatch; Membership, Mary Forrester; and Publicity, Larry Dean.

Daan Zwick provided the entertainment at the Annual Meeting and supper by showing us his colored slides taken during a trip to the Cascade Mountains in Washington with the Genesee Valley Hiking Club of New York. Daan is a member of both clubs and a former caretaker at our Taft Lodge.

The annual Oyster Supper at Taylor Lodge, cooked up by Beverly and Don Remick, proved as popular as ever, with sixty-two people snowshoeing in over deep snow on Jan. 19th for it.

This year Mr. Eric Sanford of the Sanford Photographic Associates of Manchester, N.H., accompanied us. He was seeking club winter photos in connection with an article written by Fran Greene of the Worcester Section for the Ford Times. Besides being proud of our choice as subjects for Fran's story, we enjoyed Eric as he fitted in nicely with our group.

KILLINGTON SECTION

Killington Section has had a successful winter season climaxed by the annual Winter Weekend which, along with the covered dish supper in the Fall, was enjoyed by a maximum crowd. The hikes have been fairly well attended with plenty of good snow for snowshoeing. Considering the amount of snow, it looks as if we won't be able to get at the trail work as early as we might like.

The next event, which everyone looks forward to each year, is the Sugar-on-Snow Party on April 6th. This was scheduled to coincide with the Trustees' meeting so that they might attend if they wish. It will be held in the Universalist Church Vestry in Downtown Rutland this year in order to give us more seating capacity.

The new schedule includes trips to Stratton Pond in June, Saltash Mt. and Rocky Pond in July and Lake Pleiad in August. Also included is a two day work party to Tucker Lodge in May.

See you on the trail.

MONTPELIER SECTION

Our fall schedule included hikes to Mt. Hunger, Texas Falls, Butler Lodge (an overnight), Scragg Mt., Scrabble Hill, and Mt. Jefferson (White Mountains).

Our winter schedule started with a "moonlight" snowshoe hike in Hubbard Park followed by cocoa and slides. The moon made a very brief appearance. Several of our members attended the Burlington Section's Oyster Stew Party at Taylor Lodge. On Lincoln's Birthday we went for a walk on the hill near National Life. Feb. 16th found us in Smuggler's Notch. On Mar. 3rd we enjoyed a cocoa party at Wiley Lodge. We had another "moonlight" snowshoe hike on Mar. 10th and again the moon condescended to peek out for a moment. A beefstew at Glen Ellen Lodge, at which members of the Burlington Section joined us, completed our winter activities. The Para-Ski Races at Mad River Glen coincided with this last activity and made the day even more interesting.

Our spring schedule starts with a sugar-on-snow party April 7th. Other planned activities are a pot luck stew at Montclair Glen, our annual meeting, a canoe trip, a walk to Nichol's Ledge, a ski trip to Tuckerman Ravine, another trip to the White Mountains, a two or three day hike on the northern section of the Long Trail, our annual pilgrimage to Camel's Hump, and a trail-clearing trip.

Our membership is now thirty-nine. Attendance on our hikes is usually from six to twelve and we often have guests.

NEW YORK SECTION

Now that the Annual Meeting is over, it's time to start having a real good time with your friends and piling up the statistics for another year! We all know we're not Statistic Hounds but why be a member if not an active one. Also, what's to prevent your bringing along a friend or so who is not a member, but wishes to become one? Now let's take a look at just how he could get to be one of us and not have the feeling that we indulge in 50 miles each time we take off!

Let's presume that all spring cleaning and the gardening is done to the point that nothing can possibly stand in the way of the Call-of-the-Wild. Real wild on Sat. May the 4th—Oriental Collections in the Tibetan Gallery of the Newark Museum as well as Oriental Cherry Blossoms nearby. Then on Sat. May 11th, spend some time at a Spanish-Moroccan Museum in a sunken meadow. This begins to sound like a Cooks Tour—no—it's GMC Regular Activities! On Sat. May 18th, be historic and go to Princeton.

There are some longer walks—not 50 tho—on Sundays: Mysterious Stone Walls; Hike thru the Rilling Hills; High Point State Park and the A, B or C choice on another day. Then there are some "Circulars"! Find out about these at Audubon-Fairchild, Stony-Brook and Tuxedo. We also eat while we enjoy ourselves. Most every walk has food somewhere—before, after or in the middle.

Your prospective member will want to swim—before meals of course! There are many different varieties—Sandy Hook, Estling and Pines Lake, with special foot bath in Stony Brook. Possibly you won't swim on the Ramapo Weekend Cruise but there are likely to be some stopovers along the way! At Thendara you won't have to swim all the time! You can, but you could also go boating and you could help count birds.

By the way, did you ever Houseclean with all your friends? Yes, and all in the same house, too? You have a whale of a good time thrown in with a little work. Don't make any other plans when there's a chance to Houseclean at Thendara.

Now we offer the Vermont Weekend. Here's a chance to get away from familiar ground as well as all the hustle and bustle of the city. Go on this tour; meet other GMCers and find out what they do for excitement in the club. All kidding aside, why not plan to attend the Annual Meeting? Many hope to go and you will not be alone at meetings nor during recreation.

Who could resist becoming a member with all this diversification?

BETTY TONKS, Reporter

WORCESTER SECTION
The first three months of 1963 has found Worcester Section enjoying winter sports, mostly snowshoeing. We have had several local snowshoe hikes which have been well attended.
About 29 of our members also enjoyed a weekend during the first part of February at the Northfield Inn, sliding, snowshoeing and tobogganning, and all the other sundry activities to be found there.
On a cold, starry, winter's night in the middle of February, 35 members came out of their warm snug homes to witness a showing in the planetarium at the Worcester Natural History Museum and to attend the business meeting. We all learned a little bit more about astronomy that night and were able to go outside after the "inside" showing and witness the real thing.
March 23rd was the night of our annual sugaring-off party which was preceded by a fine church supper. Our sugar master, Jim Green, did himself proud again this year, as the 76 people who attended will agree. We were privileged to have with us for this evening our club President and his wife, Mr. and Mrs. Ben Rolston. Our entertainment was provided by Mr. Fred Holdsworth, Jr., Vice President of the Appalachian Mt. Club, who gave an illustrated lecture "Tides from Nantucket to the Bay of Fundy." Eleanor Patterson and her entertainment committee are to be commended for their fine work in making this evening such a success.
Club officers for this year are: President, Fred H. Eldridge; Vice President, Taisto V. Salo; Secretary, Miss Dorothy Forsberg; Treasurer, Miss Marion Elvidge. Bob Humes has been re-elected a Trustee, as well as our Trails and Shelters Committee Chairman.

With the approaching good weather many will again start hiking on the Long Trail and we believe you will be pleased to find that the portion of the Trail starting at the Arlington Road and heading north to the Willis Ross Shelter, has been relocated to the west. No longer will you have to plow through the muddy logging road. Thanks for this praiseworthy effort is due our good members, Bob Humes and Rachel and Jim Marston.

LINNEA ELDRIDGE, Reporter

ANY VOLUNTEERS?
From individuals and groups we occasionally receive offers to perform some work on the trails. These people, who enjoy using the trails and the shelters and who realize the monumental task of maintaining them, would like to help us.
Even though all of our Long Trail and most of our side trails receive various amounts of clearing, brushing and blazing each summer, there is always room for trail improvements. Yes, we surely can use volunteers.
Therefore, we find that we have volunteers and we also have work which these volunteers can do. But how to tie in the offers with the need? Perhaps we can coordinate these elements and put our volunteers to work!
So if you'd like to put your clippers, axe, saw and paint brush to work, write a note giving your name and address (and, if representing a group, the size of your group), tell us the portion of trail in which you are most interested, and mail the details to Ben Rolston, 323 12th Street, Schenectady, N.Y.
Our Trails and Shelters Committee and the Trails Chairmen of the Sections will determine where we can best use your efforts. Your pay will be a couple of blisters, some fresh Vermont air and sunshine, a ravenous appetite, and a wonderful feeling of satisfaction of having played a part in the GMC program of "making the mountains play a larger part in the life of the people".

NEW ENGLAND TRAIL CONFERENCE
The 27th annual New England Trail Conference was held at Amherst, Mass., on March 30th. A larger-than-ever attendance enjoyed an unusually fine program. It's amazing how our John Vondell can find such excellent speakers year after year. We went to the White Mountains, the State of Washington, the Alps; we saw the wild animals of Africa; we climbed Kilimanjaro; and we saw the wonders of nature right in our own back yard.
We saw many GMC'ers at the Conference. Those who weren't there surely missed a great program. You'd better make plans to go to Amherst next year OR ELSE - - - (you'll miss another fine program)!

THE NEW TRAIL AND SHELTER SIGNS
The response of the Club to the request for sign-boards has been excellent. The first ones to be delivered came from Louis Stare, Jr., a member-at-large from Bass River, Mass., out on Cape Cod. He and his father made 50 of them and brought them to Craigue Perkins on March 5th. This takes a big bite out of our requirement of somewhat more than 200 of them. Louis has made the good suggestion of painting the boards with Cabot's medium moss green shingle stain. It is about 3/4 creosote.
Other members are also making signboards and we will probably have nearly enough by annual meeting time. More can surely be made on an emergency basis if it turns out that we are short.
Now we need to plan the operation of putting up the signs and boards. We have members up and down all of Vermont so this will be easy. Members who live near the locations of the signs are needed as volunteer installers.
The locations of our signs will be as follows:
1. On the Long Trail at every crossing of a road. At each crossing there will be two signs on boards, one north and the other south. We will place these signs near the road but not so near that they can be seen from the road. They are for walkers, not for people driving by;
2. Two signs, no board, will be placed at each shelter;
3. Each side trail will have two signs, one on a board located at the lower end of the trail so that someone hunting the side trail can spot it easily. The other sign, on a board, goes at the point where the side trail meets the Long Trail.
All of the signs are different. Members who will put up signs can collect them and the boards at the Long Trail Lodge at the annual meeting. If you cannot come to the meeting, please arrange to have someone else pick them up for you.
With many helping hands, the next Long Trail News should have a good report.

WINTER WEEKEND AT TALL TIMBER
The bright sun and cloudless blue sky made the forty hikers forget the frigid air and biting wind as they packed their gear up the hill to the Perkins' camp, Tall Timber, on February 16th. A total of 50 people registered, coming from New York City; Schenectady, N.Y.; Worcester and Shrewsbury, Mass.; Springfield and Rutland, Vt., with 26 staying overnight.
Beef stew, the kind that sticks to the ribs, and all one could eat, was served at noon in two sittings to about 40 people. This grand meal with all the trimmings gave everyone extra energy for the afternoon activities.
After lunch, six hikers, led by Ben Rolston, snowshoed to Tucker Lodge, arriving at 4:30. After a short rest they continued on the main road and hiked back to Tall Timber on foot, enjoying the colorful alpine glow as the sun set on Pico Peak.
The Springfield group chopped umteen cords of wood to help keep Jack Frost out of their cabins, while others took short snowshoe hikes over the beautiful countryside and snapped many pictures of the sparkling white snow and the bluest sky imaginable.
The hearty supper of corned beef hash, cabbage salad, and chocolate bread pudding more than satisfied the appetites of the hungry hikers. The lively after-dinner discussions were suddenly hushed as many found themselves engrossed in trying to extricate the pencils, with strings attached, which Harold Valiquette had deftly fastened to sweater or shirt buttonholes.
Around nine o'clock twelve of the group left the cozy farmhouse and braved the 15° below zero temperature to hike to the cabins—to sleep? During the night the temperature dropped to 20° below. Those in the farmhouse and workshop retired soon, and all met the next morning for a welcome breakfast featuring hot oatmeal.
After breakfast had digested, one group snowshoed to the Beaver Pond near the Old Chittenden Road crossing and beyond to the Long Trail, arriving back just in time for dinner. Others took advantage of the second day of pure blue skies to take further short hikes and more colored pictures. Still another group spent the morning enjoying the excellent skiing at Pico.
Another sumptuous meal, starring fricasseed chicken breasts, was enjoyed by the Winter Weekenders at noon on Sunday. After dinner, gear was gathered up into packs as all reluctantly prepared to head down the trail to the cars.
No one will ever forget the hospitality of the Perkins', the warmth and cheer of the comfortable farmhouse, the delicious meals so ably planned and prepared, and the good fellowship of old and new friends.

THE DAYS AND CAHEES
