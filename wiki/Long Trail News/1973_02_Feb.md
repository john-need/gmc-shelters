SUCH IS THE WAY
   It was a very cold night with much hurrying and scurry-
ing of clouds. The moon kept peeking out once in awhile
just to see how her labor was coming. There was a loud
clap of thunder up near the summ it. Nol a very good nighi
to be born, but, none-the-less, iL was his night. There was
another clap of thunder that lit the night for a fraction of
a second. At this moment he was born; small, naked, and
yet handsome. For a moment there was no up or down;
he did not understand what was happening to him or where
his mother was. He blacked out, for how long he did not
know, but when he awoke, he was on the ground just be-
low the summit.
   He felt very strange lying there, but soon the moon
shone down, and that cheered him up considerably. He
began to take stock of his world. It was very quiet and
peaceful on the mountain. He looked up and saw that the
sky was filled with strange little points of light. What a
strange and exotic world I have been born into, he thought
to himself.
   He lay there in the darkness looking up at the points
of light for a long time. Then slowly he became aware
of a sound that was not made by the wind. It was a soft
sound that drew slowly closer, until at last he could see
the strange creature that was responsible for it. At first
he was afraid of the creature because of its great size and
its enormous fiat feet. But something in the way it moved
slowly and quietly toward the summit dispelled his fear
and turned it slowly into curiosity. Why had this creature
come up the mountain in the dark to stand on the summit?
He wondered at the sweet smell. It came from the small
clouds that the creature made from the piece of wood
stuck in its mouth. And then he saw a strange glow come
from the piece of wood. Could this be where the points of
light came from? Maybe this strange creature wandered
over the summits leaving a trail of light points in the sky
behind it. He thought about this for a moment, and when
he looked up the creature was gone. All that was lef'
were its enormous tracks and a trail of light points in the
sky.
   He was alone again and for a long time he thought
about the creature and of the light points. The light points!
Where are they? The sky is no longer dark; it is pink. Such
a strange world. He began to feel strange as the sky be-
came lighter. It was a good feeling, he decided. As the
feeling grew stronger, he could not contain himself, and
so he danced and laughed.
   He carried on for a long time and then began to feel
tired and sick. He could not understand it. The sky was
so bright; he had been so happy. Now he was dying. Such
a strange world.
   He died quietly up there near the summit, and, in the
spring, the spring beauties will grow on his grave.
   Such is the way with snowflakes.
          EDWIN F. ROBERTS, End-to-Ender No. 459
