The net profit was $5,054, of which $3,678 was left after capital investments. After expenses, consisting of interest, taxes, insurance, caretaker, etc., $953 is now on hand of the Club's share of the profit.

The meeting voted that out of the above sum $500 should be paid on account of the indebtedness, and that it was the sense of the meeting that if possible the debt should be reduced by at least $1,000 yearly.

Mr. Boyce stated that the contract between the Club and the Treadway Company for the management of the Lodge had been renewed for another term of five years on the same terms as heretofore.

Appropriations for the coming season were made amounting to $1,695, being about as heretofore. The appropriation for the patrol was $800, to include the building of a new cabin to replace the Seth Warner camp.

It was voted that a committee be appointed by the president to draw up a program for the annual meeting, covering the three days, May 28, 29 and 30. Also that a nominating committee be appointed to nominate a board of trustees to be presented to the annual meeting of the Club. Neither committee was named at the time.

The vice-president was requested to correspond with the Sections with a view of having each of them appoint a correspondent for the News, so that more Section news could appear in it.

Otto G. Koenig, supervisor of the Green Mountain National Forest, attended the meeting. He stated that the Forest Service had adopted as a trail sign on their portion of the Trail a section of board stained a brown color, with grooved letters painted a buff color, with directions north and south. These would be placed at trail intersections and to call attention to natural features along the Trail.

Some resolutions were read, passed by the Club in 1915, setting forth their intention to name some peak in the vicinity of Mt. Horrid in honor of Albert G. Farr, of Brandon and Chicago, an early benefactor of the Club. This has never been done, and the matter was left with Mr. Allis to investigate and see what mountain as yet unnamed in that vicinity and on the Trail would be appropriate to comply with the resolutions.

Those attending the meeting were trustees Puffer, Smith, of Burlington, Voter of Middlebury, Marsh of Brandon, Boyce, Fay, of Proctor, Ross of Rutland, Bigelow of Manchester, Congdon of Arlington, Allis of New York. Prof. White of Middlebury and Miss Tye also attended.

ANNUAL MEETING AND PROGRAM

Notice is hereby given that the postponed annual meeting of the Green Mountain Club, Inc. will be held at the Long Trail Lodge, Sherburne Pass, Rutland, Vermont on May 28th, 1938 at eleven o'clock A. M.

The trustees, at their meeting in September, 1937, agreed that all the business of the annual meeting of the Club would be postponed from its regular date in January to sometime in May in the hope that more members might find it possible to attend at that time. Consequently the regular meeting was held, with but a few present, at the time called for by the by-laws in January and all of the business formally postponed to a date to be later decided on.

At the March trustees' meeting May twenty-eighth was selected for the date and arrangements have been made for a program to last throughout the Memorial Day holiday. All of the facilities of the Long Trail Lodge will be held for members' reservations until May 21st, and after that date members will have preference for remaining rooms, if any. Please send your applications as early as possible to Grover C. Wright, Manager, Long Trail Lodge, Sherburne Pass, Rutland, Vermont. Reservations will be made in the order of application.

There are ample facilities in Rutland and on the Sherburne Mountain Road to take care of any members the Lodge is unable to accommodate. You are assured of a place to stay even if you cannot decide on your plans until after

REPORT OF WINTER SPORTS COMMITTEE

By LAWRENCE W. DEAN

For some time the Green Mountain Club has felt the need for devoting more attention to winter sports, as a club. With this thought in mind the trustees created a winter sports committee, naming the writer as chairman. It was felt something should be done about this "sick" branch of the club, but what, was the big question.

It appeared that a diagnosis should be the first step. With this in view a questionnaire, listing questions on various phases of winter sports, was made up and sent to each of the ten member sections. This questionnaire was mailed out early last autumn with seven sections responding.

When the questionnaires were sent out it was hoped they would be returned early enough so that the results could be studied and a report made ready to be passed on at the annual meeting. This hope could not be realized as the last report was not received until well into January. Then again, the board of trustees chose to postpone the date of the annual meeting until well after the winter sport season had passed, leaving diagnosed and diagnoser behind.

That Green Mountain Club members may know what steps have been taken by the winter sports committee the chairman solicits the aid of the Long Trail News as an informer. An effort is here made to summarize the thoughts of those sections reporting.

In all but one case it was agreed that winter sports should become increasingly a part of the Sections' outing program. It was agreed that since the Green Mountain Club is not a profit making organization it could not build ski trails or erect ski tows as do the ski clubs which are profit making organizations, assisted by profit sharing communities. It is generally agreed that where it is possible a get-together of the Sections in a sort of winter carnival or outing where all could take part in snowshoeing, skating and skiing would be very enjoyable. Such an outing would and should have group games, friendly contests, not contests for the skilled alone to participate in, but for all. To hold such a get-together would demand a central location and even then the distance would be the great draw back. Possibly, in the future, the annual meeting might be held in conjunction with the get-together and it is believed by the chairman that were it so worked out such annual meetings would be more largely attended.

The diagnosis seems to reveal that distance is the big handicap that joint outings of all the Sections confront. To offset this handicap, it is suggested that the Sections might be sub-divided into three regions for convenience: northern, central and southern.
