END-TO-ENDERS RECOGNIZED

Saturday evening, Sept. 3rd, was designated as End-to-Ender Recognition Night. After opening remarks by Dave Otis, Burlington Section President, the meeting was turned over to Ben Rolston of the End-to-Enders Committee. (Ben is an End-to-Ender three times, and believed to be the only one since the late Capt. Dorney.) Ben called each person honored to the front and asked for the highlights of their trips and the length of time required to complete the project. Some 15 hikers were honored during the evening. Ben advised the gathering that Harry Walton, who had spent a couple of nights at the camp after coming off the Trail on completing the whole Trail, had qualified for the End-to-Ender Award but had left camp before the recognition night.

! ! ! 100TH END-TO-ENDER ! ! !

Mr. Harry A. Walton of Dalton, Massachusetts, celebrated the 50th Anniversary of the G.M.C. by completing the Long Trail and being the one hundredth person to receive the End-to-Ender Award. Harry Walton started hiking the Long Trail when he was "fresh out of high school" in 1937. That year he and some chums went from Blackington to Long Trail Lodge. Resuming, in 1938 he went north to Mansfield. Twenty-two years then went by, and this year of 1960 he decided to become an End-to-Ender. In his words, "first of all it was the GMC'S 50th year, and secondly I wasn't getting any younger." Congratulations!

THE LONG TRAIL PATROL

The Patrol started work June 23rd with a gang of three men all the time and four occasionally. The first job was a new roof on Cooper Lodge plus repairs to the windows and the bunks. Due to the fine cooperation of the Killington Basin Ski Group we were able to ride their lift and to send all supplies up on it. This enabled us to save much time and to use heavy roofing in the repair.

Next the Patrol cleared the Appalachian Trail link from the Long Trail Lodge to the Barnard Road (18 miles). Then they cleared the New York Sections trails and repaired their camps where needed. The New York Section paid for this work.

Next the Patrol moved north and brushed the trail from Canada to Hazens Notch, then cleared blow-downs etc. from Hazens Notch to Smugglers Notch. All cabins in this region were repaired and creosoted except Sterling Pond Camp which the Montpelier Section repaired.

After that they moved in to old Breadloaf Shelter. They tore this down and then built an "Adirondack" type open shelter on the same spot that Breadloaf had occupied. It was built of logs cut in the neighborhood under a permit from the U.S. Forest Service. This shelter sleeps 6 to 8 people like the other "Adirondack" shelters. It is now called the Emily Proctor Shelter in honor of Miss Emily Proctor for whom a shelter had been named very early in G.M.C. history. This had been located about ¾ mile below the Breadloaf Shelter. Travel had changed through the years so that very little use was made of the shelter and it was torn down by the U.S. Forest Service in 1959.

Walter Collins O'Kane in his book "Trails and Summits of the Green Mountains" says on Page 28, "The building of shelters was begun in 1913. Miss Emily Proctor provided a fund through which three log lean-tos were erected, one at Birch Glen, one at Breadloaf Glen, and another south of Mt. Horrid."

Because Emily Proctor started the building of shelters it seems decidedly fitting to keep her name on the list of them.

After Emily Proctor Shelter was done the Patrol gang was disbanded for the season. The leader, Harris Abbott, and the truck functioned after that at the Lake Eden Intersectional, where they furnished transportation whenever it was needed, which was often.

THE LAURA COWLES TRAIL

The Laura Cowles Memorial Trail is now ready for hikers.

This trail is the shortest trail to the tip-top of Mt. Mansfield. It branches off the Sunset Ridge Trail about a mile above the State parking area, on the west side of Mt. Mansfield. It joins the Sunset Ridge Trail near the crest of the mountain, a short distance from the top of the Chin, the highest point in the Green Mountains. This trail is about 1½ miles in length, as measured by Prof. Buchanan.

The Laura Cowles Trail was founded by Laura's son John in 1931, when John was caretaker of Taft Lodge. It has been known as the Cowles Cut-Off Trail, affectionately dubbed the "Cowles Keep Off Trail", so underdeveloped. In recent years work has been done on the trail by Prof. Buchanan and others.

Recently, Dr. John T. Cowles, Ph.D., an End-to-Ender, has thoroughly cleared and marked the trail, with help of a son Ethan, and a bit of help of John's father, the undersigned.

Obviously, a short-cut trail to the tip-top of the Green Mountains, to the capstone of Vermont, is potentially an outstanding, most popular trail. The Trail has other distinctive features. It starts high up, from a parking, camping area, with caretaker, shelters and toilets. The Trail is specially blessed with cheery little brooks and cold springs; and it runs through a charming fernery, the spinulose wood fern, the "fancy fern" of florists.

In its lower portion the Laura Cowles Trail is a delightful "Footpath in the Wilderness". In its upper part the Trail is a challenge to the elect, for mountaineers only, as befitting the top of a lofty mountain.

In fair weather, a delightful alternate descent will be provided by the Sunset Ridge Trail, when the descent from the ridge on that trail shall have been made safe.

Laura Golden Cowles was a pioneer in opening the Green Mountains for the recreation of women as well as men, in winter as well as in summer.

UP THE TRAIL APIECE—BURLINGTON

A Big Find!!!

A Burlington Section member, Clyde H. Smith, one of the Section's "mountain goats", while digging around the little traveled parts of the highest peak in the Green Mountains uncovered a spectacular secret of the Mountain a few days ago.

Clyde found a horizontal rock obelisk nearly 39 feet long and believed to weigh some 75 or so tons sticking out from a mid-way point of a 100 foot sheer cliff. The base of the rock at the cliff is eight feet wide and seven thick and tapers out knife-like to a width of some 17 inches at the tip end.

It is expected that the rock, now dubbed as the "Cantilever Rock", may become one of the mountain's points of interest and attraction when a trail is completed to it. Smith is planning Section excursions to open a trail to the spot this Autumn.

The discovery kept secret by the mountain down through countless ages has made paper headlines in this neck-of-the-woods and may appear in magazines and papers nationally ere long.

Hats off to Clyde for his discovery.

NEW YORK SECTION

In the fall and winter seasons GMC members are usually on trips, on trails, on lakes or at camp; few can be found in the City. Following are reports of three of our early fall activities, many more interesting ones are in process of planning and it will be necessary for members to refer to their bulletins then have trouble trying to figure out which trip to take.

"The Mianus River Gorge Trip, Saturday Sept. 17th. The Mianus River Gorge Wildlife Refuge and Botanical Preserve consists of 132 acres at the present time and extends 1.73 miles along the banks of the Mianus River. The trails are easy and go thru stands of hemlocks from 200-300 years of age. The area is full of deer as well as gray and red fox, raccoons, red and gray squirrels. It is close to Bedford Hills and open most of the year. The Todd family, property owners close by, very kindly guided and every one enjoyed a good outing."—Milford Streeter, Jr.

My first canoe trip with the GMC, Sept. 24-25, by Tom VanderSleen, 13 yrs. "Boy, my first canoe trip sure was fun! We started out at Folsom Crossing and went down the Great Egg Harbor River. It certainly was not the kind of river that I expected. In many places it was very narrow. The first part was twisty and winding and we had to go around pretty many dead and alive trees that were in our way. We paddled for about 2½ hours and then we made camp. We had a little cove to ourselves, which had beautiful white sand all around it. For supper Don our host, had a whole steak for each of us, which was very filling, but went down easily after our paddling. That night, after supper, while sitting around a cozy campfire, we saw the satellite Echo pass through the sky.

THE INTERSECTIONAL AND POST 592

As we assembled on the Long Trail at the crossing of Route 105A, our enthusiasm was high and the weather was ideal for a woodland walk. We were to sample that most northern section of our Trail and to continue on to that most appropriately-named camp, Journey's End. Friendly conversation, the study of nature's wonders, the anticipation of a hoped-for view around the next bend or over the next ridge-and the half miles passed quickly behind us. Even sooner than expected, we arrived at the sharp turn in the trail, and just ahead of us in the clearing was our first goal-Post 592.

With much ceremony, we each crossed into Canada, then posed for photos with hands linked around the border post. We could see the swath cleared thru the woods that separates Vermont from our friendly neighbor to the north, and distant views into Canada were enjoyed by all. As we stood at the border we thought of the thrill that awaited Ray Goss and his two sons from Connecticut. For they were due to arrive at the border the next morning and thus become our newest End-to-Enders!

But we were hungry. So on we hiked to our final goal, Journey's End Camp. Out came an assortment of lunches which were eagerly devoured amid much relaxation around the camp. But soon it was time to return, so ahead went the drivers with thanks to Harris Abbott who drove them around to the cars on Route 105A. As we returned to Lake Eden we all appreciated the fine planning of the Intersectional Committee that made it possible for us to explore the scenic beauty of Northern Vermont. We fell in love with the north country and all look forward to returning.

BEN ROLSTON

MEMBER-AT-LARGE
REVIEWS INTERSECTIONAL

Over 150 persons registered for the Intersectional Fiftieth Anniversary Week at Lake Eden, Vermont on the Mt. Norris Scout Reservation. Each arriving party was greeted by gracious Larry Dean, Chairman. He showed the camping areas and facilities and helped in countless ways to get us all settled.

The campers stayed from one day to ten days and had a wonderful experience hiking the Long Trail led by capable leaders, who made each trip a delightful and valuable occasion. The weather was most cooperative with only two nights and one day of rain.

Swimming and boating on Lake Eden were enjoyed by all.

Sunday, August 28th, the first hike was started with Don Havens, leader. Sixteen persons drove to the trail on Route 118 at Frying Pan Brook and spent 2½ hours climbing Mt. Belvidere to the Ranger Station where the views were magnificent. The air was cool and clear up there but the hiking was warm. After a lunch hour, several retraced their steps, the others hiked on, one group going as far as Tillotson's Camp and out by way of the Frank Post Memorial Trail, still others going on to Hazens Notch.

Another hike that same day was almost a trail-blazing experience with Larry Dean leading a group up Mt. Norris.

A smaller but more rugged group went up Jay Peak that day and raved about the perfect view they had.

Each day groups went out on interesting hikes, one to Ritterbush Pond and Camp, one to Lake Willoughby and up Mt. Pisgah led by Bob Humes. This one ended in a thorough soaking for all as a shower caught us on our return down the mountain, only adding to the fun of the day.

Another interesting trip was a hike led by Don Wallace. The ride over to old Jay Campsite was beautiful and the group numbered 18. We climbed Domey's Dome and several walked out to Mt. Gilpin for better views of Owl's Head, then we lunched and on the way home, "bush-wacked" out. A unique experience for most of us. Don was a very able leader and brought us out just where the truck would pick up the drivers.

A trip to the Ruberoid Asbestos mine on a beautiful sunny day was a once in a lifetime treat for over 35 of us. A guided tour proved most fascinating and instructive.

Still another delightful trip was the day a large group led by Connie Brown, End-to-Ender, went from Route 105 to Journey's End Camp. Having driven up to Jay Notch, the hike itself proved to be a long down hill trek, something a lot of hikers had often looked for. Beautiful views at Post #592 and other spots, much helpful information on flora and fauna from our good friend, Ben Rolston, a veteran hiker, a large group of oldsters and youngsters and those in-between, a really wonderful day. It was the last hike of the week for most of us as rain set in Saturday night and continued thru Sunday, eliminating a long looked for hike up Owls Head. Maybe next year!

Long to be remembered was the night we had the big campfire with group singing, ghost stories, and toasted marshmallows while a bright moon shone overhead.

Also, very enjoyable were the evenings in the recreation hall with an open fire glowing and much singing, games and beautiful slides of Vermont shown by Professor Buchanan, with his inimitable commentary. Also some nice views on the Long Trail by Harris Abbott, of the Long Trail Patrol, who helped to make the whole occasion more comfortable with his assistance. Another evening we enjoyed Professor Vondell's beautiful pictures and also the fabulous scenes of the Koerbers' trip to Europe and skiing in the Alps.

Every morning and evening tireless Larry Dean checked each camping area to offer help. This besides arranging the program of hikes and evening entertainment with his co-chairman Don Havens. We are all indebted to Larry and Don and the Burlington Section for such a complete celebration. This also includes their wonderful wives. Another excellent addition to the weeks program was Professor Roy Buchanan and his lovely wife, plus two or three grandsons. Our GMC President Don Kent was on hand all week. He was active and helping in many capacities, also lending his story telling talents to several occasions.

We owed much to Ed Leverin', Professional Scouter who led in singing and stories and to Betty Paulson and Shirley Strong.

All in attendance concede this to be a most outstanding affair.

MARIE MORRIS
