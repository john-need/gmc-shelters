The following ideas were many of those expressed for the consideration of the Trustees. In the rapidity of the discussion others may have been inadvertently not recorded. If such is the case, please do not hesitate to call them to the attention of President Attenborough.

Membership—Craigue felt that a larger membership would:
1. Give us a larger voice in supporting conservation issues which could protect our trail, and
2. Provide funds for a central office and compensate for increasing trail and shelter maintenance costs which can be expected from increasing trail use.

In a straw vote the group favored the idea of an associate membership for non-users of the trail. This would be for persons who wish to support the aims of the Club but who do not necessarily wish to be active members. A straw vote suggesting dues increase was rejected.

Other ideas expressed were:

1. We should encourage the membership of those who are using the Trail. One way of doing this might be to contact persons who sign registers.
2. Guidebooks might be sold at a decreased rate to members.
3. A special issue of the L.T. News (with perhaps an outside mailing) might be written to publicize the activities, purposes and image of the GMC.
4. Information about the Club could be distributed at ski lodges, White Mt. Huts, etc.
5. Improved sectional activities and programs might increase membership.
6. More publicity might be given regarding the $10 group membership.

Youth Groups on the Trail—Don introduced the ideas of:
1. Sponsoring workshops for youth group leaders in safety and conservation.
2. Publishing information relating to trail safety, etiquette and conservation.
3. Appointing advisors in each section to aid youth groups in trip planning and trail use.

A straw vote favored positive action on all three suggestions.

Another idea expressed was that we might contact young ski groups to develop awareness and appreciation of the trail in these potential summer trail users.

Trail Standards—Ben proposed trail standards which would:

1. Retain the L.T. as a footpath in the wilderness.
2. Maintain a width sufficient for one tall man with his overnight pack to pass comfortably.
3. Minimize excessive wetness.
4. Provide blazes and trail signs sufficient so that a prudent hiker could follow them.

A straw vote favored this definition for trail standards.

Other suggestions were:

1. We might publish a trail maintenance manual for the GMC.
2. Trail standard in such a manual should not be so specific and uniform as to cause the L.T. to lose its individuality.
3. At the next annual meeting we might have a display and demonstration of trail clearing tools.
4. We could consider setting aside a bit of land for group camping about 1/4 mile off the main trail to relieve the pressure of shelter use in popular areas.
5. We might consider ways of making certain sections of the trail more desirable by cutting more open vistas.
6. We might encourage contributions toward trail maintenance from members who feel they would like to support the GMC in this way.

ANNUAL MEETING REPORT

The GMC Annual Meeting was held on June 1, 1968 at the Bavarian Castle in Proctorsville, Vt. Fine spring weather prevailed and a record 145 members and guests attended, breaking the old record of 134 set in 1966 at the Long Trail Lodge—68 were from Vermont, 33 from Mass., 110 from New England, 24 from New York and New Jersey combined, 3 each from Penn. and Quebec, 2 from Ohio and 1 each from Minn., Colo., and South Vietnam!

Highlight of the morning session was a thought-provoking panel discussion, chaired by Art Koerber, on "New Thoughts for the GMC." Normal business was crowded into an unusually long afternoon session, but 84 members were revived by the excellent dinner served at the Castle.

Dr. Charles C. Houston, Professor of Community Medicine at UVM, presented an outstanding slide lecture in the evening. His first series of slides were taken in 1950 on the rather calm expedition which mapped out the route up the south side of Mount Everest—the route followed by Hillary and Tensing in 1953. The second series documented the tragic third American expedition to K2, which Dr. Houston led in 1953.

MORTIMER R. PROCTOR

On April 28, at the age of 78, one of the Green Mountain Club's greatest benefactors was taken from our midst by a heart attack at his home in Proctor.

Perhaps Mr. Proctor was most known to the average GMC'er because of his generous gift to the Club in 1923. In that year, Mr. Proctor and his mother gave to the Club the Green Mountain Club House, later to be known as the Long Trail Lodge. The sale of the Lodge to the Treadway Hotel chain in the 1950's produced the funds for our Trust Fund, now one of the Club's major sources of income.

Mr. Proctor, who served as Vermont's governor from 1945 to 1947, was one of the early greats in our Green Mountain Club. He served as a Trustee for many years, and was our Club president from 1926 to 1932.

Mr. Proctor's latest gift to the Club was in 1964, when he gave the funds for the construction of Sunrise Shelter. When he offered to finance a new shelter, he was most pleased at our suggestion to build a replacement for old Sunrise Camp. When President Humes dedicated the new shelter in October 1964, it was regrettable that business on the West Coast prevented Mr. Proctor from participating in the dedication.

Mr. Proctor was a great outdoorsman, a lover of our Trail, and a truly devoted member of our Club for so many years. His passing is a great loss to us all.

MINUTES OF THE ANNUAL MEETING, GMC,
HELD JUNE 1

The 58th annual meeting of the Green Mountain Club, Inc. was opened at 10:30 a.m. at the Bavarian Castle in Proctorsville, Vt.

Don Wallace moved and John Rohrbaugh seconded the motion that the minutes of last year's meeting be approved as printed. Accepted.

The meeting recessed for a panel discussion organized by Madeline Miles and moderated by Art Koerber to hear members' thoughts on problems and programs of GMC focusing on Membership, Youth Groups, and Trail Standards. Discussion closed at 12:30 and after being reconvened, the meeting again was recessed for lunch. Resumed at 2:10 p.m.

Section reports were heard from all but Sterling Section and Middlebury Mountain Club. Mr. Heitmann, Deputy Commissioner of the Vermont Forests and Parks Department, presented an encouraging report stating his department hopes to participate more actively with GMC on the trail system in State Forest lands. A proposed statement of Cooperative Agreement was presented to President Bob for the Trustees to consider. It is similar to the agreement between GMC and USPS. Bob Deemer reported on activities of USPS over the past year and stated the closing of the Ripton Job Corp Center had taken place because of budgetary cutbacks.

A written summary of the Trust Fund, submitted by Ted Goddard, was read by President Bob. It stated that income and net worth are up slightly from last year. A motion was passed requesting that printed copies of the Trust fund report be submitted at the annual meeting each year and that the report should list the number of shares and values.

Treasurer George Saunders reviewed the financial report noting that the balance on hand is up above that of a year ago. He submitted two suggestions. First, the club might study needs and use for funds before raising more sums of money. Second, GMC review by-laws regarding Sections dues payments to Main Club. Both the Treasurer's and Trust Fund summary reports were accepted.

Following a few comments from the Club's corresponding secretary, Miss Minerva Hinchey, Ben Rolston introduced the new Guide Book stating that copies are now available. He noted that the Club sells more wholesale copies than retail. He also showed a 1968 edition of the Dartmouth Outing Club's Trail Guide.

Peggy Pons asked for Long Trail News suggestions. She stated the cost of printing has gone up again over the past year. The membership approved the idea of reporting in the LT News any information concerning conservation-oriented meetings. She asked that members inform her of meetings and provide information that would be interesting as a news story.

Conservation Committee report: The Vermont Natural Resources Council is compiling a list of priorities and in response to a request from the Council GMC has submitted its concerns for the Green Mountains.

Plans for a brochure explaining the objectives of GMC were presented.

Two hundred seventy-seven end-to-enders have been reported and many more are expected soon, according to Mrs. Bullock who reported for Carin Schon.

Alan Gormly stated that some new material has been gathered, but still has to be put together. It is hoped that the historical material can be collected at one location.

Attendance: According to Gil Myrick, Annual Meeting Committee Chairman:
     Total in attendance by mid-afternoon: 143 persons.
     Sections: 28 from Killington, 28 at-large, 26 from Burlington.
     By States: 66 from Vermont, 33 from Massachusetts, 13 from New York. Other areas represented: New Jersey, Ohio, Minnesota, Colorado, Quebec, and one person just returned from Vietnam.

Myron Morris reported on plans for the 1968 Intersectional to be held at Lake Eden and stated the flyers were distributed with the May edition of the LT News.

John Paulson reported for the Landowners Committee and stated that ownership is known between the Canadian border and Appalachian Gap and in Southern Vermont. Progress is continuing on this project.

John Rohrbaugh stated that memberships-at-large are rising due in part to yellow signs inviting membership in GMC. Signs may be had, free of charge, from the GMC office.

Art Koerber reported on legislation relating to a National trail system. There are two bills, HR 4865 and S 827 which would establish a Nationwide system of trails. The trails under consideration are the Pacific Crest, Continental Divide, Potomic Heritage, and Appalachian. He explained how the bills could help protect the future of the Long Trail between Wallingford and Sherburne Pass (over which the Appalachian Trail runs). He suggested that members write to Senators asking that they vote in the affirmative on the bills. Art also suggested members urge their representatives to write the chairmen of the Interior and Insular Committees asking them to bring up the bills.

Rod Rice reported that three new shelters have been built. They are Congdon Shelter, 11½ miles east of the former Deerview Shelter, and International Paper Company shelter on Stratton Pond to be named after John Vondell, and a small open-faced shelter donated by Atlas Plywood Company. No shelter was destroyed by fire this past year.

Jack Harrington and a crew of three are lined up for Patrol work starting June 19 or 20. Work will concentrate between Johnson and the Canadian border and in the Sterling Pond area.

By-laws: Two changes proposed. The Amendment warned in the May Long Trail News to Article II, Section 5 was passed by better than a two-thirds majority vote. The second amendment, a technical point, passed unanimously.

John Rohrbaugh moved that the Club instruct the By-laws committee to present to the 1969 Annual Meeting an amendment limiting the terms of office of the officers of the Club. The motion was passed.

Election of officers resulted in the following:
     Miss Minerva Hinchey, Corresponding Secretary
     Mr. Granger Kammerer, Member-at-large Trustee
     Charles B. Gulick, 3rd, Clerk.

The meeting was adjourned at 5:30 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
CHARLES B. GULICK, 3rd, Clerk


GMC FEATURED ON EDUCATIONAL TV

On May 29 the Rod Rice Family were guests on Vermont's ETV program "Vermont Almanac." Rod and Emily gave a most interesting and informative discussion about the Club, its purposes and its membership, as well as facts about the Long Trail, its facilities and opportunity for hiking in a wilderness environment. Rod, Jr., David and Ricky spoke, respectively, on the history of the GMC and the building of the Long Trail, the values of hiking the Trail and what it means to be an "End-to-Ender", and the "reunions" and activities of members at our annual Intersectionals. The program was concluded with the whole family narrating a quick pictorial trip along the Trail.

Many thanks go to the Rices for the marvelous script and their well planned and executed presentation. The coverage of the Club which they presented brings pride to every GMC'er and the information about the Trail was a fine public service for Vermont. Because of the efforts of the Rices many people are more aware of the GMC and its contribution to Vermont's outdoor recreation which makes the unique Long Trail available for the public to enjoy.

opened up.
   From French Camp the Patrol went on to Barrows where
they worked north to Frying Pan Brook, making repairs at
Parker and Ritterbush along the way. They report the trail
in good shape as far as Belvidere Mt.
   This week, July 15th, the Patrol has been moved to Jay
Camp and are working on Jay Peak, relocating the trail on
the north side where several ski trails have been built across
the Long Trail, obscuring it in many places. We hope to
have this area in good condition in time for the Intersec-
tional at Eden so that all hikers interested may be able to
travel it with no trouble.
   There is still some blasting being done around the summit
of Jay and the management of the ski area cautions hikers
to heed any danger signals in the blasting area.
   The black fly season seems to be over the peak—unless
it's the new fly dope the Patrol is using this year. It's called
Old Woodsman and seems to be a mixture of pine tar,
pennyroyal, citronella and various other things. It is easy to
locate members of the Patrol at least a half mile away. It
looks like a good substitute for creosote but smells better.
A good repellant though.

MONTPELIER SECTION

April 7 was a lovely spring day, and 10 members took to the trail between Jonesville and Bolton Lodge, finding very little snow. April 19 was our annual meeting and all principal officers were re-elected: Doris Washburn, President, Frances Holmes, Secretary and Dave Hine, Treasurer. April 21 a large number turned out for a Sunday afternoon hike, on an old road in Worcester. Various members turned back when they felt like it, but some of us penetrated to Eagle Ledge, where we could get a glimpse through to the further side. Spring flowers were just beginning to appear.

In May, 15 canoeists had an outing on the Otter Creek, observing many birds, coons, woodchucks, etc. Also in May a hike up Camel's Hump was substituted for a trip to the Adirondacks because of bad weather. Six members attended the annual meeting in Proctorsville. In June, a day of trail clearing was held in the Sterling Pond area. Work on the cabin will be done later.

June 23 an all-day hike was held over Mt. Belvidere and down to Hazen's Notch. In July we had a climb up Wheeler Mt. in Barton and a picnic at Crystal Lake. Later in July repair work was done to the interior of Sterling Pond Lodge. A trail blazing party is planned for early August. July closed with an overnight trip to Jefferson, N.H. and a climb up Jefferson and Adams. (Beautiful weather!) Future events include blueberrying on a nearby mountain, and mountains near and far to be climbed.

PIONEER VALLEY SECTION

A work party of nineteen reported for duty at the Congdon Camp on May 18, led by Dick Metcalfe. Undismayed by the rain, most everyone stayed over until Sunday, as planned. Although three retreated to motels and two slept in their cars, two set up a tent outside the camp and six settled themselves comfortably inside, where there are ample accommodations for eight. About nine o'clock six hikers from the Trail, weighed down with heavy backpacks, made heavier still by the pouring rain, arrived to spend the night. Somehow room was made for all twelve people. Dorothy Knight retreated under the table with her sleeping bag and the latest arrival slept between the stove and the door. Improvised clotheslines draped with wet socks and miscellaneous clothing overhead, backpacks and a dog below, added to the crowded conditions, but all declared they fared quite well and will remember this as an "Unusual Experience."

The GMC annual meeting at the Barn of the Bavarian Castle in Proctorsville, Vermont, on June 1 was attended by thirteen from the Pioneer Valley Section. It was a pleasant memorable day owing to the fine arrangements made by Gil Myrick, Chairman, who chose this interesting and spacious place.

Bob Boucher led a hike in the Pittsfield State Forest area on June 15. During lunch on top of Tower Mountain the group was joined by Ken Marx, a member-at-large from Pittsfield, Massachusetts. He handed out home-made cakes packed in especially, and he also handed in an application form and became a member of the Pioneer Valley section. New Members are enrolled any time and any place. Peter Grasso had joined during the May work party when he handed in his application form between swings of his brush axe.

WORCESTER SECTION

If you combine a beautiful day in April, 30 people all eager for a spring hike, and interesting terrain over which to hike, you have the makings of a most enjoyable Sunday afternoon which we certainly had. This hike was led by the Arthur Dieldsen's and had the added incentive of "48 caves" to explore and the most appreciated refreshments both at the hike site and again back at their home.

Again in April we enjoyed another local hike with many interesting diversions. We discovered the grave of a former slave and his family deep in the woods on a rocky hillside far from civilization. This discovery was made possible by a study of historical records by our leader. We also explored old cellar holes without any appreciable luck and saw an old porcupine half asleep way up in a tree. The latter was viewed from the top of some high ledges which none of us ever knew existed.

On the last Sunday in April, 27 of us tramped around the woods of the Rutland Brook Wild Life Sanctuary in Barre, Massachusetts. A huge mass of trailing arbutus (mayflower) was found much to the delight of everyone who immediately got down on the ground to sniff their sweet fragrance. We saw an inactive beaver dam and later on discovered where the beavers had moved their endeavors.

A lot of birds were spotted on our now annual birdwalk on the first Sunday in May and delicious pancakes and sausages were eagerly consumed back at our camp and served up by some of our men folks.

Much work was accomplished by our male members at our regular spring work party at Stratton in spite of a rainy Sunday. Luckily Al Gormley got the roof tarred on the Willis Ross Shelter on Saturday.

Bob Humes led us up Mount Wachusett over some old Indian trails on Sunday, May 26. The wild azalea was in bloom on the mountain side along with many other spring flowers. This delightful hike was topped off by refreshments back at the car which Bob thoughtfully provided.

A canoe trip on the Quabbin River with the AMC was enjoyed in June along with the usual swims and sunburns.

On Sunday, June 16, Betty and Richard Church led us up Mt. Monadnock over seldom used trails. A white throated sparrow singing to his love was most accommodating. An excellent picture could have been secured if anyone had thought to bring along the proper lens. Did any of you ever climb this mountain twice in the same day? Well, we did!

This year 66 people enjoyed the shortcakes at our annual Strawberry Social at our camp and for a change it didn't rain. In the evening our attic treasures were auctioned off by Eleanor and Bill Patterson and by my better half, Fred Eldridge. They sold everything they had which shows how good they are at this job. One item, an electric grille, has been resold 3 years in a row, each time returned by a satisfied customer who has gone on to a larger product. Maybe next year it will be your turn!

LINNEA ELDRIDGE, Reporter

DARTMOUTH OUTING CLUB TRAIL GUIDE

When we received a copy of the new DOC Trail Guide, an accompanying letter stated: "If you note any similarities between the DOC Trail Guide and the Long Trail Guide, they are purely intentional. We are deeply indebted to the GMC for what we feel to be a very practical format."

This well-written Guide describes the DOC's portion of the Appalachian Trail (74 miles), from Vermont Highway 12 to New Hampshire Highway 112 at Kinsman Notch, plus numerous connecting trails.

We are pleased to note that the editor (Doug Coonrad), and two of the writers (Dave Agan and Phil Koch), are Long Trail End-to-Enders.

This Guide, the first one since the 1932 DOC Handbook, may be obtained for $1.75 plus 25¢ for postage and handling from the Dartmouth Outing Club, Dartmouth College, Hanover, N.H. 03755
