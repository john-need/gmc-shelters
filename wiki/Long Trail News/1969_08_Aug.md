       In looking back over the spring outings, it is interesting
    to note that there appears to be a correlation between the
    number of participants and the expenditure of energy in-
    volved for any specific activity. Only four hikers climbed
    Laraway Mountain (on snowshoes in mid-April), five spent
    the better part of two days traveling 18 miles east from
•   Sherburne Pass along the Appalachian Trail, 10 reached
    the top of Jay Peak, and a very few faithful members
    turned out for trail clearing and work parties.

    to visit a sugar house (could samples of the Vermont
    "nectar" have been an added incentive?), 143 attended the
    annual sugar party and square dance, 28 rambled a mile
    or two up a woods road in Richmond to a spot affording
    lovely mountain views, 25 hiked to Bolton Lodge with a
    spaghetti dinner as the goal, and 19 in a fleet of six canoes
    paddled along Otter Creek.

    struggled to the top of Mt. Elmore (in late April), and 18
