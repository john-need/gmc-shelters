   On the surface, at least, most of us seem reasonably
convinced that these problems do indeed exist and that
the great amounts of time, effort, and money devoted to
dealing constructively with them are justified. However,
there remain at least a few skeptics who believe that the
situation is being overdramatized, and undoubtedly there
are many others who willingly accept what is said as true
but who nevertheless have not yet been forcibly struck
by the seriousness of rapidly increasing trail use and its
impact on the trail system. Just as slide shows and travelogs
are no real substitute for an actual Long Trail experience,
neither can repeated messages about trail overuse and
abstract figures on a budget sheet have the impact of an
actual observation trip where one consciously tries to ob-
serve the deficiencies and ravages of the trail itself in-
stead of looking at the views and the other attractive fea-
tures which normally occupy our attention. You may not
like all that you find, but until each and every one of us
is personally and acutely aware of just how much remains
to be done to insure a quality trail in a quality environ-
ment, the chances for success in effectively coping with
the worst threat to the Long Trail as we would like _to
know it-the hiking public itself-will be that much more
limited.

   Another feature of the May 20th trip was the planting
of a Delicious apple tree at the BM lodge with the hope
that some years hence hikers will find a little fresh fruit
to enhance their trail eating fare. Ten days later the tree
not only was sprouting healthily but was already bearing
a large Mcintosh apple-with a wire stem.
   June 6 saw a special and major work trip for trail clear-
ing and blaze repainting of the LT south from French
Camp to the top of Whiteface Mountain. French Camp,
incidentally, is in beautiful shape thanks to the interest
and efforts of Mollie and Pat Yadanza of Sparkill, N.Y.
who about two years ago became the camp's "foster par-
ents" under the GMC's "Adopt-A-Shelter" program.

balance of the summer including trips to hopefully refind
and explore two old trails with a view to a pos£ible later
