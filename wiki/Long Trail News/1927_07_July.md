THE LONG TRAIL NEWS

Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.
Brandon, Vermont, July, 1927

It has been decreed by the powers that be that there should be an issue of the News midway between the April and December issues in order to satisfy the demands of the Club and the world in general, and so, as Lindbergh remarked on another historic occasion, we are. This will enable us to give the latest news while it is resident in the minds of the hiking season, when it will be of more interest than if postponed four months. Whether this experiment shall become a permanent feature will depend upon what the demand for it seems to be.

Special Meeting of the Trustees.

As arranged at the annual meeting of the trustees in March, a special meeting was held at the Lodge in Sherburne Pass June 11, forenoon and afternoon. Many matters of interest to the welfare of the Club were discussed, and reports were made by the trail committee, the treasurer and the secretary. Reports from the former, and from the groups and sections having in charge the different divisions of the trail, indicated that good progress had been made in clearing the trail, and that it would be in good condition by July 1 or earlier. The Worcester section will take care of the northernmost portion ending at Jay Peak this year, but hereafter will prefer to put in their work nearer home, on the southern part of the trail. It was announced that three new cabins will probably be built this season, one in the vicinity of Jay Peak, one at Sterling Pond, and one near the Massachusetts line. President Proctor reported on the results so far achieved by the various methods for enlarging the membership which had been tried, none of which seemed to have been particularly successful, the most so being the publicity articles on the Long Trail which had been written by members and syndicated in a large number of newspapers. A letter from a member was read, which expressed the writer's disapproval of a membership drive, as he feared the result would be the overcrowding of the Trail and depriving it of its wild woodland charms, but his alarm seems to be premature and there are no immediate prospects of any hot dog stands on the Trail, unless at points where it crosses highways. A plan tried by the Middlebury section was described, which consisted of leaving addressed postal cards in Pleiad lodge, containing printed instructions for filling out and return by those who wished to know more about the Club, and those who were ready to join, and it was decided to try this plan on a larger scale and deposit such cards in some of the other cabins. Complaints of vandalism at some of the camps were made, and a committee was appointed to formulate rules or suggestions for the conduct of hikers who probably err thru ignorance. It was voted to send Theron S. Dean as representative of the Club to Montgomery, Vt., to attend a "Jay Peak day" gathering June 25.

The trustees were unanimous in their approval of the changes which had been made since last season at and about the Lodge, and in their appreciation of the cuisine and of the attentions of the efficient hostess, Mrs. Griffen.

Those in attendance were J. A. Allis, New York; George F. E. Storey, Worcester, Mass.; Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; Miss Edith Estabrook, Brattleboro; Prof. L. B. Puffer and Miss Mabel Brownell, Burlington; Profs. R. H. White and P. C. Voter, Middlebury; Prof. W. C. O'Kane, Durham, N. H.; W. M. Ross, Rutland; Miss Laura E. Woodward, Montclair, N. J.; Louis Bigelow, Worcester, Mass.; Herbert W. Congdon, Arlington; E. S. Marsh, Brandon; the four last named acting as proxies for trustees unable to attend.

Mr. Bigelow, in the afternoon, as an appetizer for the evening meal, climbed Killington, making the round-trip to the summit and return in four and one-half hours. Others contented themselves with climbing Deer's Leap and walking to "Adirondack View." Misses Brownell and Estabrook drove to Brandon Sunday and did the Du-Val trail, underestimating slightly the time it would take and missing the train, but well satisfied nevertheless.

Hikers' Golden Rules.

The following have been prepared by the committee for that purpose, and will be posted in every cabin on the Trail:

A lot of men have worked hard to build these trails and shelters in order that all may enjoy the woods and mountains freely.

LET'S HELP.

Hide rubbish where it can't be seen or else burn it.

Build camp fires only in safe places, and before leaving see that every spark is put out.

Take care of shelters: Help them to last that they may be enjoyed as long as possible.

Renew dry wood when used: A supply on hand is a joy to the tramper coming in late at night or in a rain storm.

Protect the wild flowers and all the other interesting, attractive objects of the wilderness.

Protect the wild animals. It is more fun to watch them than to kill them.

LET'S ENJOY THE TRAIL.

Take time to see things as you hike.

Learn the names of the mountains that you see from the summits.

Carry a compass and a guide book.

Support the Green Mountain Club. Keep the mountains green and the trails white.

Help us with new shelters and trails.

Always keep in mind the Golden Rule and
Remember the other fellow.

Obituary.

E. C. Horners, a leading business man of Rutland, Vt., passed away early in June. A former trustee of the Club, he was present at the annual meetings and was much interested in its welfare. He will be long remembered for his many sterling qualities and much missed from the circle in which he was prominent.

A New Section Formed.

The Club was ably represented at a "Jay Peak Day" celebration June 25 by James P. Taylor and Theron S. Deane. The rendezvous was the hamlet of Montgomery Center, whence the celebrants proceeded to a point near the beginning of the trail up the mountain, where they indulged in a basket picnic and the band played. Then Mr. Taylor delivered an address replete with wit and wisdom. After a historical sketch of the Club and the Long Trail Mr. Taylor said that the Trail is a distinctive feature, which has brought more publicity to the state than anything else, with the single exception of President Coolidge. "We are riding in automobiles and growing fat and lazy" he continued, "If we don't get out and walk, our children will be deprived of the use of their lower limbs and will have to be carried around in baskets. The first thing we know." "Jay Peak is a distinctive mountain" he said, "and you should appreciate it and honor it and give it the same high regard that the Japanese people give to Fujiyama."

Mr. Dean spoke with his usual magnetic enthusiasm, and at the close of his address called for volunteers to work the trail and build a cabin. When 25 responded he said that a section of the Club would be formed then and there, and it was, twenty men and five women joining and paying their dues. It is expected that more will join and an organization be completed in the near future. This is an encouraging beginning. A few years ago a Jay Peak section was started with members principally from Newport and neighboring towns, but after languishing a while it died in infancy. It is hoped that the constitution of the new section will be more robust, and that it will enjoy a long life of prosperity and usefulness.

A Long Trail Marathon.

Irving D. Appleby of Roxbury, Mass., who claims to have tramped the entire Long Trail from north to south in 1926 in a record time variously stated at 12 days, 12 days 5 hours, and 14 days 7 hours, is at present writing making an attempt to equal or exceed this record from south to north. He started from Williamstown, Mass., July 2, and expected to be at Long Trail Lodge July 4. He was accompanied by F. C. Jameson, described as the champion heel-and-toe walker, who soon found out that something besides heel-and-toe walking was needed on the Long Trail, and gave out at Stratton Mountain. They emerged from the Trail at Manchester, and Appleby proceeded alone. As a result of the delay he was two days

Notes.

Some of the members of the Williams College Outing Club are working on an extension of the Long Trail to the summit of Mt. Greylock. A good move.

Progress is being made on the Appalachian Trail from Deer's Leap to Hanover, N.H. Dartmouth college boys have completed the trail five miles west of Hanover. The G.M.C. Trail committee are working on it between the Sherburne Pond and Lake Lakota.

Miss Laura E. Woodward, of Montclair, N.J., for many years the hard-working secretary of the New York section and now its president, attended the special meeting of the trustees, afterwards visiting Prof. Monroe down on the farm. This was her first visit to Vermont.

Publicity Articles.

Willis M. Ross attended the seventh annual national conference on state parks in May, held at Bear Mountain in the Palisades reservation, N.Y. He went as the representative of President Proctor, and told the delegates what the Green Mountain Club had done in opening up the Green Mountains through the Long Trail.

The following articles written by members of the Club, have been or will be published this year in nearly 200 newspapers in New England, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Ohio and Illinois. They have aroused great interest and brought in a vast number of inquiries. It is probable that other articles will be written and published next year.

Two representatives of the Boston Globe have been travelling over the Trail, for the purpose of preparing an illustrated article for that paper. North of Sherburne Pass they lost the Trail and spent the afternoon in finding it again, coming out to the highway in their wanderings. But that was before it was cleared and marked.

The Vermont Long Trail is Completed. By Raymond H. Torrey.

Capt. Herbert Wheaton Congdon, well-known cartographer of the Long Trail, lecturer on and general booster for the same, ex-high bailiff and present sheriff of Bennington county, and representative from Arlington (Vt.) to the legislature, has abandoned the state of single blessedness and become a Benedict. His hosts of friends extend congratulations, and wish him a long and happy married life.

What Vermont's Long Trail is Like. By Herbert W. Congdon.

Theron S. Dean of Burlington represented the Club on the Vermont Special, which in May toured the Middle West and Canada, stopping at many of the principal cities. He preached the Long Trail gospel as opportunity offered, and reported much interest and many inquiries. Mr. Dean also represented the Club at the meeting of the State Chamber of Commerce at Montpelier, June 2 and 3, with an exhibit of the large 7 foot map of the Trail, and photographs, and a placard giving information concerning the Trail. The exhibit aroused much interest.

Two of our members, Mr. and Mrs. Paul M. Redington of Shrewsbury, Vt., have been climbing mountains in Maine, New Hampshire and Vermont this year, and have also climbed some of the Rockies, and think as a result of their experience that the Green Mountains, altho not as high as some of the others, offer more in the way of attractive scenery than any of them. They climbed Mt. Washington, Jay Peak and Mt. Mansfield in the same week.
