THE LONG TRAIL NEWS
Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.

Rutland, Vermont, April, 1928.

The Long Trail News by bi-monthly publication, the committees, at their last meeting, voted to at last to give it a trial. The principal question of it, then will be, whether sufficient worth-while material can be had for such a periodical. This will depend largely on the sections. If the secretary of each section will it stand in at the end of every other month will of the important and interesting items in such section, it can probably be done. Also letters on any Club topics will be welcome. Let us make it the news organ of the sections, as well as the membership at large. Secretaries, send in your contributions for the next issue not later than May 25.

Annual Meeting.

The annual meeting of the Club was held January 14 at the Berwick Hotel, Rutland, Vt., forenoon and afternoon. It was graced by the presence of the so-called "Three Musketeers", the three young women who tramped the entire trail last summer, being the first women to perform that feat, viz., Misses Hilda M. Kurth, Kathleen M. Norris and Catherine E. Robbins. They came as the guests of the Club, and gave interesting accounts of their experiences.

Each section reported on its activities during 1927, including its work on the Trail, from which, together with the report of the trail committee, it appeared that the entire Trail had been inspected and put in order, and the camps repaired where needed. W. M. Ross, for the trail committee, reported the expenditure of $698 on trails and shelters during the year. The New York section reported, by Miss Woodward, its President, that it had sent $1100 for flood relief to Prof. Will S. Monroe, together with quantities of supplies, and Prof. Monroe told how he had distributed them.

The treasurer's report showed that the Club is in excellent financial condition, receipts for the past year having been $2,429, and disbursements $1,691, the larger items of the latter being $700 for trails and shelters, guide books, $284, secretary's salary and expenses, $278, Lodge, $150, printing, $143. A $500 bond of the Monongahela West Penn Public Service Co. was purchased for $507.45. The assets on January 9 consisted of the above bond; Liberty bonds, $1450; German bond, $100; and cash in banks, $1890.

The secretary reported that the total membership is now 1635, a gain of 94 for the year. This is made up of membership at large, 556, including 35 life members, a gain of two in the latter, and in the sections: Bennington, 46; Burlington, 145; Rutland, 300; Middlebury, 126; Montgomery, 25; New York, 362; Proctor, 26; Worcester, 49. Over 650 inquiries were received, and 884 guide books were sold.

Trustees were elected as follows: Robert M. Ross, Montpelier; Wallace M. Fay, Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; J. L. Griswold, Bennington; Prof. L. B. Puffer, Burlington; Profs. R.H. White and P. C. Voter, Middlebury; E. S. Marsh, Brandon; Herbert W. Congdon, Arlington; C. J. Cleveland, W. M. Ross, Rutland; Fred H. Tucker, Boston; Geo. F. E. Storey, Worcester, Mass.; Prof. Walter C. O'Kane, Durham, N. H.; R. H. Torrey, J. A. Allis, New York.

W. M. Ross was re-elected clerk.

In the absence of the president, the vice-president, Prof. White, presided.

There was some discussion of the sayings and doings of Mr. Appleby, which culminated in the following resolutions, introduced by Prof. O'Kane and unanimously adopted:

Resolved: That the Green Mountain Club believes in the use of its trails for real enjoyment and appreciation of the mountains and forests, and for release and relief from the hurry and confusion prevalent in modern life, and that it welcomes especially all who come with the purpose of seeking out quietly and discerningly the beauties of Vermont's great wilderness.

Resolved: That the Green Mountain Club is not interested in speed records on the Trail.

When the above was handed in to the Clerk, as he says, the second resolution had been deleted, so that it does not appear in the records. How this could be properly done without submission to the meeting for reconsideration does not appear. We therefore give the resolutions as passed, with this explanation.

   We have not space for all of his notes and comments, but note that a wild cat beheaded a rabbit in his immediate presence on this trip, and the same thing happened (he says) in 1926. He says that he lost in various ways 2 days and 18 hours, so that he can hardly see how his actual hiking time on the Trail, eliminating time lost on account of bad trail, amounted to more than 7 or 7½ days.

The Long Trail Lodge.

   The popularity of the Lodge continues unabated, as shown by the fact that the gross receipts last year were nearly $1000 ahead of 1926, but financially the season of 1927 must be considered a failure, as, apparently due to mismanagement somewhere, the profit of $1015 shown in 1926 was turned into a deficit of $1332, to which must be added the expense of the care-taker during the winter. The gross receipts in 1927 were $14,147, and it would seem as tho some profit should have been saved from that amount of receipts. Insurance carried on the property is $18,300.
   Mrs. Harriet V. Bates, a Massachusetts lady, has been engaged as hostess for the 1928 season. She has had several years experience in the management of a hostelry of about the same capacity as the Lodge.

Trails and Shelters.

   The trail committee are not announcing any great amount of new construction this year, but plan to outdo themselves, if possible, in putting the Trail and camps in good condition. The New York section will, as heretofore, take care of the Monroe Skyline part of the Trail, and will no doubt do their usual thorough job. The new Montgomery section will care for the Trail from Belvidere Mountain to Jay Peak, and will build a new camp at Hazen's Notch. The Worcester section will be assigned a portion of the southerly end of the Trail.
   The Lake Pleiad section of Middlebury will build a new camp between Breadloaf and Wilson mountains, to be known as Breadloaf lodge, and a log tower on Breadloaf mountain, giving visitors the full benefit of the fine view from this point. They will also build some new side trails, and clear and re-locate others.
   Work on the Du Val trail extension in Brandon and Goshen is well advanced, and the complete trail will be ready by June 1, affording a walk of about 20 miles, about one-fourth of which will be on the Long Trail. A blue-print map of this trail has been made.
