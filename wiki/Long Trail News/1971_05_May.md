THE NEED FOR A CONSULTANT—NOW

The Green Mountain Club, Inc., has done a commendable job for many years using volunteer help for the most part. Times are changing. There is much more usage of the Trail, environmental deterioration, and land ownership complications. There is need for legislative action and trail agreements. We are unable to remain a small, contained club, happily maintaining our trails and shelters. We must get involved with major problems—like it or not—if the Long Trail System is going to survive.

Important projects are already underway which require much more attention now than volunteer help can provide. These include the legislature joint resolution, completion of our land ownership maps, review of present legislation and trail agreements, study of possible future trail legislation, preparation of a manual of procedure, trail standards and shelters and the tax status of the Club.

Some of these items are "one shot" in nature; some will be with us for a long time. It was felt that a "catalyst" was needed to secure more volunteers and to help spearhead the projects.

We proposed that Shirley Strong who was available be contracted with as a consultant to work on specified projects for the Club approximately three days weekly between March 1 and July 31. After considerable discussion, the Trustees voted to contract with Shirley as a consultant.

We are certain that much will be accomplished in this short period of time and this emergency will prove to be an excellent investment. We are confident that Miss Strong's final report to the Club on her activities as consultant will demonstrate that "the price was right."

SNOWMOBILE AND ATV LEGISLATION

Restrictive legislation for the operation of snowmobiles and ATVs in Vermont has been a key environmental issue this legislative session. The Green Mountain Club endorsed one bill and urged strengthening another bill at hearings held by both Senate and House Committees.

S-55, the bill endorsed by the Club, would regulate the use of off-road vehicles such as trailbikes and ATVs (All Terrain Vehicles) and would, among other provisions, restrict their operation to private property with written permission of the land owner, or on public trails specifically designated for such use by the Agency of Environmental Conservation. The Agency would be authorized to designate such trails only when it might find that "natural, fish and wildlife and other recreational aesthetic and public values" would not be affected. President Shirley Strong testified on behalf of the bill and said in conclusion, "The Club strongly supports the provisions of this bill." At this writing (March 26) the bill was still in the Senate Natural Resources Committee. With strong support for such legislation, there is little doubt but what this will be passed.

S-63, the bill that the Club urged be strengthened, would tighten up on the present laws governing snowmobiles. It would impose noise limits (not to exceed 82 decibels on the A scale at 50 feet) by September 1972 and would authorize the Department of Public Safety to establish lower noise limits after September 1973. This bill would also prohibit operation of snowmobiles on trails and other areas of public lands designated by the Agency of Environmental Conservation and posted accordingly, or on private lands that have been posted as closed to snowmobiles. President Shirley Strong urged that these provisions be made more restrictive; specifically, that snowmobile use be prohibited except on public land posted to permit such use and on private land with the permission of the land owner. She also urged that more adequate publicity be provided about areas on public lands closed to snowmobile operation, to include newspaper publicity, published maps and pamphlets, etc. This bill has good prospects for passage probably in close to its form as originally introduced. An amendment to prohibit operation on private lands except with the permission of the land owner, though strongly supported by conservationists, was defeated in the Senate.

In addition, a resolution has been introduced calling for a study committee to consider all matters concerning snowmobiles and other off-road vehicles, and especially an in-depth study of the trespass laws. Its report would be due in the fall.

At least twenty other bills of special interest to Green Mountain Club members have been introduced. These include bills concerned with conservation, pollution, farm and forest land taxation, stream flow alteration, endangered species of wildlife and plants, non-returnable cans and bottles, roadside camping, marine toilets, etc.

DAVE OTIS, Chairman

RESOLUTION RE: GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB AND
HIKING TRAILS

   This spring the Vermont Legislature passed the following joint resolution (J. R. S. 22) regarding the Green
Mountain Club and hiking trails in Vermont. The resolution, proposed by the Club, was sponsored in the Senate
by Senators Gibb, Doyle, Partridge, Newell and Jones,
the first three of whom are GMC members.
   Its passage recognizes the GMC's leadership role and
represents a significant step in the Club's efforts to ensure
the preservation and proper use of the Long Trail and
other hiking trails in Vermont, including the Appalachian
Trail.

J. R. S. 22

   Whereas, the Green Mountains constitute a most characteristic natural feature of our state, and
   Whereas, the Green Mountains in themselves afford the
inspiration for the naming of Vermont, and
   Whereas, the Green Mountains are symbolic of the
strength, beauty and integrity of our natural environment,
and
   Whereas, the Green Mountain Club was founded in
1910 by well-revered Jim Taylor for the purpose of "making the mountains play a larger part in the lives of the
people," and
   Whereas, the Green Mountain Club has continuously
and effectively carried out its stated purpose by pioneering,
developing, maintaining and protecting the famous Long
Trail as a "foot path in the Wilderness" along the crest of
these mountains from Massachusetts to Canada and has
augmented this with an extensive system of side trails, and
   Whereas, the continued existence of the Long Trail
system and the preservation of its usefulness, beauty and
natural character is threatened by the rapid encroachment
of residential, commercial and business activity, now
therefore be it
   Resolved by the Senate and House of Representatives:
   That the Green Mountain Club be recognized by the
General Assembly as the founder, sponsor, defender and
protector of the Long Trail system for now and future
generations to come, and be it further
   Resolved: That the Green Mountain Club be entrusted
with the responsibility for leadership in developing policies
and coordinating efforts of its members and other similar
organizations and individuals equally concerned with the
preservation, maintenance and proper use of hiking trails
for the benefit of the people of Vermont, and be it further
   Resolved: That the Green Mountain Club be requested
to conduct a continuing study of all matters pertaining to
hiking trails and that the club report to the General Assembly such action as it deems may be required to insure
the preservation, maintenance and proper use of the Long
Trail system and other similar hiking trails with due regard
for the rights and interests of land owners over whose
lands the various trails may pass, and be it further
   Resolved: That a copy of this resolution be forwarded
by the Secretary of State to the President of the Green
Mountain Club, Inc., Rutland, Vermont.

SUMMARY OF TRUSTEES MEETINGS

Action taken by the Trustees at recent meetings and not covered elsewhere in the News is briefly described below.

February 6, 1971 Trustees' Meeting

The trustees rescinded their action taken at the October 3, 1970, meeting relative to establishing an "executive committee." In lieu of this, President Strong appointed an advisory committee consisting of the five trail supervisors, Granger Kammerer, Bob Attenborough, George Pearlstein, Nick Metcalf, Rod Rice, Doug Wallace and Dane Shortsleeve. The advisory committee will assist her in making decisions on GMC business between trustees' meetings.

The trustees voted to renew the agreement with the Howard National Bank of Burlington Vt. for the handling of the trust fund. The trustees gave full authority to the bank for handling the portfolio but stipulated that either party could cancel the agreement on a thirty day notice and that there would be no cancellation fee.

Because of the several important projects now underway by the GMC that need full time attention, it was decided to contract with Shirley Strong as a consultant to work approximately three days a week for the period March 1 to July 31. She will receive a compensation of $2,100 including services transportation, postage and telephone. She will be working on the joint IGC solution, completion of land ownership maps, review of legislation, preparing a manual of procedures, trail agreements and trail and shelter projects.

In order to learn more about what the members-at-large think about long range objectives of the GMC, it was voted to send out a survey form to them with their renewal notice.

A contribution of $500 was approved for the work of the Green Mountain Profile Committee.

The 15,000 "Short Hikes on the Long Trail" folders published February 1970 have been used up and it was voted to re-publish this folder in essentially the same format as the last time. The new edition will include more emphasis on proper use of the trails.

Upon request of the Burlington Section the trustees voted to publish a separate map of the trails on Mt. Mansfield as one of the services of the Club. It was felt that there was a definite need for such a map and that if the MC did not publish it someone else would. In addition to the map showing the trails coded for difficulty there will be a text included that will explain the fragile ecology of the mountain and how hikers can help to maintain this ecology. By having this text the map will also serve as a useful educational tool. The map will be printed on a good quality water repellent paper and will sell for fifty cents. There will be an initial printing of 5,000.

Much of the meeting was devoted to hearing reports from people responsible for the various trails and shelters programs and discussing how the Club can maintain good facilities in the face of increasing use. The discussion included dump problems, caretakers for the summer, the Long Trail Patrol, the "Adopt a Shelter" program, trail relocation and a report from a special committee studying the summit of Mt. Mansfield. President Strong announced a special workshop meeting on May 1 and 2 that will be devoted entirely to trail and shelter problems.

Consultant Shirley Strong gave a report of her accomplishments for the first three weeks under her contract. A detailed report will be made at the conclusion of the contract.

Publicity Chairman, George Plumb distributed copies of the Publicity Committee's planned program. The committee has developed a one year schedule of monthly releases to help inform the public about the work of the GMC. He also announced that the new slide program with a taped narration will be available in April.

The trustees devoted a considerable amount of time considering the many needs of the GMC and how these needs are to be reflected in the budget. They gave tentative approval to a budget calling for expenses of $19,920. Adjustments and final approval will be made at the May meeting.

President Strong announced that she had appointed George Pearlstein to serve as chairman of the Nominating Committee. Members of the committee are Arthur Koerber and David Otis.

GEORGE PLUMB, Clerk

The trustees voted to increase the order for the Long Trail Guidebook from 8,000 to 10,000. This decision was made because there are already orders for about 2,000 of the new edition and the trustees would like to have a supply that would last at least two years.

BURLINGTON SECTION

Officers elected at the Burlington Section annual meeting January 16 were Newell Briggs, president; Kenneth Boyd, vice president; Marion Holmes, secretary; and Ronald Nemcosky, treasurer. Dr. Hubert Vogelmann (UVM), Chairman of the Green Mountain Profile Committee, spoke to the group about the ecology of mountain areas, describing particularly the delicate balance of natural forces slowly established over a long period of time.
   As most of us know too well, it is possible for man to alter this balance radically in only a few hours. With this in mind, the Burlington Section is emphasizing better maintenance and proper use of the trails in the heavily hiked Mt. Mansfield area. A map of the area will include new regulations (see details elsewhere in TN). Bill DeGroot is expanding the trails committee and the caretaker program will be continued and enlarged.
   The record snow depth for 1970–71 was no deterrent to members who turned out in good numbers for a variety of outings. If anything, the intense cold and deep snow were an added challenge.
   Snowshoes or cross-country skis were a "must" for most trips. Hikers shared one trail with snowmobilers in an atmosphere of, according to one observer, "mutual tolerance."
   Some trips were picturesque, some gourmet and many, fortunately, were both. A brilliant winter sunset lighted hikers on their way out from a spaghetti dinner at Bolton Lodge. A hike in the Adirondacks followed one of the winter's heavy snowstorms with a brook beside the trail offering the combined scenic effects of water, snow and ice. A hike at Breadloaf explored some of the Robert Frost country, including a cabin the poet used, and was marred only by the incompatibility of a poodle and a Labrador retriever who were also making the trip.
   Late in February eleven hikers bivouacked in Gorham Lodge near the summit of Camel's Hump. On a weekend backpacking trip in the Adirondacks, a group of ten slept in the snow despite below zero temperatures. The following day they climbed East and South Dix. Four continued to hike Hough and Dix while the remainder returned to break camp and await the after dark arrival of the others.
   The annual sugar-on-snow party was well attended and an excellent opportunity for newcomers to sample a Vermont specialty. Members gave high praise to Mr. Pike, who demonstrated sugaring, to the scenic beauty of his Stowe sugarbush, and to the efficiency of the youth group who served the dinner.

SALLY S. SPEAR, Reporter


CONNECTICUT SECTION

The annual meeting of the Connecticut Section was held on March 23, with the following slate elected for the coming year: President–Bob Poole, 1st V.P.–George Bragdon, 2nd V.P.–Herb VanWinkelen, Treasurer–Ben Means, Secretary–Glenna Hale.
   Connecticut Section is in the throes of getting building materials to the site of the new Kid Gore Shelter they are building at the Caughnawaga site on the Long Trail. Last fall they felled and peeled the requisite logs for framework but still need sawed lumber for bunks, rafters and shingles for roofing. This site is so remote that some effort (I might say heroic effort) has been made to seek a feasible route for the purpose of hauling the material in by snowmobile. On February 7 and again on March 20 several members, some with snowmobiles, assembled at the site of the former town of Somerset, 6½ miles north of Route 9, along the western branch of the Deerfield River. By ferrying the explorers part way in to the site by snow vehicle it was hoped the best route to the actual site could be discovered. Two attempts have not yet yielded the secret, which may well be locked beneath several feet of snow, much more being found there in late March than in early February. So the matter is still in the hands of Mother Nature, Vermont having had an inordinate amount of snow this winter.
   Some energetic members made a safari to Condon Shelter to celebrate New Years. Otherwise the Section has many plans for trips, both hiking and canoeing, and work parties galore, but by the time this news appears, most of that will be history.
   There is before the Connecticut Legislature a bill for the protection of the section of the AT that is in Connecticut. During the public hearing one of our members, who is a senior at Kent School, is to be there and indicate the GMC's interest in it.

LYDIA JUNE, Reporter

MONTPELIER SECTION

The snow fall on the first day of spring this year broke the all time record with the season of 1970-1971 having a greater fall than the previous record which was made in the winter of 1886-1887. PIONEER VALLEY SECTION

Members of the Pioneer Valley Section took advantage of the season again this winter with a schedule of frequent snowshoe hikes. Thirty-three members and guests snowshoed in to the Bouchers farm in Plainfield, Massachusetts for a well-earned meal of delicious clam chowder and assorted home-made goodies. Two routes were taken to the farm. Those of us who took the longer tour were surprised to find food left as Dick Metcalfe, who led the other group, arrived before us.

Our annual hike in to Congdon Camp, a joint hike with the Connecticut Section on the Tunxis Trail in Connecticut, and hikes in Whately and Tyringham, Massachusetts rounded out our recent activities on snowshoes.

The fifth annual meeting of the Pioneer Valley Section was held on February 14. Preceding a buffet supper, E. Porter Dickinson led seventeen avid snowshoers over icy snow on a hike in the Dry Brook Glen area south of Mt. Holyoke. (I'm told that the brook was far from dry!) Thirty-six members and seven guests were entertained before the business meeting by William A. Tompkins, of the Forest Park (Springfield) Museum. Mr. Tompkins presented an excellent slide show of our National Parks from the viewpoint of the naturalist. (Would you notice the only plant whose roots are seated in four states? Or the first plant in the country to feel the sun's rays each day?) At the business meeting reports were given on the trustees' meeting and by the Trails and Shelters Activities and Membership chairmen. Changes in by-laws were discussed as was participation in local earth Week activities. We will have an exhibit at the Springfield Science Museum on April 17 and 18, highlighting the Long Trail. The following officers were all re-elected: President, Peter Grasso; Vice President, Robert Boucher; Secretary, Dorothy Knight; and Treasurer, Elizabeth Ahearn. Assigned by the President were: Activities and Membership, Dorothy Knight; Trails and Shelters, William MacArthur; Long Trail News Reporter, Douglas James; Conservation, Mason Ingram; and Trustee Richard Metcalfe.

In his Trails and Shelters report Bill MacArthur outlined plans for moving Seth Warner Shelter away from the adjacent wood road to a location across the brook and closer to the new Long Trail relocation. It is expected that the move will be made in early April when the snow is a foot or so deep, with the help of a bulldozer and plenty of muscle.

Members at large and other hikers are invited to join us in our outings and may obtain information about our activities from our Secretary, Mrs. Dorothy Knight, 152 Lucerne Road, Springfield, Massachusetts; telephone (413) 783-6052 evenings. Some of our future plans include: May 9-joint hike with Worcester Section in Shenipsit State Park in Connecticut; May 15, 16-Green Mountain Club Work Days on the Long Trail; June 13-joint hike with Worcester Section to Redemption Rock and Wachusett Mountain; June 26, 27-mountain laurel camping weekend in Southwestern Massachusetts; July 10-hike on Taconic Ridge.

NEW ENGLAND TRAIL CONFERENCE

The 54th annual meeting of the New England Trail Conference was held at Smith College, Northampton, Mass. on April 3, 1971. The GMC was well represented by over thirty of its members.

During the business meeting, reports were read from member organizations on trail and shelter conditions. Bob Attenborough reported that the Long Trail was generally in good condition—mentioning the relocations in the Glastonbury area, and possible changes in the Stratton area—all of which will be well marked with signs. He spoke of our "no dump" and caretaker programs and the new edition of the guidebook.

The afternoon session was divided into four groups: Nature Along the Trail, Trail Standards and Conservation, Health and Safety on the Trail and Favorite Trails.

Ed Garvey, who recently completed the AT end-to-end, gave us some of his observations. He had very little trouble with the "Footway," but blazings and markings were inadequate in some areas. More serious was the lack of markings to water supplies—springs and streams. He felt that the "no dump" system is the only answer for the future.

Les Holmes, Executive Director of ATC, outlined some shelter standards that the ATC has set up. He was concerned with the problem of youth groups on the trail, and feels that they must adhere to these standards that have been established.

The evening program "A Respect For Life" was a colored slide presentation by Les Campbell, FPSA, Hon. NEC, of Belchertown, Mass.

The prevalent question throughout the entire day seemed to be "how can we protect the wilderness?"

BETTY CHURCH

MT. MANSFIELD PROJECT

The Burlington Section has set up a special committee, with Charles Hadden as chairman, to promote better use and management of the Mt. Mansfield area this summer. This committee is coordinating efforts on behalf of the Green Mountain Club, the Forests & Parks Dept., and the University of Vermont in planning a program of action and study.

Summer plans include two new regulations—no open fires on Mansfield (hikers to this area should carry a stove) and no camping on the top along the ridge (at special request of the University). Signs including these new regulations and promoting the "carry in, carry out" policy, noting the caretaker program and the limited sanitary facilities on the top of the mt. etc., will be erected at trail heads. Publicity via news releases and articles will also be used to alert the public that this area is a fragile area and its use must be restricted accordingly.

A traffic flow study, trail statistics, and improved trail maintenance are also planned. The expansion of the GMC caretaker program in the Mansfield area is an important part of the program, with a caretaker at Taylor as well as at Taft and Butler this summer. Through cooperative efforts of University personnel, additional studies of the vegetation, geology, drainage patterns, etc., may also be undertaken this summer, providing a better overall picture of the mountain and its problems—people!

ARTIST VIEWS ENVIRONMENT

GMCer Arnold W. Lahee, whose paintings have been on exhibition in rotation at the Northfield Savings Bank since November 1966, is to have what he calls his "jumbo pre-humous exhibition," some two to three hundred paintings, at Norwich University from May 15 to June 7, 1971. By profession a research economist and teacher, he took up painting by a series of happenstances when he was almost fifty, in order to crystallize for permanent enjoyment the scenes he loved to see on his vacation camping and mountain climbing trips: cascades, forest pools, mountain precipices, sunsets and sunrises, moonlight, spring and autumn foliage, winter snow, breaking waves on rocky shores, etc.

Although for the most part self-taught, Lahee's paintings have been exhibited in many museums throughout the United States, in countries of Europe, and since coming to Northfield, in various places in Vermont also. Mr. Lahee has won numerous awards by his paintings and is listed in Who's Who in American Art.

GMC'ers would enjoy viewing many of their favorite Vermont scenes as well as those in the north Jersey area where Life Member Lahee spent countless hours enjoying the out-of-doors.

OUT ON THE TRAIL
OR
THE LONG, LONG WALK

As your reporter hasn't been out on the trail for quite a while, due, of course, to the length of the winter, he would like to report on someone who HAS been out walking.

Meet Andy Borujko, of Chase, Michigan. Andy, an energetic chap of 49, wanted to do "his thing" for many years. Finally, in 1970 he broke away from the routine of working for a living, and set out for the adventure of his lifetime.

We met Andy on December 29. We were returning from El Salvador and Guatemala, heading for Veracruz and Texas, just past the midpoint of our five-week winter vacation in our VW Camper.

In Mexico about 70 miles north of the Guatemala border, we met this hiker, walking southward. We recognized him as an American, and his backpack naturally drew our curiosity. So we stopped for a chat. It proved to be one of the most interesting chats of our entire 8500-mile trip.

Andy was wearing a knit cap and a flannel shirt—to protect him from the blistering Central American sunlight. As he filled his canteen from our jug of purified water, he told us his story.

He had left Anchorage, Alaska, on March 1, and had walked every inch of the way to this point! After studying Fletcher and reports of other long-distance walkers, he had decided to tackle the longest walk ever attempted.

Then averaging 50 kilometers (over 30 miles) per day, he was heading for Guatemala and the balance of Central America. Destination? Punta Arenas, Chile, on the Strait of Magellan, the southernmost city on the mainland of South America!

We have our 262-mile End to Enders. The Appalachian Trail has its 2,000-mile walkers. But here was a 12,000-mile walker. Truly the world's longest walk. Estimated to take a total of about 16 months.

As he left us, and we watched him briskly heading for South America, all we could say was that he amazed us. Yes, put us down for a copy of the book which he plans to write after his journey has been completed.

BEN ROLSTON

MOUNTAIN STUDY GUIDES

In furthering a better understanding of the value of mountain lands, the Green Mountain Profile Committee is preparing mountain ecology materials especially for distribution to schools in Vermont. Two mailings of a mountain study guide will go out the last of April—one to high school students and the other to grades 4 through 6. Students of the Burlington High School Environmental Studies Class are helping to prepare the study guide for grades 4-6.

Both study guides will describe the ecological characteristics of the Green Mountains, how the mountains are endangered and what can be done to protect them. Suggestions for field studies will be included. Accompanying the materials will be a directory to local and national environmental organizations.

Copies of these materials will be available to the public for a small charge and can be obtained by writing to the Committee at P.O. Box 268, Burlington, Vt. 05401.
