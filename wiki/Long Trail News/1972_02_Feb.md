The meeting was called to order by the President at 10 AM. After reviewing club activities of the past year, he outlined the agenda for this meeting. He said its general purpose was to commence a design for a comprehensive long term program of the GMC for consideration by the Trustees at the February meeting.

Next the minutes of the previous meeting were approved. The Treasurer presented his report of the financial status of the Club which was approved. The President commented that in the past the Treasurer had taken full responsibility for the annual budget preparation. He felt this to be an undue burden to place upon one person and thus announced a new budget process.

Each person responsible for financing a program is to prepare a detailed budget of estimated expenses and submit this to the President by December 15. The President, in turn, will give these to the Budget and Finance Committee who will prepare a composite budget proposal. This is to be discussed at the February meeting of the Trustees and then adopted in final form at the April Trustees' meeting. Not only will this serve to distribute responsibility of the budget preparation, but will also cause greater focus on GMC programs.

The Budget and Finance Committee next presented the following proposed dues schedule:

This schedule precipitated discussion about the reaction from sections, how many copies of the LTN need be sent to a family, should there be corporate or sustaining memberships, and could there be reduced dues for senior members. It was decided that more study was needed on these points and the Committee should report its findings at the next meeting.

The President informed the meeting that the estate of Eugene L. Bamforth had left the GMC an amount of $28,456 for the sole use of trail maintenance. The following motion was, upon amendment, unanimously accepted:

"The Eugene Bamforth legacy be placed with the Howard National Bank and Trust Company, Burlington, Vermont, to be invested at their discretion in the manner arranged for the general investment funds of the GMC, Inc. That a separate report of the holdings and income will be kept by the Bank and reported to the club annually.

That only the income from this fund be used by the club for the sole purpose of trail maintenance, except in the case of dire emergency.

And that the Board of Trustees have the right to withdraw from this fund for the above stated purpose at any time as specified in the investment contract now covering the existing investment funds."

Al Mead then reported on the 1971 Intersectional. First he reviewed the statistics: 268 members and guests participated, 40 hikes accounted for some 3000 hiker miles, 113 attended the dedication of the Kid Gore shelter, 2 community meals were interspersed with programs on nature, map study, hike leadership, GMC slide program, signs, outlying mountains. Entertainment included square dancing and a talk by Roy Buchanan on "40 Years on the Long Trail." A profit of some $250.00 was realized.

Then Al reviewed the goals and accomplishments of this year's Intersectional. He said that the charge to the Committee was two-fold: To involve section members, and to bring forth programs that could be used in section program planning. He stated that involvement of section members left much to be desired, however, the programs had been quite successful.

In closing he suggested that notice and agenda of future Intersectional be prepared sufficiently in advance so that they can be announced at the Annual Meeting. The Trustees expressed their thanks to Al and his committee for a very successful Intersectional.

Consultant Shirley Strong then presented her report. Shirley pointed out the increasing complexity of planning and legislative action on the local state, and federal level, and the increasing responsibility of the GMC in these matters. At the state level she cited participation in District Environment Commission hearings relating to the LT and hearings on the East-West Highway, updating of Vermont's Outdoor Recreation Plan, writing of Forest and Park regulations for Camel's Hump and Mansfield areas as the type of involvement the GMC now faces. On the federal level Shirley cited protection of the AT via the National Scenic Trails Act, the North Country Trail Study, USFS Trail Management Plan, as examples of programs to which the GMC has an obligation for involvement.

She then commented on the increasing public interest in the GMC and its LT as reflected by the much greater interest shown by the news media. It is important that the club be prepared to properly communicate its policy, programs, and trail information with the public. She reminded the club of the challenge of last year's GMC Resolution and expressed the hope that the club would not let this challenge pass. In closing she urged the formation of an Executive Committee of the GMC to most effectively deal with these increased complexities and responsibilities.

Gardiner Lane was then introduced to the meeting by Shirley. Gardiner will carry on her work after her November 1 departure to Washington. The Trustees thanked Shirley for her leadership and dedicated efforts on behalf of the GMC and expressed the hope she would return whenever possible to visit and advise the club.

John Nuffort next offered a motion for the formation of an Executive Advisory Council. In supportive discussion of his motion John stated that its passage would help accomplish the following:

1. Qualified members would more readily accept the Presidency.

2. The Council, heavily comprised of present and outstanding former Trustees, would aid the President in observing established traditions and recent Trustees' decisions.

3. Trustees would be able to concentrate more on genuine problems in shorter meetings without having to review lengthy agenda of routine items not involving authorization of funds or changes in policy.

4. The Executive Advisory Council would attract participants who might not be stimulated by an informal status.

After discussion, the following motion was unanimously passed:

"Creation of an Executive Advisory Council be approved by the Trustees and submitted to the membership as an amendment to the by-laws.

Members of the proposed Executive Advisory Council will be appointed by the President, subject to approval by the Trustees, to serve one year terms. No member may serve more than three consecutive years.

Both President and Clerk will become automatic members of the Council which will be advisory in nature and meet at the call of the President. Membership will be a maximum of nine, comprising at least two-thirds present or former trustees."

BY-LAW CHANGES

   The By-Laws of The Green Mountain Club, Inc. were
printed in full in The Long Trail News, November 1970,
with revisions printed in February 1971. Please refer to
these issues for the complete wording of the proposed
changes.

   Approved by the Board of Trustees at the meeting on
Feb. 5, the following proposed amendments to the By-Laws
and the Articles of Association will be voted on at the An-
nual Meeting, May 27, 1972, at the Weathersfield Meet-
ing House.

PROPOSAL TO AMEND BY-LAW I.1 & I.4

In sections I.1 and I.4, it is proposed to change "21 years
of age" to "18 years of age"

REMARKS:
   The Green Mountain Club is a corporation. State law
restricts voting at corporate meetings to those members
who are of legal voting age. This was our intention
when the age was specified as 21. The age 21 is no
longer correct. The change proposed above will state
our intention clearly and correctly.


PROPOSAL TO AMEND BY-LAW I.7

I.7. It is proposed that this section be amended by deleting
   the period at the end of the first sentence and adding
   the words "to the Corporation.", and by adding the fol-
   lowing sentence to the section: "A Life Member under
   18 years of age may not vote or hold elective office."

REMARKS:
   Life Membership formerly was defined under section
5 of the old Article VI. There it was specifically pro-
vided that the contribution be made to the Corporation.
In the rewriting of the definition that provision was
omitted, either inadvertently or otherwise. It is desirable
that it be retained, especially to reinforce the provisions
of the new dues schedule now before the Trustees for
consideration.

   The Life status is really a separate class of member-
ship, and the provisions of none of the other classes
relate to it directly. There is no age restriction for ad-
mission to the category, but it does seem reasonable
that a Life Member should not have voting or office-

PROPOSAL TO AMEND BY-LAW 11.1 & 11.4

11.4. Change this section to raise the number of Trustees

11.5. Add at end of the first sentence "and shall be ex-officio, voting members of the Board of Trustees."

We allow a former trustee to be elected president, vice-

Trustees' meetings.

PROPOSAL TO AMEND BY-LAW IV.7

It is proposed to add a section to Article IV as follows:

IV.7. The President may appoint an Executive Advisory
Council, subject to approval by the Board of Trustees,
which shall be advisory in nature and shall meet at the
call of the President. The Executive Advisory Council
shall consist of not more than nine members, at least
two thirds of whom shall be present or former Trustees,
and shall include the President, the Clerk and not more
than seven additional members. Membership shall be
for a term of one year and no member shall serve for
more than three consecutive terms.

REMARKS:
The growing volume and complexity of Club business
has reached a point where it is very difficult for any one
person to handle it effectively and in timely fashion.
Recent experience with an informally constituted council, operating with the knowledge and consent of the
Board of Trustees, has clearly shown that a small group
of persons, who have a thorough knowledge of Club
policy and tradition and who are willing and able to
meet frequently with the President, can provide him
with much wise counsel and assistance in carrying out
his duties. Formalization of the council, it is felt, will
remove any lingering doubts as to its legality and, more
importantly, serve to attract highly qualified potential
members who presently decline to serve. It should be
noted that the council would be advisory in nature and
that it would have no authority to make policy or
financial commitments. By assisting the President in
sharing much of the routine burden, however, it would
serve to attract highly qualified people who are not now
interested in the job of President. By carrying out in
advance many of the routine non-policy matters which
now tend to take up too much time of the Trustees at
their infrequent meetings, it would allow the Trustees
to spend a much higher percentage of their valuable
meeting time on more important matters.

PROPOSAL TO AMEND ARTICLES OF
ASSOCIATION

It is proposed to add two articles to our Articles of Association as follows:

VIII No part of the net earnings of the corporation
shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its members, trustees, officers, or other private persons, except that
the corporation shall be authorized to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to make payments and
distributions in furtherance of the purposes set forth above.
No substantial part of the activities of the corporation shall
be the carrying on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting
to influence legislation, and the corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the publishing or distribution of statements) any political campaign on behalf
of any candidate for public office. Notwithstanding any
other provisions of these Articles, the corporation shall
not carry on any other activities not permitted to be carried on (a) by a corporation exempt from Federal income
tax under Section 501 (c) (3) of the Internal Revenue Code
of 1954 (or the corresponding provision of any future United States Internal Revenue Law) or (b) by a corporation,
contributions to which are deductible under Section 170
(c) (2) of the Internal Revenue Code 1954 (or the corresponding provision of any future United States Internal Revenue Law).

IX Upon the dissolution of the corporation, the Board
of Trustees shall, after paying or making provision for the
payment of all of the liabilities of the corporation, dispose
of all of the assets of the corporation exclusively for the
purposes of the corporation in such manner, or to such
organization or organizations organized and operated exclusively for charitable, educational, religious, or scientific
purposes as shall at the time qualify as an exempt organization or organizations under Section 501 (c) (3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1954 (or the corresponding provision of any future United States Internal Revenue Law),
as the Board of Trustees shall determine.

REMARKS:
At the October Trustees meeting it was unanimously
voted to apply for a tax exempt status under Section
501 (c) (3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1954. Adoption of these two proposed amendments is essential
in order to meet the requirements of the Internal Revenue Service. In essence, the proposed Article VIII prohibits members or non-members from deriving financial
gain from the Club other than reasonable compensation
for services rendered; restricts the Club from engaging
in any substantial activity involving propaganda, lobbying, or other means of influencing legislation or elections; and generally prohibits the carrying on of activities
not permitted under the above Section. Based on past
and present activities as well as projected plans, it is
believed that these restrictions have little or no significant effect on our operations.

The proposed Article IX simply provides that should
the Club ever be dissolved, any assets remaining after
provision for outstanding liabilities shall be disposed of
to one or more organizations having similar purposes
and able to qualify for the same tax exempt status. It is
not necessary at this time to designate specific beneficiaries.

This year we had 27 men, 10 women, and 3 juniors complete their End-to-End walk. It took the longest of these over nine years to finish, while the shortest time recorded this year was only 18 days. There were two nine year old boys who tied for the youngest for the year. Eleven states and five sections were represented, in addition to 31 At-Large members. Thirty others were not members of the GMC, or failed to report their membership in their trail report.

As usual, there were three major ways of completing the End to End walk. Some people just started at one end of the Long Trail, and hiked to the other end. These people usually completed the hike within a month. Others did it a little at a time, either on day hikes, or by sleeping at some of the shelters or in their cars. This takes from three to five years on the average. The third group, usually Scouts, complete the trip by walking a different 'fifty mile' section each year for five years. At least one member did almost all of her hiking at GMC Intersectionals.

Several people this year showed that it is not necessary to camp out overnight in order to walk the entire trail. They did it completely by day hikes, either by spotting cars at each end of the desired section, by hitchhiking, or in the case of one energetic man, by hiking back to the starting point each day. This means that he covered over five hundred miles in order to earn his End-to-End award.

Two final points. It is not necessary to be a GMC member in order to earn the End-to-End award. However, if you are a member, either At-Large or Section, when you send in your report, please include that information. Also, the Trails and Shelters people appreciate any comments on trail conditions, particularly if they are recent, since the End-to-End reports are a good source of information about work which needs to be done.

IRA T. ELLIS, JR., Chairman
End-to-End Committee

NEW BOOK

APPALACHIAN HIKER, Adventure of a Lifetime by Edward B. Garvey

This book is well written with a complete history of the author's backpacking experience on 2,000 miles of the Appalachian Trail, during a six months period. It contains valuable information on Guide Books, maps and mileage fact sheets which give distances between shelters, grocery stores, and Post Offices.

Recommendations on the pack, sleeping gear, shoes and clothing are provided and also suggestions on food and how to prepare it. The day to day experiences which are given in detail are of most interest.

I highly recommend this book for those who plan to hike the whole trail, only part of it, and for those who enjoy good reading.

DAVE VAN DUSEN

BURLINGTON SECTION NEWS

Winter activities for the Burlington section include a variety of snowshoe and cross country ski trips as well as a sliding party and the annual oyster stew supper. This year's oyster stew supper was the 19th served by Bev and Don Remick at Taylor Lodge on Mt. Mansfield. They began the outing in 1952 when they were college students.

Outings chairman Fred B. Smith reports that total attendance for 1970-71 outings was 784 with an average of 20 persons an outing.

MONTPELIER SECTION

We had a good fall season for hiking which shifted suddenly and definitely to snowshoeing and ski touring after the Thanksgiving snowstorm. In October, two sections on the Long Trail were hiked, one in the pouring rain when one of our members finished his last section to become an End-to-Ender. The other was in bright autumn sunshine where members watched hundreds of wild geese flying near or below them as they hiked from Burnt Rock Mountain to Montclair Glen.

Members climbed Camel's Hump, Mount Monadnock in New Hampshire and, during the hunting season, hiked over Hawk Cliff and Indian Head Mountain in the Ausable Lakes area. Two cookouts were held, both well attended. One, an overnighter, was to Taylor Lodge, the other to Beaver Meadows Lodge, into which we snowshoed and skied for a grilled steak dinner.

Hikes have had anywhere from two to sixteen people attending, our short hikes enabling families with young children to come.

This winter holds a variety of ski-touring, snowshoeing and winter mountaineering trips, with the Section's Annual Meeting set for April 7.

STERLING SECTION

The Sterling Section closed out the fall hiking season with a very successful work trip Nov. 6 to Whiteface Lodge where a number of repairs and improvements were carried out. The major one of these was the laying of a new (creosoted) board floor to replace the old wire mesh "floor." A shelf was also installed in the open-faced shelter, the roof was patched and some trail signs put up. This lodge can now be said to be in excellent shape.

Giving a major assist to the section in this work were members of the Morrisville Boy Scout troop.

At our annual meeting Jan. 21 in Morrisville there was action on several matters in addition to election of officers and other regular business. The membership approved a motion strongly opposing a general $1 recreational land use fee which a state legislative committee had recently proposed and directed its Trails and Shelters Committee to give some study to the possible re-opening of old trails or establishment of new trails in our area and to report back to the membership with its findings.

The section membership also expressed concern over the proposed increase in the parent GMC assessment on the sections and then anticipating that at least some increase would be approved, voted an increased section dues schedule for 1973 as follows: Single adult, $4; single junior (18 or under), $2; and family, $7.50.

WORCESTER SECTION

A group of over twenty joined Ann and Bob Humes for the first organized hike on the Semuhenna (Happy Hiking) Trail on Mount Wachusett. Bob first flagged this trail three years ago, and he and Ann spent a good deal of time this fall finishing the cutting. The Mount Wachusett Advisory Commission had recently made it an official trail.

A more recent hike was held in December at the Asumet Holly Reservation, an Audubon Society Sanctuary, on Cape Cod. The weather was bleak and cold, but nineteen hardy ones enjoyed the lovely trails and most unusual holly trees.

Clyde Gay showed slides, "The World Around You," at the Christmas party.

Again, we will be hiking on new trail in late January, this time led by Lester Vielle. He and his Explorer Scouts have flagged a fourteen mile trail from West Millbury to Wallum Lake in Douglas. This has been cut to Oxford, and it is this six mile section we will christen.

GUIDE BOOK CHANGES

On Page 142 of the 1971 edition of the Guide Book of the Long Trail, there appears a notice which reads: "Changes in the trails and shelters will be published in the Long Trail News in a form suitable for cutting out and pasting into your Guide Book." In compliance with that notice, the Guide Book Committee has prepared the following listing of major trail and shelter changes since the Guide Book was printed in 1971:

Pages 28 and 29: Seth Warner Shelter has been moved closer to the Long Trail. However, the blue-blazed Seth Warner Trail must still be used to reach the shelter and, farther west, the water supply.

Pages 31 through 37: The relocation of the Long Trail between Vt. 9 and Glastenbury Mountain Shelter was not completed in 1971. 1972 hikers must still use the route up the valley via Fay Fuller Camp and Bolles Brook. No attempt should be made to follow the plastic flagging of the new route. Signs and white blazes will indicate when the new route is ready for use, and details will appear in the Long Trail News.

Page 38: The new Kid Gore Shelter was completed by the Connecticut Section of the GMC in 1971. This log shelter, with bunks for 8, is located a short distance southeast of Caughnawaga Shelter, and utilizes Caughnawaga's reliable water supply.

Page 42: The relocation of the Long Trail between the Arlington-West Wardsboro Road and Stratton Pond was not completed in 1971. 1972 hikers must still use the present route via the road and Grout Job. Signs and white blazes will indicate when the new route is ready for use, and details will appear in the Long Trail News.

Page 61: In 1971 Clarendon Lodge was converted into an open shelter. Also, the Long Trail was relocated between Route 103 and Clarendon. Upon reaching Route 103, northbound hikers should now follow the highway east a short distance, then ascend an old town road to the shelter, passing, en route, the Crown Point Military Road marker.

Page 142: The Mathieu family has moved from the residence described. Hikers should make other parking arrangements when using the Journey's End Trail approach to the north end of the Long Trail.

General: The caretaker program will be continued at heavily used shelters during the 1972 season. See Page 85. There is also an increasing need for the carrying of shelter materials by large groups, and the carrying of pack stoves to conserve the dwindling supply of firewood at heavily used areas. See Pages 49 and 85.
