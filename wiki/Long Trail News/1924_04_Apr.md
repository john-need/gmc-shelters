THE GREEN MOUNTAIN NEWS
Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.
Brandon, Vermont, April, 1924

The present year will be an important one in the annals of the Green Mountain Club, for it will witness the opening of the new clubhouse, and the Club's initiation in the ownership and management of a house of its own. Too much should not be expected of the results of the first season, which will be experimental. The management must feel its way and learn by experience. No doubt mistakes will be made, and changes in policy be found necessary. We will tell the story in our December issue, and meantime hope for the best. No steps have yet been taken toward building the proposed Monroe Lodge on Camel's Hump, but we expect this will eventually materialize. We are not, however, making any predictions as to when. This matter is in the hands of the New York section.

Annual Meeting.

The annual meeting of the Club was held at the Hotel Berwick in Rutland, Vt., January 12 last, with an attendance of about 50, New York city Montclair, N. J., Boston, Worcester, Mass., and several towns in Vermont being represented. W. M. Ross of Rutland was elected Clerk, and the following trustees were elected.
Prof. W. S. Monroe, Montclair, N. J.; Walter Mueller, New York; Fred H. Tucker, Boston; Herbert M. Dockham, Worcester, Mass., and the following from Vermont: J. Lawrence Griswold and George A. Mathers, Bennington; Theron S. Dean and James P. Taylor, Burlington; Profs. R. H. White and P. C. Voter, Middlebury; Mortimer R. Proctor, Proctor; E. S. Marsh, Brandon; Capt. Herbert W. Congdon, Arlington; C. J. Cleveland, C. P. Cooper, W. M. Ross, Rutland.

The treasurer's report showed balance on hand from last year, $1272.69. Receipts: annual dues, $906; life members, $125; guide books, $207.33; gifts, $58.52; interest, $129.18; from sections, $134.13; Haselton legacy, $1000. Total, $3832.87. Disbursements: trail committee, $560.59; ice house, $448.82; insurance, $220.80; Green Mountain News, $66.38; printing and stationery, $48.70; Associated Mountaineering clubs, $15; N. E. Trail Conference, $19; slides, $12; U. S. bonds purchased, $1073.95; advanced for clubhouse, $100; other current expenses, viz., officers' expense accounts, expenses trustees' meetings, expense clubhouse opening day, postage, etc., $277.36. Cash in Proctor Trust Co., $770.04; in First National Bank, Brandon, $220.23.

Nomenclature.
(From The Green Mountain Emporium, Montpelier, Vt., September, 1839.)

We have among our mountains, Mansfield, Ascutney and Camel's Hump, or "Rump", as some are disposed to call it, either of which affords scenery vying in natural interest with the most celebrated classic hills of antiquity; but when can these, with their present grating names, live and breathe along the lines of song? "Killington Peak"; if the former name of the town, from which it derived its appellation (now changed to Sherburne) could be applied to the mountain, and the mean allusion to its summit be discarded, would do much better. It may still become, with its wild, romantic hills that crowd around its foot to pay it homage, the theme and the favored retreat of many a literary wanderer.

  Article I, Section 1 of the By-laws was amended to read as follows: Any person may become a member at large of the Green Mountain Club, Inc., upon application to and approval of the membership committee. Each applicant must be vouched for by two members of the Club or furnish two references. No money shall accompany the application. Each section shall have the right to regulate its own membership.

   A committee was appointed to indite and send a letter to Dr. Louis J. Paris, of Burlington, Vt., who is soon to leave the state, in behalf of the Club, expressing appreciation of and gratitude for the years of devoted service Dr. Paris has given the Club from its formation, and regret that the Club is to lose those services.

  About 20 members spent the night at the Clubhouse in Sherburne Pass, where they were joined Sunday by about 30 of the Rutland members and the day was spent in snowshoeing in the vicinity, some climbing Pico and Killington and visiting the caves. Upon their return dinner was awaiting them in the spacious dining room, and the evening was pleasantly spent before the great fireplace in talking over experiences and plans for the future.

     Annual Meeting of Trustees.

  The annual meeting of the trustees was held March 8 at the Hotel Berwick, Rutland. Eleven were present, viz., Messrs. Monroe and Mueller, of the New York section, Tucker of Boston, and Dean, White, Voter, Marsh, Cooper, Cleveland, Ross and Mathers of Vermont. J. A. Allis of New York was also present and acted as a substitute for one of the absent members.

  The following officers were elected: President, C. P. Cooper; Vice President, Will S. Monroe; Treasurer, E. S. Marsh; Corresponding Secretary, Herbert W. Congdon. And committees as follows: Membership, C. J. Cleveland, W. M. Ross, F. H. Tucker, Ellen Steele, and the fifth to be from Rutland and chosen by the chairman; House committee (club house), Geo. N. Harman, C. P. Cooper, R. H. White; Guide book, C. P. Cooper, chairman, and he to choose associates; Trails and camps, W. M. Ross, and he to select the others from the chairmen of the trails committees of the sections; Publicity, H. W. Congdon, chairman, to select his associates; Nomenclature, P. C. Voter, chairman, to choose the others.

  The principal topic of discussion before the meeting was the management of the new clubhouse at Sherburne Pass, a unique mountain house, fully furnished and equipped, with outbuildings, representing an expenditure to date of some $17,500, with much more remaining to be done before the plant can be considered complete, if that time ever comes. With considerable expense still necessary before it will be ready for public use, it was the sentiment of the trustees that the Club should go slow for the early part of the season in the construction of new trail and cabins, till such time as the clubhouse should at least be self-supporting, and the year's budget was made up with that in view. The existing trail will be cleared, and the Marjorie Hulburd portion of the trail completed, at least, and perhaps a cabin built there. What else will be done this year will depend on future developments.

  The trustees had lunch and dinner together, and in the evening a majority of them went by motor and sleigh to the clubhouse and spent the night. Sunday was devoted to snowshoeing in the vicinity, to discussion of the viands so bountifully provided and well cooked by chief steward Ross, and to gatherings before the big fire in the living room. Messrs. Allis and Mueller climbed Killington on snowshoes.

Communication.

STATE OF WYOMING
State Training School, Lander
CARROL THOMPSON JONES,
Superintendent.

March 10, 1924.

The Green Mountain Club,
Rutland, Vermont.

Gentlemen,

I am planning a trip of exploration into absolutely virgin territory in the Wind River Range of the Rockies this coming summer and would like to offer to real climbers an opportunity to join my party.

We have back of Lander glaciers which have never been visited by white men and peaks nearly fourteen thousand feet high which have never been scaled nor named. I consider this an opportunity for real exploration in which the more hardy of our mountain climbers ought to be interested. Last year I climbed Gannett Peak, the highest point in Wyoming, and this year want to climb the two next highest peaks and take steps to have them named Mt. Harding and Mt. Wilson.

This will be a co-operative enterprise and I have no commercial interest in it. If you will be good enough to let me know how I can bring this opportunity to the attention of your members I shall appreciate the favor very much. Furthermore, I shall be glad to furnish to any of your members information in regard to the vacation possibilities of various parts of Wyoming.

Thanking you for any help you can give me in this matter, I am,

Yours very truly,

C. T. Jones

Notes.

The article in our December issue on the Marjorie Hulburd trail, with accompanying map, has been reproduced in the Hyde Park News & Citizen and in the Barton Monitor, and we trust will have a good effect in arousing interest in the completion of the trail in the north part of the state, and bring additional members to the Jay Peak section.

The Worcester section is very much alive, and issues regular bulletins with programs of coming outings and meetings.

The Club will place a bronze tablet in the clubhouse, commemorating the fact that the house was the gift of Mortimer R. Proctor, and the furnishings the gift of his mother, Mrs. Fletcher D. Proctor.

From Beloit, Wis.: "We of the middle west envy you Vermonters your many opportunities of exploring those wonderful mountains throughout the whole year."

From New York city: "Because we love the Green Mountains of Vermont very much indeed and hope to get up there for at least a little while each summer, we want to be members again of the Green Mountain Club."

A new edition of the guide book will be published this year, the edition of 1922 being practically exhausted. Needless to say it will be the best yet. It will be ready early in May.
