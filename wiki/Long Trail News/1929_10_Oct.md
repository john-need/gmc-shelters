Long Trail Week.

The Club's experiment of a personally conducted trip on the Long Trail has been carried to a successful conclusion. The program, published in the last issue of the News, was carried through without a hitch and with only minor changes, and our twenty "paying guests" have gone to their homes filled with enthusiasm for the Trail but especially for Long Trail Lodge, its hostess and her staff. Even so well-seasoned a member as Capt. Congdon had not realized heretofore what an ideal center the Lodge makes for those who want to tramp the Trail free from the burden of a pack and the bother of camping, and it is a matter for wonderment that so few have used it. There are walks for a week from it that do not need a guide or even a companion.

The program, calling for alternate days of hiking and auto-riding, was the work of our New York section member, Fritz von Briesen, and it is due to his enthusiasm and generosity that a Trustees' dream has come to pass. We are glad to report that we have been able to return to him untouched but with dividends of goodwill and gratitude, the $100 he deposited with the Club Treasurer to take care of the expected deficit. We are still more glad to report that the members who took the trip have only praises for his wisdom in laying out the trip which Capt. Congdon put into effect.

Good fortune attended the trip from start to finish. The registration just filled every available nook in the lodge even if it did not reach the limit of 25 that was originally set. The weather was good, but varied; the parts of the Trail that were covered were beautiful, distinctively individual, and always full of interest. Nothing could have been more enjoyable than the hospitality of Long Trail Lodge, and we all deeply appreciated Mrs. Bates' cooperation, forbearance and unfailing kindness while we can never forget how good those meals were! We left the Lodge with real regret.

Thanks are due to Mr. Swift at Bread Loaf Inn, who worked magic so we were comfortably cared for even if we did arrive on the night of summer-school Commencement and in the midst of all its confusion; but the crowning feature of the trip was our last dinner together, at Bristol Inn, where the management presented us with a birthday cake as beautiful as it was delicious, with which we surprised "Daddy" Tucker who in the few days we were together had become as dear to the new friends as he is to the old. Capt. Congdon wants to go on record that his work would have been a real burden if it had not been for Tucker's constant co-operation and helpfulness; and we can all testify that when "Daddy" left us on the next-to-the-last day his absence was most keenly felt.

At this last of the nightly meetings the members of the party very generously authorized Capt. Congdon to turn over any balance remaining to the Trustees to use as they see fit. At that time several bills were lacking which were expected to be large, but again our good luck held, and thanks to the unexpectedly large number of member-owned autos that were with the party, expenses were kept below the estimate, so $126.25 remains on hand.

Checking over the original estimates, it may be seen how easily our profit might have been a deficit under conditions only slightly different. The estimate was based on two "deadheads" and thirteen "paying guests"; instead of that we had twenty who paid. Three of these registered late and therefore paid the extra $5.00 penalty.

Long Trail Lodge to be Enlarged and
Improved.

The following extensive alterations and
additions to Long Trail Lodge have been
decided upon, and work has been started
on them, in accordance with plans drawn
by Paul W. Thayer, the original architect
of the Lodge:

The present kitchen enlarged will become
the dining room, with a seating capacity of
approximately one hundred.

On two sides of this room there will be
built an outdoor porch, which can be protected from the weather when necessary
by large glass windows, opening outward.

This porch will seat an additional seventy-five.

To the southeast at an angle of 45 degrees to the axis of the present building there will be a new wing, containing a serving room, kitchen, cooler, and other kitchen facilities.

Hillside will be used one half for laundry and one-half for a bake room.

The present wood shed will be made over into a dormitory for those employed at the Lodge.

The above changes were deemed necessary from the fact that the Lodge the past season proved to be inadequate to accommodate many of its would-be patrons. A large number of people were turned away on account of lack of accommodations. It is very evident that it is increasing in popularity and usefulness. The trustees feel, however, that with these additions completed no further enlargement will be advisable. They think that the alterations, as outlined above, will add both to the charm and value of the Lodge. But to make it still larger would tend to introduce too much of a commercial atmosphere, and the woods charm would be lost, to a certain extent, at least.

New Camps.

Besides the two described in special articles, the following have been completed or are under construction: The camp contributed by Dr. W. H. Beardsley of Springfield, Vt., is finished. It is located a short distance south of the country road in the Bennington section, and will be called the Seth Warner lodge. In the same section is the new camp on Glastenbury Mountain, built by the Bennington section. A camp under Tillotson Peak, north of Sterling Pond, was completed. The New York section is building a camp at Birch Glen. It is expected another camp in the Bennington section on or near Harmon Hill will be built in October.

Mrs. Frank White of Rutland has promised a closed stone camp to cost about $900 to $1000.

Patrol Work.

The trustees at their March meeting decided that the Trail should be patrolled and reports of the condition of the Trail and camps be made to the Sections, and blazes painted. This work was done from Camel's Hump south to Griffith Lake prior to the Trustees' meeting June 8, under the direction of Wallace M. Fay. At that meeting it was voted to equip the patrols with axes and cross-cut saws and do a thorough clearing job where needed. The following sections of the Trail were cleared and painted, in addition to the patrol work: Style's Peak to Stratton Pond; Bennington road to Glastenbury and to Hannon Hill; Country road to Hannon Hill; Lincoln-Warren road to Mt. Roosevelt; Dunsmoor lodge to Sterling Pond; Jay Peak to Hazen's Notch.

Reports on the general condition of the Trail this year have been very favorable.

New Camp of the Worcester Section.

This structure, located on Stratton Pond, was built by a party of twenty-three members of the Worcester Section in five days, August 17-21 last, under the leadership of George F. E. Storey and Louis L. Bigelow. The lumber was contributed by W. A. Savage of Randolph, Vt., and all of the labor was donated. Much difficulty was experienced in getting the material in. It had to be carried by man power over an old swampy corduroy road, a most arduous undertaking. Fortunately a troop of boy scouts happened along just at the right time, and were of great assistance in the transportation work.

It is delightfully located in the midst of spruce trees, with no other sign of human habitation visible, on the banks of the pond, with the beautiful hills rising on each side and sloping down in the middle distance, reminding one of Echo Lake in the White Mountains.

It is this camp which will be visited by the trustees and others on October 12.

New Camp at Hazen's Notch.

This, which is now completed, is an entirely closed cabin, with shutters on two sides. It is fitted with a good cook stove and has bunks for from eight to twelve people. It is situated about five minutes walk south of the main road thru Hazen's Notch, directly on the Long Trail, and about six miles from Jay Peak. Back of it is a small brook, said to be never failing.
