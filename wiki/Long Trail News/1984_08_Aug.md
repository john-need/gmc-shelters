                          BUCHANAN SHELTER ON LINE
                                             BUCHANAN SHELTER ON LINE
        After the 1983 hiking season, it became
     evident that a new shelter was needed be-
     tween Duck Brook Shelter and Puffer Shel-
     ter. The 1982 relocation project completed
     by the Long Trail Patrol and the Burling-
     ton Section had placed Bolton Shelter in-
     accessible to the average hiker. It was de-
     cided to build a shelter halfway between
     Duck Brook and Puffer at a location of two
     tent platforms that had been constructed in
     1983 by the Burlington Section.
        The plan and design of the new shelter
     was submitted to the Shelters and Trails
     Committee in the Fall of 1983 and ap-
     proved by the Board of Directors in Janu-
     ary 1984.
        Materials were delivered in late April at
     a location about 72 mile from the building
     site. (The site of the new shelter is on the
     Bolton Valley ski area's property. It is ac-
     cessible through the network of cross-coun-
     try ski trails.) The Bolton Valley Corpora-
     tion offered to transport the material to the
     building site with their bulldozer, however,
     because of the land being so soft due to
     melting snow this proved impossible and a
     reliable way of transporting materials was
     resorted to, i.e. packing it in on your shoul-
     der.
        Work was started on the project the first
     weekend in May by lugging material to the
     site as they were needed, i.e., foundation
     posts first, then the sills, decking, walls,
     roof, etc. The work being done by several
     work parties from the Burlington Section
     and friends of Burlington Section, includ-
     ing a work party headed by Don Wallace at
     the time of our Annual Meeting.
        We appreciate all the help that was given
     to us and a special thanks to Jack Lance of
     the Burlington Section for his unique
     knowledge in the building trades, who
     seemed to know just how to do things at the
     proper time to allow us to complete the
     project before the heavy 1984 hiking sea-
     son.
        As of this writing, this shelter is habit-
     able though a little more needs to be done
     and an outhouse needs to be constructed.
        We learned several things in the con-
     struction of this shelter, but mainly that
     building a shelter in the black fly time
     hinders progress and if it were to be done
     over again, we would prefer to build in Au-
     gust or September.
        As the shelter is located on the Bolton
     Valley cross country ski network, we expect
     it to be used summer and winter and do
     hope that it will convey a message to the
     cross country skiers as to the type of activi-
     ties of the Green Mountain Club.


     Burlington Section
     Basil Goodridge
