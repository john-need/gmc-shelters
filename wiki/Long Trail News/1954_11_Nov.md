NEW SHELTER DEDICATED IN STORM
TO MEMORY OF PROF. L. B. PUFFER

More than 20 members of the Burlington section of the Green Mountain Club gathered at their Bolton Lodge on the Long Trail to pay tribute to the late Prof. L. B. Puffer and to dedicate a new trail shelter to his memory Sunday, Sept. 19, 1954.

Mrs. Olive Faulkins and Miss Helen Puffer, daughters of the professor, represented the family.

Donald Havens, club president, conducted the dedication ceremony and spoke of the late Prof. Puffer's enthusiasm for hiking and mountain-climbing, how he had served as president of the local section for several years as well as president of the Club at Large. He also served as trustee of the club for years.

The new dedicated log lodge, nearly completed, is some four miles north from Bolton Lodge over Bolton Mountain at about a half-way point between Bolton and Taylor Lodges.

The site was scouted out by Prof. Roy O. Buchanan, Capt. Clyde Johnson and Don Havens.

The new cabin was built by volunteer labor under the directions of Prof. Buchanan, a constant hiking pal of the late Prof. Puffer, assisted by his son, Chester Buchanan of Schenectady.

Capt. Claude Johnson and many others of the local group assisted with the task because its location is not readily accessible.

Prof. Buchanan also spoke at the ceremony as did Elton Beal who outlined plans for a memorial plaque to be placed in the Puffer Memorial Lodge.

Mrs. Faulkins, speaking in behalf of the late Prof. Puffer's family, expressed appreciation for the club's remembrance of her father in the form of the new lodge.

Before the ceremony, the ladies of the club got together and prepared a full course chicken dinner at Bolton for the group, under the chairmanship of Miss Ruth Aseltine.

Lt. Donald Remick, just recently returned from a tour of duty in Germany, was able to be present, after being away from club activities several months.

Early Sunday morning, 12 men of the group headed by their club president, Havens, went to Bolton Lodge to carry in lumber for the new Puffer Lodge door and floor and to make Bolton ready for the dedication party to be held in the late afternoon and evening.

Shortly after the group began filing up the trail over Bolton Mountain with their heavy loads, rain came down hard.

But the group continued up the slippery hard trail some three miles in a severe electric storm.

At the lodge, the group stopped only long enough to unpack its loads, eat lunch and do a few necessary things around the building before making the return back up over and down the mountain again.

By this time, the trail had become a brook.

The group had to fell a tree to cross a stream. The men formed a chain to assist them across through the deep rushing water at another place.

Prof. Buchanan said he had never seen the likes of this in all his years on the trail.

A LUMBER JOB ROAD

For the benefit of those who took part in the discussion at the Intersectional as to whether or not the Trail from our camp site to Griffith Lake led over an old railroad bed, Fred Field has dug up the answer.

After talking with an old time resident of that area, Fred learned that there never was a railroad on that part of the Trail. What looked as if it might have been a railroad bed was a reinforced lumber road for snaking lumber from the Old Job and Griffith Lake.

A mile north of the Intersectional camp site where Norse Lodge (red and white buildings) now stands, was the Foley Farm and Lumber Mill. From this point a narrow-gage railroad did go up on to the mountain.

KILLINGTON SECTION REPORTS

Like the other Sections who have tried to hike in Vermont this season, we have run into plenty of rain. Regardless of this fact, though, only one scheduled hike had to be cancelled. The trip to Killington planned for September 5th was not taken due to early morning rain. Ironically enough the sky cleared soon after the hikers and their guests from Glens Falls, N. Y. decided not to make the climb that day.

This Fall, Section members, in three hikes have covered 13.2 miles of Long Trail from Brandon Gap to Boyce Shelter. This brings their goal, End-to-Enders award, that many more miles nearer completion.

The Intersectional meeting in Danby was enjoyed by fifteen Killington Section members.

Work has been started on the re-building of the camp one half mile south of Spring Lake, and a name for the camp has been officially selected. It is to be called "Sunnyside."

WHAT'S BEEN HAPPENING IN BENNINGTON?

Last March we, of the Bennington Section, held our annual meeting in the midst of a snow storm on top of Woodford Mt. Driving conditions were slippery and some who were not fortunate enough to own snow tires had difficulty in making the grade. Among the eighteen who did was Dr. Ross, a charter member who received an especially warm welcome. A few hikers were hardy enough to struggle part way into Little Pond. They returned with good appetites well suited to the excellent meal which Ted Goddard and the Paulsons had prepared.

At the business meeting which followed new officers were elected; John Paulson, president; Ted Goddard, vice president; Helen Paulson, treasurer; Hazel Goodale, secretary.

Highlight of the evening was a letter from Miss Erna Camradt who is well known to many GMC members. Erna has been traveling in Egypt and has been spending some time in Germany where her family is located.

Since March, when bad weather has not dogged our heels, several trips have been made over portions of our section of the Trail. A much delayed trail clearing trip from Thendara shelter to Woodford Hollow was finally made on a hot day in June, more perfect for black flies than for us. We wish we had let the flies have the Trail that day, however, we can now report with satisfaction that a good portion of the Trail has been completely cleared, blowdowns removed, and the entire distance repainted.

Thendara Shelter has been put in order; corner posts reinforced, roof patched, new table top, new benches, and the brush cleared away. Credit for much of this work goes to John and Helen Paulson who made a special trip for the purpose. Another special trip made by John Paulson and Ted Goddard was to Seth Warner camp which now has a new roof of ninety pound roofing paper, some new boards, new table top, reinforced bunks, and has been generally tightened up.

Member Ed Bullock has covered the Trail from Seth Warner to Sucker Pond and reports the most needed item there is paint. Weather permitting, we hope to take care of this soon.

Besides work on the Trail last spring, we enjoyed a four mile hike over Deer Ridge and across Mt. Hope Farm which is located at the foot of Mt. Greylock in Williamstown, Mass. Again owing to poor weather two week-ends were required; one for the eating of our covered dish supper and to enjoy Mr. Gardner's beautiful slides of Mt. Hope, and one to take the hike previously rained out.

August first was a beautiful day and it seemed as though nothing could go wrong with the weather on such a day. Little Rock Pond was the objective. As we approached Danby, sunshine all about us, we could see that both Danby and Little Rock Pond were having weather all their own. A storm seemed to be hanging over those spots. We went on, however, hoping it would pass. But after half an hour of waiting in a heavy downpour which gave no sign of ever stopping, we went on to Hapgood Pond. Here picnicking, swimming, and boating were in full progress in glorious unbroken sunshine. Determined to hike, we climbed Styles Peak from Mad Tom. Looking north over Danby we could see heavy clouds still emptying their load.

The Fall program got underway in a damp heavy fog on Mt. Greylock. Another trip planned for Crane Mt. in the Adirondacks with the Troy Outing Club received equally damp treatment. We don't know what the future holds but whatever it is it is probably wet and we are now fully equipped to meet same.

HAZEL M. GOODALE

NOTES FROM THE NEW YORK SECTION

Members of the New York Section met for the first fall get-together on Sept. 24th at Seamen's Church Institute. After a good dinner, several members showed movies and slides of their summer travels. We heard fascinating tales of a pack trip into the Uinta Mountains of Utah; a river voyage in Dinosaur National Monument; a visit to Bermuda; hiking in the Catskills; and excursions through Zion and Bryce National Parks, Grand Canyon and the State of Colorado. Our returned Navy man, Ernest Anderson, spoke on part of his travels in Europe. Another member showed pictures of the hurricane damage to his property in Rhode Island. Attendance at this dinner was excellent and all enjoyed the evening.

The Labor Day week-end saw fifteen of our members off on the annual Long Trail hiking trip.

The first Sunday in October was Open House Day at Camp Thendara. Besides those who were there for the week-end, others hiked in on Sunday and still more came in by cars for the day. A good crowd was on hand to enjoy the pleasant warm fall weather and friendly get-together.

Sunday hikes are now scheduled regularly and several interesting Saturday afternoon outings are listed, to say nothing of week-end trips.

Our new bulletin, covering the winter months will be out about Nov. 1st.

It looks like a busy and pleasant fall and winter season for the New York Section.

LILLIAN P. LODGE

REPORT FROM THE WORCESTER SECTION

With our fire-place officially dedicated we proceeded to have an active and busy summer. We have had many local outings and various out of town outings. A few of our group were able to attend the annual meeting; also the Intersectional.

Some of our highlights have been the Strawberry Festival and our annual beach trip to Crane's Beach in Ipswich, Mass. On August 4th we spent a delightful evening at a garden party at the home of Mr. and Mrs. George Arkwell. On September 12th we did something a little different. We put on a chicken barbecue at cost which brought out forty-two of our members. What a feed along with good fellowship!

Between meetings we had some swimming parties and some card parties for social evenings.

BARRE SECTION NEWS

On October 17th eight members of the Barre Section hiked one and one-half miles to Taylor Lodge on the Lake Mansfield road. The day was beautiful with good visibility but the foliage was long since passed, due to early seasonal frosts and heavy rains. However, the group saw a bit of wild life in the form of a porcupine in front of the Lodge and a herd of deer on the drive from Lake Mansfield which added excitement to the day's venture. The Trail was found to be in excellent condition with scattered wet spots. The "Happy Wanderers" enjoyed a cookout supper at the Lodge and Harris Palmer also took group pictures.

On Sunday, September 26th, a less fortunate group of fifteen left Barre with brilliant rays of sunshine indicating a "good day overhead." The "good day overhead" lasted for thirty miles. When the point of destination, namely Mt. Mansfield, was reached a heavy dew sort of dropped from the heavens. Being an optimistic group, $1.50 each was freely expended for the glorious chair-lift ride up the mountain to view the fall foliage, only to be confronted with California fog and Vermont downpours to the top of the mountain and back again. No cookout that day!!

Other enjoyable events this past season included a trip to Nichols Lodge on May 16th, a barbecue on June 12th and a hike to Wiley Lodge on August 22nd. Only four members attended the last named event at which time they enjoyed a corn and weinie roast.

The first social event of the fall season was a Halloween party held at the home of Dr. Robert Parker on Saturday, October 30th.

DORIS JONES

LONG TRAIL PATROL REPORT
SUMMER OF 1954

The Long Trail Patrol worked on 93.6 miles of trail putting 72.1 miles in good condition and 21.5 miles in fair condition. "Good condition" includes cutting out blowdowns, cutting brush, and painting blazes. "Fair condition" includes cutting out blowdowns, cutting the worst of the brush, and some painting done.

The following trails were left in good condition: The Long Trail from Taft Lodge to about a mile north of Hazen's Notch Camp; from Taylor Lodge to Bolton Mt.; and from Governor Clement Shelter to Consultation Point. The Hazelton Trail from Barnes Camp to the Toll Road. The Appalachian Trail from Long Trail Lodge to the Barnard Gulf Road. The side trail from Pico Junction to Pico Shelter over the top of the mountain.

The Long Trail was painted from a mile north of Hazen's Notch to the Canadian border and from there to Journey's End Camp. From Bolton Lodge to Bolton Mt. no painting was done but the Trail was worked over.

These are the conditions that prevailed B.C. (before Carol). The only extensive sample of the Trail that I saw after the hurricane, Hazen's Notch to Tillotson's Camp, had about as many blowdowns as we had taken out. This didn't happen to be too many but it sure was discouraging.

Work was done on the following shelters:

Clarendon Lodge-Built a privy. This is definitely a model camp.

Puffer Lodge-Skidded in the logs for the front.

Sterling Pond Camp-Creosoted and tarred a couple of holes in the roof. Excellent shape.

Whiteface Shelter-Nailed the tin back on the roof, built a scenic privy.

Beaver Meadow Lodge-excellent.

French Camp-New tarpaper, jacked up the floor some and replaced one sill but the floor still lists. New boards on the floor and the area under the bunks sealed off. Stovepipe replaced. One rail privy. Right now this camp is in good usable condition but I would hate to guess how long it will last.

Barrows Camp-Creosote, tarred the roof, stovepipe. Excellent.

Parker Camp-New tarpaper, dug the dirt away from the sill. Applied chicken wire, creosote. Some repairs to the privy. Camp excellent shape but the stove still smokes.

Ritterbush Camp-New boards and tarpaper on the roof. New table and benches. Floor patched. Creosote. Stovepipe. Privy put back on the foundation. Good shape but some dry rot in the back plate and part of the roof.

Tillotson Camp-Creosote, floor patched, tar on the roof, new stovepipe. In excellent condition but will need new tarpaper in a few years.

Hazen's Notch Camp-Dirt dug away from the sill, creosote stovepipe. Excellent.

Jay Camp-New boards and tarpaper, sill, corner post and rafters replaced in one corner. Floor patched. New table. Creosote. Chicken wire in front. I think we saved it for the present but some more work could be done to bring it up to standard.

Laura Woodward and Shooting Star Shelters-We didn't do anything to either of them. They remain in good shape.

Journey's End-New stove-creosote. Excellent.

WILLIAM ALLARD

REPORT OF THE TRUSTEES MEETING

A very well attended meeting of the trustees was held at The Green Mountain Club's office at 63 Center street, Rutland, Vermont on Saturday, November 6th.

Progress was made toward the development of a plan for the funds to be received from the sale of The Long Trail Lodge property. A committee consisting of Chairman Ted Goddard, William Mitchell, George Petrocelli, Mrs. Lillian Lodge, and a fifth member to be named, was appointed to consult with various Trust Agencies and to report at the April Trustees meeting.

Roy Buchanan presented a report of the Long Trail Patrol which appears in detail elsewhere in this issue.

An agreement was reached on the portion of the Trail for which each Section is responsible.

Eugene Bamforth made a brief report on the progress of the work on the new Guide Book. His committee has decided on a complete revision of the present Guide Book. It will be another year or more before the edition can be made ready. In the meantime, all Guide Books sold will be accompanied by a supplement correcting the more troublesome errors.

Fred Field, chairman of the Section Service Committee, has offered help to various Sections with suggestions and program material. He is also looking into the possibility of establishing one or two new Sections.

Louise Goeres, chairman of Publicity and Membership, reported some of her experiences in trying to gather information about The Green Mountain Club here in Vermont. She found few people who had heard of the Club or knew the location of The Long Trail. Several ideas for better publicity were offered. Miss Goeres needs the help of two or three Vermont residents to help her carry out the extensive program so obviously needed.

Frances Green reported for the End-to-Enders Committee that Mr. Ben Rolston of Schenectady, New York has earned the End-to-Enders award.

Mrs. Lodge urged the trustees to take an active interest in the many conservation problems that are constantly arising.

The trustees adopted the suggestion of Mr. Bartlett that the next Intersectional Get-together be held at the same place as this year, in Danby, Vermont.

Committee chairmen were invited to use the columns of THE LONG TRAIL NEWS in connection with their work.

PRESIDENT'S LETTER

Since the By-laws of the Green Mountain Club are not available in printed form, it seems desirable to summarize those parts that are of particular importance to Section members and officers.

Local organizations, or Sections, of the GMC may be established by the Trustees upon application by an interested group. The Trustees may disestablish a Section under certain circumstances.

Each Section controls its own membership. A Section member has full privileges as a member of the GMC, but a Section may limit its Section privileges to whomever it chooses.

Sections set their own dues, but they must be at least $2 for a member and $.50 for a junior member, payable in advance. Junior members are under twenty-one years of age and may not hold elective office.

Each Section elects a President, a Secretary, and such other officers as it chooses.

It is the duty of the Section President to report orally or in writing the activities and membership of the Section at the annual meeting of the GMC.

It is the duty of the Section Secretary, by June 1st of each year, to send to the GMC office at 63 Center St., Rutland, Vt. a complete list of the names and addresses of the Section's members and junior members.

By April 1st of each year the Section must send to the Treasurer of the GMC an assessment of $.35 for each member and $.25 for each junior member as shown on the membership list of the preceding year. Other funds and assets of the Section are under its control, and the GMC is not responsible for its debts.

Each Section is obligated to maintain to the satisfaction of the Trustees those trails and shelters assigned to, and accepted by it for maintenance.

If the GMC is to function properly it is important that every Section do its part. Section officers are urged to see to it that these By-laws are complied with so that our records are complete and accurate, and the condition of our trails and shelters satisfactory.

MIDDLEBURY COLLEGE
MIDDLEBURY, VERMONT

Work on a new cabin in the Pleiad Section of the Long Trail has just been completed by members of the Middlebury College Mountain Club. The cabin, christened Skyline Lodge, is named after the governing board of the college organization. Construction began in the fall of 1952 and was completed earlier this autumn.

Skyline Lodge is located on the east side of Battell Mountain between Skylight Pond and the Long Trail. Authorization for its construction in the Battell Forest was issued by the local Forest Ranger. It can be reached by way of a short, well-marked side trail which leads to the east off the Long Trail. Another

END-TO-ENDERS

   The 72nd End-To-Enders award has recently been given to Mr. Ben Rolston of Schenectady, New York.
   The following is a summary of Mr. Rolston's report of his End-To-End hikes on the Long Trail:

   "It was a thrilling moment, reaching Montclair Glen Camp at 12:55 P.M. on Sunday, August 8th, 1954. An ambition of many years was finally achieved, and a four-year project had just been completed.
   "It all started on April 15, 1951 when I curiously tried my first section of the Long Trail from the Massachusetts Line to the Bennington Road. I liked it! So in September of that same year I hiked the next section to the Arlington Road.
   "This Long Trail had me most enthused. By that time I began to wonder if I shouldn't try to complete the entire trail. So in May of 1952 I did the section from the Arlington Road to the Manchester Road—my first overnight trip on the Trail, camping at Willis Ross Camp on Stratton Pond.

   "My mind was made up, my goal was set. End to End! So that year I took 5 more trips. Memorial Day weekend gave me three days to do the section from the Eden Road to the Canadian Border, camping overnight at Jay and Journeys End Camps.
   "In August a two-day jaunt from the Manchester Road to the Clarendon Road, camping at Greenwall Shelter. Another August trip, just a one-day hike, from the Clarendon Road to the Long Trail Lodge.
   "A one-day September trip took me from the Long Trail Lodge to Brandon Gap. Later that month a two-day hike took me from Brandon Gap to Lincoln Gap, staying at Proctor Shelter overnight. Thus 1952 faded away into very happy memories with my goal much nearer.
   "Due to extensive hiking in Massachusetts, Connecticut, and New York, 1953 allowed me only one Long Trail trip—a two-day hike in May from Lincoln Gap to Montclair Glen Camp, with the night spent at Cowles Cove Shelter. A bad storm prevented me from going over Camels Hump that trip as anticipated.
   "This year a nice long weekend over the 4th of July—and I chalked off the section from the Winooski River to the Johnson Highway, with a night at Taft Lodge and another at French Camp. What a crystal clear day to go over mighty Mt. Mansfield, a hiking experience I'll never forget !
   "August 7th took me from the Johnson Highway to the Eden Road. A quick trip in to Wiley Lodge for camping that night, waking up full of excitement early that next morning, with only majestic Camel's Hump between me and my goal. Up, up, over the top, down, down, at last reaching Montclair Glen Camp, a moment I'll always remember."
