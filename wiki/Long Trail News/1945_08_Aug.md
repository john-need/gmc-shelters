TRUSTEES MEETING
June 2, 1945
RUTLAND FREE LIBRARY, RUTLAND, VT.

The meeting was called to order by President Dean.
The trustees present were: Tye, Perkins, Dean, Puffer, Bartlett, Vondell, Burkhardt, Goddard, Petrocelli, and Buchanan.
The minutes of the last meeting were read and accepted.
Mr. Dean was reelected President.
Mr. Goddard was re-elected Vice President.
Mr. Boyce was re-elected Treasurer.
Mr. Burkhardt was re-elected Auditor.
The budget proposed at the April meeting was adopted with the exception of the item for emblems, which was raised from $25 to $35. This raised the total budget to $965.
Prof. Puffer and Mr. Vondell were named to prepare a corrected "Short Trips on the Long Trail" folder for possible publication next year.
The meeting adjourned.

June 21, 1945

Hon. D. K. Hoch,
Representative from Pa.,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Sir:—
I am pleased to inform you that the Green Mountain Club, Inc., which built, and now maintains, the Long Trail over the Green Mountains of Vermont, voted at its recent annual meeting to place itself on record as in favor of the passage of your bill for the establishment of a National Trail System. The Green Mountain Club is willing, and would prefer, to continue to maintain and operate the Long Trail and its shelters, but we realize that many parts of our country cannot be provided with similar opportunities for healthful outdoor recreation without government assistance. We therefore hope your bill will be acted upon favorably soon.

Respectfully yours,
L. W. DEAN, Pres. G.M.C., Inc.

WHO IS THE EDITOR?

At the Annual Meeting last June the editor made his usual plea for more contributions from the sections, or from individual members for the LONG TRAIL NEWS. Of course the more others contribute the less he has to write, and the less he has to do the more attractive he finds his job. After he had spoken his piece some one got up and remarked that if the editor would let his readers know who he is and where he lives it might be easier to furnish him with material. The editor (he is really only the temporary acting editor, for the time being) therefore makes the following confession: his name and office address are, Louis B. Puffer, Waterman Bldg., University of Vermont, Burlington, Vermont.

Now send in your contributions!

A BOOST FOR VERMONT

The International Business Machine Company publishes a magazine called "Think", presumably in the hope that more people will, and in the August issue there is a well illustrated article called "The Backbone of Vermont," by Walter and Margaret Hard. This is naturally of interest to Green Mountain Club members since the Long Trail follows the said backbone for two hundred and sixty-two miles, climbing up and down over the somewhat variable sized vertebrae, and we are glad to pardon the editor of "Think" for calling a picture of a log spring-house on a farm in Underhill "One of the wayside camps maintained by the Green Mountain Club." The article combines description of Vermont's scenery with a bit of its geology and some of its history in a way that should interest all who read "Think". I wish it could be brought to the attention of all who think they can read. A few quoted sentences follow:

"Vermont produces more maple products than any other state in the Union. And there is another harvest which Vermont garners. It is the harvest derived from the surpassing beauty of its mountain landscape. Year by year artists cry to secure that beauty upon their canvases. To-day a traveler, pack on back, may follow the well marked trail of the Green Mountain Club which is known as the Long Trail. It extends from the Massachusetts boundary line along the ridge of the Green Mountains all the way north to the Canadian border. The traveler will be assured of comfortable shelter in the properly spaced camps which are maintained by the club."

This is the kind of publicity we like. It is free and unsolicited, and by telling the public our trail is well marked, and our shelters comfortable and properly spaced it is a spur to the club to see to it that people who read and believe this statement are not disappointed when they actually come on the trail.

We copy the following, without permission of course, from the March-April issue of "The Adirondac," published by the Adirondack Mountain Club.

WITCH WATER

There is a very delightful verse about a Hill Billy—and we don't mean the pistol packing, banjo playing variety—who was out in the woods one day. Hearing the tinkle of hidden water, he searched for it. Now there is something very compelling about a small pool of "lonesome water" in the ferns. The fragrance of all plant life intermingled is beyond description and it is small wonder that he knelt and drank of the refreshing spring. It was a great mistake, however, for one taste of this bewitching water and nothing else mattered but the "woods, streams and hills, the wild-life that lives therein." Nor could he ever leave for he was bound to the hills.

We would not be at all surprised if this Hill Billy were the first ADK-er. (or the first GMC-er.)

NEWS NOTES
NEW YORK SECTION
GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB

During the summer, the regular Sunday hikes of the New York Section become one or at most two a month, but many other activities continue or begin. Hikers who favor the hills tend to forget that New York is a coastal city. Two trips to Fire Island—one as guests of the New York Section of the Appalachian Mountain Club—supply first-hand information on what sand, sea, and sun are like.

And speaking of water, there has been plenty from above this spring and summer, but twenty-six waterproof canoeists spent a damp but cheerful day on the Passaic River in June.

Whether or not the cold, rainy spring was responsible no one is certain; but all who have been on June and early July hikes have agreed that the oldest inhabitant has never seen the mountain laurel more profuse and beautifully colored than it has been this year.

Combination outings have included not only the Appalachian Club—G.M.C. expedition to Fire Island, but a joint week-end at Camp Thendara with the Philadelphia Trail Club, when forty-nine people would have bulged out the walls of the camp excepting for the fact that it was so hot walls were forgotten in favor of the waters of Lake Tiorati.

The annual Bird Census held jointly with the Torrey Botanical Club in May did not produce such a long list of bird-visitors as usual, perhaps because of the exceptionally cold and belated spring.

CONSTANCE M. HALLOCK,

The following is from the July-September 1945 Bulletin of the New York Section of the Green Mountain Club. It may be of interest to other sections, especially in Vermont.

ADVICE TO WALKERS

Outings are not postponed on account of weather.

All-day trips, while providing some rough and strenuous walking, are planned for the average hiker.

All railroad and bus fares are subject to change without notice. You may still buy round-trip, but no more excursion tickets.

Bring your own lunch and equipment on all-day outings.

Leaders are cautioned to obey all fire regulations in building campfires.

Members should accompany guests on all outings.

SATURDAY WALKS are arranged for the convenience of those who may wish to come directly from the office to the walk. Low-heeled shoes and office clothes will be found suitable for such outings.

It is important that those planning to attend club outings check train or bus schedule either with leader, or at transportation terminal, as these time tables frequently change without much advance notice.

DOWN MASSACHUSETTS WAY

Altho Worcester is not in Vermont, our G. M. C. Section has an annual Sugaring-off Party, and this year was no exception. Sixty-seven members and guests enjoyed an excellent church supper, in spite of rationing, and topped it off with sugar on snow. A long hike in the afternoon sharpened many of our appetites, and after eating, a collection of colored slides entitled "The Lure of New England Spring" gave us close-ups of all (and many more) of the wild flowers that we had found on the hike.

Another Big Event for us was when five of our members who live in Amherst (Our Amherst Annex) came to Worcester for a week-end at Camp Cobb. Arriving on Saturday afternoon, they were joined by local members. Before sundown a forty foot flag pole was set up, more parking space was cleared (just in case!!) and our wood pile had grown considerably. Plans for a Long Trail Work Party were formulated. On Sunday a grand dinner prepared by three members was enjoyed between hikes, and "Old Glory" was officially raised over Camp Cobb.

Our two business meetings were well attended, and at both colored slides of local interest were shown.

Friday, April 27, 1945

A message:
   To you my friends, I regret that I cannot be with you here today.
   I also regret that I cannot bring to you the many pleasant experiences I have had with the woods folk in the days spent here. A careful record of these happenings have been kept in my journals of the past years and the story of "Spruce Chalet" may be written some day. Among the many beauties have been the snow-laden spruce trees as seen upon a starry or moon-light night in the past winters. Standing in the little open space at the outskirts of the thicket, one could listen to the silence while gazing up at the snow-laden boughs which hemmed him in upon all sides. Great mittened hands would reach out toward you as you looked out past the cone-laden tips, far up and far out into the milky white depths of the starry heavens.
   Be it a night in winter, when that old, old, very old wind howls its song out in the cold forest, when white fleecy clouds scud across the face of the almost-full moon, when you snuggle under the blankets to watch the light from the spruce wood fire dance and flicker upon the ceiling—or, be it a morning in June when you awaken early to listen to the early bird songs—It all has to be lived to be appreciated. The pen of the writer cannot quite describe, and the brush of the artist cannot quite depict that certain something. That certain something is much within the observer and there may be as many different versions of nature as there are observers.
   You people are out here today to live nature. Your powers of observation are already developed, and your fires of enthusiasm need no kindling. So tarry awhile and enjoy your lunch. Hike our forest trails. Go out to Lookout Ledge. Relax or climb the tower (slowly and carefully). Watch a beautiful panorama of mountain scenery unfold. Look down upon acres of forest campy with its harmony of soft spring tints and drink in the beauties of the Otter creek valley.
   Don't forget to place your name in the register. It will be cherished as your autograph in the years to come. Also, don't forget to come again for you, like the woods folk, are always welcome at Spruce Chalet.

Thank you.
DANIEL EDDY POTTER

From the Burlington Free Press, August 27, 1945.

GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB MEMBERS ENJOY OUTING AT CAMP ABNAKI

   Twenty-one Burlington Section Green Mountain Club followers spent this past weekend at Camp Abnaki in North Hero. The opportunity affording this outing was made possible through the cordial arrangements of Associate Camp Director Eddie Bartlett and the courtesy of Camp Director Clyde G. Hess.
   In spite of a variety of weather, some of it nasty, a thoroughly grand time was had by the group beginning mid-afternoon Saturday and ending when Eddie bid the mountaineers adieu Sunday evening.
   Volley ball, tennis and badminton were the favored sports enjoyed by both sexes. Mildred Weidman and Natalie Persing topped the badminton contests. Lieut. Ray Persing and Ken Prior set a fast pace for their teammates in the volley ball events.
   Around the warm cozy glow of the fire place in the "Ad building" the group was led in song by Walt Weidman, formerly of Burlington and now of Malden, Mass., Boy Scout executive, and Zama Tousley directed the bunch in games. Mrs. Belle Cobb gave an entertaining reading.
   There was the usual hike and a few braved the chilly winds and cool water to go in bathing. Some of the group set their shoulders to the oars.
   The clean well kept camp chow hall was a popular spot for the hungry outdoor folks. Camp Chef Dan Macintyre was the most popular chap in camp. His hot doughnuts, date muffins and blueberry pies found ready consumers.
   The mountaineers will hit in along the Long Trail next outing, going to the Bolton area, according to schedule on Labor day weekend.
   Larry Dean served as group leader on this week's outing.

   The Killington Section lists on its schedule of hikes, "June 17, Sunday,—Pancake Breakfast and Day at Adams Camp, Shrewsbury." Pancake Breakfast! There's an idea worth copying by other, or all, sections,—when we can get some bacon or sausage to go with it.
