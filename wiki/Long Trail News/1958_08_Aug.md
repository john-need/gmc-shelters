## PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE

As your elected Trustees at their May 31st meeting following the Annual Meeting, reappointed me, your President for another year, I will continue to help steer The Green Mountain Club along a steady course guided by the By-laws, the action of your Trustees, and by suggestions offered by you, the members. In this last regard, it was refreshing to observe at our 1958 Annual Meeting that the members present took an active part in the discussion, and debated at length subjects which have frequently in the past been read and voted on with no comments from the floor. Although this delayed our adjournment and cut into the scheduled afternoon walks, your officers and Trustees will continue to encourage member participation in all of our problems.

Although I can not promise a detailed answer to all letters I wish to encourage members to direct to my attention their comments and suggestions pertaining to ways and means to strengthen and improve our Club and our Trail. Our Club is only as strong as the interest shown by its members.

ARTHUR R. KOERBER, President

## NOTES ON THE ANNUAL MEETING

The Green Mountain Club's Annual Meeting held May 31st at Long Trail Lodge in Sherburne, Vermont was one of the liveliest we have had for some time. From the point of view of group participation, work accomplished, and attendance, it may be reported as a very successful meeting.

Don Kent did an excellent job as Chairman of arrangements for the meeting, and presiding officer, President Koerber, followed through with a full agenda.

The usual Section reports were given, and from them it was learned that many Sections are not in particularly good health but are doing the best they can under the handicap of shortage of man power.

It was encouraging to learn that the Sterling Section is active again.

The president of the New York Section, John Rohrbaugh, gave an invitation to GMC members to visit the New York Section at Thendara the weekend of November 8-9. Details of this may be found elsewhere in this issue.

Intersectional Chairman, John Martin, passed out information sheets regarding the 1958 Intersectional to be held August 16-17 at the Craigue Perkins camp grounds known as Tall Timber in Mendon, Vermont.

End-to-Enders Awards Chairman, Alan Gormly, reported that 88 awards have been given out.

In conjunction with our 1960 Fiftieth Year Anniversary, a 1960 Edition of the Guide Book will be printed, according to Chairman, Ted Talbot.

After giving her report, Mrs. Lillian P. Lodge, Chairman of Conservation, read a resolution which was passed by the Club. Due to its importance, we are placing it in a more prominent spot elsewhere in this issue.

Larry Dean, after serving for many years as Chairman of the Historical Committee, turned in the data he has collected, and also presented his resignation as chairman of the committee.

The following trustees were re-elected: John Paulson, John Martin, Donald Kent, Roy Buchanan. Newly elected was Harlan Farnsworth. Ted Goddard was re-elected Clerk, and Minerva Hinchey was re-elected Corresponding Secretary.

Evening of the Annual Meeting

After a long day of seriousness, it was a pleasure to have for an evening's entertainment, something on the lighter side; something to smile and laugh about, something to complete an already pleasant and profitable Annual Meeting. When Don Pent presented the speaker of the evening, the audience sat back with hope and anticipation, and it wasn't many minutes before these feelings were realized.

Mr. C. Ross McKenney, the speaker of the evening, gave forth in a French Canadian dialect with story after story of the north country. He had his audience with him from the start as was evidenced from the smiles and laughs of the group. After a while, he changed his style of delivery and told some interesting nature stories, and ended with a beautiful poem.

John Martin, with his projector and screen showed slides that members from various Sections had brought to the meeting.

RESOLUTION AND MOTION PASSED AT THE ANNUAL MEETING OF THE GMC
MAY 31, 1958

Resolved

That, whereas public lands in Vermont have recently been given over to commercial interests, the Green Mountain Club is firmly opposed to further opening of public lands or wilderness areas in Vermont to commercial interests.

Also, that any proposed future developments be widely publicized six months in advance of any action, so that all who are interested may have ample time to consider and approve or oppose the project.

Motion—

The Trustees are hereby directed to give the above resolution wide publicity throughout the state, including the State Dept. of Forests and Parks, National Forest Service and Vermont Development Commission.

CONSERVATION

We think the following is noteworthy: At the session on "The Future of the Appalachian Trail" at the Appalachian Trail Conference held at Mountain Lake, Virginia on June 2, 1958, the following motion was made and passed: "That the Chairman appoint a committee to investigate, in consultation and cooperation with local clubs, members, and other interested parties, the situation with respect to the surroundings of the Appalachian Trail and the possibilities of perpetuation of the Trail in the several states, to report to the Board of Managers from time to time;

"And, as directed by the Chairman and the Board from time to time to take such steps as may seem advisable in connection therewith, whether it involves Federal or State ownership, purchase of land or easements, or other methods;

"And also, to raise funds for such last named purpose, if such action appears desirable."

LONG TRAIL CONFERENCE
On Saturday, November 1st, immediately following the brief Trustees' meeting, there will be conducted the Second Long Trail Conference.
   It is hoped that all Sections will be represented by their Trail Chairman or by others thoroughly familiar with their Trail conditions and with the amount of work done during the past year. Representatives of the Forest Service will be there to report on the Trail in the National Forest, and Roy Buchanan will report on the activities of the Patrol which covers the parts of the Trail not in the National Forest nor assigned to the GMC Sections.
   Among the subjects to be taken up are: detailed trail and shelter reports, amount of volunteer labor supplied by the various Sections, use of volunteer labor supplied by summer camps, suggestions for next year's Patrol activities, estimation of cost of Trail and Shelter maintenance.
   The general purposes of the Conference are to improve the Long Trail by better knowledge of its condition, better understanding of the money and labor necessary, and encouragement of the many groups involved in the problem.

CRAIGIE S. PERKINS,
Trail Conference, Chairman

NEW TRAIL AT LITTLE ROCK POND
The area around Little Rock Pond is, we think, one of the most beautiful spots on the Long Trail. So it is a pleasure to report that Green Mountain, the mountain that slopes down to the Pond's edge, now has a trail made to its summit. Over a weekend in June, Ben Ralston, with the Scoutmaster of Troop 48 of Schenectady, and six of the patrol leaders of his troop camped at Little Rock Pond, and completed the construction of this new trail. The following is Ben's report:
   "Beginning at the leanto bridge, the trail makes a gradual ascent to the low part of the ridge, then continues along this ridge to an interesting humpback rock, which it traverses. Just above this rock an open viewpoint is reached, giving the hiker an excellent view of Rutland and the Coolidge Range. Continuing its ascent, the trail alternates between spruce woods and open rocks, with several views of Little Rock Pond below. Soon a spur trail is reached, which leads a short distance west to an open rock. From this point, the view extends from South Wallingford directly below, to the Coolidge Range, with the Otter Creek Valley, the city of Rutland and its environments, and the distant Adirondacks. A short distance beyond the spur trail, the Green Mountain Trail terminates at an open rock, giving excellent views to the east and south. The route which the Long Trail follows to the south can be seen for many miles, and the view extends toward Manchester and the Taconics. The actual summit of Green Mountain is not attained as it is heavily wooded. The length of this new trail is .7 mile."

NOTES FROM THE NEW YORK SECTION
The New York Section has undertaken an ambitious plan in the building of a shelter (Grenvelle T. Anderson Memorial) at Stony Brook on the AT in Stokes State Forest, N. J. about three miles north of Culver's Gap. On April 19-20 a scheduled work party gave the project a vigorous start with enthusiastic log pealing and laying the foundation. After many laborious weekends, the shelter is nearing completion, thanks to the faithful workers for making this worthy project possible.
   The last Sunday Walk of the season on June 29th attracted 15 who hiked into Thendara where they had a delightful dip in Lake Tiorati. Come September, Saturday and Sunday walks will be resumed.
   On July 4-5-6, thirty-eight (including 5 Vermonters) enjoyed the long holiday weekend at Thendara. During July and August our camp will be open mid-weeks as well as weekends, giving vacationists the opportunity for hiking, swimming, canoeing, or just relaxing, and our hospitable hosts will be happy to have members from other Sections join us in these pleasures.
   Eager campers are looking forward to the Intersectional, August 16-17, at the Perkins Camp in Mendon, Vermont, and also the annual Labor Day Long Trail trip, which will repeat the itinerary of the 1947 trip when this popular outing was initiated. Campers and "Inners" will take off for the Catskills, October 11-13, when we hope that nature will reward us with a glorious array of fall colors.

GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB TRUST
The Green Mountain Club Trust, created by the monies received from the sale of the Long Trail Lodge, has now been in operation for several years with the Howard National Bank & Trust Co. of Burlington, Vermont as trustee.
   The investments of the trust are, of course, conservative with approximately 30% in common stocks, 19% in preferred stocks, and 42% in bonds. The balance is in the savings bank. The estimated income for this year is slightly over $2,000 or over 4% on the value of the trust.
   The Trust Committee has worked closely with the bank in the selection of the securities, and is in touch with the bank from time to time as need arises; altogether a happy arrangement, and a welcome income for the Club.

TED N. GODDARD, Chairman

REPORT FROM THE KILLINGTON SECTION

In planning its spring and early summer schedule of activities, Killington Section concentrated on Work-Outings which would cover our section of The Trail. We felt that this would afford some good hikes and also put our Trail in condition for the hiking season. The results were disappointing. On the first trip a sufficient number showed up for a fairly adequate work party. The next trip brought out three trail workers; the third, two; the fourth, one; and the last two. This meant that all the actual work which could be accomplished was the re-painting of blazes in most cases in one direction only; putting up some needed arrows; and an occasional bit of clearing.

In spite of this failure of our Work-Outings to accomplish what we had hoped, they have at least given us a first hand check of the condition of our section of The Trail this season, with the exception of the Pico Shelter to Cooper Lodge stretch. In spite of conflicting reports varying all the way from good to terrible we can report that this section can be rated as fair to good and can be hiked without undue difficulty. This is not nearly enough, however. There is much Trail and Shelter work which could and should be done. The Trail will not remain in even a usable condition indefinitely without it.

We feel that this points up a serious situation which exists not only in Killington Section but in GMC in general, namely, the lack of a feeling of responsibility on the part of enough able members to take an active part in carrying out the main purpose of GMC—the maintaining of The Long Trail and its shelters. What is the solution? We wish we knew. It is a problem which needs serious consideration resulting in a program which can be put into action.

VERA A. PERKINS

NEW YORK SECTION'S TRAIL REPORT

To date (July 14) this season the New York Section has run one Trail Crew which operated in the Camel's Hump area this June.

A Trail report to date finds the following: From Lincoln Gap to the Jerusalem Trail conditions vary from fair to poor. The Long Trail from the Jerusalem Trail to Appalachian Gap, and the side trail to Glen Ellen Lodge are both in good to excellent condition. The Trail from the Gap to Baby Stark remains an unknown this year since it has not yet been scouted, but it should be no worse than fair, and the Trail from Baby Stark to Huntington Gap is good to excellent.

There is a short stretch north of Huntington Gap which is grown in with spruces, but the Trail from Cowles Cove to the Winooski River is almost entirely good to excellent with a few fair spots on the north side of Camel's Hump.

The Trail from Allis's Gallery (located a mile north of Burnt Rock Mt.) to just south of the summit of Camel's Hump is freshly lettered to the extent that even the ever present birches were pulled up by the roots. The Beane Trail, Forest City and Burrows side trails are all wide open. The Jerusalem Trail is fair to good but the other side trails need work badly. All shelters are in very good condition at the latest report.

Projected work for the summer includes clearing the fair and poor sections of trail mentioned above, and a blazing and sign painting campaign, all to be held in September by the fall Trail Crew.

The New York Section is joining the Burlington Section in launching a vessel of sorts on the Winooski River which will ferry any hikers across the river who wish to avoid cold feet.

WILLIAM BARTLETT, Chairman,
N. Y. Section, Trails & Shelters

BENNINGTON SECTION REPORT

Thendara Camp succumbs to old age and a hard winter.

Deer View built to replace Thendara.

Thendara—the Vermont Thendara, that is! Heavy snow, suspected vandalism and old age combined to bring down Thendara shelter in Woodford last winter. A place that had bunks for ten people ended by having space for two or three under the peak of the roof where it rested on the ground.

Young Ted and I cleaned up the site a few days before the annual meeting in May, and salvaged what we could, which wasn't much. Then, early in June, lumber was bought, and we started rebuilding. Fortunately the lumber was delivered to the site, and we were able to drive to it so the back breaking work of building a shelter was eliminated. Five and a half work days later we had a new camp completed, creosoted, and ready for use.

It is a 12x8 lean-to, fairly heavily made, and should last a while. We figure it should sleep eight without crowding, and more if necessary.

We have renamed it DEER VIEW so as not to confuse it with the New York Section's pet Camp. Also, DEER VIEW is a local name which the old shelter had gone by, and there were nice fresh deer tracks over the cleared site the morning we started building.

T. N. GODDARD

"A TRIBUTE TO VERMONT"

"A few regions God has made more beautiful than others. His hand has fashioned some dreams or symbols of heaven in certain landscapes of earth; and we have always thought that the Almighty intended when He formed the hills of Vermont and shook out the green drapery of the forests over their sloping shoulders, and roaded them fall in folds like the robe of a king along their sides, to give us a dim picture of the new creation and the celestial realm. Italy is a land of rarer sunsets and deeper sky, of haunting songs and grander memories. Switzerland is a region of more towering sublimity and unapproachable grandeur. But in all the scenes of God there is none that so shows the exquisite genius of creative art; the blending of all that is beautiful and attractive with nothing to terrify the eye; the mingling of so much of the material glory, both of the earth and the heavens, with so little to appeal the sense. Vermont in summer is the Almighty's noblest gallery of divine art."

W. H. LORD
New England Magazine
(August 1891)

LONG TRAIL PATROL REPORT

We have cleared the Trail from Smuggler's Notch to Tillotson Camp. This is clear of blowdowns but not mowed except in bad spots. We have painted blazes for that whole 36 miles except 3½ miles between Ithiel Falls and Barrows Camp which was painted last year. In addition we have put in glass at Ritterbush and put new hen fence across one end that was broken. New glass was put in French Camp's east window, and 26 bullet holes in the roof of this camp were patched with tar.

Today, July 3rd, we set the poles for, and erected road signs at Rt. 15 west of Johnson, at the cliffs in Smuggler's Notch, and at the foot of Taft trail in Smuggler's Notch.

Camps French, Barrows, Parker, Ritterbush, and Tillotson are now O.K. Taft and Butler have caretakers this year.

ROY BUCHANAN, Chairman
Trails and Shelters

REPAIR JOB AT BOLTON LODGE

On the weekend of June 7-8 the Properties Committee of the Burlington Section did an extensive repair job at Bolton Lodge. There were 24 people on the job Saturday and Sunday.

They rebuilt about three feet of brick chimney, replaced several roof boards, shingled the whole front half of the roof, replaced chimney flashing, and reshingled near the chimney, put in two complete new windows, put in new boards for a whole bunk and half of another one, replaced two settee backs, built up a toilet foundation, and replaced the inside of a toilet, and cleaned up the area. Quite a job!

ROY BUCHANAN
