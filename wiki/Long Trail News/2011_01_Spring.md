                    Thanks to the
                    twenty-four
                    photographers
                    who entered
                    GMC’s centen-
                    nial year photo
contest! The judges found this
year’s field of images so compelling

ten, and could easily have increased
it further. Many of these great
photographs will grace the pages of
the Long Trail News and appear on
GMC’s website in coming months.

Sloan. Emily received a Green
Mountain Club guidebook and
map collection (four books and four
maps) for her photograph of Green
River Reservoir. The winning ­image
was taken during a September
canoe camping trip near the loon
recovery project zone that Emily
helps monitor.

Kelly O’Reilly were first prize
winners in our three categories,
winning their choice of a GMC
t-shirt or the club’s award-winning
centennial coffee-table book, A
Century in the Mountains: Celebrat-
ing Vermont’s Long Trail.

Krisch, Sheri Larsen, Ed Linton,

Gore, and Ryan Smith were singled
out for honorable mention and
awarded a GMC centennial logo
fleece blanket. Look for their excep-
tional photos in this and subse-
quent issues of the Long Trail News.

Larson, Susan Shea, Megan Duni,
and Brian Prendergast.

                                   Eden Forest Conserved
                                                                       A
                                                                                   5,720-acre tract of forestland adjacent to the
                                                                                   Long Trail corridor in Eden and Johnson has
                                                                                   been conserved! This big timberland parcel
                                                                                   is located east of the trail corridor previously
                                                                       protected by the Green Mountain Club which runs
                                                                       between Bowen and Butternut Mountains, and includes
                                                                       46 miles of headwater streams that flow into the Gihon
                                                                       River and 120 acres of wetlands. The Trust for Public
                                                                       Land purchased a conservation easement over the land
                                                                       from Green Crow with federal Forest Legacy funds, and
                                                                       conveyed the conservation restrictions to the State of
                                                                       Vermont this past November. The conservation ease-
                                                                       ment prevents development and subdivision, allows
                                                                       sustainable timber-harvesting, and requires that the
                                                                       land be kept open to the public for hunting, hiking,
                                                                       skiing, fishing, and snowmobiling on an existing VAST
                                                                       trail. GMC supported the Trust for Public Land’s multi-
                                                                       year efforts to conserve this property by writing letters
                                                                       of support and testifying before Congress.
                                                                           The conservation of Eden Forest is part of a larger,
                                                                       long-term effort by the Trust for Public Land, Vermont
                                                                       Land Trust, and the Staying Connected Initiative to
                                                                       conserve 20,000 acres of productive forestland in the
                                                                       Northern Green Mountains that are important for wild-
                                                                       life, water quality, public recreation and local econo-
                                                                       mies. This work will build on GMC’s accomplishments
                                                                       over the past twenty-five years in protecting 25,000
                                                                       acres surrounding the Long Trail System in the heart of
                                                                       the Green Mountains.
                                                                                                                   –Susan Shea

Directors. General directors represent the Green Mountain Club membership as a whole
and serve alongside section directors elected by the sections. Each year the Nominat-

­candidates in the spring issue.
