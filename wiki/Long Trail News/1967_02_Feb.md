PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE

Ben Rolston is known to all of us. He is the editor of
the Guide Book of the Long Trail and he already has
been editor of three editions. The Guide Book changes
in this issue of The Long Trail News were written up by
him. He assisted greatly in writing the present Short Walks
on the Long Trail folder. He writes the "Out on the Trail"
piece that we have all come to expect in this newspaper.
He has four End-to-End awards, no one has more. He
was president of the club for two years. He maintains quite
a bit of the trail personally. In short, Ben is a real active
member—our most active member.
   At last we have been able to give Ben's name to a
shelter. The new shelter that was built last summer, about
a mile south of Noyes Pond, is now ROLSTON'S REST.
The trustees named it on October 8 and it was dedicated
the next day. Ben was there—and we think we took him
by surprise. This is a new shelter on a new site on private
land. It replaces the Noyes Pond Camp, also on private
land, that has been removed by the owner.
   This issue of The Long Trail News is the first to be
produced by a new printer, the Queen City Printers Inc.
of Burlington. They also print our Short Walks folder for
us. It now is possible to use pictures. For the first issue
we use just one, a picture of our elder statesman and subject of our "cover story," Roy Buchanan.
   Black and white pictures (no color shots) of club activities with appropriate descriptions will be welcomed by
the Editor. They will be used, as fast as space permits.
   We are hopeful that the News will be delivered to the
members quite a bit earlier in the month.
   Jack Harrington (Burlington) will be the new chairman
of the Long Trail Patrol Committee. This is good news
for the club. The work done by the Patrol is essential to
our trail and shelters. The Patrol has primary responsibility
for 53 miles of the Long Trail and for 10 shelters. When
needed, they also assist the Sections.

JOHN ROHRBAUGH, President

PATROL BOSS ROY O. BUCHANAN RESIGNS

We inaugurate the use of pictures in The Long Trail
News with this picture and a story about Prof. Buchanan.
(He is a retired professor from the University of Vermont).
   Roy resigned as Chairman of the Long Trail Patrol
Committee at the October trustees' meeting. He was forced
to do this by advancing cataracts which have reached the
point of making it unsafe for him to go on the trail. This
stage of development occurs for everyone who has the
misfortune to get cataracts. Roy of course knows this.
He also knows that his many friends join him in hoping
for the better sight that he may acquire later.
   This though was most definitely not just the resignation
of another committee chairman. This was the resignation
of the man who actually started the Patrol in 1931, 36
years ago. He did it after a suggestion made by Mortimer
Proctor then GM president and later the Governor of
Vermont, and by Wallace Fay, then the trails chairman.
Roy not only started our Patrol, he ran it for the whole
36 years. He did a great deal of the trail work himself
too.
   The closed camps on our trail are of Roy's design, a
design that evolved as he built the first few and tried to
make them more and more efficient. He has had a hand
in the building of 37 of the Long Trail camps, some open

VOLUNTEER TRAIL WORK

Volunteer maintenance work on the trail and on the shelters is most welcome. More of this is being done all the time. There always is work that should be done and that is not being done because something else seems to be more important. Don't be bashful!
   The Chairman of Trails and Shelters is Roderick C. Rice, 77 Mansfield Ave., Burlington. He will direct your activity where it is needed. In any case, it is essential that Rod be fully aware of what is going on in trail maintenance. All trail work clears through him, in advance. This is required by Section VIII, 2 of the Club by-laws, a very sensible requirement. We have so much that needs doing that we cannot afford to have duplication of efforts.

PROPOSED BY-LAWS CHANGE
THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES

It is very helpful to the Board of Trustees that we have a trustee who is an official of the United States Forest Service. Our by-laws cover this.
   It would also be helpful if we could have a trustee who is an official of the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks. Our by-laws do not cover this.
   So the following changes in Article II, Section 4 of our by-laws will be moved at our annual meeting on May 27th. The Commissioner of Forests and Parks, Mr. Robert B. Williams has agreed to election if these changes are voted.
   The changes are:
(1) paragraph 1, The number of trustees shall be changed from 19 to 20;
(2) paragraph 1, Delete the words "and the" before "Green Mountain National Forest Service l";
(3) paragraph 1, Add the words "; and the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks l." after "Green Mountain National Forest Service l";
(4) Add a new paragraph 6 between the present paragraph 5 and the present paragraph 6, the present paragraph 6 thus becoming paragraph 7, "An official of the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks shall be named by the Board as a Trustee."

LONG TRAIL PATROL

I refer you to the November issue of The Long Trail News for the report of trail and camp work during the 1966 season.    But the vandals are on the job, as always. Up to now they have burned two camps this fall, Deerview and Buffum. So now the trails are overflowing and I have to dig out here twice a day. The chimney on my house still sticks out of the drifts and I know where the mailbox is but Enough is enough!

ROY BUCHANAN

BURLINGTON SECTION

The outstanding event on our fall calendar was of course the dedication of the new Cascade Shelter at Duck Brook, built this summer by the Long Trail Patrol, including three generations of Buchanans. Eighty-six people and several dogs turned out for the big day, including a photographer from the Burlington Free Press, resulting in a large feature with many, many pictures the next week. Out of town members included past presidents of the main club John Vandell and Ben Rolston, and at least four sections other than Burlington were represented. Gerry Grow had made a beautiful sign for the shelter; Don Havens, in charge of the event, served cider and doughnuts to all after Roy had christened the shelter with some strong Essex Junction water and his usual unforgettable comments.

Compared to the above, anything else looks pale, but we did have a lovely overnight to Little Rocky Pond, where we were joined by the Ken Kidds of Northfield and Terry Gulick, and the following day picked up Jamie Tarbell of Intersectional fame and went off to find Lost Pond. The exceptionally mild weather for late October made for a wonderful weekend.

To fill in the usual hunting season lull, entertainment chairman Helen Farrington scheduled a pot luck supper at the newly re-decorated Grange Hall in Colchester, and reported an excellent turnout. By the time the hunters were back home and we were out again, exploring the farther reaches of former Fort Ethan Allen in the moonlight (?), it had turned very cold and windy, and after some star-gazing when clouds permitted, most of the hikers were glad to get back to Wilda Gigee's house for something warm to drink.

The group who followed Sam Hatfield to Sterling Pond in October found that as usual, vandals had been at work, and the cabin was in poor condition. A note to those who may be planning to hike on Snake Mtn. near Vergennes: Dot Myer reported after a hike she led there that prominent no trespassing signs were displayed on the lower end of the trail, and although the owner was quite willing to have GMCers go up, it would be well to check in with her first.

When the LT News appears, we will have had the pleasure of seeing John Vondell and his slide show entitled "Some Subjects That Interest My Camera," at the Burlington Section Annual Meeting, and incoming section president Dane Short will have taken a group of snowshoers up to his pride and joy, Camp Hoot Owl, in Duxbury on the side of Camel's Hump. Other new section officers are Harris Abbott, Vice-President; Betty Myers, Secretary; Lee Brown, Treasurer. Helen Farrington is the new outing chairman, so if you expect to be in the area and want to join us on a trip call her: 802-863-2088. Remaining outings on the Fall-Winter Schedule are: Feb. 5, Ski-Snowshoe outing at Jay Peak; Feb. 18 Ritterbush Camp; Mar. 4-5 overnight in the Adirondacks; Mar. 4, day hike to Buchanan Lodge.

JAN ABBOTT, Reporter


ASCUTNEY TRAILS RE-OPENED

"The Idea of the Long Trail was born in the minds of some schoolboys and a schoolmaster who had vowed to make themselves at home on the mountains of the Green Mountain State." So wrote James P. Taylor in "Footpath in the Wilderness."

He went on to explain . . . . "The mountain nearest to the school on the 'Grand Campus' of Vermont Academy, Ascutney Mountain, was hospitality itself. A Fifth Avenue of a trail up the mountain, well-marked springs, a steel-roofed, granite-walled shelter hut on the summit—all were the work of the Ascutney Mountain Association organized by the mountain lovers of historic Windsor-on-the-Connecticut."

After mastering Ascutney, the boys found other mountains not only lacked such deluxe facilities, but were without basic trails and shelters. And so the concept of the Long Trail System began to develop.

Once again Windsorites have provided an invitation to hikers to visit Mt. Ascutney. Herbert G. Ogden and son, Herbert, Jr., have scouted, cleared and blazed the old Windsor Trail or so-called bridle trail.

Mr. Ogden writes . . . . "The Windsor Trail starts on the black top road which branches off the road (No. 44) from Brownsville to Windsor to go to the Ascutney Park entrance. A birch lane takes off from the Wheeler house (between houses No. 3 and No. 4, counting from where you leave No. 44 and ignoring mobile homes). Close to half of it follows a logging road up along Mountain Brook. There is an excellent spring about two-thirds way up, after which some 13 switchbacks go on to the top. The last one-fourth mile or so is through thick spruce—a spruce tunnel. It comes out at the site of the old Stone Hut on the northern end of the mountain. Another (Parks) trail goes from there to the observation tower on the summit. Total length of the Windsor Trail (to the Hut site) is 2.39 miles with a total of 2360 ft. climbed. With the weather just right you can see the Atlantic Ocean at Portsmouth, N. H., from the top!"

During this winter Mr. Ogden plans to work up a rough leaflet on these trails and their history, inviting comment and anecdote from former users. Interested parties may write to Herbert G. Ogden, RFD No. 1, Windsor, Vermont.

The Ogdens are also planning to organize the Ascutney Trails Association, whose function will be to maintain the Windsor Trail as well as other old and new trails on Mt. Ascutney.

PIONEER VALLEY SECTION

I am sorry to report that Deerview Shelter at the end of our section was burned to the ground sometime late in November and is a total loss. It may have been the work of vandals as they have been active in the area lately and have also broken into some of the private camps nearby. There is some hope that a new shelter can be built this year as one is needed to break up a long section of the trail.

On the pleasant side, we have enjoyed some good hikes this winter, with a growing attendance and some new members joining us each month. Early in December we hiked 7 miles of the Appalachian Trail north of Greenwater Pond in Massachusetts; the day before New Year's we hiked around Porter Lake in Springfield, Mass., and enjoyed a casserole supper at Dot Knight's house; January 8, we had a Snowshoe Hike in North Blanford, Mass., with about 20 hikers and one dog attending with weather conditions very poor with rain and fog all day.

Some of us hike every Sunday on unscheduled hikes and we would welcome anyone who would like to join us. In the past we have hiked Mt. Holyoke, Mt. Tom, some of the Metacomet Trail and other walks in the area. If you are interested call or write me at 11 Lakevilla Ave., Springfield, Mass., Tel. 783-7595.

Our annual meeting will be held on Sunday, February 5, at 1:00 at the Sugar House on Rte. 47, just north of Sunderland, Mass. Those who wish to go on a short hike will meet there at 11:00 and we will hike somewhere nearby for two hours. We will have sugar on the snow and griddlecakes for dinner and have our meeting afterward. We hope to set up a hike schedule soon, to cover several months ahead and we will put it in the LT News in the next issue.

A hike is planned for the first Sunday in March in the area of Goose Pond in the Berkshires in Massachusetts, on the AT. Write for the details if you are interested in joining us.

RICHARD METCALFE, Reporter


WORCESTER SECTION

We have had a busy and interesting fall season starting with that absolutely beautiful weekend of October 8-9 when 16 of us spent these two days in Vermont with headquarters at the Green Mountain House in West Wardsboro. On Saturday we hiked in to Stratton Pond bringing a trail lunch which we enjoyed in a delightfully warm and sunny spot. Saturday night we played games (yours truly is a whiz at charades—ask anyone who was there) and walked some more on the quiet roads. On Sunday morning we hiked south to the George Storey Shelter and in the afternoon we, of course, unwillingly packed up to return home.

On October 23, a group of 24 hiked the Metacomet Trail to Mt. Grace and on Sunday, October 30, 13 of us took a short afternoon hike in deer country around the remote hamlet of Hardwick. On November 13, a hike in the vicinity of Burncoat Pond near our Leicester camp brought out a group of 12 who later returned to our camp for coffee and a sing-a-long.

Our Annual Meeting was on October 19, at the Adams Square Congregational Church. GUIDE BOOK CHANGES

On Page 2 of the 1966 edition of the Guide Book of the Long Trail, there appears a notice which reads: "Changes in the trails and shelters will be published in the Long Trail News in a form suitable for cutting out and pasting into your Guide Book." In compliance with that notice, the Guide Book Committee has prepared the following listing of major trail and shelter changes since the Guide Book was printed in May of 1966:

Page 26: The new Pioneer Valley Section of the GMC has been assigned the maintenance of the Long Trail between Massachusetts and Deer View Shelter.

Page 29: Deer View Shelter was destroyed by fire in November, 1966. Plans are underway for a relocation and new shelter site in that area.

Page 43: South Bourn Pond Shelter, a frame leanto with bunks for 6 to 8, was built by the USPS in 1966. It is located at the southwest corner of the pond (36.8 m.), near the old (1957) shelter which will be dismantled.

Page 48: Griffith Lake Shelter, a frame leanto with bunks for 6 to 8, was built by the USPS in 1966. It is located on the east shore of Griffith Lake (57.3 m.).

Page 51: As Lost Pond was found to be a quaking bog with rare plant life, the trail from Lost Pond Shelter to the pond will not be built.

Page 56: Buffum Lodge was destroyed by fire in November, 1966. Plans for its replacement were not known at the time of preparing this article.

Page 58: Due to continued vandalism, the windows of Cooper Lodge will not be replaced. Therefore, in inclement weather the use of Governor Clement Shelter or Pico Camp is recommended.

Pages 65, 66 and 67: Northbound from Sherburne Pass, the Long Trail now utilizes the Appalachian Trail eastward for ¼ mile, then follows the former Circle Trail to the north side of Deer Leap Mountain. Tucker Lodge (now 1.7 miles from Route 4) is reached via the north end of the former Tucker Lodge Loop. The former trail from Route 4 to Tucker Lodge has been closed.

Page 67: Rolston Rest Shelter, a frame leanto with bunks for 6 to 8, was built by the Long Trail Patrol in 1966. Its location (100.0 m.) is about a mile south of old Noyes Pond Camp which will be dismantled.

Page 83: New Theron Dean Shelter, a log leanto with bunks for 6, was built by the Long Trail Patrol in 1966 to replace the old shelter (151.5 m.).

Page 94: Cascade Shelter, a frame leanto with bunks for 8, was built by the Long Trail Patrol in 1966. It is located on the Jonesville-Bolton Lodge relocation near the crossing of Duck Brook (175.9 m.).

Page 125: The Long Trail now crosses a new section of Vt. 118 at a point south of the former crossing at Frying Pan Brook.

Pages 131 and 132: During continuation of the construction of extensive facilities on Jay Peak, hikers should use caution in following the route of the Long Trail in the summit region.

                        OUT ON THE TRAIL
                                   or

               DUFFIN' AND PUFFIN' WITH MAURI

         Up, up we went, southward from Lincoln Gap, on
November 12th. Mauri Wintturi, the star of our show;
Dave Govatski, Mauri's sherpa; and the writer, who went
along for the sheer enjoyment of seeing the grand finale
of Mauri's several-year project of huffing and puffing his
way over the entire length of the Long Trail.
   To our hero that day, Mt. Grant seemed like Annapurna;
Mt. Cleveland was Mt. Everest; and that unnamed 3400-
foot peak evoked strange words not found in my pocket
dictionary. But Mt. Roosevelt was looked upon with some
favor—because it had a view, and because it meant that
the end was near and the rest of the way was all downhill!
   Finally we reached it! The Clark Brook Trail junction.
This, to Mauri, was Journey's End, the completion of the
Great Experience.
   First, to revive our hero, a sip of the fruit of the vine,
brought along for medicinal purposes only. Then, in what
is believed to be a "first" on the Long Trail, the presenta-
tion (by an authorized member of the End-to-End Com-
mittee), right on the Trail, and only five minutes after it
happened, of Mauri's cherished End-to-End Certificate.
Then came the photographing, with the trail sign in the
background for authenticity.
   In just a few minutes it was over. What had begun a few
years ago as a sample Long Trail hike had become the
Great Challenge in the life of our hero. And he huffed and
puffed his way through what may have been one of the
most difficult End-to-Ends on record.
   For in order to complete the Trail, Mauri made 49
individual hikes! And as much of his hiking was done alone,
he did much backtracking and utilized many side trails. A
final tally shows that Mauri, in order to become an End-to-
Ender, did an actual 428 miles in order to hike the 260
miles from Massachusetts to Canada! And, most unusual,
only ONE night was spent on the Trail during those trips—
that being a night at Gorham Lodge.
   Mauri surely deserves his certificate. Not only has he
completed the Long Trail, but he has unselfishly spent
many, many weekends performing trail work—with the
Worcester Section at Stratton Pond, with the writer in the
construction of the Baker Peak relocation, on the GMC's
portion of the Appalachian Trail east of Sherburne Pass, on
the Metacomet Trail, and elsewhere.
   Hail to our hero! May he huff and puff his way over
many trails in the future. And thanks, Mauri, for being
a real friend. I've surely enjoyed hiking with you. Please
stop at our campsite for tea—often.
                                                 BEN ROLSTON
