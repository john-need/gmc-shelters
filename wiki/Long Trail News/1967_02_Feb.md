                  PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE
   Ben Rolston is known to all of us. He is the editor of
the Guide Book of the Long Trail and he already has
been editor of three editions. The Guide Book changes
in this issue of The Long Trail News were written up by
him. He assisted greatly in writing the present Short Walks
on the Long Trail folder. He writes the "Out on the Trail"
piece that we have all come to expect in this newspaper.
He has four End-to-End awards, no one has more. He
was president of the club for two years. He maintains quite
a bit of the trail personally. In short, Ben is a real active
member-our most active member.
   At last we have been able to give Ben's name to a
shelter. The new shelter that was built last summer, about
a mile south of Noyes Pond, is now ROLSTON'S REST.

                           OUT ON THE TRAIL
                                      or

                DUFFIN' AND PUFFIN' WITH MAURI

            Up, up we went, southward from Lincoln Gap, on
         November 12th. Mauri Wintturi, the star of our show;
         Dave Govatski, Mauri's sherpa; and the writer, who went
         along for the sheer enjoyment of seeing the grand finale
         of Mauri's several-year project of huffing and puffing his
         way over the entire length of the Long Trail.
            To our hero that day, Mt. Grant seemed like Annapurna;
         Mt. Cleveland was Mt. Everest; and that unnamed 3400-
         foot peak evoked strange words not found in my pocket
         dictionary. But Mt. Roosevelt was looked upon with some
         favor-because it had a view, and because it meant that
         the end was near and the rest of the way was all downhill!
            Finally we reached it! The Clark Brook Trail junction.
         This, to Mauri, was Journey's End, the completion of the
         Great Experience.
            First, to revive our hero, a sip of the fruit of the vine,
         brought along for medicinal purposes only. Then, in what
         is believed to be a "first" on the Long Trail, the presenta-
         tion (by an authorized member of the End-to-End Com-
         mittee), right on the Trail, and only five minutes after it
         happened, of Mauri's cherished End-to-End Certificate.
         Then came the photographing, with the trail sign in the
         background for authenticity.
            In just a few minutes it was over. What had begun a few
         years ago as a sample Long Trail hike had become the
         Great Challenge in the life of our hero. And he huffed and
         puffed his way through what may have been one of the
         most difficult End-to-Ends on record.
            For in order to complete the Trail, Mauri made 49
         individual hikes! And as much of his hiking was done alone,
         he did much backtracking and utilized many side trails. A
         final tally shows that Mauri, in order to become an End-to-
         Ender, did an actual 428 miles in order to hike the 260
         miles from Massachusetts to Canada! And, most unusual,
         only ONE night was spent on the Trail during those trips-
         that being a night at Gorham Lodge.·
            Mauri surely deserves his certificate. Not only has he
         completed the Long Trail, but he has unselfishly spent
         many, many weekends performing trail work-with the
         Worcester Section at Stratton Pond, with the writer in the
         construction of the Baker Peak relocation, on the GMC's
         portion of the Appalachian Trail east of Sherburne Pass, on
         the Metacomet Trail, and elsewhere.
