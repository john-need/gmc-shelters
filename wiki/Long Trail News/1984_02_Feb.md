LONG TRAIL HIKE-A-THON

The letter below was received at the Club office in mid-November:

During the month of September I spent twenty-one very pleasant days hiking solo end-to-end (south to north) on the Long Trail. This was my third end-to-end venture. My other two trips (one in each direction) were done by "bits and pieces" over several years.

I started at Blackinton, Mass., on September 2nd and arrived at the Canadian border on the 22nd. I hiked a total of 277.4 miles. My wife Beverly provided me with transportation to and from the Trail along with two food drops (Route 103 in Clarendon and Route 2 in Jonsonville) so at no time did I leave the Trail during these three weeks. I thoroughly enjoyed each day and the challenges each presented.

Being mindful of the limited operating budget of the Green Mountain Club I decided to enlist the support of many of my friends and turn my trail trek into a "HIKE-A-THON" for the Club by having them pledge a few cents a mile. This would provide me with an added incentive and would assist the Club in its many programs of trail maintenance and education.

Enclosed is a list of contributors with their addresses along with a check for $260.00, the total amount pledged.

I enjoyed visiting with GMC personnel along the way, including Jeff at Stratton Pond, Jim on Camel's Hump, Sue on Mt. Mansfield, and Ellen and Ben at Taft Lodge. They were all very considerate and knowledgeable.

I would be most willing to share my documentation and experiences with any interested parties. Keep up the good work—it is much appreciated.

Very Sincerely,

Norman R. Cyrs (signed)
131 Commonwealth Ave.
Springfield, VT 05156

LONG TRAIL PHOTOS, HISTORY, MEMORABILIA SOUGHT

Many plans for a celebration of the Club's 75th Anniversary in 1985 are underway, not the least of which is the publication of a pictoral history of the Long Trail and Green Mountain Club.

Such a book received conceptual approval by the Club's Board of Directors at their March 26, 1983 meeting. What is planned is a quality product: an 8 by 11 inch, 128 page volume with a 4-color cover, 8 color pages, and otherwise chock full of interesting black and white photos. Vermont authors Will and Jane Curtis and book designer Frank Leiberman (who served as art director at Vermont Life magazine for ten years) have shown a great interest in the project, and are currently surveying available historical material.

The Club has hopes of marketing the book at a retail price of under $15, with a substantial discount for Club members.

The Board's approval for publishing a Long Trail history was "conceptual" for one obvious reason - money. Clearly, such an undertaking would be expensive, and beyond the financial resources of GMC alone. Support for this worthwhile project is being sought, however, and it was my pleasure to announce at the January 7 meeting of the Board that the Club has received its first contribution: a grant of $1,000 from the Cecil Howard Charitable Trust of New York City.

Volunteer Club historians like Victor Johnson, Ralph Bryant, Bob Hagerman and George Sholes, to name only a few, have over the past decade uncovered a wealth of fascinating early Long Trail history. These photographs and documents (including parts of the Club's original hand-tinted lantern slide collection) have been turned over to either the Vermont Historical Society or the Bailey/Howe Library at UVM for safe keeping. Curiously, early Club records have also been found at such far-flung places as Baker Library at Dartmouth College, Yale University and the New York Public Library.

Much of the Club's history, it is feared, may still rest in obscure attics or be tucked away in private photo albums. John Willey, another of our volunteer historians, succeeded just this fall in rescuing twelve file boxes of Trail-related documents from the former residence of Theron Dean, an early Club founder.

With the Club seriously considering the publication of a Long Trail history, I urge any who have access to or knowledge of interesting photographs, documents or other memorabilia on the Long Trail to please contact GMC's Montpelier office. There is also a need for high quality Long Trail photographs (color or black and white) of any age - the Club will pay mailing and reproduction costs, and give appropriate credits for any photos chosen.

Our Club is custodian of a very great heritage, having established the first of the long-distance hiking trails in this nation. It would be a pity to see any of that great heritage lost or forgotten.

PRESTON BRISTOW, President

   Boy Scout Troop 355 of Pennington, Vermont, under the leadership of Scoutmaster Art Kaiser cleaned waterbars, removed blowdowns, and brushed 1.6 miles of LT from Melville Nauheim Shelter to Hell Hollow Brook and also performed some site clean-up at the shelter.

   Boy Scout Troop 5 of Norwich, Connecticut, led by Scoutmaster Gil Stebbins, moved the toilet at Greenwall Shelter, brushed the trail to the water supply, and cleaned the shelter area.

   At-Large Members Paul and Joanne Woodward of Fitchville, Connecticut spent a total of three weeks on the Long Trail. During that time they reblazed a total of nineteen miles of trail, brushed nine miles, and spent a week caretaking at the Stratton Mountain Firetower. For a firsthand account of the Woodwards' experience, see Paul's article elsewhere in this issue.

   Worcester Section member Ed Faron, no stranger to the Stratton Mountain area, also spent a week caretaking at the Firetower and working on the Stratton Mountain Trail.

   Boy Scout Troop 44 of East Brunswick, New Jersey, under the direction of Scoutmaster Lee Goslin, dug a new pit for the outhouse at Bromley Camp, policed the camp area, and cleaned waterbars from the camp to Routes 11 & 30.

   To each of these individuals and groups, the Club says "Thank you for a job well done!"

   Before long the 1984 Field Season will be upon us. Once again we're hoping to involve as many volunteers as possible into our trail maintenance effort, and our Field Assistants give us the flexibility of providing a wide variety of experiences for would-be volunteers. How would you like to join the Long Trail Patrol for a week, two weeks, or a month? Work with a GMC Caretaker? Spend some however much time you have available on a particular maintenance project? If you're interested in spending some time on the Long Trail and performing some type of maintenance work, just write to the Club office and let us know what you have in mind.

   This volunteer activity is a great way for a hiker to get a break from his regular work-place. So, take a vacation and get away from the regular routine of weather and news broadcasts, housekeeping, schedules and so on. Make your own weather forecast; cook over an open fire; visit some of the natural wonders on this earth; and talk with some interesting people. It's a good way to spend a week, month or a summer.

PAUL WOODWARD

ADOPT-A-SHELTER REPORT

   The 1983 season for shelter adopters came to an end with the arrival of cold weather and snow. However, we do feel that it was very successful, with nearly all of the shelters being adopted. It is very helpful to the Trails and Shelters Committee if we can get a final report from the Adopters as to the approximate number of hikers that register at their shelters, along with the physical condition of the structures, water supply, etc. This helps us assign priorities for next season's work. A large percentage of Adopters do this already and we are most appreciative, but some either forget or neglect to.

   As all veteran Adopters know, the shelter adoptions are on a year to year basis and this is so that there will be no confusion as to who adopted which shelter. There is a complete list of 1983 Adopters listed below. If you would like to re-adopt your shelter for 1984, please get your request in to the Program Director as soon as possible. If anyone definitely does not want to re-adopt, we would appreciate knowing this also so that we will be able to honor new requests. Anyone that would like to adopt a shelter for the first time may also send in a request, as there are some shelters that are available every year.

   The Trails and Shelters Committee is very appreciative of the work that these shelter adopters have been doing for the past several years and do hope to show this in small way. We urge as many Adopters as can to attend the Trail Maintenance Workshop in Danby this coming spring, which is further explained in this issue. The workshop is informative as well as being enjoyable and offers opportunities to meet new friends as well as old ones.

   A new stove was donated by a member and installed along with new stovepipe at Journey's End Camp by Northern Field Assistant Ben Davis, Hazens Notch Camp Adopter Fred Judd, and yours truly. The following week the stove at Parker Camp was repaired and new pipe installed by Fred and myself.

   Please send all shelter correspondence to me at: RD #2, Fairfax Road, St. Albans, Vermont 05478. Telephone (802) 524-4863.

HERBERT W. REED
Adopt-A-Shelter Program Director

8 WEEKS & 14 TRIPS UP
BELVIDERE MTN. LATER ..

The summit of Belvidere Mtn. received
quite a bit of attention this past summer.
Those of us who remember the half-de-
stroyed old Ranger's cabin, the scars of nu-
merous fires, and a fire tower with no
guardrails and a rotting platform will be
surprised the next time they climb Bel-
videre.
   Work on cleaning up the summit came
about through a joint effort between the
GMC and Vermont Job Service's Summer
Youth Employment Program. SYEP is a
CETA-funded project offering jobs to un-
employed youths. The GMC provided
tools, arranged for supervision and an al-
most unlimited amount of trail work;
VJS/SYEP ran the program and provided
the salaries. The project was set-up to last
for 8 weeks and provide jobs for 12 unem-
ployed youths, and operated out of the
Morrisville Job Service Office. Bob Linde-
mann from the Sterling Section and John
Lepinski from the Killington Section were
the work Supervisors (slave drivers).
   Besides the Belvidere Mtn. project, the
crew did routine maintenance and clipping
of the trail between Madonna Mtn. and
Tillitson Camp. This included relocating
just about every outhouse and quite a few
trips up Whiteface to dry up the infamous
"Sterling Section Swamp Trail." Following
the conclusion of the program most of the
crew members still don't believe GMC
members volunteer to do this kind of work!
   As a side note, the Trails & Shelters
Committee continued the rejuvenation of
the Belvidere Tower this fall by replacing
half the platform with pressure-treated
lumber, replacing the top flight of stairs,
and installing a safety fence around the
platform.
                      BOB LINDEMANN

STERLING SECTION NEWS

  A minor change occured in the Sterling
Section this past fall. At the Annual Meet-
ing of the Section last January, the mem-
bers decided they wished in some way to
honor Robert L. Hagerman. It was quickly
decided that renaming an already existing
shelter or location was inappropriate. Dur-
ing our walk-through an overlook was lo-
cated along the Sterling ridgeline about 7/2
mile north of Chilcoot Pass. The view is to-
wards the south, showing two of Bob's fa-
vorite areas, the Sterling Ridge and Mt.
Mansfield. Hikers passing thru this section
of trail will now find a small log bench and
sign dedicated to Bob's memory.
           BOB LINDEMANN, Reporter

BURLINGTON SECTION

Butler Lodge, built in 1933 by the Long Trail Patrol is one of the oldest lodges or shelters still in use on the Long Trail. When the Burlington Section realized that this lodge would be fifty years old this past summer, we decided to have a birthday party. This was held on August 22.

We combined the party with hiking and blueberry picking. Thirteen members from three Sections, as well as the Caretaker and Northern Field Assistant attended. Most of us hiked up the Frost Trail first, picking blueberries along the way. We came with all sizes and shapes of blueberry pails. The berries were not as plentiful as we had hoped. Nobody brought home enough to make a pie, but we did have plenty for blueberry pancakes.

Most of the group got to the top of the Forehead of Mansfield (spurred on by the idea of soft drinks at the summit house after a hot climb). Then we went to Butler Lodge by various trails; some took the Long Trail Forehead Bypass, others the Wampahoofus, and others the Rock Garden.

At Butler we enjoyed a supper of blueberry pancakes and sausage, and for dessert a delicious birthday cake made by Ruth Goodridge.

DOT MYER, Reporter

ST. ALBANS SECTION

Our December Activities Planning Meeting was held at Charlie Vermont Restaurant in St. Albans adding a little bit of an extra flavor to our planning session. A lot of fun activities are in the offing; we'd like to see more of our members attending.

A Christmas party was held on December 21 at the home of Joanne and Bob Wolf. Fifteen people attended. A good old gab fest was held reminiscing about the good times of the past summer, which, unfortunately, I had been unable to participate in due to family obligations out-of-State, but it was fun listening. Gifts were exchanged and a sing-a-long of Christmas Carols accompanied by Bob and Joanne's daughter Julie on the piano, were enjoyed and there were lots of good refreshments.

There were five guests attending. Many thanks to Bob and Joanne for hosting a good time at their lovely home.

MARY LAROCK, Reporter
