what an 8 month pregnant woman was doing on the mountain. I never asked. My first words after being introduced were, "Were you up here 8 months previously?" and later, "What a neat place to have a birthday party!"

The Green Mt. Club has checked records and this is the 1st recorded instance of a baby being born on the Trail. GMC "ADOPT A SHELTER PROGRAM"

The Green Mountain Club and the National Forest Service maintain about 100 shelters on the Long Trail System. Would you personally like to help with this job by "adopting a shelter"?

Basically by "adopting a shelter" you would be agreeing to make periodic inspection trips to the shelter of your choice; doing the routine housekeeping chores such as sweeping out the floor, if needed; replacing the log book; keeping the foundation porky-proofed; packing out any rubbish; and reporting the results of your inspection trip with an eye to other repairs that may be necessary, such as windows, stove pipes, stove, etc. This may be the most important part of the job because regular maintenance time is often wasted finding out what needs to be done. All shelters should be inspected at least twice a year and some of the more popular cabins should be checked monthly during the summer. In addition, all shelters should be creosoted at least every three years and this could be part of the job if you wished.

Would you "adopt a shelter"? Shelters are available on a first-come-first-serve basis.

The GMC is also responsible for the Appalachian Trail east of Sherburne Pass. Most of the recent maintenance on this stretch of trail has been on an individual basis and some 2 or 3 mile sections are available for "adoption".

If you would like to "adopt a shelter" or "adopt a stretch of trail" or would like more details about either program, please contact: Donald M. Wallace, Mech. Engr'g Dept., Norwich University, Northfield, Vermont 05663.

GEORGIA TO MAINE VIA THE AT

At Sherburne Ed Garvey is greeted by President Shirley Strong and Killington Section President Wilbur Weston.

Edward B. Garvey of Falls Church, Va., had hiked several hundred miles of trail "in bits and pieces" but retirement gave him the opportunity to realize a "dream of a lifetime" by hiking the entire 2,000 miles of The Appalachian Trail. Leaving Springer Mt., Georgia April 4 and arriving at Mt. Katahdin, Maine October 7, he took two breaks of two weeks each, first for the Appalachian Trail Conference triennial meetings at Shippensburg, Pa. and later for his daughter's wedding. Since Ed Garvey was not out to break any speed records, he enjoyed the going, visiting with hikers, trail workers and those responsible for the trail along the way, stimulating much publicity for the AT.

As an officer in the Appalachian Trail Conference, the new chairman of the shelter committee and member of the National Scenic Trail Advisory Committee, Mr. Garvey combined business with pleasure. His voluminous notes and reports on trail and shelter conditions, drinking water sources, mileage, elevation and other physical data, ownership of land, responsibility for maintenance, location of highways, post offices and grocery stores will be valuable resource material for the new guide book he plans to compile.

The first week in August found Mr. Garvey traversing the section of the AT that coincides with our Long Trail. He reported heavy use with many hikers using our shelters which average only six miles apart, more frequent than along other sections of the AT. At each shelter Mr. Garvey posted a printed card indicating the date he had passed through and the address of the Appalachian Trail Conference (1718 N Street, N.W., Washington, D.C. 20036) for those interested in information or affiliation. He plans to do a series of articles for their official publication Trailway News.

The Killington Section was highly praised by Mr. Garvey for its new "No Dump" program and the members for their efforts in removing the dumps which proved most successful this summer. Being a "man of action" Ed Garvey always carried a plastic bag to pick up litter along the trail.

Upon completion of the long hike, Mr. Garvey said in part, "I say with conviction that while hiking the Trail in itself is an adventure of a lifetime, it is the opportunity of meeting and visiting with people that makes this experience so much richer."

GUIDE BOOK IS SOLD OUT

If you've had trouble trying to find a Guide Book recently, the reason is simple—the 1968 Edition is sold out! Frankly we don't know whether to feel sorry or happy about this. On the one hand we are sorry to disappoint those who could not get books, but at the same time we are pleased that the strong demand for Guide Books is one indication of the increasing popularity of The Long Trail.

Right now the Guide Book Committee is hard at work preparing a new edition of the Guide Book—the twentieth edition since 1917. We expect that the new book will be ready by early Spring. Those who cannot wait that long, however, to get updated information for those special trips they may be planning are welcome to write to the GMC office with their inquiries about specific sections of the trail in which they are interested. In addition, the February issue of the Long Trail News will carry the annual summary of significant trail and shelter changes—this time the listing should be quite impressive since several major changes are in various stages of progress.

As the Committee pursues its task of preparing a new Guide Book, it shall constantly be conscious of the following comment which appeared in the register of Whiteface Shelter some time ago: "Over the years we have come to the conclusion that the person who estimates the times between shelters wears no pack, takes no rests run most of the way and wears a watch which is very slow!"

GEORGE PEARLSTEIN, Chairman


GMC DISPLAY AT LUMBERJACK ROUNDUP

A display by The Green Mountain Club for the Lumberjack Roundup at Killington Mountain on August 14th and 15th was maintained by Doris Washburn assisted by Granget Kammerer, George Plumb and Jean Codling.

The display consisted of pictures along the trail (shelter, terrain flowers, views, etc.) plus slogans for courtesy on the trail, appropriate equipment for backpacking and day hiking was exhibited, and a higher elevation map of the state of Vermont emphasized the location of the Long Trail. Special stress was made of the Carry In—Carry Out policy.

Several hundred persons viewed the display and many inquiries were made from persons interested in hiking but just didn't know where to start. Most people were interested in the shorter hikes and family hiking.

Short Hikes on the Long Trail and GMC and You folders were distributed.

I think this display was a worthwhile endeavor to help educate people about the "Footpath in the Wilderness."

DORIS E. WASHBURN, Publicity Committee


PROFILE COMMITTEE AFFILIATES WITH NATURE CONSERVANCY

In mid-October the Green Mountain Profile Committee affiliated with the Vt. Chapter of The Nature Conservancy as a special project in Vermont. Chairman Hubert Vogelmann (ecologist at the University of Vermont) stated that this action will further the Committee's educational program to emphasize the value of mountains in Vermont as well as elsewhere and the Committee's efforts to protect and preserve mountain areas as wildlands.

The Committee has prepared a brochure and has initiated a funding program. Funds will be used for expansion of its educational program and for acquisition of some key areas of mountain wildlands—initial efforts being in the Camel's Hump area this fall.

Ed. Note: The GMC, as part of its conservation efforts, made a substantial contribution both this year and last year to the Profile Committee. The Trustees, feeling the goals of the Committee are of prime interest to all GMC members, voted October 5th to handle a mailing of brochures to its members for the Committee when appropriate.


WHY CARETAKERS?

This past summer the Burlington Section decided to continue to expand its caretaker program. Caretakers were employed for Taft and Bolton Lodges from June 17 thru Labor Day. These caretakers were guaranteed x number of dollars plus what they collected in fees. Fees were charged as follows: .75 per night for non-member; .50 per night for GMC member; and .25 per night for members of large camp groups (B.S.A., Y.M.C.A., etc.) Caretakers also engage in a little free enterprise by selling, for a reasonable profit, condiments and other necessary materials that a hiker may wish to buy.

Above and beyond their daily tasks, our caretakers engaged in some trail work on the Long Trail and side trails near their respective lodges. They were most congenial and offered an accurate source of Trail information to all hikers. Our caretakers also encouraged all hikers to pack out all unburnable trash that they had packed in.

Expense in setting up a caretaker was more than I had originally anticipated, however all the equipment purchased will be useable from one caretaker to the next. Also, the projected saving in shelter repair will more than pay for this equipment.

I feel that there are many things to look for in a caretaker, such as an outgoing personality and the ability to cope with all people. Today with a greater number of people using the trail and its facilities we are getting a greater variety of personalities. However the most important requirement is that a caretaker must be able to cope with himself.

Caretakers serve as multi-purpose employees—as public relations people, historians, environmentalist guides and a friend to all hikers. Also, caretakers offer us an outstanding opportunity to obtain some very accurate statistics on the trail and shelter use.

Anyone who would like more information about our caretaker program is encouraged to write to Kenn Boyd, 714 V2 Shelburne Rd., South Burlington, Vermont 05401.

I would have to term our summer a very positive success and would like to thank our caretakers Gordon Walker, John McMullen and George Mollo for their outstanding efforts.

KENN BOYD, Properties Chairman
Burlington Section

And I will crawl in the mess of my making, forever. Amen.

BY-LAWS OF THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB, INC.

I. Membership
  1. Any person 21 years of age or over may become a member-at-large of the Green Mountain Club, Inc.
  2. Each Section shall have the right to regulate its own membership.
  3. A member of any Section is a member of the Green Mountain Club, Inc.
  4. The Corporation and the Sections may admit Junior Members under 21 years of age. Such Junior Members may not vote or hold elective office.
  5. The official membership list shall be kept by the Corresponding Secretary.
  6. Any organized adult or youth group (such as Scout troop, school club, youth camp, outing club) may become an Organization Member of the Green Mountain Club, Inc. upon payment of annual dues of ten dollars. Two persons, designated as leader and assistant leader (or secretary), shall receive the LONG TRAIL NEWS for the organization.

II. Officers
  1. The officers of the Corporation shall be Clerk, Corresponding Secretary, Board of 21 elected Trustees, President, Vice President, Treasurer, and Auditor. All officers shall serve until their successors are elected and qualified.
  2. The Clerk shall be elected for a term of one year at the annual meeting of the Corporation. The Clerk shall also serve as the Clerk of the Board of Trustees. He shall be an ex-officio, voting member of the Board of Trustees.
  3. The Corresponding Secretary shall be elected for a term of one year at the annual meeting of the Corporation. He shall be an ex-officio, voting member of the Board of Trustees.
  4. The 21 Trustees shall be apportioned to the Sections, Members-at-Large, the Green Mountain National Forest Service, and the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks as follows: Bennington 2, Brattleboro 1, Burlington 3, Connecticut 1, Killington 2, Montpelier 1, New York 3, Pioneer Valley 1, Sterling 1, Worcester 2, Members-at-large 2, Green Mountain National Forest Service 1, Vermont Department of Forests and Parks 1.
     Each Section shall elect its Trustees in the manner and at the time used to elect its officers. Each Section shall notify the President of the Green Mountain Club, Inc. by April 15 of the election of its Trustees. If such notification is not received by the President he shall consider the offices open and instruct the nominating committee accordingly.
     A Nominating Committee, appointed by the President at least four months previously, shall, at the Annual Meeting, present the name of one member for each vacancy of Section Trustees and of Trustees representing Members-at-large. The name of one member shall also be presented for Clerk, and one for Corresponding Secretary. Additional nominations for all vacancies may be made from the floor. Voting shall be by paper ballot whenever there is more than one nominee for a vacancy. Election shall be by plurality of members present and voting at the Annual Meeting.
     An official of the Green Mountain National Forest Service shall be named by the Board as a Trustee.
     An official of the Vermont Department of Forests and Parks shall be named by the Board as a Trustee.
     The term of office for a Trustee shall be three years. Trustees who have served six years continuously in office shall not be eligible for re-election until after the lapse of one year.
  5. The President, Vice President, and Treasurer shall be elected for a term of one year by the Trustees from among those members of the Club who have at any time served as Trustees. No officer except the Corresponding Secretary shall hold the same office for more than three consecutive years. The Auditor shall be elected by the Board of Trustees for a term of one year but shall not be a member of the board nor hold any other office in the Club. The duties of all shall be those commonly assigned to such officers.
  6. Officers elected by the Board of Trustees may be removed by it at pleasure.
  7. Officers may appoint or remove deputies at pleasure.

III. Corporate Meetings
  1. The corporate year shall begin May first.
  2. The annual meeting shall be held on the Saturday which falls nearest to May 30th of each year. It shall be the duty of the Clerk to mail notice of such annual meeting to each member at least two weeks in advance, if such notice has not previously appeared in the LONG TRAIL NEWS.
  3. Special corporate meetings may be called by the President, or by seven Trustees, provided notice stating time, place, and object thereof be mailed by the Clerk to each member at least one week in advance.
  4. Corporate meetings shall be held at Rutland, Vermont, or at any other convenient location in Vermont, at the direction of the Board of Trustees.
  5. Fifteen members of voting age shall constitute a quorum at any corporate meeting.

IV. Trustees
  1. It shall be the duty of the Trustees to conduct the business of the Corporation.
  2. The regular meeting of the Board of Trustees shall be held immediately after the annual meeting of the Corporation.
  3. Another meeting of the Trustees shall be held on the second Saturday in April, unless the President sets another date.
  4. Special meetings of the Board of Trustees may be called by the President, or by seven Trustees, provided notice stating time, place, and object of such meeting be mailed by the Clerk to each Trustee at least one week in advance.
  5. Five members of the Board of Trustees shall constitute a quorum.
  6. The regular meeting of the Board of Trustees shall be held at Rutland, Vermont, or any other convenient location in Vermont. Special meetings shall be held at the place designated in the call.

V. Local Organizations
  1. Local organizations, to be known as the Green Mountain Club, Inc., (Name) Section at (Place), may be established by the Board of Trustees upon application.
  2. Each Section shall elect a President, Secretary, and such other officers as it chooses for the proper conduct of the Section business.
  3. It shall be the duty of each Section to maintain to the satisfaction of the Trustees such Trails and Shelters as may be assigned to and accepted by it for maintenance.
  4. It shall be the duty of the President of each Section to report orally or in writing the activities and membership of the Section at the annual meeting of the Corporation.
  5. It shall be the duty of the Secretary of each Section to supply the Corresponding Secretary with an accurate list of the names and addresses of members, junior members, and life members before June 1st of each year.
  6. A Section shall be disestablished in case its membership falls below ten. All former Section members shall become members-at-large.
  7. A Section may be disestablished for other reasons by a two-thirds vote of the Trustees present at any regular or special meeting after due consideration provided notice of such proposed action be mailed by the Clerk to the President and Secretary of the Section at least one week in advance. All former members of the Section shall become members-at-large.

VI. Contributions
  1. The annual contribution for members and junior members-at-large shall be five dollars and two dollars, respectively, in advance.

4. Each Section shall forward to the Treasurer by April 1st
   of each year an assessment amounting to fifty cents for
   each adult member and twenty-five cents for each junior
   member as shown on the official membership list for the
   preceding year.

VIII. Trails and Shelters
1. The trails and shelters of the Green Mountain Club, Inc.
   shall be only those trails and shelters which have been
   officially approved by the Trustees of the Corporation.
2. All projects for building or relocating Green Mountain
   Club trails and shelters shall be submitted in advance
   for approval to the Chairman of the Trails and Shelters
   Committee.

IX. Amendments
1. These by-laws may be amended at any meeting of the
   Corporation by a two-thirds vote of the members present
   provided notice of such amendments be included in the
   call for the meeting.
2. It shall be the duty of the President to present to the mem-
   bers such by-law amendments as may be proposed by a
   majority of the trustees or by any twenty-five members.

Note: This set of by-laws supersedes all previous by-laws printed
and includes those amendments and additions approved at
the annual meeting of the Corporation on 30 May 1970.

BURLINGTON SECTION

Hearty congratulations are in order for Shirley Strong who became an End-to-Ender in October! We're mighty proud of our President, and also of Gerry Grow who, at 80, completed The Long Trail on the same hike that Shirley did. That 11-mile trek is described elsewhere in this issue by George Saunders who accompanied them.

At the other end of the age scale from Gerry is Matthew Stein, 13-year old son of Dot and Ben Stein who, with his friend Jon Kohn, 16, was featured in the Burlington FREE PRESS when they completed The Trail in September. They hiked the 145.7 miles from Lincoln Gap to Blackington, Mass. together in August with only two "re-fueling" stops where their parents met them along the way.

The stillness of woodland trails, not only in Vermont, but also in New Hampshire and New York State was broken a number of times during the summer by shouts of congratulations as hikers achieved long-sought-after goals. Among successful Burlington Section aspirants were President Harris Abbott who finished the ADK 46 in the Adirondacks, and Vice President Newell Briggs and his wife Ruth who completed the AMC 46 in the White Mountains. Also, on that week's camping trip in August Marion Holmes, Elsie Washburn and Fred Smith conquered the final peaks to become AMC 46-ers.

Because of a change of jobs which took him to Albany, Jim Vlamis had to resign as our able Outing Chairman. Jim put his innovative ideas to good use in planning an interesting and varied schedule, and we'll miss him. However, we are fortunate that Elsie Washburn accepted the assignment of filling out his term.

Highlight of Elsie's hike up Ampersand Mt. in the Adirondacks was having Roy Buchanan along. It was good to see Roy on The Trail again.

The caretaker system at Taft, Bolton and Butler Lodges will be continued and possibly expanded next summer. It is felt that the saving on the cost for repair of damage caused by vandalism more than justifies the expenditure.

THE LAST 11 MILES

It started at 8:30 A.M. on October 10, 1970 in the fog of Smugglers Notch for a trip that was to take us from Route 108 to Route 15. The higher we went the thicker the fog became until reaching Sterling Pond the visibility was less than fifty feet. And the three Goddard College students we met in Sterling Pond Lodge insisted that upon awakening that morning they found the fog three feet high inside the lodge.

At the top of Madonna Mountain we could look down the lift line and once in awhile see the base station as the fog rolled back and forth. But on reaching the slab of Morse Mountain the sun appeared to reveal trees with only a few yellow leaves. The sun was warm as we relaxed and ate our lunch in front of Whiteface Shelter. It was almost too warm as we began that oh so steep ascent of Whiteface Mountain. But the wind had a touch of coolness as we talked to a single Johnson College student on the summit. He was going the other way and told us from now on it was all down hill for us. It was downhill but so long that we began to lose faith in those white blazes that we usually follow with so much confidence.

But it ended at 5:30 P.M. at French Camp. It ended many miles, many promises to be kept, and end-to-end for our president Shirley Strong. It also ended forty years of hiking The Long Trail for Gerry Grow of the Burlington Section and now at 80 years old an end-to-ender.

STERLING SECTION

The Sterling Section has stirred to life again as some 35 area residents gathered Sept. 24 to formally re-activate this long dormant chapter.

At a meeting held in Morrisville the group elected acting officers, adopted a schedule of annual dues, created a committee to draw up a set of by-laws and planned several hikes for the month of October.

The Sterling Section will re-assume responsibility for upkeep of that portion of The Long Trail running south from Rt. 15 in Johnson past French Camp, over Whiteface Mt. and another lodge there and on to Chilcoot Pass. In addition the section will maintain a side trail which runs from the Mud City area of Morristown into the Beaver Meadow Lodge and two connecting trails from there to the Long Trail. The section is also expected to sponsor each year a series of pleasure outings for members as well as others interested in hiking and related outdoor activities.

Acting officers elected to serve until the section's annual meeting to be held Jan. 21, 1971 were: President, Robert L. Hagerman of Morrisville; Vice President, Charles Vile of Eden Mills; Secretary Miss Carole Mahony of Morrisville; Treasurer, Harold Eaton of Morrisville; and Trustee of the parent GMC, Miss Wilda Gigee of Hyde Park.

Members of the committee formed to draft by-laws are Atty. Richard Sargent and Brent Teillon, both of Morrisville and Charles Vile.

The dues schedule adopted is the following: individual adult member, $6; individual junior member (16 years old or younger), $3; and family membership (all family members living at the same address), $10. Dues for the calendar year 1971 are to be payable by Jan. 1 in order to be eligible to vote at the annual meeting.

Those interested in joining the section can send their dues to Miss Mahony at Box 134, Morrisville, Vt. 05661; checks should be made payable to Sterling Section, GMC.

Two pleasure hikes were scheduled-one Oct. 4 to the Owl's Head Trail at Groton State Forest and the second Oct. 18 to Hardwood Pond in Elmore-and one work trip Oct. 31 to the Beaver Meadow Lodge area.

Two other highlights of the reorganizational meeting were remarks by GMC President Shirley Strong of Burlington who brought greetings from the parent club and by Willard Sanders of Morrisville, who provided some colorful history of the Sterling Section and the building of the Beaver Meadow Lodge which Miss Strong subsequently described as "one of the finest on the Long Trail."

The meeting drew interested hikers from such relatively distant points as St. Albans and Craftsbury Common as well as from several Lamoille County towns.

ROBERT L. HAGERMAN, Reporter

CSSV INTERVENES IN VT. YANKEE PROCEEDINGS

One of the big conservation issues in Vermont is the Vt. Yankee Nuclear Power Plant now under construction at Vernon, Vt. In late August the Conservation Society of Southern Vermont intervened in the proceedings before the Public Service Board and, as the initial intervener, it took a direct and important step in setting the stage for a more thorough review of the environmental effects of such a plant. Further, it provided an opportunity for other groups to express their views and concerns, and to demand that strict controls be imposed on Vt. Yankee.

Legal costs for this intervention have been costly and the Society has requested financial assistance in meeting these costs. Individuals wishing to help should mail contributions to the Conservation Society of Southern Vermont, Bondville, Vt. 05340.

The Vt. Natural Resources Council, the Lake Champlain Committee and the National Wildlife Federation also intervened as parties in the Vt. Yankee proceedings-calling for stricter controls on the construction and operation of Vt. Yankee.
