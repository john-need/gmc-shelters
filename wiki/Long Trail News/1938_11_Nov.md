ANNUAL MEETING

At the meeting of the Club held in May last, it was voted to hold the next meeting on the Saturday nearest to Columbus Day in October, 1938, in order to ascertain whether a spring or a fall meeting would be more popular as a permanent date. The result indicated unambiguously that the spring meeting was favored, for while the weather was unusually fine for the season, the attendance was not over one-third that of the May meeting, and very few of those present came from outside the State. So it was decided nem con that the permanent annual meeting should be held in May on the Saturday nearest Memorial Day, and the by-laws will be changed accordingly. Due notice of the proposed change will be given the members before that meeting.

J. A. Allis, who was appointed at the last meeting to investigate and report on a suitable unnamed mountain to be named Farr Peak or Mountain, in accordance with resolutions of the Club adopted in 1915, reported that he had scouted the Trail and peaks in the vicinity of the Brandon-Rochester Pass, and had found that a ridge existed a little south of the highway and just beyond Goshen Mountain, having two peaks, the northerly of which according to the guidebook is 3,325 feet high, and the southerly 3,525 feet, neither of which were named. This would make the southerly peak the highest elevation between Killington and Breadloaf Mountain, unless Bloodroot Mountain equals it. The map in the guidebook puts the height of this at 3,525 feet, but the text at only 3,500. He recommended that the ridge including the two peaks be named Farr Mountain, and the southerly peak be called Farr Peak, and it was unanimously voted that the mountain and the peak be named as recommended, and they are so named, and will hereafter bear those names in Club publications. At present the Trail is located a short distance below the summit of this mountain, and the Proctor Section and the Patrol were appointed a committee to investigate and report whether they would recommend that the Trail be relocated so as to pass over the summits of these peaks, or that a loop trail be cleared from the Trail to one peak, thence to the other and back to the Trail.

E. A. Boyce, chairman of house committee, reported a good season at the Lodge, with satisfactory gross receipts, but a smaller net, owing to increased and extra expense. He had not received final figures. It was voted that out of earnings coming to the Club $500 at least be paid on the indebtedness, and $1000 if possible.

Mr. Boyce stated that the Club's investment in two cabins at the Lodge has proved quite profitable, one (Spruce) having been refunded in full out of rents, and the other (Ledges), built later, one-fourth refunded, and stated that

more cabins were needed and could be rented at profitable figures, and recommended that the Club invest not to exceed $1000 in another cabin on the same plan, and it was so voted. Such cabins would seem to be an ideal investment for the Club, for as long as the demand continues, as it bids fair to do indefinitely, the investment is soon returned from rents, and the cabin is paid for and belongs to the Lodge which belongs to the Club, thus increasing its assets and its yearly earnings. While the cash advanced is outstanding, the Club receives 6% interest on it.

The amount of damage to the Trail by the rain and windstorm of Sept. 21 and the means of repairing the damage were discussed. It was reported that in some parts the damage was small, but in others fallen tree trunks and branches had almost obliterated the trail, while on steep slopes the sand and gravel was washed away, leaving bare rock, making travel difficult. The clearing and repair of the Trail will be a major task and an expensive one. As much as possible of it will be done this fall. In this connection a pleasant incident was the receipt of a check for $100 from Miss Shirley Farr of Brandon, a life member. This was for the purpose of helping to repair trail damage caused by the storm. It is hoped that others will follow this good example. The only cabin reported seriously damaged was that known as the Blue Triangle.

During an intermission the members partook of an excellent chicken pie-new cider dinner.

FUTURE FARMERS ON THE LONG TRAIL

Agricultural students of various high schools of Vermont, under the name of Future Farmers of America, joined in their annual Long Trail hike on October 8 of this year. Twenty-three schools were represented by 167 boys. Owing to unfavorable conditions in parts not as many schools or boys participated as last year, nor was as much of the Trail gone over, but portions of it were covered from south of Bennington to the Canadian line. A report showing the conditions on the sections of the Trail traversed has been made and no doubt it has been and will be useful to the patrol in their heavy task of repairing the damage caused by the great storm of Sept. 21. Considerable clearing was done by the boys as they went along, which in the aggregate will be a sizable contribution toward the work of restoring the Trail to its normal condition.

FROM THE SECTIONS

The Worcester Section journeyed to the Quabbin reservoir, Enfield, Mass., on Oct. 27, and celebrated Hallowe'en by a party at Boylston, Mass., the program, as stated, consisted of "games, dancing and prancing, cider and eats galore." This ought to have caught the crowd, if anything could.

The Burlington schedule calls for a "chopping and chinking party" at Butler lodge for Nov. 11. That sounds like work. For Nov. 19-20 there is an over-night visit to Taylor lodge, and on Dec. 3 a supper and milling at Rock Point. Sunday, Dec. 18, Arrowhead Mountain, Milton, Vt., and Dec. 31-Jan. 1-2, New Year's trip to Bolton lodge.

The Killington (Rutland) Section will extend their outing schedule into early winter, the program calling for a walk from the Long Trail Lodge to the Pico shelter on Oct. 30; November 11, to the Clement camp in Shrewsbury; Nov. 27, to Bald Mountain, and Dec. 11, to Buffum shelter, east of Wallingford.

NOTES—Continued

The Appalachian Conference announces that its ninth biennial meeting will be held at Katahdin, the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail. Conference sessions combined with trips in the Katahdin region will occupy the period from August 18 to 26, 1939. In view of the interesting character of this locality and its accessibility from New England, it would seem that there should be a sizable delegation from the G. M. C. in attendance.

The first Long Trail wedding took place September 5 last at Prospect Rock, a sightly outlook on the Long Trail east of Manchester. The bride was Bertha M. Wood of Putney, Vt., and the groom Kenneth C. Chamberlain of Dummerston, Vt. The officiating clergyman was Rev. Gordon Thompson of Dummerston. A small party of friends attended, including Mr. and Mrs. C. J. Hawley of Holden, Mass. Mr. Hawley is one of the trustees of the Club. The party had a walk of 4½ miles along the Trail from their cars to the Rock. Nature smiled on these nuptials, as the weather was perfect, and the view into the Battenkill and Dorset valleys unusually fine.
