                                                EXECUTIVE DEPARTMENT


                                          A    P R 0 C L A MA T I 0 N


         WHEREAS;                             the Green Mountain Club has for 75 years
                                              effectively carried out its stated purpose
                                              by pioneering, developing, maintaining and
                                              protecting Vermont's Long Trail, a primitive
                                              footpath spanning the crest of the Green
                                              Mountains from Massachusetts to Canada; and

         WHEREAS;                             the Long Trail served as an inspiration for
                                              the creation of the Appalachian Trail, now a
                                              National Scenic Trail, which in its 2100-mile
                                              route from Katahdin in Maine to Springer
                                              Mountain in Georgia, passes through the
                                              State of Vermont; and

         WHEREAS;                             the Green Mountain Club is this week hosting
                                              the 25th biennial meeting of the Appalachian
                                              Trail Conference at Green Mountain College
                                              in Poultney;

         NOW, THEREFORE,                      I, Madeleine M. Kunin, Governor of the State
                                              of Vermont, do hereby proclaim the week of
                                              August 2 through 9, as


                                                             VERMONT HIKING WEEK


                                              and urge citizens of this state to explore
                                              the Long Trail, the Appalachian Trail, and
                                              the many hiking trails in Vermont, and to
                                              join with me in congratulating the Green
                                              Mountain Club for its pioneering work.


                                                                          Given under my hand

                                                                          and the Great Seal of

                                                                          the State of Vermont,

                                                                          this thirtieth day of

                                                                          July, A.D., 1985.

    Weather conditions began deteriorating in
mid-October. Bob Shedd, TomRoe(afriend
of Bob's), and GMC Caretakers Chip Heden-
burg and Steve Stoddard finished construct-
ing the walls and were then forced to stop
building for the winter months.
    Lumber and roofing materials still needed
to be packed to the site. This was solved by
the Manchester District and an Air National
Guard Rescue Unit out of New York. The
Unit provided a helicopter to fly materials up
Glastenbury Mountain. All who envisioned
packing 320 board feet of 16' x 6" pressure-
treated lumber and 14 sheets of4' x 8' metal
roofing were extremely pleased!
    Snow at the 3560' elevation building site
typically lasts until late May, so building was
scheduled to resume in June of 1985. The
winter of 1984I1985 was mild by Vermont
standards, however, and since most of the
snow had melted by mid-April, the decision
was made to finish construction in early May
and avoid Glastenbury's notorious black flies.
The Club hired ex-Caretaker and current
carpenter Mike Russom, 1984-1985 Stratton
Pond Caretaker Jim Kuras, fall 1984 Strat-
ton Pond and 1985 Griffith Lake Caretaker
Steve Stoddard, and carpenter Fred Abraham
to finish construction. The gang began work
on a rainy May 6th and completed construc-
tion May 17th. The crew also removed the
old Glastenbury Shelter. As of this writing,
all that remains to be done is to apply a preser-
vative and chink the walls.
   In honor of Ted Goddard, Sr.'s long in-
volvement with and dedication to the Green
Mountain Club, the Club's Board of Direc-
tors voted to name the new structure Goddard
Shelter.
   The Club expresses its appreciation to the
U.S. Forest Service, Manchester District,
especially Nort Phillips, for supplying much
of the building materials, tools, transporta-
tion, technical advice and, of course, the
helicopter; Bob Shedd for all his advice and
assistance; Boy Scout Troop 353; the "pack
mules" of Troop 355 and leader Art Kaiser;
and all the others who assisted on this project.
   The first two Glastenbury Shelters lasted
a total of 56 combined years. Let's hope God-
dard Shelter lasts at least that long!
