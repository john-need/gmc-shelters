THE LONG TRAIL NEWS
Published by The Green Mountain Club, Inc.

Brandon, Vermont. April, 1926

W. M. Fay, chairman of the house committee of the Lodge, reported that, while gross receipts were large, expenses were ditto, leaving a balance of $13 profit, with a hard winter ahead. During the season 5648 guests were entertained, and 5302 other visitors registered. Meals were served to about 5400.

The by-laws were amended to include an auditor in the list of officers, and to make the membership fee in the sections not less than $2 a year.

The membership of the Club, as reported by the treasurer, was as follows: At large, 441; Bennington section, 30; Burlington, 88; Middlebury, 63; New York, 339; Montpelier, 16; Proctor, 25; Rutland 300; Worcester, Mass. 41. Total 1343.

It was voted to make arrangements with Miss Lulu M. Tye, of the Rutland Chamber of Commerce, to act as paid secretary for the Club, and relieve the treasurer of the routine duties of collecting dues, mailing notices, etc.

In the evening, H. W. Gleason of Boston gave an illustrated lecture on the John Muir Trail in the Sierra Nevada mountains, at the High School hall.

The Annual Meeting of the Trustees was held Saturday, March 13, at the Berwick Hotel, Rutland, with the following trustees in attendance: Messrs. Proctor, White, Voter, Story, O'Kane, Griswold, Fay, R. M. Ross, Dean, W. M. Ross and Cleveland and Miss Estabrook. Two or three of the others were represented by proxies.

Officers were elected as follows: President, Mortimer R. Proctor; vice president, R. H. White; treasurer, C. J. Cleveland; corresponding secretary, W. M. Ross; editor Long Trail News, E. S. The Boys' Book of Canoeing. By Elon Jessup. New York, E. P. Dutton & Co., 1926. $2. Contains all the information a boy would want on canoes and canoeing. Profusely illustrated from photographs and drawings.

The Glittering Mountains of Canada. By J. Monroe Thorington. Philadelphia, John W. Lea, 1925. With 107 illustrations, 7 panoramas and 6 maps. $4.50. Treats of mountaineering and exploration in the Canadian Rockies. Records the results of three expeditions into the mountain area of the Continental Divide between the Canadian Pacific and Canadian National Railroads, with less strenuous excursions among some of the beauty spots.

The Meadows. Familiar Studies of the Commonplace. By John C. Van Dyke. New York, Charles Scribner's Sons, 1926. $2. Frontispiece. Interesting nature studies of the birds, trees, fields, animals, the passage of the seasons and the varied phenomena of the Raritan valley, New Jersey. Much in the style of Thoreau.

Each chapter is prefaced by a little poem, one of which we quote as specially applicable to the present season:

LATE SPRING.

Still deep the snow in wood and field,
   The north wind sharp and chilling.
We watch the winter slowly yield,
   The spring come half unwilling.
The light grows brighter day by day,
   But still we are no warmer.
The storms of March keep spring away,
   The snow beats in the dormer.
It's freeze and thaw and freeze again
   With ice drips from the gutters.
The wind is raw and cold the rain
   That slashes at the shutters.
But then some night the wind will veer
   To southard without warning.
And we shall find that spring is here,
   With blackbirds, in the morning.
