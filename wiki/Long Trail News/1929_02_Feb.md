Rutland, Vermont, February, 1929

The Annual Meeting

This took place Saturday, January 12, at the Hotel Berwick, Rutland, Vt., with an attendance of about 60, and was one of the best meetings yet held. The Sections were very fully represented, delegates being present from each Section but one, Montgomery, and that, as well as the others, sent a written report. The meeting began at 10:30 a.m., and continued, with an intermission for lunch, till nearly 6 p.m. Besides reports from the Sections, reports from the various officers were read, trustees and a clerk were elected, and there was an open forum for discussion. After the business meeting the members listened to two interesting and instructive addresses on the Appalachian Trail and other matters.

President Proctor submitted a very comprehensive report, covering the accomplishments of the past year and outlining future progress. After giving due credit to the Worcester members for their excellent work in re-locating the Trail over Glastenbury Mountain, he went on to say that "the Long Trail is now permanently located except possibly for minor changes, and is the first step in our plan leading to the final realization of our dream conceived in 1910. The next step will be to complete the necessary camps. The Trail committee has decided that 53 will be necessary to provide sufficient shelter along the entire Trail, 44 of which are now ready for use, leaving 9 to be constructed in the next two years. The third step toward making the Trail an absolutely self-sufficient unit will be the building of more approach trails." "Some practical way must be found this year to provide proper upkeep of all parts of the Trail and shelters. This is our chief and foremost duty. If we are neglectful in this respect we have little right to ask the support of the visiting public." The Burlington Section was commended for its construction of Bolton Mountain Lodge, "the finest of the smaller camps along the entire Trail", and Prof. Voter received due commendation for his services in editing "our finest guide book yet published." Prof. O'Kane was commended for his "most attractive and valuable folder to date", and announcement was made that Prof. O'Kane has written an article describing short trips on the Trail, which will be featured as publicity in 1929.

Then came some remarks on the photographic contest, memberships, distribution of Trail supervision, and a discussion of the situation at the Long Trail Lodge, with due recognition of the good work done by Mr. Fay as chairman of the house committee, and by Mrs. Bates as manager. "Mr. Webster of New York generously contributed his services to the improving of our bookkeeping system, and next season we will have the best system we have ever had. In behalf of the Club I wish to thank Mr. Webster most earnestly for his help. By taking advantage of several operative facts learned last year, this year we will be able to know very accurately what the Lodge can be expected to show financially. Personally I believe it can and will show a fair profit."

After casting a couple of bouquets in the direction of the News and its editor and the trustees, he closed by recommending that the Club at large unite with some Section in a field day. "Such a gathering should be planned some time ahead."

The report of C. J. Cleveland, treasurer, set forth that the assets on January 9, 1928, consisted of cash in banks, $1790.25; Monongahela West Penn. Pub. Serv. bond, $500; German bond, $100; Liberty bonds, $1450; U.S. Savings cert. redeemed, $100. Receipts during the year amounted to $9906.30, itemized as follows: Receipts from L.T. Lodge, $7136.92; dues, $1621; from Sections, $190.20; life members, $100; guide books, $474.96; gifts, $106.34; interest, $142.23; booklets, $22.50; postage, $44.86; sundries, $66.29. Disbursements totalled $12,970.97, consisting of general Club expenses, $2,108.60; Lodge expense, $8864.70; Argentine bond purchased, $1009.67; note paid, $988. Assets Jan. 9, 1929: Cash in banks, $175.58; Monongahela West Penn. bond, $500; Liberty bond, $100; German bond, $100; Argentine bond $1000. Liberty bonds amounting to $1350 were called during the year.

Slides belonging to the Club have been called in, and there are now at the Secretary's office 236 slides.

The report of W. M. Fay, chairman of the house committee of the Long Trail Lodge disclosed that the loss in operating the Lodge in 1928 was $1982, including winter care and insurance, but as some of this was for expenses incurred in 1927, the actual 1928 loss is reduced to $1514, comparing with a loss of $2134 in 1927. This may be in part accounted for by the rainy season, the poor condition of the roads and the fact that the Lodge was opened too soon and closed too late. It should not be opened before June 15, and closed immediately after Labor Day. Before and after those dates it is operated at a loss. Gross receipts fell off $4000 from 1927. With normal receipts the Lodge should show a fair profit. Much credit is due to Mrs. Bates for good management. She has built up a reputation which should be of value in the coming season. President Proctor has generously financed the building of an electric line to connect the Lodge with the lines of the Vermont Hydro-Electric Co., which is a great improvement over the old Delco system.

Prof. P. C. Voter reported for the guide book committee that 748 out of the edition of 1500 had been disposed of, leaving enough on hand for 1929. Receipts, including advertisements, have been more than enough to pay expenses, so that future sales will be clear profit. In 1930 it is probable a new edition will be needed.

The report of Geo. F. E. Storey for the membership committee was mostly devoted to suggestions for increasing the membership. To this end he advocated more news stories in the papers, illustrated if possible, that each present member endeavor to get a new one, and that an active committee be appointed to foster one or more outings, at which there will be guides, equipment and good fellowship for the tramper starting out alone or new at the game.

W. M. Ross for the trail committee reported receipts from the treasury of $755, and disbursements of $661, leaving a balance on hand of $94. Principal disbursements were $118 for the Appalachian Trail, $106 for Belvidere camp and trail, Marjorie Hulburd trail, $93, Manchester, $81, Mould's trail, $75, Webster camp, $71, etc.

Miss Laura E. Woodward, president of the New York Section, submitted a full report of the activities of that Section during 1928, prefacing it with the statement that the Section's aims might be expressed by the words "aspiration, inspiration and perspiration"; the first two they succeeded in attaining to a considerable extent, but the last more infrequently, owing to their remoteness from the Trail. The membership remained stationary, new admissions exactly equalling withdrawals. An account of the weekly outings was given, of which there were 50, the annual dinner, at which Stefansson spoke, activities in trail work in the New York City region, etc. Appended was a statement from Grace G. Carpenter, chairman of the Section's trail and shelter committee, as to work done on the Monroe Skyline Trail. This was ready on schedule time, notwithstanding unusual difficulties caused by rain and flood, the huts on Couching being opened July 4. The chairman is grateful to Prof. Monroe for his valuable assistance. About 400 people passed over this part of the Trail.

The report of the Worcester Section, read by Mr. D. L. Evans, was mostly taken up with their excellent work in relocating the Trail over Glastenbury Mountain, of which we have already given an account. This Section increased its membership over 1927.

The report of the Burlington Section, read by Mrs. Ernest E. Smith, the President, mentioned as the chief achievement of the year the building of Bolton lodge, which we have already described. The trail committee think the Trail from the top of Bolton to Taylor lodge could be improved by relocating. Statistics from Taft lodge show that 750 visited it, 250 spending the night. Final Report, Committee on Photo Contest of 1928

As your Committee stated in the preliminary report published in the last issue of The News, the richness of the material submitted in the 1928 Photo Contest seriously handicapped a selection. Our difficulties coming to the ear of our President, Mortimer R. Proctor, he generously helped us by adding six prizes to the original three, and increasing the values of the Second and Third Prizes.

The awards have therefore now been made on the new basis, as follows:-

First Prize, $25, to S. K. Hardy of Littleton, Mass., for the picture entitled "Northerly from Molly Stark's Balcony".

Second Prize, $20, to Charles R. Tucker of Randolph, Vt. and Brooklyn, N. Y., for the picture entitled "Lake Mansfield From Taylor Lodge".

Third Prize, $15, to Helen G. Wright of Glassboro, N. J., for the picture entitled "Ritterbush Pond".

Fourth Prize, Class A, $10, to Eric P. Jackson of Hillsdale, Mich., for the picture entitled "Clouds rising from the Summit-ridge of Mt. Mansfield".

Fifth Prize, Class A, $5, to E. Mabel Brownell of Burlington, Vt., for the picture entitled "Almost up to Dunsmoor Lodge, Washington's Birthday 1927", a very beautiful snow-shoe scene.

Fourth Prize, Class B, $10, to Lewis H. Bodman of Burlington, Vt., for the picture entitled "Looking Across Winooski Valley from the Summit of Camel's Hump".

Fifth Prize, Class B, $5, to L. L. Bigelow of Shrewsbury, Mass., for the picture entitled "Sterling Pond".

Fourth Prize, Class C, $10, to Edward R. Denio of Middlebury, Vt., for the picture entitled "Near the Summit, Camel's Hump".

Fifth Prize, Class C, $5, no award.

No award is made in Class D for the Fifth Prize because all the pictures eligible for it were made by those who have already been awarded prizes, and under the rules of the contest no entrant may be awarded two prizes.

The added prizes for the three classes were for pictures as follows: Class A, pictures of the Trail or camps; Class B, pictures showing views from the Trail; Class C, pictures of or from the Trail judged solely for pictorial beauty, and not necessarily conforming to the conditions calling for news interest and depiction of typical Trail life or scene.

The Committee renews its awards of Honorable Mention to Charles R. Tucker and Harold E. Smith for their splendid groups of pictures covering many miles of the Trail, Mr. Tucker submitting a group of 140 pictures almost all of which were "in the running" for prizes in the various degrees and classes.

The Committee further recommends that if another contest be held that the conditions thereof call for award of prizes to groups of not less than three pictures, rather than to individual pictures; this for the purpose of making it easier to decide on the winners. Your Committee submits its awards with diffidence, knowing that it has required a good deal of yielding of its three members' individual opinions in order to obtain substantial unanimity, and realizing that a different jury might have made quite different awards with equal justice from the rich material entered by the contestants.

The Committee desires at this time to express its sincere and hearty thanks to Mortimer R. Proctor for his generosity in giving the prizes and his assistance in judging; and to our contestants for their zeal, enthusiasm and ability, while it also is conscious of the patience of all concerned during the necessarily slow deliberations which had to be carried on by mail.

Respectfully submitted,
HERBERT WHEATON CONGDON,
P. CONANT VOTER,
WALTER C. O'KANE,
Committee on Photo Contest.

LOOKING FORWARD

The Green Mountain Club has been vigorously working on a construction program by which the Trail and shelters would be permanently located and completely constructed by January, 1931, in which year we hope to appropriately celebrate the twenty-first anniversary of the founding of the Club.

The past year witnessed the completion of the Trail and the 44th camp. To provide adequate shelter for the entire Trail ten more camps are needed. We will exert every possible effort to have them finished by January, 1931.

As each camp costs about $250 a total of $2250 must be raised. We earnestly hope you will feel that the benefits to be derived from the Long Trail warrant your joining in our final effort to realize an ambition of many years. Any contribution will be most gratefully and appreciatively received. For a gift of $250 we would like the privilege of naming the camp for the donor.

Mortimer R. Proctor,
President.
