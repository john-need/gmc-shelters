ANNUAL MEETING OF TRUSTEES
It is most useful in those parts of the Trail that
are popular and easily accessible by motor.
Eleven of the 16 trustees attended the meeting at the Berwick Hotel, Rutland, forenoon
and afternoon of Saturday, March 10, with
acting-president Congdon presiding. Both the
acting-president and the president, Mortimer
R. Proctor, announced that they would be unable to serve in that capacity longer. C. J.
Cleveland submitted his resignation both as
treasurer and as trustee. A vote of thanks was
tendered him for his long and faithful service.
A committee was appointed to submit nominations to fill these vacancies, who reported the
names of Wallace M. Fay of Proctor for president, and E. A. Boyce of Proctor for trustee
and treasurer, and they were unanimously
elected. Other officers elected were: Prof.
P. C. Voter, Middlebury, vice-president; office
secretary, Miss Lula M. Tye, Rutland; corresponding secretary, W. M. Ross, Rutland;
auditor, J. A. Allis, New York; editor Long
Trail News, E. S. Marsh, Brandon; cartographer,
Prof. L. B. Puffer, Burlington.
Appropriations of something over $1400 were
made for Club activities during the coming year,
including $650 for patrol work on the Trail.
The committee on requirements for Long
Trail buildings reported by W. M. Fay that the
types of such buildings seem to fall into three
general classifications, viz., shelters, camps and
lodges, which are defined as follows: A shelter is
a rough, open face building of the Adirondack
type, of dimensions as stated, to accommodate
six people at most. It has no stove or other
equipment, but has an open fireplace in front
for cooking and heat. The shelter is recommended for an overflow near much used camps
and lodges, also where the terrain is unusually
rough and the distance between two of the
present stations seems long, and to accommodate those who want to sleep in closer contact with the out-of-doors.
A camp is larger than a shelter and more
pretentious. It is of the closed-open type with
windows, and has wooden shutters by adjusting
which it can be used either closed or open. It
should have a stove, table, benches and a wooden
floor.

It should accommodate at least sixteen persons,
and should be equipped with stove, table,
benches, cooking utensils, dishes, etc., often
with a fireplace, plenty of windows, and in some
cases screened.
It is recommended that before the Club or
any section constructs either type of building,
specifications thereof be first approved by the
trustees or a committee of them. Also, that no
buildings will be maintained by the Club or included in the guide book unless officially accepted by the trustees. The written report
included diagrams and elevations of a shelter
and a camp.
Mortimer R. Proctor reported for the committee appointed at the Club meeting in January to keep in touch with the Wilgus plan to
construct a parkway along the Green Mountains. He stated that information had been
received from Washington that nothing had
been done so far except to appropriate $50,000
for a survey of the proposed route, and that as
the fund set aside for public works had all been
allocated, it was not likely that anything more
will be done about the parkway scheme unless
more money should be forthcoming.
It was voted to publish 15,000 copies of a new
and enlarged edition of the folder "Short Trips
on the Long Trail," which has proved very
popular. The State of Vermont will use and
pay for 5,000 of these, the Club circulating the
remainder.
M. A. Mattoon of Laconia, N. H., and A. C.
Van Nort of Bennington, of the Federal forest
service in charge of the Green Mountain National
Forest, attended the meeting, and stated the
size and limits of the present Forest, and the
location of other tracts which the government
expects to acquire for the purpose. They said
that 60,000 acres have already been purchased
or are under option, and it is expected that this
will be increased eventually to 100,000 acres.
It was voted that hereafter all camps and trails
to be constructed by the Club on National
Forest lands or lands likely to become such be
first referred to the Forest service for approval

SHORT TRIPS FOLDER

This latest Club publication, authorized by the trustees at their meeting, has just made its appearance, under the efficient editorship of Professor O'Kane. It contains a map of Vermont and twelve illustrations. Details of ten or more one-day trips and six week-end trips are given, covering the Trail from Massachusetts to Canada. In addition there are directions given on how to reach the Long Trail, an explanation of what the Long Trail is like, short articles on guide books and short trips from your motor car, and a page each devoted to suggestions as to equipment and food and cooking. Those interested should write to the Secretary of the Green Mountain Club, Mead Bldg., Rutland, Vt., for a free copy of this valuable folder.


OBITUARY

Daniel L. Cady, poet, well known for his rhymes of Vermont rural life and poems on other subjects, died at Burlington, April 1, at the age of 73. He was a member of the Green Mountain Club.


NOTES

The Long Trail Lodge will open May 25.

Announcement has been made that the Batten Forest, thru which the Long Trail passes, has been acquired by the government, and will become a part of the Green Mountain National Forest, which it is now expected will eventually be much larger than at first contemplated.

William Hazlett [illegible], who spends his summers breadloafing in Vermont and writes for the Saturday Evening Post, whose opinion of the Wilgus parkway plan we quoted in a previous issue, has a story in a recent Post founded on the controversy over that plan. It was proposed to put such a parkway thru a Maine forest, in which the Trail Club maintained a trail. The Club fought the project, and there was much excited oratory and newspaper publicity. Boss, salesman for the Earthworm Tractor Co., mixed up in it in an effort to sell some tractors. It is finally discovered that a lumber company holds options on most of the forest which will be exercised if the parkway plan does not go thru and the forest will be destroyed, in consideration of which the Trail Club withdraws its opposition, according to the story, and the parkway scheme triumphs.

"In order to 'make the Vermont Mountains play a larger part in the life of the people' we need your support as a member; a blank will be sent on application. The dues are small."
