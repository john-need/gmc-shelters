The last formal meeting of the Trail Conference on Monday morning included a very lively open discussion with all those present encouraged to discuss any subject remotely pertinent to the Appalachian Trail. Stan Murray, ATC Chairman, again proved his excellent leadership qualifications by keeping the discussion within reasonable bounds. The Conference ended with the adoption of resolutions, the presentation of awards and the election of officers. The GMC was formally thanked for its activities as host club. Max Sauter, GMC, was awarded a certificate of thanks upon his retirement from the Executive Committee after many years as a member of the Board of Managers followed by nine years as Vice Chairman. Also honored for his many years of work for the Appalachian Trail and his many years on the Board of Managers was Fred F. Schuetz, New York Section GMC. Fred was given an honorary membership in the ATC in recognition of his devoted service. Art Koerber, GMC, was retired from the Board of Managers after six years of service, however his retirement only lasted a few minutes for he was then elected to one of the three Vice Chairman posts. Ben Rolston, past GMC president, was elected to the Board of Managers. Although Art and Ben will officially represent all of the clubs in the Northern Section and the New England District, it is evident that the Green Mountain Club is well represented.

Throughout the three day Conference the quiet, back-of-the-scenes guidance of President Bob Humes was evidenced in his capacity as General Chairman of the Conference, ably assisted by Ike Bartlett, Assistant Chairman. Also noted was the familiar voice of Ben Rolston, Reception Committee Chairman, over the PA system, frequently urging people to register. Ben was assisted behind the registration desk by Dorothy Rolston, Florence Kelton, Rhoda Orvis, Connie Brown, Bob Attenborough and Peggy Pons.

The exhibits, under the guidance of Art Koerber, were a huge success due to the large number of contributing clubs. Among the finest were the wildflower display presented by the Brattleboro Section and the inspiring Long Trail camp scene sponsored by the Burlington Section, and the very fine exhibit by the Green Mountain National Forest. Many thanks to all the clubs and groups that shared their interesting and varied exhibits.

The site selection committee, who worked so hard in 1962 and 1963 checking several different possible locations deserve much of the credit for the success of the Conference. The Stratton Mt. Village, Inn and Base Lodge proved to be an excellent combined location for the event.

ART KOERBER

CONFERENCE HIKES

From suggestions by Art Koerber a nice schedule was worked up for the four days following the Conference. With Stratton Mt. as the overnight base, the hikes covered the Long Trail from Grout Job to Vt. Route 140. Twenty to over sixty hikers enjoyed the walks, for many, their first acquaintance with the Long Trail. Those with cars cooperated generously with transportation to the trail.

Special thanks go to Mr. Swezey of Manchester for a column of jeeps to bring 32 weary hikers from Prospect Rock down to Route 11.

MAX H. SAUTER, Hikes Chairman ATC

The Vermont Natural Resources Council will hold a public meeting at Mt. Ascutney Ski Area on the afternoon of Saturday, Oct. 3rd. PAST PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE

After two very busy and most enjoyable years in office, your 1962-1964 president joined the ranks of past presidents on May 30th.

Those two years will always be remembered with great pleasure. For it was during those years that such pleasant associations with so many wonderful people were enjoyed and appreciated.

Many letters were written. Many meetings were attended. Many visits with members in many cities and villages were made. Many outing and trail walks with other GMC'ers and guests were enjoyed.

Now that my part of the GMC journey has been completed, and the wheel has been turned over to my very able successor, I know that his skillful use of the accelerator will propel our GMC vehicle toward the achievement of greater goals in the year ahead.

It is my pleasure, and duty too, to thank those who have been of immeasurable help during the past two years:

Our Trustees who, through their faithful attendance at meetings, their hard work on committees, and their correspondence and support, have helped me in carrying our Club program;

Other members, though not Trustees, have given such valuable assistance on committees on which I had asked them to serve;

The entire staff of the United States Forest Service who have given such splendid assistance and cooperation toward improving the Long Trail System so that it may better serve the increasing number of hikers who use it each year;

And to ALL of our members, and non-member friends of the GMC, who have, through their attendance at Club functions and by their dozens of letters, given me their suggestions, ideas and support.

Although retired from office, I shall not retire from giving my time, efforts and support to our new president and his program of "making the mountains of Vermont play a larger part in the lives of the people."

Dorothy and I extend our best wishes to you all.

BEN ROLSTON

BURLINGTON SECTION
Up The Trail Apiece—
   Attendance on our weekly outings from March 7th have ranged from 8 to 72. The annual maple sugar party was enjoyed by 115.
   Among the "head-liner" events were:
   The maple sugar party on April 4th began with a tricky climb up Arrowhead Mt., which sharpened the appetites for supper. After a ham supper and all the maple-sugar-on-snow they could eat many of the group took part in a period of square-dancing.
   The Burlington Section has rented a camp in Ranch Camp Valley from Mr. Burt, solely for the use of section members and their friends. The Valley is up in the Smuggler's Notch region, near the ski areas. In earlier years of the skiing era, it was a popular spot for cross country skiing and lumber-man style meals. Leland Brown, section treasurer and Joseph Frank arranged the lease. On April 12th a "house warming" party was staged at the camp and some 72 people came in for a look-see and refreshments.
   Dane Shortsleeves, section trail chairman, found it rough getting help on the trail clearing bees this spring, so he sprung an idea that finally got a big party out. He promised a baked-bean supper and all the trimmings if they would come out and work on the trail; free eats . . . but "no workie, no beans." He got the workers and the workers got one of the best bean feeds they ever gathered around up at the Ranch Valley Camp.
   Several from the Burlington Section attended the Appalachian Trail Conference in the Stratton Mt. ski region for one or more days.
   Two out-of-state outings were scheduled in late spring and early summer. The snowshoe trip into the Adirondacks on March 21-22 was headed up by Fred Smith and drew 14, hungry for punishment, tramping in the Heart Lake region. On the 4th of July weekend Larry Dean got 8 out to climb Mt. Moosilauke in near foul weather—minus views from the top. The party spent the night of the 4th in Wildwood, the newly developed National Forest Campground, well up in Kinsman Notch, N.H.
   Our loyal section member, Miss Lois McCay, of Montreal, made the trip down from over the Border to take part in both of the out-of-state climbs. She and some of her Youth Hostel friends have also joined in on two other section events this past spring.
   We of the Burlington Section hope one and all of the other Green Mt. Club Sections will be represented at the Intersectional Week Camp at Mt. Norris Scout Reservation in Eden, Vt., this August. In the past, we've had a large attendance of Members-at-Large; let's double the number this year. We'd like to have you all come and we'll try to show you a good time. Roy and his tam-o'shanter will be there—he promises to model it without cost to anyone.
LARRY DEAN, Reporter

KILLINGTON SECTION
   Spring seemed to be late in coming to the Green Mountains—this section of them, at least—but it made up for its tardiness by being one of the loveliest in recent years, with many unusually warm days.
   The Killington Section proclaimed the season on April 4th with its annual sugar-on-snow party, once again scheduled to coincide with a trustees' meeting. A record crowd of oldsters, youngsters and in-betweens attended this year.
   Trips on the trail covered several of the shelters and favorite mountains, with participation ranging from high (close to 30 on a trip to Sunnyside in early March) to solitary—a solo trip to Bird Mountain by Mark Wiley in mid-June. Several new younger members have joined our ranks recently, and their fresh accounts of trips make for good reading in "Smoke and Blazes," ably edited by President Harold Valiquette, with especially appealing "Coming Events," written by Susan Tarbell.
   The club's annual meeting at the Long Trail Lodge on May 30th was a highlight of the spring—we are only sorry we couldn't provide warmer weather for our friends that weekend. The excellent picnic lunch prepared and served by the Burlington Section was a welcome innovation.
   Another activity was a rummage sale, ordinarily held in the fall, but postponed until late February. Although the sale was a financial success, as usual, netting over $100, the section plans to have it in the fall again this year. Trail clearing and shelter repair continued, as always, with Churchill Scott still seeking more volunteers.
   As of now, everyone is looking forward eagerly to a "Mystery Hike" on Aug. 8th, for which leader Terry Gulick has recommended bringing "waterproof footwear (boots or bare feet), camera, lunch and a cup, and a jeep if you or your friends have one."
   See you at the Intersectional!
ANN REYNOLDS, Reporter

WORCESTER SECTION

Seems like spring and summer merged into one this year. The weather for the most part was fine and several good local hikes were enjoyed by an average of 20 people per hike. A couple of work parties helped to straighten out our camp in Leicester for a summertime of swimming and picnicking for all those who enjoy it so much.

Some of us, including yours truly, enjoyed the Annual Meeting in Rutland over the Memorial Day weekend, at which we were proud to see Bob Humes, a Worcester Section member, installed as President. On the way home from the meeting, 6 of us hiked in to the new George Story Shelter south of the Wardsboro Road and flushed out a sleeping porcupine at the shelter. Never saw anyone run as fast as he did when he discovered he was no longer alone.

The month of June gave us our annual strawberry social, which was well attended. A sudden thunder storm with lots of rain gave us reason to ponder for a while. It all turned out alright, however; our woodsmen got the outdoor fires going, everyone had plenty to eat, and the shortcakes were delicious. The committee, Florence Salo, Peggy Pearson and Barbara Watson, deserve a heartfelt "thank you." An auction was held following the "eating hour" which was not only a financial success but a joy to those lucky people who went home with some of the treasures.

A trail clearing party went up to Stratton in the month of May and took care of our part of the Long Trail. Don't know what the Club would do without this devoted band of workers who take care of this responsibility.

LINNEA ELDRIDGE, Reporter

HAVE YOU HEARD?

MOUNTAIN FLOWERS OF NEW ENGLAND

is now a reality, thanks to four members of the Appalachian Mt. Club, namely; S. K. Harris, J. H. Langeheim, F. L. Steele and M. Underhill. Those who have been intrigued by the bright spots of color along the mountain trails can now find the answer to their questions "What is it?". In this book they have described and pictured not only the Arctic-Alpine species, but also all plants found on the mountaintops of New England and New York and some that ascend to alpine areas from spruce forests below. The terminology and descriptions in this pocket size (approx. 5" X 7") guide are as clear and simple as possible for the layman and although scientific names are recommended the various common names are also given. The keys to the plant families and species make it a quick reference on the trail and over 200 line cuts help to verify your identification. Those who have seen Miriam Underhill's photographs of flowers on display at the AMC huts will be delighted with the 143 color reproductions of many species. This authoritative guide is a MUST for every flower-lover's library and should be on the reference shelf of every library and mountain resort. It may be purchased at a cost of $4.50 from the Appalachian Mt. Club, 5 Joy St., Boston, Mass. 02108.
