GUEST SPEAKER

Robert Williams, Vermont Commissioner of Forest and Parks, was the after-dinner speaker at the Annual Meeting held on May 27, at 'The Stable' in Bomoseen. Before coming to Vermont, Mr. Williams was a Professor in the College of Forestry at Syracuse, where he was educated. His undergraduate degree was in Forestry and his graduate degree in Landscape Architecture. Just previous to the Annual Meeting, Mr. Williams was appointed to represent the State Natural Resources Agencies on the Vermont Planning Council. At the Annual Meeting, Mr. Williams was appointed a Trustee of the Green Mountain Club, representing State Forests and Parks.

Mr. Williams began his talk with a thumbnail sketch of the Forests and Parks Department. He also mentioned that this summer for the first time it will be possible to purchase for $5.00 a family pass for use of the State Parks.

His main topic was "Multiple Use of Forest Lands," which he defined as determining what a unit of land is best suited for, and then compatible uses which will not detract from the major use. As an example of single use management, he gave Smugglers Notch. A bigger area, e.g., a 20,000-acre forest, would require multiple use management.

Mount Mansfield he described as predominantly a recreation area, with selective tree cutting that will not detract from its primary use. A problem occurs when aesthetics conflicts with economics and utilitarian aspects, e.g., the ETV lines on Mount Ascutney.

Another problem is how much public land should the State own because of its effect on the tax structure. However, it is a misconception that the State does not pay any taxes on their land. There is a question of how much land it can afford. Half of the State of Vermont is in public ownership. What will be the relationship with private enterprise, with other agencies? What about the problem of financing—paying the bill? Fees or free? We are going to be getting more Federal grants; maybe this is the answer.

Planning he said, is one of the keys to the whole thing—financial, natural resources, and everything and the citizenry must get involved. How can we be informed on everything that we need be? Mr. Williams presented questions as well as answers. The audience also presented questions in an interesting question and answer period which followed.

Mr. Williams concluded his talk with a comment on the weather (for those uninformed, following a warm spell, Vermont had a snowstorm, with six to eight inches of snow, on May 25). A neighbor's little boy complained to his father, "Daddy, I didn't get swimming all summer."

A special guest at the Annual Meeting was Mr. Michael DeBonis a recently appointed member of the State Board of Forests and Parks, from Poultney, and a landowner in the area. Mr. DeBonis has a long acquaintanceship with Conservation since he worked with the U.S. Soil Conservation Service for many years.

MARION GORHAM

MINUTES OF ANNUAL MEETING

President John Rohrbaugh opened the 57th Annual Meeting of the Green Mountain Club at "The Stable," Castleton, at 10:30 a.m., on May 27, 1967. The minutes of the last Annual Meeting were approved as published.

President John commented on the importance of Committee Chairmen and the value of the telephone in communications among them. A written report was submitted.

REPORTS

Long Trail Patrol: Roy Buchanan presented a most descriptive report on activities of Jack Harrington and organizational activities of the Patrol for this summer. He also mentioned some 43 inches of snow still existed on top of Mt. Mansfield as of the Annual Meeting. The GMC truck is in Johnson. Boys could start work June 20, when schools finish. There is a possibility of Spring ice storm damage. New York Section would like a new camp built.

Section Reports:

Sterling: No report presented.

Montpelier: Marion Gorham stated that an activity is scheduled for every other week with one-half dozen to 30 people attending. Two Trail clearing hikes were carried out last year and a new toilet facility will be located at Sterling Pond Lodge this summer. Thirty to forty paid members now listed.

Burlington: Harris Abbott highlighted activities held during the year pointing out that many varied activities were offered for all members. Year-ending membership over 200 people. Trail and shelters in fair to good condition. Interest in Section being shown by youngsters.

New York: Peggy Pons reviewed the highlights of last year. Almost 2,000 members and guests enjoyed the use of Camp Thendara. A newly formed family outing committee is off to a good start. Total membership now at 437. A new natural resources committee has been formed and succeeds the former Conservation Committee.

This year it is hoped to develop a new site at or near the Paris Skidway. Repairs are scheduled for Gorham Lodge.

Killington: Janet Martin reported that the Section voted to express an interest in Bald Mt. as a natural wildlands area for Rutland. Bucklin Trail area on Killington is scheduled to be lumbered.

Brattleboro: Rex Coleman presented an enlightening report stating that ice damage was prevalent on the Trail into Bromley. Camp and trail south in good shape.

Worcester: Alan Gormly stated there are over 100 adult members and 15 to 20 juniors. A work party already held this spring. Unknown extent of ice damage, but some evidence has been seen where such damage has occurred. A new shelter suddenly appeared on the south side of Stratton Pond. (President John commented: "Now we have instant shelters!")

Middlebury: Forty student members in Club. Some Trail clearing done last fall and the rest is to be done by summer.

Bennington: John Paulson mentioned that among activities conducted were joint hikes with Taconic Club members. Described a junior program instigated by Marion Hardy which is hoped to encourage new memberships. So far two members have become more active as a result.

Pioneer Valley: Jim Barrett stated that blowdowns have been cleared, Deerview Shelter was burned last fall and the outhouse is out of commission. Much jeep and motorcycle traffic is causing a study of possible relocation of this Section of the Trail, possibly to the east of Sucker Brook.

TREASURER'S REPORT

George Saunders presented a written copy of the report. He pointed out that Guide Book sales have doubled and that Club is in good financial condition.

CONSERVATION

Shirley Strong spoke for Bill Bartlett. She presented an outline of plans for preserving the natural beauty of the Mountains which would protect what is already established, bringing to the attention of everyone the Green Mountain Wildlands Profile map on display. Such a plan not only would benefit hikers, but also other travelers in cars.
   Pointing out recent developments on mountain tops and slopes, Shirley mentioned the new Bolton Development, consisting of some 8,000 acres, some changes on Mt. Mansfield, and a planned Madonna Mountain expansion.
   Encouraging step in this area consists of legislation showing more interest in mountain natural area preservation such as protection of Camel's Hump and the newly publicized land classification plan of the Department of Forests and Parks. Shirley further pointed out the passage of H119 which included the designation of mountain tops as natural areas.
   She concluded stating that the plan calls for the best use of mountain areas based on many factors.

Lunch recess taken at 12:30 p.m.

Meeting resumed about 2 p.m., with Shirley continuing.
   She mentioned that the Wildlands Profile Concept proposes the framework for a land use plan for the mountains, incorporating various types of areas including natural, scenic and recreational.
   Resulting from a floor question, Shirley stated that this plan would include present ski developments and also calls for specific guidelines in the development of future ski areas.
   Mr. Ogden, Killington Section, recommended to the Trustees that they get copies of legislation and write up the pros and cons in a resume. Then these should be sent to the membership asking each member to read it and indicate their favor to their representatives.
   Congressional bills were then discussed by Shirley, specifically H4865 and S827. She pointed out that these bills are revised from last year and commented on their effect on the Appalachian Trail and thus the Long Trail.
   Mr. Merrill, former Forests and Parks Commissioner, stated that the success of the GMC has been due to its being a private organization and so should oppose any Federal bills on controls and take over of the Trail system.

Membership was encouraged to get copies of the bills and write to Congressmen regarding them. A floor recommendation to form a Legislative Committee was received.

END-TO-END

Carin Schon reported that eight new end-to-enders have been registered since January 1.

TRAILS AND SHELTERS

Rod Rice reported that five new shelters have been built—two by the Long Trail Patrol and three by the USFS; two Shelters lost by fire accidentally and one intentionally. This was the one replaced by Rolston's Rest.
   Relocation necessary at Jay Peak. Some confusion may develop on Mt. Mansfield, while new trail construction is carried on. Recommended leaving blowdowns occasionally. Increase in Trail cleaning has been noticed. Caution was given to have supervision and to discourage blazing too much. Possibility exists that there will be an unusual number of visitors due to Expo 67. Short walk folders are in the Vermont building at Expo.
   Don Wallace, New York Section, moved to adopt a "carry-it-in, carry-it-out" concept with respect to non-disposable trash and to use what means possible to promote this concept. Passed.

PUBLICITY AND MEMBERSHIP

Bob Attenborough reported that membership is to be punched up on IBM cards. Two Sections completed as of Meeting time. Slide collection now contains 65 slides; more would be welcome. Black and white pictures are desired for miscellaneous reasons, mainly articles. Policy on short-walk folders will be to supply individuals, while groups will be referred to the State. Recruitment: Pioneer Valley has grown 400 percent; most Sections are static. A government sponsored survey on hiking being conducted and if approved by GMC, members would receive a questionnaire to answer. Such questionnaire would be mailed from GMC office. Approved.

UNITED STATES FOREST SERVICE

Bob Deemer stated the Job Corp Camp is in operation and would have some conservation work done along Long Trail. Hope to open out some vistas south of Linclon Gap. Hope to improve signs on USFS sections of Long Trail from Job Corp manpower. He reported that considerable ice damage occurred above the 2,200-foot level in southern USFS section.

BY-LAW AMENDMENT

Article II, Section 4, was amended to increase the number of Trustees by one. This allows a member of the Vermont Forests and Parks to serve as Trustee. Proposal was passed unanimously (Robert Williams, Commissioner of Forests and Parks, was elected).

NOMINATIONS

President John read list of nominations submitted by Bob Humes, Chairman: Miss Minerva Hinchey, Corresponding Secretary; C. B. Gulick, 3rd, Clerk; Ben Rolston, representing members-at-large. No other nominations were presented and Clerk was instructed to cast one ballot.
   Other Trustees elected or re-elected were: Hazel Bullock succeeding her husband, Bennington; John Paulson succeeding Marion Hardy, Bennington; Myron Morris succeeding Bill Bartlett, New York; Betty Weeks succeeding Churchill Scott, Killington; Dave Otis succeeding Marion Gorham, Montpelier; Rod Rice succeeding himself, Burlington.
   A motion made last year by Ted Goddard, and tabled, was brought up by Don Wallace. His motion to bring it back on the floor was lost.

Meeting adjourned at approximately 4:30 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
CHARLES B. GULICK, 3rd, Clerk

GMC COMMITTEE CHANGES

As you read through the list of Committees for 1967-68 you should notice some new Committees and the rearrangement of some existing ones. There were many reasons involved but basically the intent was to accomplish more and to let each person work most effectively in an area of his particular interest.

The fact that we now have a By-Law Revision Committee does not imply there is anything lacking in our present by-laws. However, times and conditions change and improvements have been suggested. Now we have a committee, which happens to be composed of three past Presidents, to carefully consider these matters.

The Landowners Committee was established to determine just whose land every bit of the Trail is on. This project was started informally some time ago. Several members have helped in their areas, but it seems that to complete the project in a reasonable time a committee is necessary.

We have a Legislative Action Committee whose function is to keep us abreast the mounting tide of proposed legislation that concerns the Club. One member is very close to the Vermont scene, the other actively following the National picture.

Publicity and Membership has now been split into separate Committees. Publicity has been put back in Vermont for more timely news releases. This Committee also has the GMC Slide Collection which is available for all Club and publicity purposes. Membership, which can as well be handled remotely, rests for the time in the New York area.

The Conservation Committee and the Trail and Shelters Committee have both been increased in strength. This reflects the increased use of our Trail and the need to preserve its surroundings. Conservation now has better coverage by area. Both the State and National Forests are represented on the Trail and Shelters Committee.

Changes and additions can be made whenever it seems in the best interest of the Club, so all suggestions will be appreciated.

The Committees carry on the Club's work throughout the year. They work hard for you and deserve your support. Get in touch with one and offer your help.

BOB ATTENBOROUGH

MONTPELIER SECTION

Summer has come in with a bang, but these notes go back to April 9, when we hiked up Bear Mt. in Allis State Park in Brookfield, and roasted hot dogs in the fireplace. April 14 was our Annual Meeting and the following officers were elected: President, Marion Gorham; Secretary and Reporter, Marion Holmes; Treasurer, David Hine; Trustee, David Otis. The election was followed with colored slides shown by Virginia Johnson, a recently returned AFS student, who had been to New Zealand, and John Goller who had travelled in Europe.

April 23, a few hardy souls climbed Mt. Hunger and were greeted with a blizzard on top. May 7, three members plus some boys from the High School ski team spent the day in Tuckerman where the skiing was fine. May 21, five members hiked from Middlebury Gap to the Clark Brook Trail and then down to Route 100. There was still plenty of snow in the woods and the brooks were overflowing.

May 27, eleven members attended the Annual Meeting in Castleton and one of our number—David Otis—was elected Vice President.

June 11 was trail clearing day and six members cleared and blazed the Elephant's Head Trail up to Sterling Pond. Returning down the Long Trail to Smugglers Notch they were caught in a severe thunderstorm and postponed further work. Much work has to be done on Sterling Pond Lodge as the result of winter vandalism. We plan to build an outhouse there in the near future.

June 18, only two showed up for a short woodsy hike on the Eagle Ledge Road in Worcester. Atop Mount Beacon

Seventy-two people, plus a dog, participated in a notable tri-partite outing conducted by Ray Darrenougue, Elizabeth Cook and Marion West. This was all fun and was attended by all ages. Everyone rode the cog railway-steepest railway of its length in the world-to the top of Mt. Beacon, 1,636 ft. elev., at the top of the Fishkill Range. Lunch was eaten at the top, near the Casino. The Shawangunk, Catskill and Adirondack Mts. could be seen. The "A" hikers went along the Ridge Trail to Breakneck Mt. and down to the Hudson River, 6 mi. of rough going. The "B" hike went along scenic ledge trails around the north and south sides of Mt. Beacon for 5 mi. of easy going, then down the Incline Railroad to their parked cars. A monument placed on the mountain by the DAR was seen. It commemorates the use of the mountain for signal fires during the American Revolution. The "C" hike was on easy scenic paths to observation points around the Casino ground, but six of the older Club members also climbed the 75-foot fire tower! Ray's "A" "B" "C" hikes grow more popular every year! The picture shows some of the crowd.

EDNA M. ROHRBAUGH, Reporter

GMC MEMBER QUESTIONNAIRE

The Department of Agriculture (which supervises our National Forests) wants to find out more about hikers, and our Club has been asked to help. In early September every member will receive a one-page questionnaire and return envelope. The questions are simple yet to a sociologist the answers, in a sufficient number, reveal a surprising amount of information.

Some hikers in the Camel's Hump area were sent a questionnaire last year by UVM as part of this project.

Prof. McKain (Rural Sociology, University of Conn.) contacted the Club to explain his part of this multi-phase project, the results of which will be available to us as well as the Department of Agriculture. The Professor, with three End-to-Enders in his own family, says that in a survey of this type it is important to get information on all members. This includes those who are now riding along on a pack-board and those who have many years since given up active hiking.

The Government is paying all our expenses in handling this and Miss Hinchey will do the mailing so that our mailing list remains private.

Let's help Prof. McKain, and ourselves with a 100 percent return of the questionnaires.

LONG TRAIL PATROL

The Long Trail Patrol was off to a late start as one of the members wasn't out of school until the last week in June. The crew is all new this year. We lost the former members to summer school, etc. We have Dave Cass from Essex Junction, an End-to-Ender and an aspiring 46'er, who is Leader, Dave Govatski from Connecticut and Douglas Montgomery from Colchester.

With Roy Buchanan along to give us some advice, the Patrol started at Ithiel Falls. They worked north through Barrows, made repairs to Parker Camp, blazed and cleared over Laraway, where there was lumbering going on. Ritterbush will need quite a bit of repair work, and then the boys will go on to Sterling Pond Lodge, which is quite battered from storms and many skiers.

SURPRISE SHELTER

For more than thirty years, on the weekend after Mother's Day, the Worcester Section has assembled a faithful trail crew on the Stratton Road and dispatched them onto the Worcester trails for the annual spring work party.

This year, the trail workers, who know the Stratton trails as intimately as their own back yards, worked their way toward Stratton Pond. They found things just about as they had expected to find them, perhaps with a few more blowdowns than usual.

Along the Pond's south shore to Bigelow Shelter. All was well. But as they progressed westward toward the Pond's outlet, they stopped short in amazement. There, before their very eyes, was a new shelter!

WHODUNNIT??

Finally, the secret has been revealed. International Paper Company did it!

Yes, only a few days before the Worcester trail workers arrived, a crew from the International Paper Company put the finishing touches on the new shelter.

What'll we name it? As yet a name has not been decided upon. But perhaps we should call it Surprise Shelter, for it surely took the Worcester trail workers by surprise!

With such heavy use of the Stratton Pond area, this new shelter will be a valuable addition to our Long Trail System. Here is a splendid example of landowner interest and cooperation. So to International Paper Company, we say THANK YOU!

BEN ROLSTON

NEW SPECIAL PROJECTS COMMITTEE

Do you have a report of trail conditions compiled during a recent hike on the Long Trail System? Did you find a shelter needing some repairs? Did your GMC Section work party do some trail clearing or shelter work this past weekend? Are you planning a Long Trail hike, thus being in need of information on conditions you may encounter? Or are you a Scout leader, or a group counselor, or just an individual hiker with the desire to contribute some time to a worthwhile project on the Trail?

For all of the above items, a new Special Projects Committee has been formed. Its coordinator will work with and report to the Trails and Shelters Committee Chairman.

The first aim of this new committee will be to compile an accurate and up-to-date card file of trail and shelter conditions. This information will be helpful to the trail crews; it will also be made available on request to those who are planning a Long Trail trip.

The second aim of the committee will be to organize special projects. A special project may consist of a shelter erection, such as the Lost Pond Shelter moving project of 1965. Or a project may be to bring up to standard a section of the Long Trail or other trail in our system, such as the 1964 standardizing of our section of the Appalachian Trail between Sherburne Pass and Barnard Gulf. Or a special project may be a trail relocation or needed repairs to a shelter.

We need all of the trails and shelters information we can obtain, in order to be able to pass this information on to those who need it. Likewise, there is much work to be done, and the successful completion of a special project can greatly help our overstrained trail maintenance program. We need your support!

The coordinator of this Special Projects Committee will be Ben Rolston. Send him your reports, and your inquiries, and your suggestions, and your offers of assistance on a special project. Mail them to 323 12th St., Schenectady, N.Y. 12306.

This new committee is an experiment. With your help, it can be very successful. We appreciate your interest.
