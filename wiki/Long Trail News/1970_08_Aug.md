have to be made into open shelters. The Club is now promoting the "carry in-carry out" idea and signs urging this practice will be placed in shelters. Volunteer group can be used to clean up old dumps and to make improvement on the footpath. However, blazes should only be made by authorized persons. Several trail relocations are being given careful consideration because opportunities for relocations are becoming more limited and implementation of the National Trail System means that any relocation should be to the best possible route.

There are only 700 copies left of the 2700 Guidebooks published in 1968. The Guidebook Committee is preparing a new edition to be published next year.

Vermont House Bill 417, Act 250, An Act to Create an Environmental Board and District Commissions, was reported on by the Legislative Committee. This Act, which passed and is now law, includes in it a section which requires "development" above 2,500 foot elevation to be approved by the District Commission. President Strong and the Profile Committee played a key role in working towards the inclusion of this section as part of the legislation.

Senate Bill 18, An Act to Encourage the Continuation of the Present Uses of Land, also passed. Its enabling legislation permits government agencies to acquire rights to land in less than fee simple title, such as scenic easements, and may help to protect The Long Trail.

The Conservation Committee reported that the Profile Committee, which The Club had earlier supported with a $500 contribution, is now available to provide information, legal and tax advice to those interested in protecting the higher elevations.

The Land Ownership Committee has a set of thirteen maps showing land ownership through which The Long Trail passes and these maps will be used to keep a continuous record of ownership.

The Long Trail News has grown to ten pages which is one reason for the increased publication and mailing costs.

The End-to-End Committee reports that 45 persons completed the trail during the last year. A check of the membership showed that there were 20 end-to-enders at the meeting.

Anniversary Year Committee plans have been working out well. Governor Deane C. Davis declared May 16 as Green Mountain Club Day and a large number of people turned out to work on the trails despite the poor weather. Special plans are being developed for the Intersectional, including an "old timers social hour," and a work day. The Photo Contest resulted in a good number of entries and the winners were announced.

A Long Range Planning Committee has been established to develop objectives and programs for The Club. Anyone having ideas on this should pass them on to chairman John Nuffort.

Officers and trustees elected at the meeting were: Corresponding Secretary, Minerva Hinchey; Clerk, George Plumb; National Forest Service Trustee, William Baxandall; Vermont Forests and Parks Department Trustee, Arthur Heitman; and Member-at-Large Trustee, George Pearlstein.

After the business meeting most of the members went on a short hike led by Herbert Ogden from the Ascutney Trails Association.

A fine turkey supper, with all the fixings, more than satisfied the hungry hikers and it was then back to the hall for the evening program. A letter of greetings was read to the membership from one of the great leaders in the development of hiking trails, Benton McKaye. Inspired by The Long Trail, McKaye conceived and saw to completion the construction of The Appalachian Trail. At ninety years of age and nearly blind, Mr. McKaye typed his own letter of best wishes to The Club. After viewing and listening to a slide show with a musical background, titled "The Vanishing Vermont," every hiker present probably realized more than ever before that Vermont has some unique resources well worth protecting. But former architect, and now author and free lance photographer, Clyde Smith, made us also realize that these resources won't last very long in their present condition, unless we do something about it.

For the first time camping was made an important part of the weekend program and many persons camped out at nearby Ascutney State Park and enjoyed a breakfast there on Sunday prepared by the Connecticut Section.

It was quite appropriate that the 60th Anniversary Annual Meeting be held at Ascutney, where the idea of The Long Trail was first conceived, and equally appropriate that the weekend be climaxed with a hike to the summit of Mt. Ascutney. The weekend was a fine blend of business mixed with pleasure and hiking, and the love of the Green Mountains was the binding that tied the whole thing together into a most enjoyable and refreshing Annual Meeting Weekend.

Chairman of the Annual Meeting Don Wallace and his workers Betty Weeks, Herb Ogden, Dave Otis and Grangel Kammerer are to be congratulated for planning and executing a fine program. GEORGE PLUMB, Clerk

SUMMARY OF THE MAY 30, 1970, TRUSTEES MEETING

Officers elected for the 1970-71 year were: President, Shirley Strong; Vice-President, Grangel Kammerer; Treasurer, John Paulson; and Auditor, Newell Briggs.

The budget for 1970-71 was adopted. It included an estimated income of $13,950 and estimated expenses of $13,890.

Because the work of the Trails and Shelters Committee is becoming too much to handle, the ideas of dividing responsibilities along a functional or geographical basis were discussed. Anyone having thoughts on the subject is invited to pass them along to Rod Rice or to one of the officers.

It was voted to authorize a Trust Committee composed of Ted Goddard and two others to be appointed by the President to renew the trust contract for five years.

Ideas for two activities were briefly discussed. One concerned the concept of a trip leader's seminar to be sponsored by the main club. Another was the possibility of having the Annual Meeting on the long weekend every year and incorporating into the weekend a workday on The Trail. GEORGE PLUMB, Clerk

SINCERE THANKS

The Trustees wish to recognize and express thanks for the gifts of money received by The Club from members and interested individuals during the past year. These included an anonymous gift of $500.

The program of Trail maintenance and preservation becomes more of a challenge each year and new projects for meeting this challenge are being initiated. Others are being considered. The added support which these special gifts and donations lend to the work of the GMC is greatly appreciated.

ROD RETIRES

Rod Rice, for many years Chairman of the Trails & Shelters Committee, has just completed a six-year term as Trustee and has asked to be relieved of the chairmanship responsibilities. He has, however, expressed willingness to continue on the Committee, to serve as chairman of the Long Trail Patrol and to assist the new chairman in every way possible. Rod's continued interest and involvement is greatly appreciated! Few members have devoted more time and effort in behalf of the GMC and the Club is indebted to such dedicated individuals.

STERLING SECTION BEING RE-ORGANIZED

Steps are being taken to reactivate the long dormant Sterling Section of The Green Mountain Club in the Stowe-Morrisville-Johnson area. Formal reorganization has yet to take place but progress to date points to its eventual accomplishment. A key factor will be how many persons with a sustained active interest can be found in the area.

Sparkplug in the re-organizing effort has been Edmour Germain of Johnson, a long-time GMC member who in his younger days served as a caretaker at Taft Lodge. But several others have also played an active role.

If present plans are realized, the section will re-assume responsibility for upkeep of The Long Trail from Rt. 15 in Johnson south past French Camp, over Whiteface Mt. and the lodge there and on to Chilcoot Pass. In addition, the section would look after the side trail from the Mud City area of Morristown to the Beaver Meadow or Moulds' Lodge and the two connecting trails from there to the LT.

Work efforts to date have focussed on this latter area with one inspection trip and two work sessions spent in trail clearing and blazing and repairs and clean-up at the lodge. One of these took place May 16, the GMC annual work day, when we were honored to have GMC President Shirley Strong join us for the working hike here.

A group of Johnson State College students helped considerably by carrying in a new stove for the Beaver Meadow camp. But there remain portions of this area where considerable work still needs to be done and at least one more work trip is planned this year though this won't complete the job.

The section has also sponsored two outings strictly for pleasure, one a hike up Elmore Mt. and the other a journey to the high peaks area of the Adirondacks over the July 4th weekend.

Any GMC Members-at-Large who might wish to associate themselves with the new Sterling Section are urged to contact Ed Germain, mailing address, Johnson 05656. Tel. 635-7004.

TED GODDARD

It is with surprise and great shock that we learn of the death of Ted Goddard on July 4. CONNECTICUT SECTION

Annual Meeting reported elsewhere adjourned in time for Connecticut Section to gather at Ascutney Campground for planning for the BIG BREAKFAST. A sample lot of pancakes were prepared in the Bragdon Camper to be sure we were using Herb Ogden's stone-ground flour and cornmeal properly. As this was being done Ron Beck was peeling a mountain of potatoes outside. As early as 6:00 a.m. the first pot of coffee was started and the salami and onions were peeled and sliced; juice was prepared, pineapple and grapefruit made ready in portions, and Joan Bragdon's six apple pies were cut and set out. But they say "self praise is no recommendation" so let a visitor, Fran Green, tell it like it was. See her report elsewhere in the News.

The hikers were able to get off at almost the appointed hour for the hike up the mountain, where Herb Ogden had agreed to meet all who made it with both hard and sweet cider.

After clean-up, three of our families who didn't get to meet Herb Ogden on top of Mt. Ascutney did enjoy a visit to the Ogden's Mill.

Herb was not there, of course, but his wife graciously showed us through the mill, which is ingeniously planned to use the water from the brook to turn the huge 13' x 6' overshot water wheel, both for pressing cider and grinding grain, as well as turning a generator for making some electricity for their own use. The water comes through a two-foot penstock from a canal at the head of the meadow, only about two feet higher than where it comes out at the mill in a standpipe behind the mill wheel. It is not necessary to screen this flow because trash just goes over the wheel and out.

Mrs. Ogden also showed us through their most interesting house, with its soapstone stoves, stencilled parlor walls—surrounding Herb's two printing presses—and man-sized kitchen-family room. She also regaled us with both sweet and hard cider that we didn't get on top of Ascutney. This product of wild, unsprayed apples, is something else again, as is the cider vinegar from the same kind of fruit. The cider vinegar and some grist from the mill we were glad to be able to purchase as most enjoyable souvenirs of our Vermont sojourn.

LONG TRAIL PATROL REPORT, JUNE 1970

The Long Trail Patrol started the 1970 season on Monday, June 22, after putting in several hours sharpening tools, buying supplies, and getting the Patrol truck in running condition again. The boys then started for Barrows Camp which they made their headquarters for the first week out.

The Trail was covered from French Camp to Larraway Mountain during the first week. The boys reported The Trail seems to be in quite good shape for the most part and required only a good brushing.

At this time the work schedule isn't too heavy, so the Patrol may have more time to improve trails. This would mean constructing waterbars, improving drainage, and putting in some corduroy walks and small bridges in the wet areas.

We have three boys working this summer; Dave Rice, who will act as leader; Bob Hirss, who also worked last summer; and a new member, Bob Tufts from Scarsdale, New York. We feel we have a good Patrol again this summer and look forward to a productive season.

As soon as work is finished to the Canadian Border the Patrol will shift to other areas—wherever help is needed. There is a possibility that clearing will be done on the Appalachian Trail from Sherburne Pass to Route 12.

Any section needing help on trails and shelters please contact us so that we can plan our schedule for the summer.

We hope to see you on the trail this summer. Good hiking to you! JACK HARRINGTON, Chairman

PROTECTION FOR HIGHER ELEVATIONS

An informed and alert citizenry is needed now for proper implementation of legislation passed this spring by the Vermont General Assembly. This environmental legislation includes protection of the fragile higher elevations of Vermont from unwise and destructive development. As of June 1, 1970, a permit, to be issued only if certain criteria are met, is required before any new development can take place above the elevation of 2500 feet (Vermont Act 250).

Applications for permits will be handled by the district environmental commissions. Citizens should be alert to such applications (notice of which must be published in a local paper), be sure hearings are held and sufficient information is provided for adequate evaluation of applications. Hearings may be requested within 15 days of newspaper notice.

The Profile Committee has prepared a list of "Guidelines for Citizen Action." It is setting up a list of resource people in various parts of the state and also contact persons to whom inquiries and concerns regarding mountain areas can be directed.

Members of the Green Mountain Club, with your knowledge of the mountain areas, can play an important role in protecting higher elevations and your assistance would be especially welcomed. For information, write

WE FOUND THE THREE MUSKETEERS!

The folder, "Hike the Long Trail," published by the Central Vermont Railway in 1927 (a real collector's item), had a short article, with photograph, about "The Three Musketeers."

These three girls—Hilda M. Kurth and Kathleen M. Norris, of Schenectady, New York, and Catherine E. Robbins of Cornwall—hiked the "300 miles of The Long Trail," leaving Massachusetts on July 25, 1927 and arriving at the Canadian Line on August 25. Even though the northern part of The Long Trail was not yet completed, they DID go all the way to Canada.

Through some phone calls and correspondence, we found The Three Musketeers! Hilda Kurth Martin still resides in Schenectady, Catherine Robbins Clifford lives in Brandon, and Kathleen Norris Rowlands lives in Poultney. And the three of them still keep in touch and reminisce about their adventure of 43 years ago.

So for our 60th Anniversary Meeting, we just HAD to invite them to be our guests! We were 2/3 successful. Hilda Martin and Catherine Clifford were able to attend our meeting. We were sorry that Kathleen Rowlands was unable to join us.

During the afternoon session, Ben Rolston, our habitual coordinator, gave a short chat about the 1927 write-up of the trip of The Three Musketeers, and asked those present to come forward. Then End-to-End Chairman John Nuffort took over. After an official welcome from the End-to-End Committee, John presented Mrs. Martin and Mrs. Clifford with Pioneer End-to-End Certificates, and gave them a certificate to be relayed by them to Mrs. Rowlands.

After the meeting, a scrapbook of the 1927 adventure drew much attention from those who were able to chat with the two Musketeers. Madeline Miles then called us outside, and Chairman Nuffort, Catherine Clifford and Hilda Martin posed for the accompanying photograph (thanks, Madeline), with Rolston somehow managing to sneak into the picture also.

To our End-to-Enders of 43 years ago, thank you for your valuable contribution to our special Anniversary Meeting—and please join us again. And we hope to meet Kathleen Rowlands at a future meeting too.
