"'83 Quetico Expedition A Success"

    On July 30, 1983, 24 GMCers and friends gathered at the Essex Junction train station and waited in the intermittent sprinkle for the 7:30 Montrealer. The train arrived at 9:00 and later spent a full hour at the border for customs inspection.

    From this dubious beginning came a very enjoyable trip. Montreal saw the group scatter to all corners; Old Montreal Notre Dame, Mount Royal, and the Botanical Gardens. By 4:30 we had received special boarding consideration so we could sit as a group going to Toronto. There we made a midnight switch to the Day/Niter we'd ride to Thunder Bay, Ontario. A highlight of the train trip was the Vista Dome observation car, offering excellent views of the scenery and of the train itself.

    A school bus awaited us at 1 am in Thunder Bay for the last leg of our journey to the outfitter, Quetico North, in Atikokan, Ont. Arriving there at 3 am we finally met our outfitter, Bernie Cox. Offered coffee and snacks, we opted for beds instead.

    After breakfast we received our packs, tents, sleeping bags, 50# food packs; picked out paddles, life vests, canoes, bought maps and other gear; and filled a pick-up truck 3 times for the last motorized transport we'd have for 11 days.

    We barely had our canoes in the water of French Lake when we sighted a bald eagle flying low overhead, and loons swimming nearby. Loons were frequent sights, and mergansers, too. Larger wildlife was rare. Only Ralph saw a moose; he had to be on the water at 6 am to see her. The only bear seen was by Joanne, and that was from the train!

    Our first lunch, on Pickerel Lake, was an experience. We hadn't thought to bring a copy of the menu and so had virtually no idea what we had for lunch or where in our 12 food packs it was kept. Consequently, the first night was devoted to searching through all the food to familiarize ourselves with our supplies and its organization. A two week food supply never looks like enough, and always weighs too much.

    After 3 days it was apparent that we should split up and let the faster paddles go their own way. Pete and Sue led a group of 8 on a longer course, doing about 85 miles with 18 portages. The other 16 people did about 65 miles and had 11 portages. One group of 4 canoes hand-held together once hoisted a tarp-sail for a fast ride on Quetico Lake.

## ANNUAL GMC SOLICITATION

In the latter half of October, GMC members were mailed the Club's solicitation letter. This annual request for contributions was established in the mid-1970's, a particularly difficult time for the Club financially because of an inflationary economy and because of the immediate need for more trail management and maintenance in the face of steadily increasing trail usage.

So successful has this once-a-year solicitation been over the years that it is one of the major reasons why the Club's overall financial position has improved. Last year, for instance, GMC members responded with contributions totalling $11,298!

Now, for the first time in four years, the Club faces the possibility of a budget deficit (due primarily to the delayed availability of the Day Hiker's Guide to Vermont). A positive response from the Club's membership will avert this possibility and help maintain our improved finances.

If you have not responded to the solicitation letter, please reconsider. To those members who have already responded, the Club extends its most sincere thanks and appreciation.

## LESTER M. VIELLE

On August 30, Lester Vielle, 66 died at the Doxy Hatch Medical Center, Salt Lake City, Utah. He had been there near one of his three daughters for a year for treatment of Alzheimer's disease.

Lester, an active leader and trails maintainer in both the parent club and Worcester Section G.M.C. from 1970 through 1982, had been a GMC Trustee from 1977-1980, on GMC's Trails & Shelters Committee 1978 & 1979, was co-chairman of the 1975 Intersectional held at the Chesterfield Boy Scout reservation, Chesterfield Mass., and a very active trails and shelters volunteer for 10 years, often with the Boy Scouts he led.

Lester, and his wife Barbara (who died during the 1977 GMC Annual Meeting at Lake Dunmore) completed hiking the Long Trail in the 1970's. He was active in the planning and development of the Mass. Mid-State Trail of 80 miles and a Scout leader 1964-1979.

Worcester Section GMC has lost its president of 1974-1976, its local Trails & Shelters chairman of 1978-1979, and a determined, energetic worker who reluctantly was forced to end his busy trail maintenance only when his progressive disease made work no longer possible in 1982.

by ANN HUMES
Worcester Section

WINTTURI SHELTER DEDICATION

Over 60 GMC'ers gathered on the Appalachian Trail outside Woodstock to honor Mauri Wintturi, a devoted Club member who passed away on January 18, 1982, through the re-naming of Gulf Shelter. Doug Tescher spoke stirringly of his many years of friendship with Mauri, and Harry Woodbury described the original construction of the Shelter, which he and Murray Stevens supervised in the early 1960's. HIGHLIGHTS OF THE 1983 INTERSECTIONAL

Goshen, Vt. - Sixty-six of us arrived at Camp Thorp on Saturday and made ourselves right at home, and with summer weather the sight of the pool called for a swim. With the exception of a rainy Monday morning, the sunny weather continued throughout the week, keeping the pool busy and the campers happy.

Full-days from Monday through Saturday were filled with hikes on the Long Trail, nature hikes, a trip to an ice cave, canoe trips, and short day trips. We had two overnight hikes, Monday morning through Tuesday afternoon: one from Middlebury Gap to Lincoln Gap, overnight at Emily Proctor shelter; another from Lincoln Gap to Brandon Gap, overnight at Boyce shelter. The different hikes covered the Long Trail from Jonesville to U.S. Route 4, and from Killington to Route 140. One man finished the LT at the intersectional. Another completed all except Route 140 to Little Rock Pond, which he hiked Saturday evening and Sunday morning after the Directors Meeting of September 10th.

The evening activities started with Bingo Monday night (repeated several times during the week) followed by a campfire Tuesday night. This Campfiring-a-Long, led by the Explorers, was held deep in the woods. We left there with a feeling of peace.

A Busy Long Trail

around the trails in Vermont, particularly the stretch of the AT running from Sherburne Pass to the Barnard Gulf road.

Square Dance a Rousing Success

The high point of the week for many of us was the Wednesday night Square Dance. We had a great turnout, lively dancing, and everyone, from eight to eighty enjoyed themselves.

Thursday night Jan Abbott showed us beautiful slides of the Abbott family's trip to Utah and Arizona. Friday night we had our auction. The many items generously contributed by the campers found new homes through the skill of our auctioneer Leonard Dailey, and the able assistance of the Explorers. Saturday night dinner - the Meade Brothers did it again. Country style pork ribs, fresh corn on the cob, tossed salad, and more, made a real banquet. It was good!

Attendance: 180 of us enjoyed this Intersectional. The Connecticut Section led with 18 members present, followed by Pioneer Valley with 15, At-Large 13, Montpelier 10, and Killington 9. A total of eleven Sections were represented, as well as the Explorers. We were happy to see Clint Banks and Leonard Dailey at camp again.

Shelter Dedicated to Mauri Wintturi

Sunday afternoon the memory of a friend of all of us, Mauri Wintturi was honored by a shelter dedication. One group hiked to Gulf Shelter from Stony Brook Shelter while the rest of us drove in. Under the auspices of the Ottauquechee and Pioneer Valley sections, this shelter was dedicated as the Mauri Wintturi Shelter. We learned a lot about Mauri from a life-long friend, as well as from others who knew him. Mauri's life was centered around the trails in Vermont, particularly the stretch of the AT running from Sherburne Pass to the Barnard Gulf road.

All of this activity was facilitated by the Intersectional Committee plus numerous volunteers, while the Explorers handled registration as well as many other jobs. We owe all these workers many thanks.

Now that you see what a good time we have at our intersectionals, come join us next year.

JOHN F. KIRBY

The portages were really much more than most of us expected. They were longer, rougher, and not large enough at either end for all 12 of our canoes. At one point there were 32 canoes from 4 groups converging at one portage, although we generally saw only a few other people each day.

Offsetting that was the serenity of the wilderness, the absence of motors, the frequent swimming stops, and some good fish to round out the freeze dried meals. Everyone had at least one fish dinner. Among the catch were pickerel, walleye pike, bass, and northern pike.

The weather was a delight. There were a few showers as we searched for our second day's camp, but Marshall got a fire started anyway. Then on the 10th day it poured several times. At one point we had to stop and drain the canoes. We were already wet so paddling at least kept us warm. On we went, until the lightning started. That came quite fast, almost as fast as we paddled for the nearest point of land, which just happened to be the largest campsite we'd seen yet, and was a welcome place to spend the night. The next morning was cloudy as we returned to the outfitters, but the afternoon was clear, sunny, and hot, as 8 of our 11 days had been.

Returning to civilization was the usual scramble to cold drinks, ice cream, fresh meat, and green salads. Capping that off was the entire group going out for crepes in Old Montreal, before getting a private car on Amtrak for the farewell leg of our excursion.

On Oct. 1, most of the group reunited for a barbeque and picture sharing marathon in Jericho, Vermont.

by ANDY SQUIRES
Excursion Chairman

ON BUILDING

The Brattleboro Section is pleased to announce the completion of Spruce Peak Shelter. The shelter marks more than one year of cooperation between the Section, the Manchester District, U.S. Forest Service, the Rutland Community Correctional Center, and the Youth Conservation Corps.

Spruce Peak Shelter has been built to replace Bromley Camp which suffers the effects of being located too close to a road. In the fall of 1982, members of the Section and the Forest Service succeeded in finding a site with water near the Long Trail on the ridge overlooking Manchester at a point 3½ miles south of Routes 11 and 30.

During the past winter, Mark Brown of the Brattleboro Section drafted plans for a 12' x 20' semi-enclosed log structure with a 4' x 20' front porch. Approval of the plans was granted by the Forest Service, and they proceeded to haul 9,000 board feet of red pine logs to a point near the building site. In order to minimize damage to the area, this was accomplished early in the spring on snow cover.

A crew from the Rutland Community Correctional Center (RCCC) worked through a cold, wet spring peeling the logs, and milling out lumber on a chain saw rig. Their efforts continued through the summer and fall, as they have opened up a large view, removed stumps, improved drainage, and helped expand the shelter site.

A SHELTER

The Brattleboro Section spent a total of eight weekends through the spring, summer and fall building the shelter. With the exception of the chain saws, all work was accomplished with hand tools. Most technical decisions, and many of the more refined saw cuts, were made by Mark Brown.

The first weekend saw the site cleared, foundation holes dug, stone pillars built, and the four sill logs installed. During this time, "Mark's Rule" was that if two workers could lift one of the foundation stones, it wasn't big enough! Almost as much stone lies below ground as above it.

On the subsequent weekend, two bags of concrete were packed in to solidify the foundation stones. One bag and a gallon of preservative helped to form a 90+ pound pack for 60+ year old section member Doug Riggs.

The logs were hauled by hand 200 yards to the building site with the assistance of log carriers. The largest logs, weighing close to 1,000 pounds, required ten members to drag and carry them to the site.

The logs were laid up in a painstaking process which used chain saws to level each successive course, and notch the ends. A transit was instrumental in assuring that all of the walls were level. BYLAWS
OF
THE GREEN MOUNTAIN CLUB, INC.

ARTICLE I - SEAL; REGISTERED OFFICE AND AGENT; FISCAL YEAR

1. Seal. The official corporate seal is an impression seal with the name of the corporation and the year and state of incorporation set forth thereon.

2. Registered Office and Agent. The registered office of the corporation is the principal office of the corporation, and the registered agent of the corporation is its Executive Director.

3. Fiscal year. The fiscal year of the corporation shall begin May 1.

ARTICLE II - MEMBERS AND THEIR MEETINGS

1. Classes of membership. The classes of members are as follows:
(a) Adults. Any person eighteen years of age or older, who either is an adult member of a section or has joined the corporation as an adult member-at-large, and whose dues to the corporation are not in arrears more than four months, is a voting member of the corporation.
(b) Juniors. Any person under eighteen years of age, who either is a junior member of a section or who has joined the corporation as a junior member-at-large, and whose dues to the corporation are not in arrears more than four months, is a non-voting junior member of the corporation and may not hold an elective office of the corporation.
(c) Organizational members. Any non-profit organization and any youth camp may join the corporation as a non-voting organizational member, designating one person to receive The Long Trail News.
(d) Institutional members. Any corporation or other business entity may join the corporation as a non-voting institutional member.

2. Dues. The membership dues of the corporation are as follows:
(a) Annual. The annual dues for each class of members, and for such subclasses of membership as the Board of Directors may designate, shall be set by the Board of Directors and may be changed from time to time. Any change shall take effect at the commencement of the fiscal year following publication in The Long Trail News.
(b) Life. Any junior or adult member may become a member of the corporation for life upon payment of a sum prescribed by the Board of Directors, and such sum may be changed from time to time for members joining for life thereafter.
(c) Delinquency. Any member whose dues to the corporation are in arrears more than four months after they are due to the corporation shall cease to be a member thereafter.

3. Annual meeting. The annual meeting of the members of the corporation shall be held on the Saturday preceding Memorial Day, or a different date in May if designated by the Board of Directors, and at such time and at such place within or without the State of Vermont, but preferably within the State of Vermont, as the Board of Directors designate. Advance notice of the date, time and place shall be given in The Long Trail News not less than ten days nor more than one hundred and twenty days before the date of the meeting.

4. Special meetings. Special meetings of the members of the corporation may be called by the President, the Board of Directors, or by members constituting one-tenth of the voting membership. Written notice shall be mailed or delivered to all members at least one week in advance, and such notice shall include the purpose or purposes for which the meeting is called.

5. Voting. Each voting member shall be entitled to one vote at any meeting of the members of the corporation. Proxy voting is prohibited.

6. Quorum. Thirty voting members shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business at any meeting of the members of the corporation.

ARTICLE III - BOARD OF DIRECTORS AND ITS MEETINGS

1. Function. The affairs of the corporation shall be managed by the Board of Directors.

2. Number. Directors shall be allocated at each annual meeting of the members of the corporation as follows:
(a) Sections. One for each hundred voting members or major fraction thereof with a minimum of one per section.
(b) Members-at-Large. One for each three hundred voting members or major fraction thereof.
(c) Governmental agencies. One each from the Forest Service of the U.S. Department of Agriculture and the Department of Forests, Parks and Recreation of the State of Vermont.
(d) Officers. Officers of the corporation other than the Auditor, if not otherwise directors, shall be ex-officio members of the Board of Directors.

3. Election. Directors shall be elected as follows:
(a) Sections. The membership of each section shall elect its directors, at the commencement of their terms or to fill vacancies, at an annual or special meeting, or the positions may be filled in a different manner if provided in the constitution or bylaws of the section. Each section may have an alternate director for each of its directorships who shall be elected in the same manner as a director.
(b) Members-at-Large. The members-at-large present at an annual meeting of the corporation shall elect the directors allocated to the members-at-large.
(c) Governmental agencies. The Board of Directors at its regular meeting shall elect a representative of each governmental agency entitled to director to serve as its director.

4. Term. Directors shall serve three-year terms, but the Board of Directors may shorten the term of any directorship for the purpose of staggering the expirations of directors' terms.

5. Eligibility. All directors shall be voting members of the corporation. Any director who has served six consecutive years shall not be eligible for re-election for a period of one year. These eligibility requirements do not apply to the directors from governmental agencies.

6. Regular meeting. The regular meeting of the Board of Directors shall be held immediately after the annual meeting of the members of the corporation at the same location. No notice need be given.

7. Special meetings. Special meetings of the Board of Directors may be called by the President or any seven directors provided that notice shall be mailed or delivered to each director at least one week in advance.

8. Quorum. One-third of the members of the Board of Directors shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business at any meeting of the Board of Directors.

ARTICLE IV - EXECUTIVE ADVISORY COUNCIL

1. Function. It shall advise the President.

2. Composition. It shall consist of not more than nine voting members of the corporation, at least two-thirds of whom shall be present or former directors, and shall include the President and Secretary.

3. Appointment. The President shall appoint the members of the Executive Advisory Council, subject to approval by the Board of Directors, as soon as possible after the annual meeting of the members of the corporation.

4. Term. Membership shall be for a term of one year with a limit of three consecutive terms.

5. Meetings. It shall meet at the call of the President with such advance notice to members of the Executive Advisory Council, directors and the president of each section as the President deems appropriate.

ARTICLE V - OFFICERS

1. Number. The officers of the corporation shall be the President, Vice President, Treasurer, Secretary and Auditor.

2. Annual election. The officers of the corporation other than the Auditor shall be elected annually by the Board of Directors at their regular meeting, and they shall serve until their successors are elected and qualified. The Auditor shall be elected annually by the members of the corporation at their annual meeting, and this officer shall serve until his or her successor is elected and qualified.

3. Vacancy. Any vacancy in an officer position may be filled for the balance of the term of the office by election of the Board of Directors.

4. Eligibility. The President, Vice President and Treasurer shall be voting members who are serving or have served at any time as directors of the corporation. The Auditor shall not serve simultaneously as another officer or as a director of the corporation. No officer other than the Secretary shall serve in the same office for more than three consecutive terms.

5. President. The President shall supervise the activities of the corporation and preside at the meetings of members, the Board of Directors and the Executive Advisory Council. The President

shall execute contracts and conveyances on behalf of the corporation and shall act on behalf of the Board of Directors in all matters under its jurisdiction.

6. Vice President. The Vice President shall assist the President in the supervision of the activities of the corporation and shall perform the duties of the President when the President is disabled. The Vice President also shall be in charge of the tangible property of the corporation.

7. Treasurer. The Treasurer shall be in charge of the receipt and disbursement of all funds of the corporation pursuant to the instructions of the Board of Directors. Correct and complete financial records shall be maintained under the direction of the Treasurer. The Treasurer shall prepare periodic financial reports, including an annual financial report, and also shall be responsible for filing all tax returns of the corporation.

8. Secretary. The Secretary shall keep accurate minutes of all meetings of the members, the Board of Directors and the Executive Advisory Council. The Secretary shall be in charge of all corporate records other than financial records. The Secretary also shall maintain the membership records of the corporation and shall be responsible for filing with the proper governmental officials all documents, other than tax returns, which the corporation is required by law to file.

9. Auditor. The Auditor shall audit the financial records of the corporation and shall make a report as of the last day of each fiscal year.

ARTICLE VI - COMMITTEES

1. Standing committees. The standing committees of the corporation shall include:
(a) Budget and Finance
(b) Guide Books
(c) Membership
(d) Nominating
(e) Public Information
(f) Trails and Shelters
(g) Clubwide Activities

Each committee shall have such functions as are assigned to it from time to time by the Board of Directors.

2. Special committees. The Board of Directors may authorize the establishment and termination of special committees for temporary activities of the corporation.

3. Appointment. With the approval of the Board of Directors, the President shall appoint annually the membership and chairperson of all committees other than Nominating Committee as soon as possible after the annual meeting of the members of the corporation. The Nominating Committee shall be appointed annually in January by the President with the approval of the Board of Directors.

4. Nominating Committee. At the annual meeting of the members of the corporation or the regular meeting of the Board of Directors, the Nominating Committee shall present the name of at least one candidate for each office and each directorship which is to be filled by election at such meeting.

5. Trails and Shelters. All proposals for the construction or relocation of trails or shelters shall be subject to review by the Trails and Shelters Committee with prompt reports of its recommendations to the Board of Directors. No proposal for the construction or relocation of trails and shelters shall be undertaken until approved by the Board of Directors with consideration of any recommendation from the Trails and Shelters Committee. The trails and shelters of the corporation shall be only those which are approved by the Board of Directors.

ARTICLE VII - LOCAL SECTIONS OF CORPORATION

1. Establishment. Local chapters, to be known in each instance as a section, may be established from time to time by the Board of Directors upon application.

2. Structure. A section may be organized as an unincorporated association or as a nonprofit corporation. Each section shall make provision for the disposition of its assets upon disestablishment or dissolution which shall be consistent with Paragraph 6 of this Article. Each section shall have at least a President and a Secretary for officers and a Trails and Shelters Committee among its committees.

3. Membership. Each section shall control eligibility for membership in it provided that no person may be denied membership by reason of sex, race, creed, color or national origin. On May 1 of each year the Secretary of each section shall provide the corporation with a report of the names and complete mailing addresses of all members by classes of membership, and such report shall be supplemented as to any changes at least at the close of the fiscal year of the section or at the date the fiscal year of the section is closed, whichever is later. Other corporate dues collected by sections shall be remitted to the corporation within thirty days of receipt.

4. Corporate dues. Each section shall remit annually on January 1 to the corporation the annual corporate dues of its members based on the number and classification of its members reported the preceding May 1 or the preceding date on which the fiscal year of the section closed, whichever is later. Other corporate dues collected by sections shall be remitted to the corporation within thirty days of receipt.

5. Field duties. Each section shall maintain to standards set by the Board of Directors the trails and shelters assigned to, and accepted by, it.

6. Assets and liabilities. Each section shall control its own funds and other assets, and the corporation shall not be liable for the debts of any section. If a section is disestablished or dissolved, it shall transfer forthwith to the corporation all of its tangible and intangible property situated in the State of Vermont which pertains to trails or shelters having the approval of the Board of Directors. Any other assets remaining at the time of disestablishment or dissolution after the payment of debts shall be transferred to the corporation unless the section has arranged to transfer such assets to a different corporation which has qualified under Section 501(c)(3) of the U.S. Internal Revenue Code.

7. Activity reports. The President of each section shall report orally or in writing the membership and activities of his or her section at the annual meeting of the members of the corporation.

8. Disestablishment. A section may be disestablished by a two-thirds vote at any regular or special meeting of the Board of Directors if its membership has fallen below ten or the section is deemed to have failed to perform its responsibilities to the corporation. Notice of such proposed action shall be mailed or delivered by the President to both the President and Secretary of the affected section at least thirty days in advance of the meeting of the Board of Directors.

ARTICLE VIII - AMENDMENTS AND PROCEDURE

1. By Directors. The Board of Directors shall have the power to amend or repeal the Bylaws by a unanimous vote at any special meeting, provided that notice of the proposed action is given in the call for the meeting.

2. By members. The members of the corporation shall have the power to amend or repeal the Bylaws by a two-thirds vote of those present and voting at any meeting of the membership, provided that notice of the proposed action is given in the call for the meeting.

3. Procedure. Unless otherwise provided in the Bylaws, all matters of parliamentary procedure shall be governed by Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised (1970).
